# Safe manipulation progress and robustness

**Both shortlisted directions address real limitations: a protected robot can stop making progress, and a successful controller can become unsafe when physical conditions change.** Task continuation is the narrower agent-focused question if avoidable stalls occur in an accessible task. Physical robustness offers a more controllable source of difficulty if parameter changes and outcome definitions can be isolated. Existing research supplies credible remedies and evolutionary precedents for both, so neither requires inventing a new planning architecture. The opportunity is to understand a consequential failure and demonstrate a bounded improvement against appropriate alternatives. UNISafe provides a promising shared foundation, but its released assets have not yet established either failure mechanism or a measured development budget. This review, dated **8 October 2026**, compares research questions; [oracle.md](../../../oracle.md) records the chosen research focus.

## Intervention changes what the task policy must learn

Consider an **illustrative** robot extracting the middle block from a small tower. Its task policy starts pulling; a safety mechanism predicts that the upper block will fall and redirects the arm. The tower survives, but the gripper now approaches from an unfamiliar angle. The task policy repeats the rejected pull, waits, or moves ineffectively until time expires. The desired outcome is continued useful manipulation under the safety constraint. This example does not assert that the released UNISafe controller exhibits that exact sequence.

UNISafe (CoRL 2025) reports normal block-plucking outcomes that depend on the task policy. With its diffusion policy, filtering changes safe success/failure/incompletion from **52/44/4% to 57/15/28%**. With its Dreamer policy, the change is **58/41/1% to 72/20/8%**. Protection accompanies more unfinished episodes, but these aggregate results do not establish why. Evaluation denominators were not established in the inspected table, so percentages should not be converted into trial counts. ([UNISafe, Table 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779))

The September 2026 preprint *When World Models Lie* gives a more specific explanation. In a different simulated arm task, placing a globe beside a vase, its authors attribute some remaining incompletions to a nominal policy untrained on states reached after safety intervention. This is an author-supported motivation for studying continuation, rather than controlled evidence that all such states permit completion. Its task and models differ from the released block-plucking system. ([Section V-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34300))

### A stall is an outcome, not a diagnosis

Our synthesis separates several mechanisms. The policy can lack competence in the new configuration, or keep proposing actions that its filter rejects. Alternatively, the filter can reject useful safe actions because its value estimates are too conservative. Prediction errors, missing contact information, incorrect action history and an inadequate time limit can produce similar symptoms. Some configurations might offer no admissible completion, although unsuccessful attempts do not establish impossibility. These distinctions determine whether the useful intervention belongs in the task policy, safety mechanism, model or task specification.

The practical evidence should therefore connect intervention to subsequent behaviour. Does progress resume? Does control repeatedly switch? Does the policy propose essentially the same rejected command? Does a longer allowance merely complete a slow detour? Intervention count alone cannot distinguish these cases. Conditional success among intervened episodes is also descriptive: different controllers can trigger interventions on different sets of difficult episodes. Strong causal attribution needs comparable situations and consistent controller memory, rather than interpreting every timeout as failed handover.

### Existing remedies act on different components

**Improving the filter itself is already effective.** The November 2025 LatentCBF preprint evaluates a fixed diffusion policy lifting an open candy bag, with 60 trials per method across three initial configurations. Its reported aggregate failure/stall/success changes from **0/62/38%** with least-restrictive filtering to **0/20/80%** with LatentCBF. The hardest configuration still stalls in 60% of trials. Its smoother intervention and mixed nominal/safety-policy training improve the safety value, while the nominal task policy remains separately trained. These results motivate comparing better filtering with better task behaviour. ([LatentCBF, sections 5-7 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18606))

**Training the task policy with its filter present is established.** Recovery RL, published at RA-L/ICRA 2021, combines a task learner, learned safety critic and backup controller, including simulated object extraction. It demonstrates that intervention and task learning can be composed. The method's name does not impose an irreversibility analysis or new recovery-label programme on this project. Its relevance is how the two controllers learn and exchange control. ([Recovery RL, sections IV-V](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.15920))

The filtered-MDP perspective explains a particularly important baseline: the agent proposes an action, the fixed filter changes it, and ordinary RL learns the resulting rewards and transitions of that composed environment. *Provably Optimal RL under Safety Filtering*, reported at IASEAI 2026, formalizes this. Its theorem assumes stationary measurable dynamics and filter, bounded rewards, safe initialization, and a perfect filter on the maximal controlled-invariant safe set: it always returns safe actions and leaves all safe actions unchanged. Convergence transfers only from an RL algorithm already known to converge on the relevant stationary discounted MDP class, with the same filter retained at deployment. **This is neither finite-data convergence for arbitrary deep RL nor a guarantee for an approximate latent filter.** Its experiments use Safety Gymnasium, not visual contact-rich manipulation. ([Definitions 2-3, Assumption 1 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18082))

Residual adaptation offers another manageable route. *Residual RL from Demonstrations* freezes a visual base policy and learns an additive correction using visual features, proprioception and the base action. Its simulated manipulation results establish a useful alternative to retraining an entire controller, without settling compatibility with a learned safety filter. Separately, *Reducing Safety Interventions* studies proactive safe replacement and projection during policy training in human-robot reaching. Together, these suggest comparing compact behavioural adaptation, ordinary filtered fine-tuning and improved action correction before attributing a gain to evolution. ([Residual RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08050); [intervention reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03339))

The data semantics matter. A task critic can intentionally learn the consequences of a **proposed action** after filtering, whereas a physical dynamics model must associate the next observation with the **executed action**, expressed in its trained coordinates. Recovery RL makes this distinction explicitly between its task and safety buffers. Recording proposal, filtered command and final applied command separately preserves both interpretations. Replacing every proposal label with the executed command would be wrong for a learner whose action means a proposal to the composed system. ([Recovery RL, Algorithm 1 and section IV-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.15920))

The unresolved practical question is consequently precise: **how much useful task competence can an existing controller acquire under an imperfect learned filter, for a limited amount of additional experience?** Sparse rewards, unfamiliar contact states and fallible safety estimates make this worth investigating. Whether they defeat straightforward filtered fine-tuning in the available task remains unknown.

## Physical robustness depends on what the robot can know

Consider a second **illustrative** block-plucking task. The blocks look unchanged, but the top block is heavier or the contact friction differs. A familiar pull now produces a different motion. The robot might succeed through a broadly tolerant strategy, infer the change from contact history, or require a better predictor. Those are different solutions to different information problems. The scientific target should be a declared family of physical changes, rather than unrestricted generalization to arbitrary objects.

UNISafe's harder evaluation varies size, weight and friction; its diffusion-policy combination reports **38% safe success, 31% failure and 31% incompletion**. The paper also acknowledges unreliable filtering for entirely different tower configurations. This shows headroom without identifying the responsible component. ([UNISafe, appendices D.2 and E.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779))

Mass, inertia and centre of mass can change behaviour without an obvious image change. Friction depends on the surfaces and contact direction. Geometry changes both physical interactions and observations, while initial pose changes the starting configuration without necessarily changing the dynamics law. Lighting and background are observation shifts, not automatically physical shifts. Constant unknown parameters, sudden changes and external disturbances should therefore remain separate cases. This categorization is an analytical framework for defining the question, not a claim that every category defeats the chosen controller.

### Robust behaviour and adaptation use different information

| Approach | What changes during deployment? | What must be demonstrated? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed feedforward robust behaviour | Actions depend on current observations; weights remain fixed | One strategy tolerates the declared variation without identifying it |
| Frozen recurrent adaptation | Hidden state changes through observation/action history | History supplies useful information, rather than merely extra capacity |
| Explicit identification or conditioning | Estimated physical parameters or a belief inform control | Available interactions identify decision-relevant differences |
| Model or policy weight adaptation | Parameters update from new experience | Improvement survives update cost, data limits and safety-evaluator changes |

These distinctions have established precedents. EPOpt, ICLR 2017, trains on the worst-return fraction of trajectories from sampled physics models, approximating a conditional value-at-risk objective. It seeks robust performance without requiring true parameters at execution. RARL, ICML 2017, instead trains against adversarial disturbances. Neither low-tail return nor adversarial training alone establishes a separately specified physical failure-rate bound. ([EPOpt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.01283); [RARL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/pinto17a.html))

OpenAI's Rubik's Cube work supplies a manipulation precedent for memory-based adaptation: a recurrent policy trained under automatic domain randomization changes hidden state while retaining fixed weights. Its analyses include memory resets and changes during execution, exposing an adjustment period. Thus, **frozen weights do not imply frozen knowledge**. Conversely, the existence of recurrent state does not establish that it has identified the relevant physics. ([Rubik's Cube, section 9](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.07113))

Explicit calibration and model adaptation offer further alternatives. SimOpt uses real rollouts to update simulator distributions and retrain policies, including drawer opening and peg insertion. *Learning to Adapt* contrasts gradient updates to dynamics with recurrent model adaptation before MPC. These methods provide useful comparisons when experience can reveal the change, although their results are not proof of safe visual manipulation under a learned filter. The acquisition and computation used for adaptation belong in the comparison. ([SimOpt](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.05687); [GrBAL/ReBAL](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.11347))

### A latent uncertainty estimate is not a physics simulator

A frozen predictor receiving the same latent state and action produces the same conditional prediction law, irrespective of an unseen mass assigned by the experimenter. It can represent alternative physics only through a suitable conditioning interface or informative histories that change its internal belief, to the extent learned. **Adding Gaussian latent noise does not establish a model of persistent friction or mass variation.** This is an inference about the proposed evaluation interface, not an objection to uncertainty modelling itself.

Identification can also be ambiguous. DROPO reports that forward pushing weakly identifies lateral friction, while position-controlled motion can weakly identify mass. Different parameter settings can explain similar observations. An estimated parameter need not be numerically correct to support useful control, but an optimizer cannot infer information that the chosen interactions never reveal. ([DROPO, section 4.3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.08434))

Robust planning and adaptive filtering already address parts of this problem. MPC-RCE learns a transition ensemble and cost classifier, then searches action sequences using worst sampled costs and feasible elites. Its Safety Gym evidence does not make an ensemble an exhaustive family of physical worlds. *When World Models Lie* instead adapts safety assessment using observed latent prediction errors and pessimistic value evaluation. It does not demonstrate that online dynamics-weight retraining is necessary; the authors acknowledge reactive response, calibration mismatch and absent formal safety guarantees. ([MPC-RCE](https://learn-to-race.org/workshop-sl4ad-icml2022/assets/papers/paper_16.pdf); [adaptive latent filtering](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34300))

**A correction to the earlier opportunity report is necessary.** *Safety filtering of robotic manipulation under environment uncertainty* evaluates uncertain mass/friction using a physics simulator and selected critical transitions. However, its authors lack a suitable fallback policy and manually emulate probing by changing the parameter distribution. It demonstrates a safety-evaluation pipeline, not a complete closed-loop robot that performs the proposed physical probing. Its assumptions about contact information and simulator accuracy also differ from a visual latent model. ([Sections III-IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12674))

The manageable remaining question is whether behaviour can preserve safe completion across a meaningful, bounded physical range when the learned safety system is imperfect. If history already carries enough information, controller adaptation could suffice. If the necessary information is absent, or the model fails throughout the range, the project becomes an identification, sensing or model-learning study. That is a valid direction, but a different entry burden.

## Evolution can support a substantive empirical contribution

Evolution has a natural role when a compact behavioural change can be evaluated through the complete simulator/controller composition. Switching, clipping and contact events make such an objective awkward to differentiate directly. CMA-ES can search that space without differentiating through the simulator, but ordinary RL also learns without simulator derivatives. This is a reason to compare tools, not evidence that evolution must win.

Two direct precedents make this route more concrete. **Behavior Policy Learning**, published in Frontiers in Robotics and AI in 2022, learns a behaviour representation from state-only solution sketches and fine-tunes compact policies using CMA-ES. Evaluation averages sparse success across randomized starts and observation noise; the work includes simulated manipulation and preliminary physical transfer. It supplies a foundation for evolutionary task adaptation, although its objective is task success rather than explicit safety under hidden physical shifts. ([Behavior Policy Learning](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2022.974537/full))

**DROPO**, published in Robotics and Autonomous Systems in 2023, uses CMA-ES to fit simulator-distribution means and variances from offline transition likelihoods, then trains a task policy in that distribution. Its manipulation evidence includes Franka pushing. DROID previously used CMA-ES with joint-torque discrepancy for door opening. These are useful evolutionary system-identification foundations, distinct from directly evolving a task policy under a fixed latent filter. ([DROPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.08434); [DROID](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.11003))

For continuation, a bounded evolutionary role is adapting a limited part of existing task behaviour while evaluating the same safety-filtered controller. For robustness, it is evaluating candidate behaviour across real simulator parameter variants. A residual, task head or interpretable motion parameterization is an option to investigate, not a genotype selected by this review. Keeping the adaptation interface comparable matters more than choosing a fashionable optimizer. Ordinary filtered fine-tuning, domain-randomized adaptation and simple random search remain credible alternatives.

The world model can still be central when fitness comes from simulator outcomes. It can supply visual representation, recurrent state, predicted transitions and the safety evaluator. UNISafe's runtime path combines a predicted next latent with learned safety critics and uncertainty checks; it should not be described as full online trajectory search. If evolution is instead scored mainly inside imagination, candidate rankings inherit model errors and require independent simulator validation. ([UNISafe filtering source](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/blob/ab0fdf87f38edefa48b54a291ec5b7156c17b6e0/dreamerv3_torch/tools.py#L482))

Safe CEM and SafeDreamer already establish constrained search and feasibility-first planning. They are relevant when the selected method performs online planning, but that comparison differs from offline policy adaptation. Similarly, quality-diversity methods need a demonstrated use for alternative behaviours; maintaining diversity is not itself evidence of safety or robustness. ([Constrained CEM](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/34ffeb359a192eb8174b6854643cc046-Abstract.html); [SafeDreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3); [GuSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09743))

A worthwhile contribution can use a standard EA: explain a reproducible failure, improve actual safe completion under controlled information and budgets, and establish the limits. Measure success, physical violations and unfinished episodes together; fewer interventions alone can reward inactivity or weaker protection. Charge adaptation experience and discarded candidates, and separate model queries from simulator interactions and wall time. This would support an informative empirical claim. Merely replacing an optimizer, improving imagined scores or showing that out-of-distribution predictions fail would leave the central question unanswered. Publication suitability remains to be established from the resulting evidence.

## Available assets make the choice conditional

UNISafe provides the strongest verified combination here of manipulation task, visual world model, safety components and labelled trajectories. Its released archive contains the Dreamer task actor as well as the world model, and the evaluator loads a separate safety filter. Autonomous policy evaluation therefore does not depend on teleoperation. A matching diffusion-policy checkpoint was not verified, so the paper's diffusion-policy figures are not automatically the immediately reproducible baseline. ([Official release](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe); [policy/filter loading](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/blob/ab0fdf87f38edefa48b54a291ec5b7156c17b6e0/reachability/evaluate_reachability_filter.py#L162))

The shared cost is establishing a faithful modern baseline. The original Isaac Sim 4.2/PyTorch environment needs migration for a 5090, including rendering and controller behaviour. The previous audit supports a **32 GB GPU as a plausible profiling target**, not a measured training or evolutionary-evaluation budget. Exact snapshot branching and enough post-intervention training coverage have not been verified. Action normalization, episode boundaries and labels need consistent accounting before performance comparisons. ([Runtime specification](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/blob/ab0fdf87f38edefa48b54a291ec5b7156c17b6e0/environment.yaml); [preserved compute audit](UNISafe%20inference%20and%20training%20compute.md))

For robustness, an important source-level confound remains: **normal and hard tasks change physics, initial posture, outcome thresholds and reward definitions**. The release contains fixed mass/geometry settings and pose-reset events, rather than a ready isolated mass/friction randomization study. Comparing the two task names unchanged would mix physical difficulty with evaluation changes. A clean physical family needs verified parameters and consistent meanings of failure and completion. ([Normal configuration](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/blob/ab0fdf87f38edefa48b54a291ec5b7156c17b6e0/takeoff/takeoff_env_cfg.py); [hard configuration](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/blob/ab0fdf87f38edefa48b54a291ec5b7156c17b6e0/takeoff/hard_takeoff_env_cfg.py))

Alternatives exchange burdens. TD-MPC2 offers task-matched MetaWorld and ManiSkill checkpoints, but its released MetaWorld interface requires state observations and no matching safety-cost mechanism was verified. LeWM Cube preserves the visual-latent interest with public weights and goal-planning assets, while adding safety-definition, readout and label-validation work. These are concrete alternatives, not evidence of automatic checkpoint transfer. ([TD-MPC2 models](https://www.tdmpc2.com/models); [MetaWorld interface](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2/blob/main/tdmpc2/envs/metaworld.py); [LeWM Cube](https://huggingface.co/quentinll/lewm-cube/tree/main))

The newer papers do not currently remove those costs. LatentCBF's public repository documents a Dubins-car pipeline, rather than verified ready arm assets. The inspected *When World Models Lie* project source has a placeholder Code link. This is a limited availability finding, not a claim that the authors cannot supply additional material. ([LatentCBF repository](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/latent_cbf); [project link source](https://github.com/mudhdhoo/WhenWordModelsLie_project_page/blob/main/index.html#L47))

| Decision | Completion after intervention | Physical robustness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central question | Can the task agent continue competently under protection? | Can safe completion survive specified physical changes? |
| Best entry condition | Substantial, avoidable stalls in the available controller | Feasible variants cause repeatable degradation |
| Additional assets | Intervention trajectories and reliable action/history logging | Parameter controls, valid scenarios and consistent outcome definitions |
| Main attribution risk | Difficult states cause both intervention and failure | Physics changes affect several components simultaneously |
| Strong simple comparison | Ordinary fine-tuning with the same filter | Ordinary domain-randomized policy adaptation |
| Main scope expansion | Filter or perception repair instead of task learning | Identification, new sensing or world-model retraining |

Our conditional preference is continuation **if meaningful avoidable stalls exist**, because it isolates a narrower agent-side question. That condition has not been established in released block plucking. Robustness becomes the clearer choice if controlled physical changes produce an identifiable weakness while the same task and labels remain meaningful. Neither requires a deadline-driven choice or a completely new evolutionary algorithm.

## Conclusion

The productive next decision is which failure can be explained with the least ambiguity. Physical changes might expose poor continuation by provoking interventions, connecting the two directions, but that causal chain needs evidence. Keeping them conceptually separate makes it possible to discover that connection without designing the method around an assumed explanation.

This is a nonexhaustive, problem-focused literature and source review. No controller was run, no clean counterfactual replay was established, and no EA advantage was measured. The missing evidence concerns failure prevalence, available information, baseline effectiveness and evaluation cost. Those uncertainties define the boundary of the present recommendation; they do not require choosing a planner architecture before understanding the problem.
