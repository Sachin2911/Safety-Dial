# Target intervention failures for stronger contributions

**The most approachable opportunities concern diagnosing avoidable stalls and adapting a compact part of an existing filtered policy.** A controlled evolutionary-versus-RL comparison has the lowest marginal method burden. Intervention-guided evolutionary variation offers a stronger candidate mechanism, while evolving difficult scenarios offers an earlier diagnostic entry before adaptation exists. A small behaviour-library audit is worth considering if different continuations solve different failures. None is an established novelty gap: filtered fine-tuning, safety-informed mutation, evolutionary curricula and safe model-based repertoires already have close precedents. These recommendations reflect primary-source research available on **13 October 2026**, not experiments or a verified runnable system. They develop the conditional manipulation focus in [oracle.md](../../../oracle.md) and the ambition in [ideation.md](../../../ideation.md); they do not select an environment or method.

## Compact adaptation offers the clearest initial comparison

**The simplest credible honours contribution is an explanatory empirical study of compact adaptation under learned safety filtering.** The question is whether evolution offers useful safe-completion trade-offs under limited experience, and what causes them. An adapter is an implementation choice, not the novelty. Behavior Policy Learning already fine-tunes compact manipulation policies with CMA-ES; residual RL already learns additive corrections for contact-rich manipulation; SLowRL combines frozen policies, low-rank adaptation and learned recovery enforcement. CoFiT already fine-tunes pretrained humanoid controllers with active filtering, constraint context and intervention feedback. These precedents leave room for a controlled visual-manipulation study, but rule out presenting compactness or filter awareness alone as new. ([Behavior Policy Learning](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2022.974537/full), [Residual RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03201), [SLowRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17092), [CoFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02341))

The smallest decisive pilot would hold the pretrained representation and learned filter fixed, then compare evolution and ordinary filtered RL on the **same bounded action residual**, alongside the unchanged policy and random search. Start with full episodes from common starts. Measure whether improvements concern post-intervention recovery, earlier approaches that avoid disruption, or generic task competence. If intervention history is supplied, both learners need it, and both need an ablation without it. Sparse versus shaped reward can be informative, provided each reward variant is shared. Count the actual trainable parameters: neither a residual nor LoRA automatically makes evolutionary search small.

A substantive result would identify a reproducible regime where evolution retains existing competence while finding useful alternatives, supported by held-out starts and an explanation of the changed behaviour. **A win over only the frozen actor would be insufficient.** If matched RL does equally well or better throughout the declared regime and evolution reveals no distinct trade-off, the proposed evolutionary advantage is unsupported. A careful negative result remains useful for honours, but supplies less support for a GECCO method claim. This is the lowest marginal method burden after baseline reproduction; it is not a claim that end-to-end implementation is easy.

## Stalled interventions could guide evolutionary variation

**The strongest candidate EA mechanism uses intervention traces to decide how to vary behaviour.** The proposed distinction is between interventions that preserve progress and interventions followed by persistent loss of progress. Generic safety guidance is occupied territory. Safety-Informed Mutations stores unsafe states and uses output sensitivities to scale perturbations. Safe Mutations through Output Gradients preserves existing behaviour, where “safe” refers to behavioural disruption rather than physical protection. Guided ES shapes search using surrogate-gradient subspaces. CoFiT further establishes intervention-feedback features. A contribution therefore needs to show why *stall-specific* information improves variation beyond those signals. ([Safety-Informed Mutations](https://emarche.github.io/assets/pdf/GECCO2022_safemut.pdf), [Safe Mutations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06563), [Guided ES](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/maheswaranathan19a.html), [CoFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02341))

One pilot could reuse the compact adapter and collect proposal/correction sensitivities on stalled traces. A guided operator would bias perturbations toward changing those outputs while limiting disruption on independently collected successful traces, retaining some unguided exploration. Selection would use simulator task and safety outcomes. **A filter correction is an imperfect label**, not proof that a proposal was physically dangerous. The hypothesis is that this information locates useful behavioural changes, not that minimizing correction magnitude solves the task.

Compare the operator with ordinary ES or CMA-ES, random search, output-sensitive mutation on all visited states and Safety-Informed-Mutations-style weighting. If surrogate gradients guide the search, include a generic Guided-ES control. Give matched RL the same adapter, observations, feedback and reward information. Ablate stalled traces against all interventions, random traces and permuted stall labels. These comparisons distinguish a useful mechanism from additional data or a better interface. Backpropagation through the entire visual actor could increase burden; a small output adapter offers a narrower first test.

This route fails its intended claim if correction counts fall without better held-out safe completion, or if generic safety guidance and shuffled labels explain the gain equally well. It also fails if improvements depend on extra uncounted trace collection. **Its attraction is a specific search mechanism with an observable falsifier**, rather than guaranteed novelty. A targeted follow-up literature check would still be necessary after defining that operator.

## Evolved scenarios can diagnose failure before learning

**Scenario search is the cheapest diagnostic entry if ordinary reset controls already work.** Freeze the actor, model and filter, and search a small valid initial-condition space for sustained intervention accompanied by poor progress. Compare CMA-ES with uniform sampling at equal total simulator steps. Separate progress, interventions and physical outcomes in the logs rather than treating every timeout as the same failure. This can establish whether there is a worthwhile continuation problem before implementing a learner.

The surrounding literature is substantial. Adaptive Stress Testing already searches for failure trajectories; BEACON combines Bayesian optimization and CMA-ES for control-system falsification; CaDRE frames diverse safety-critical scenarios as QD; ACCEL evolves high-regret environments for curricula. **Finding difficult scenes with evolution is therefore not itself a contribution.** The narrower question is whether intervention/progress information discovers *avoidable continuation failures* more efficiently, and whether those cases teach useful behaviour beyond generic difficulty sampling. ([Adaptive Stress Testing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02188), [BEACON](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05925v2), [CaDRE](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13208), [ACCEL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/parker-holder22a.html))

The decisive evidence is a positive witness: an alternative behaviour completes a discovered case safely under the same filter and outcome definitions. Where validated restoration permits, compare continuations after the same history. Otherwise use matched full episodes and state the weaker causal scope. Extend the original policy's horizon as a diagnostic control. Check whether the apparent stall instead comes from prediction error, missing history, overly restrictive intervention or an unavailable safe route. Unsuccessful finite recovery search does not establish unavoidability.

A curriculum contribution is a later option. Give both evolutionary adaptation and ordinary RL the same discovered cases, then compare with uniform starts, generic hard-state selection and replay prioritization. Charge discovery and witness search to total cost; a shared precomputed curriculum can isolate selection benefits only if its acquisition cost remains visible. Search-selected cases are unsuitable for estimating deployment violation rates, which need independent held-out sampling. Deprioritize the method if random sampling finds equally useful cases, scenes violate the task's validity constraints, witnesses remain absent, or adaptation gains disappear against generic curricula and equal total interactions. Scenario search can still provide an informative diagnostic even when it does not justify a new optimizer or curriculum.

## A tiny library tests whether diversity survives filtering

**A repertoire becomes attractive only when behaviours have complementary successes.** Distinct proposals can be mapped by a filter into the same executed recovery command, making nominal diversity redundant. A possible contribution is explaining this diversity collapse and retaining alternatives that remain distinct after filtering. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated gap.

The closest work sets a high bar. RF-QD uses learned-model repertoire selection for safe autonomous exploration and recovery. QDAC-MB builds on DreamerV3 for diverse adaptable skills. GuSS uses learned-model random shooting and MAP-Elites under safety constraints. QD-Plan already detects stalls, rolls back and switches archived embodied planning behaviours. The proposed manipulation distinction would be continuing the original physical task through a learned filter, without physical rollback, and showing why executed diversity matters. ([RF-QD](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.03655), [QDAC](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/grillotti24a.html), [GuSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09743), [QD-Plan](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08523))

Before building QD, evaluate a small fixed bank of adapters across difficult contexts. Compare the best individual with an oracle chooser over the bank to measure headroom. **Oracle selection is a diagnostic upper bound**, not a deployable controller. If headroom exists, compare equal-size banks selected by executed diversity, proposed diversity, random subsampling and ordinary clustering. Compare selectors with a simple continuation rule and an RL option selector using identical observable history. Count candidate generation and selector training, not just archive size. Abandon the repertoire argument if one adapter closes the headroom, clustering matches the proposed descriptor, or useful selection requires privileged state unavailable during deployment. This audit is smaller and more informative than committing immediately to a world-model QD stack.

## Screening and generic robustness have heavier overlap

World-model candidate screening deserves a ranking diagnostic before a method commitment. DA-QD already searches imagined repertoires and simulator-evaluates selected skills; SafeDreamer already performs constrained search in imagination. The useful remaining question is whether the available manipulation model ranks bounded adaptations accurately enough to save interactions without rejecting novel safe behaviour. Test a common candidate pool in both imagination and simulation first, measuring rank agreement and false rejection or acceptance. A faithful imagined filtered controller can require substantial additional engineering. This is secondary to establishing an actual adaptation benefit. ([DA-QD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08522), [SafeDreamer](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=tsE5HLYtYg))

Bounded model-mismatch evaluation is a useful extension, while generic safe evolutionary robustness faces strong prior overlap. SMC-ES couples ES with statistical model checking. The latest version of **Safe and Robust Neural Policy Learning with Statistical Verification for Sim-to-Real Deployment in Robotics**, dated 2 October 2026, integrates scenario-based ES, verification and operating-region expansion; its first version used the LyEvO name. Filter-parameter optimization also has direct Safe Barrier Bayesian Optimization precedent. A narrower extension would ask whether adaptation changes visitation toward reliably or confidently incorrectly predicted states. It needs controlled task definitions and independent physical outcomes, not transferred certificates. ([SMC-ES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15003), [current robustness paper, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06481v2), [Safe Barrier Bayesian Optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19349))

## Conclusion

The practical ordering is **compact matched adaptation for the simplest empirical study, stall-guided variation for the clearest EA mechanism, and scenario search for early diagnosis with a conditional curriculum extension**. The small-library audit is an alternative if complementary continuations emerge. These are choices to investigate, not a replacement implementation plan. A working manipulation baseline remains the shared cost: no candidate system was verified runnable here, and the [UNISafe compute audit](UNISafe%20inference%20and%20training%20compute.md) does not establish throughput, valid restoration or avoidable-stall prevalence.

Across every choice, record safe completion, physical violations and unfinished episodes together. Charge all simulator interactions, including discarded candidates and adaptation data, and report model queries and elapsed time separately. Preserve proposed, filtered and executed actions; physical dynamics histories need executed actions, while a filtered-environment task critic can intentionally model proposals. A fixed learned filter does not establish unchanged safety after adaptation. With zero violations in n independent episodes, the approximate 95% upper rate bound is 3/n. The contribution will come from identifying a consequential mechanism and testing it convincingly, not from assembling three components or assuming GECCO acceptance. Detailed evidence is preserved in the [adaptation](../research_notes/Adjacent%20opportunities%20for%20safe%20evolution/intervention_adaptation.md), [library](../research_notes/Adjacent%20opportunities%20for%20safe%20evolution/behaviour_libraries.md) and [reliability](../research_notes/Adjacent%20opportunities%20for%20safe%20evolution/robustness_reliability.md) notes.
