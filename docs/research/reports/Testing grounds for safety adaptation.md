# Testing grounds for safety adaptation

**Broaden the research question to frozen pretrained controllers, including VLAs, while keeping LeWM as the opening experiment.** My strongest new candidate is SafeLIBERO with the released pi0.5-LIBERO policy. Block Plucking remains the existing candidate with the lowest setup cost, but its learned benefit is unresolved. ManiSkill2 peg insertion with a released Diffusion Policy is a useful fallback. Choose one main safety problem through bounded qualification, then add one cross-backbone test if the shared recipe works. The unit of selection is the combination of task, pretrained policy, safety monitor and adapter interface. An environment name alone does not establish an experiment.

**SafeLIBERO offers a concrete VLA problem: avoiding obstacles while retaining the ability to finish.** The released AEGIS benchmark contains 16 LIBERO-derived tasks with obstacles near targets or across travel paths. Its paper reports 57.8% task success and 17.1% collision avoidance for the full-action pi0.5 baseline, versus 67.5% and 68.9% for AEGIS. These are separate rates, not joint safe completion. Collisions do not terminate episodes. The authors also identify failures after safety corrections move the policy into unfamiliar states. That suggests a focused question: can a compact learned adapter sustain task progress through and after avoidance without task-specific recovery rules? ([AEGIS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11891), [released benchmark](https://github.com/THU-RCSCT/vlsa-aegis).)

The starting policy is already available: OpenPI releases `pi05_libero`, a checkpoint fine-tuned for LIBERO. It consumes camera images, proprioception and an instruction, and returns action chunks. Freeze that released checkpoint for the adaptation study; ordinary pretrained pi0.5 and this task-adapted checkpoint are different starting points. ([OpenPI](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi).)

Several details need qualification. SafeLIBERO's released collision check uses obstacle displacement as a proxy, so it need not detect every contact. Preserve the published metric and declare any additional contact audit separately. The landing page displays 18.1% baseline avoidance where the paper reports 17.1%. The released evaluator also differs from standard OpenPI LIBERO rendering, making reproduction of the exact camera and execution recipe important. AEGIS uses additional perception and geometry information; comparisons must disclose and match information access where appropriate. It supplies a strong existing safety reference, not an automatically comparable camera-only controller. ([Monitor example](https://github.com/THU-RCSCT/vlsa-aegis#-automated-collision-check), [evaluator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/THU-RCSCT/vlsa-aegis/main/main/main_aegis.py), [landing page](https://vlsa-aegis.github.io/).)

**Block Plucking supplies a more physical failure definition and substantial existing engineering.** The problem is extracting the middle block while avoiding drops, displacement and unstable post-extraction behaviour. UNISafe releases the environment, Dreamer and filter checkpoints, data and evaluation instructions. Your current study uses those frozen components in its own planner condition; it is distinct from reproducing the released filtered-policy evaluation. ([UNISafe assets](https://github.com/CMU-IntentLab/UNISafe/tree/isaaclab).)

The limitation is scientific: Search 2 achieved sustained safe completion of 9/48 for native, 16/48 for an untrained selector and 14/48 for CMA. That does not establish learned improvement over the untrained adapter. Its large residual probes were also destructive. A new interface qualification could test recoverable failures, but existing infrastructure alone should not determine the main study. Compare the prospective opportunity with SafeLIBERO rather than allowing an unlimited sequence of Block Plucking changes. ([Search 2](../../../SafeBlock/Search2Pooled.md), [interface results](../../../SafeBlock/Stage1Results.md).)

**ManiSkill2 peg insertion is the most practical generic-policy fallback.** Policy Decorator releases pretrained Diffusion Policy checkpoints and evaluation commands for `PegInsertionSide-v2`, alongside faucet turning and chair pushing. A plausible safety problem is recovering from insertion misalignment while respecting a declared contact-load limit. The released task is not already that safety benchmark: force measurement, the limit, feasible safe execution and outcome scoring would need validation. A simulated force requirement also does not establish prevention of real hardware damage. Pin the released ManiSkill2 runtime instead of assuming its checkpoints transfer unchanged to ManiSkill3. ([Policy Decorator](https://github.com/tongzhoumu/policy_decorator).)

Policy Decorator is also a close methodological baseline because it already learns residual refinements around frozen imitation policies. If the project broadens beyond world models, its contribution must concern safety adaptation and the measured value of evolution. Frozen-backbone residual learning itself is established. Isaac Lab Factory and FORGE are attractive for force-aware assembly, but the official FORGE page still labels code as forthcoming, and an exact author-provided checkpoint was not verified. They are conditional alternatives rather than the first expansion. ([Policy Decorator](https://github.com/tongzhoumu/policy_decorator), [FORGE](https://noseworm.github.io/forge/).)

**VLAs can support the outer recipe, but the connection point changes.** A world model can predict consequences of candidate actions. A typical VLA exposes proposed actions, chunks or internal policy features. OpenVLA-OFT, for example, provides a continuous action head and chunked execution. A compact state-conditioned correction module or low-dimensional head adapter could therefore be trained while freezing the backbone. Useful authority remains an experimental question. ([OpenVLA-OFT implementation](https://github.com/moojink/openvla-oft).)

The earlier proposal to rank predicted futures specifically requires a predictor. Sampling several VLA action chunks produces candidates, not predictions of their consequences. Adding a learned world model would create additional training and information requirements. For a portable method, define a common contract around accessible observations/features, nominal actions, history and a compact intervention. Evolve the adapter, not the entire VLA. Compare gradient-based learning on that same adapter with the backbone equally frozen. Generality would mean a shared learning recipe with fresh weights across systems, not identical weights or interchangeable latent spaces.

**Finding the problem first means diagnosing failures before choosing an optimizer.** Distinguish failures of perception or language grounding, insufficient task competence, unsafe preferences, inadequate control authority and prediction error. An adapter that changes commands cannot be assumed to repair missing information. Admit a task only after reproducing baseline competence, validating a passive monitor, observing recurring avoidable violations, demonstrating feasible safe completion, and establishing useful adapter authority through the permitted interface. Measure reset reproducibility and complete evaluation throughput, including VLA inference. Public checkpoints and fast simulator benchmarks do not settle those questions.

Use the same predeclared criteria across candidate tasks and retain rejected screens. Choose development cases before optimizer outcomes, then reserve fresh final cases. Privileged simulator labels can score violations without silently becoming policy inputs. The existing PushT result illustrates the importance of authority: native completion and frequent violations coexisted with failed adapter probes. ([PushT qualification](../../../SafeLeWM/results/16-pusht-validated.md).)

My proposed next decision is a comparison of **SafeLIBERO and Block Plucking as candidate main settings**, followed by one selected task family. SafeLIBERO is the strongest new lead because it pairs a released VLA, a documented safety gap and an existing intervention reference. Block Plucking retains an engineering advantage. A later test on another backbone would ask whether the adaptation recipe survives the change. This recommendation changes neither the current protocols nor experiment authority; it identifies what a new qualification proposal should resolve.
