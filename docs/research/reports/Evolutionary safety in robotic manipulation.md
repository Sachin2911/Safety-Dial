# Evolutionary safety in robotic manipulation

**Evolutionary methods already contribute to safety-aware robotic manipulation, including real-hardware studies.** The closest verified precedents evolve policy repertoires, tune contact-rich insertion controllers, and adapt collaborative trajectories around people. Other work evolves grasp alternatives or searches for failures in grasping models. The important distinction is what each evolutionary component contributes and where the safety requirement and execution-time protection come from. These papers establish more specific achievements than autonomous discovery of safety without specifications. This is a selective primary-source map checked through 3 November 2026, rather than a systematic review or a novelty determination. Its strongest direct policy and collaboration examples are from 2022, with related grasping and planning developments from 2024 and 2025. [SafeAPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.13248); [industrial insertion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03017); [collaborative trajectories](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41315-022-00240-4).

**SafeAPT is a close precedent for evolving safety-aware behaviours and adapting them to hardware.** Rituraj Kaushik, Karol Arndt and Ville Kyrki published *SafeAPT: Safe Simulation-to-Real Robot Learning Using Diverse Policies Learned in Simulation* in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 7(3), 6838-6845, in 2022. MAP-Elites builds a repertoire in simulation. Within each goal-space cell, it retains policies with higher minimum safety scores across sampled dynamics. The evolutionary stage therefore explicitly searches for safety under the sampled variations. Online adaptation uses Gaussian-process reward and safety models with constrained Bayesian selection. [Publication record](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/safeapt-safe-simulation-to-real-robot-learning-using-diverse-poli/); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.13248).

The hardware example is Kuka hockey, with safety defined by puck clearance from the table edge and a supplied 0.1 m limit. The paper reports no observed violations in eight hardware repeats, each with a 15-episode budget, while some simulated conditions exhibit violations. Its probabilistic reasoning assumes accurate safety-model estimates. Safety scores, dynamics variations, repertoire structure, confidence settings and model choices remain supplied. The result belongs to a hybrid system: offline evolution contributes safer alternatives; online Bayesian modelling and selection contribute adaptation. It does not establish unrestricted safety or elimination of manual design. [SafeAPT methods and experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.13248).

**Industrial insertion supplies a direct CMA-ES precedent for automating difficult controller tuning.** Rui Chen, Chenxi Wang, Tianhao Wei and Changliu Liu presented *A Composable Framework for Policy Design, Learning, and Transfer Toward Safe and Efficient Industrial Insertion* at IROS 2022. The framework combines designed modules with learning, and uses CMA-ES to tune coupled policy and controller parameters. Hardware experiments use a UR10 inserting IC sockets, chosen because they are more robust than IC chips. This distinction matters when interpreting the demonstration as evidence about handling delicate components. [CMU publication record](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/a-composable-framework-for-policy-design-learning-and-transfer-toward-safe-and-efficient-industrial-insertion); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03017).

Protection includes a force-triggered retraction state machine and Cartesian tracking constraints on velocity, acceleration and orientation. The optimization objective retains weighted terms for time, collisions, peak force and failures. Parameter bounds and the modular behaviour structure also remain designed. Its contribution is directly relevant to reducing manual parameter adjustment, but it does not remove safety penalties or behavioural rules. The authors keep the training configuration fixed and identify adaptation to changing environments as future work. Transfer to other manipulation tasks requires redesigning task-specific modules. [Insertion framework methods and limitations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03017).

**Collaborative trajectory optimization shows evolution working with an active protective controller.** Andrea Maria Zanchettin, Costanza Messeri, Davide Cristantielli and Paolo Rocco published *Trajectory optimisation in collaborative robotics based on simulations and genetic algorithms* in the International Journal of Intelligent Robotics and Applications, 6, 707-723, in 2022. A genetic algorithm adjusts waypoints, speeds, interpolation and blending. Candidate trajectories are assessed in a digital twin using a human-occupancy model. Selection prioritizes a collision-risk proxy before traversal time. A physical ABB YuMi performs an assembly task with a person. [Full paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41315-022-00240-4).

Speed and Separation Monitoring remains active during execution. The search changes motion around that protection mechanism, while explicit constraints, an initial trajectory and mutation settings remain supplied. Its risk proxy represents constraint slack under estimated occupancy, rather than a generally calibrated probability of injury. This is a useful precedent for improving productive behaviour within a safety architecture. It also shows why fewer protective stops cannot, by itself, establish that the optimizer learned a new safety requirement: an improved trajectory can simply avoid triggering an existing controller. [Collaborative trajectory methods and validation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41315-022-00240-4).

The planning literature extends beyond human collaboration. Suping Zhao and colleagues' *Multitask-Based Anti-Collision Trajectory Planning of Redundant Manipulators*, published in Biomimetics in 2024, uses a genetic algorithm across task order, joint configurations and trajectory coefficients, with modeled obstacle constraints and simulation evidence. This is relevant prior art for evolutionary motion generation. A trajectory optimized for a modeled scene and a reusable feedback policy evaluated on unfamiliar tasks make different claims. [Zhao et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomimetics9110679).

**Quality-diversity grasping provides alternative behaviours, with safety handling supplied elsewhere.** Johann Huber and colleagues presented *Speeding up 6-DoF Grasp Sampling with Quality-Diversity* at IROS 2024. The method evolves diverse grasp poses, evaluates grasp retention in simulation, and demonstrates transfer to a Franka arm with parallel and Allegro grippers. It is a substantial manipulation precedent for evolutionary repertoires. Its execution arrangement also uses MoveIt and RRT-Connect collision handling, rejects table-colliding grasps, supplies hand-closing synergies and selects mutation scale through grid search. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06173); [project demonstrations](https://qdgrasp.github.io/).

A diverse repertoire could provide options to a safety-aware selector. That is an inference about how such a component could be used; the paper's grasp-generation and transfer evidence does not establish general injury or damage prevention. The useful lesson for attribution is that evolved grasps and supplied collision avoidance coexist. We should examine both when describing how much behaviour the evolutionary method discovered.

Aurel X. Appius and colleagues extend this direction in *Task-Aware Robotic Grasping by evaluating Quality Diversity Solutions through Foundation Models*, a manuscript revised in March 2025. It combines QD solutions with semantic segmentation and language-model selection of task-appropriate grasp regions, with physical Franka validation. It connects evolutionary diversity to contextual grasp choice. Its evaluations concern task suitability and grasp execution, rather than a general harm bound. This is relevant to choosing among behaviours for different purposes, with its safety relevance still needing a separately specified interpretation. [Revised manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14917).

**Evolution can also test manipulation systems by searching for failures.** Naif Wasel Alharthi and Martim Brandão presented *Physical and Digital Adversarial Attacks on Grasp Quality Networks* at ICRA 2024. Evolutionary multiobjective optimization searches for perturbations that degrade selected-grasp quality while keeping the perturbation small. The threat models include a small added scene object and a single modified depth-image pixel. Actual real-robot attack evaluation is listed as future work; the word physical describes the threat model rather than demonstrated hardware attacks. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.martimbrandao.com/papers/Alharthi2024-icra.pdf).

This supplies another role for evolutionary search: discovering weaknesses in the models used to choose actions. It does not itself learn a safe controller or establish that correcting the discovered failures prevents all relevant harm. For our thinking, it makes the evaluator part of the research question. A controller selected through a learned grasp-quality signal inherits limitations in that signal unless another mechanism addresses them.

Taken together, these precedents change the specificity our research needs. **Using evolution to improve safety-aware manipulation is already established territory.** A contribution about reduced manual design would have to identify the burden reduced: choosing controller parameters, specifying recovery rules, balancing penalty weights, designing representations, or constructing a reusable set of behaviours. Automatic optimization of one part does not measure the total engineering effort across the system. Likewise, benefits from a hybrid method do not isolate an evolutionary advantage over alternative optimizers.

The verified studies leave distinct questions available for deeper reading, rather than proving a gap. How much safety comes from the candidate behaviours and how much from execution-time protection? Which hand-designed choices remain after learning? How does performance change outside the development conditions? What evidence separates safe completion from inactivity or task success despite violations? Answering these from each paper will help us formulate a contribution that can be compared fairly with the actual prior art.

Start with **SafeAPT**, then **Chen et al.'s insertion framework**, then **Zanchettin et al.'s collaborative trajectories**. Together they cover evolving behaviour alternatives, tuning contact-rich policies, and adapting motion within a protective architecture. The 2024 grasping and adversarial-testing papers then broaden the possible role of evolution. This search supports that reading route; it does not establish a first-work claim or the absence of newer close precedents.
