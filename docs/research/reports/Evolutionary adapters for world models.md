# Evolutionary adapters already have working precedents

Evidence checked 30 October 2026. This is a focused literature review for the proposed LeWM adapter, not an experimental protocol.

**Yes: researchers have successfully evolved reusable controllers using learned predictive models, and have successfully combined learned behavioural proposals with planning.** The evidence supports investigating the adapter idea. It does not establish the entire proposed system: a frozen LeWM, a separately frozen learned motion checker, explicitly supplied new hazards, and an evolution-trained proposal adapter that improves safe completion. The most relevant papers demonstrate different parts of that design. Their reported reward, task-success, survival and safety results measure different things and should remain separate. My interpretation is that the immediate opportunity is to test a behavioural proposal adapter around the existing planner, with its learned component evaluated independently. The literature gives concrete designs and failure modes; it does not supply a performance guarantee or a verified novelty claim.

## POPLIN closely matches the proposal and planner interface

**POPLIN, Learning to Plan with Policy Networks, is an ICLR 2020 paper with a 2019 preprint.** POPLIN-A initializes action sequences using a policy, then CEM refines action perturbations. POPLIN-P searches policy-parameter perturbations. Persistent policies use behaviour cloning, adversarial training, or an averaging update. The averaging variant works as a planning initialization despite weak standalone performance. An appendix experiment also obtains effective parameter-space planning with fixed zero base weights. Its learned models are updated during training, and it does not evaluate our introduced-hazard safety problem. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.08649) and [author implementation](https://github.com/WilsonWangTHU/POPLIN) make this the first architectural reading priority.

For our design, the important distinction is between a controller that directly succeeds and a controller that helps another search succeed. A useful initializer need not be a competent standalone policy. Conversely, successful planning does not demonstrate that a trained initializer contributed anything. We should therefore inspect the adapter's raw proposals, the planner's changes to them, and the resulting executed behaviour. A random or untrained adapter is an informative control because a network's action parameterization can itself change search, even before useful weights are learned. This is an experimental implication of the evidence, not a result already established for LeWM.

## Controller evolution and behavioural proposals have worked

**World Models, Ha and Schmidhuber, NeurIPS 2018**, evolves a compact controller with CMA-ES after representation learning. Its 867-parameter CarRacing controller achieved mean reward 906 +/- 21 over 100 tracks; evolutionary training used the actual simulator. The separate VizDoom experiment evolved a controller inside a learned latent environment and transferred it back, reaching mean survival 1,092 +/- 556 steps over 100 games at the reported temperature setting. Low-temperature dream training also produced severe model exploitation. These are task and survival results, not explicit hazard-constrained completion. The [primary project](https://worldmodels.github.io/) distinguishes the experiments; [author code](https://github.com/hardmaru/WorldModelsExperiments) is available.

This establishes an important component of the proposal: a small persistent controller can be learned by evolution over predictive representations. It does not make model-only training an automatic property of our implementation. Our current evaluator accepts action sequences and predicts latent task costs and protected-body paths. An adapter acting repeatedly inside imagination additionally needs a consistent prediction of every input it receives, together with goal and termination semantics. That training environment would need to be specified and checked.

**Behavior Policy Learning, Tsinganos et al., 2022**, is a particularly useful behavioural-adapter precedent. A neural high-level controller produces future target states for a fixed model-based low-level controller. CMA-ES fine-tunes imitation-initialized policy weights in simulation using binary task success. One optimized policy completed 8/12 physical pick-and-place configurations. This is preliminary task-completion evidence, not a collision-free completion rate. Demonstration sketches and simulation training are required; the work does not use a frozen visual world model or newly supplied hazards. The [primary paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2022.974537/full) explicitly describes fine-tuning around an imitation initialization rather than discovering the entire behaviour from scratch.

The implication for LeWM is to consider a restricted behavioural output with a useful starting point. A short action proposal or bounded correction exposes a clearer learning problem than asking a large network to replace the complete planner. This is a design inference. The appropriate restriction must still leave enough authority to solve the failures we are targeting.

**Black-DROPS, IROS 2017**, learns probabilistic dynamics and reward models and searches parameterized policies using uncertainty-handling BIPOP-CMA-ES. It demonstrates model-based evolutionary policy search, including experiments on a physical four-DOF arm. Its neural-policy cart-pole treatment reports 93.33% task success over 120 runs, compared with 92.5% for PILCO. Models are updated between episodes, and the objective is task performance rather than our fixed external-hazard predicate. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.07261v2) and [official code](https://github.com/resibots/blackdrops) are useful robotics references. The repository cautions that low physical-interaction time does not imply low computation time.

## Safety evidence supports the question and exposes transfer risks

**Safe-LOOP, from CoRL 2021 with proceedings published in 2022**, combines actor-guided population planning, learned dynamics and explicit cost constraints. Its controller uses an actor and a shifted previous plan as priors; ensemble predictions inform constrained search. It evaluates Safety Gym navigation and simulated RC-car control. Actor learning uses gradients, and the safe experiments learn their models and policies rather than attach an adapter to a permanently frozen pretrained predictor. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.10066), [proceedings record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/sikchi22a.html) and [code](https://github.com/hari-sikchi/LOOP) provide a close safety-oriented planning counterpart. Its reward/cost curves are not interchangeable with our strict safe-completion counts.

**Micro Neural Policies for Safe Real-Time Robotic Control is an October 2026 preprint.** Its ES-Res method evolves neural residuals around a fixed certified linear controller. In simulated Quadrotor verification, ES-Res passes for 5/5 tested network architectures at both frequencies; those are architecture counts, not optimizer repeats. The physical Quadrotor residual controller crashes at both 50 and 100 Hz, while a selected pure-ES policy completes waypoint tracking. The authors attribute the residual failure to baseline model mismatch. The [full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08541v1) therefore supplies relevant safety-aware ES evidence and a direct warning against assuming that a fixed base automatically makes a residual composition reliable.

**PSyCo/Safe Neural Evolutionary Strategies** provides an additional safety-specific precedent. It evolves neural policies under probabilistic constraints and combines policy search with Bayesian verification. Its Obstacle Run study uses five repeats with 20,000 training episodes per repeat, plus verification. It has neither a pretrained visual world model nor our proposal-planner interface. Its [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scico.2021.102620), [accessible manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03898v2) and [code](https://github.com/lenzbelzner/psyco) show that safety-constrained policy evolution is an established direction with substantial evaluation requirements.

These safety studies motivate measuring the composed controller, rather than accepting either predicted feasibility or a safe base as sufficient evidence. In our setting, the adapter and planner can exploit predictor errors or combine individually reasonable components into poor behaviour. Evaluation must retain actual completion, violations, unfinished episodes and engagement, with explicit denominators. Safety geometry, margins and prediction allowances remain fixed specifications rather than evolvable shortcuts.

## A behavioural adapter can be tested without replacing SafeCEM

The simplest investigation consistent with this literature keeps frozen LeWM, the learned motion checker and the current SafeCEM as the shared substrate. The new genotype is the proposal adapter's weights. Its phenotype produces a short action sequence or bounded correction using causal observations, goal information and explicitly supplied hazard information. Our virtual hazards are not automatically present in LeWM's visual inputs. The existing planner can refine the proposal and apply its execution-time check. A separate evolutionary action planner remains an optional later component.

That separation gives us two questions with different controls. Does a proposal adapter help the planner? Does evolutionary training produce a better adapter than ordinary learning or parameter random search with the same information and budgets? A planner-only arm and an untrained-adapter arm address the first question; repeated optimizer runs on the same adapter representation address the second. All proposed outputs should retain their provenance, so an improvement cannot silently come from a different fallback or safety threshold.

Model-query and environment-interaction budgets should both be explicit. Training cost, demonstrations or initialization data, validation, failed starts and online search all belong in the accounting. A lower online planning cost is not automatically lower total acquisition cost. Fresh evaluation source cases should be chosen before new outcomes; changing seeds on familiar scenes does not make them independent geometries. None of this review's papers establishes which adapter size, action basis or training budget will work for our pipeline, or that poor initialization explains its present stalls.

For reading, start with POPLIN for the interface, World Models for controller evolution, and Behavior Policy Learning for structured evolutionary fine-tuning. Use Safe-LOOP to examine constrained search and Black-DROPS to examine uncertainty-aware evolutionary evaluation. The October 2026 residual result is a useful check on assumptions about composition and transfer. A defensible contribution would describe the particular LeWM adapter and show its measured effect under fixed safety requirements. This focused search found no verified demonstration of every proposed component together; that boundary identifies work to investigate, not an absence claim about the literature.
