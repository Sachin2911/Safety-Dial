# Robotic manipulation capabilities and safety

**Robotic manipulation has expanded along several directions: broader task repertoires, more precise interaction, and longer sequences of autonomous actions.** These advances come from combinations of planning, feedback control, demonstrations, reinforcement learning, predictive models and better sensing. They do not form a single progression in which a new neural model replaces everything beneath it. A language-conditioned policy can depend on a conventional motion controller; a reinforcement learner can start from demonstrations; a world model can help check another policy's actions. Safety must therefore be assessed for the assembled system, its particular hazards and its operating conditions. The examples below provide a selective map of developments available by 3 November 2026, rather than a cross-paper leaderboard. The distinction between capability and safety is especially visible in systems that combine learned policies with explicit control constraints. [HIL-SERL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21845v3); [OSCBF](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06736v3).

A useful way to compare the work is to ask four separate questions. How many tasks and objects can it handle? How precisely can it control physical contact? How long can it operate without human correction? Which safety properties have actually been evaluated or enforced? This is our organizing framework, not a standard benchmark. It prevents a successful demonstration of task breadth from becoming an unsupported claim about contact safety or unattended operation.

**1. Model-based planning and control remain fundamental.** These systems represent the robot and environment, calculate feasible motions, and use feedback to track them. MoveIt provides collision and constraint checking, including an allowed-collision mechanism for intended contact. cuRobo illustrates a continuing development within this family: accelerating inverse kinematics, geometric planning and trajectory optimization through GPU parallelism. Progress in manipulation includes faster and more capable optimization alongside larger learned models. [MoveIt planning scenes](https://moveit.picknik.ai/main/doc/examples/planning_scene/planning_scene_tutorial.html); [cuRobo research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2023-05_curobo-parallelized-collision-free-robot-motion-generation).

Their strength is that geometric requirements can be represented and checked explicitly. Their scope follows the representation: collision-free motion relative to a scene model does not establish that an object will not be crushed, that liquid will not spill, or that every obstacle was perceived. That is an inference about the coverage of the checks. These components can support a learned system by translating a proposed hand motion into joint motion and restricting particular actions.

**2. Imitation learning makes demonstrated skills reproducible by a policy.** Instead of writing every motion rule, a person provides examples and a model learns to act from observations. ALOHA supplies a bimanual teleoperation platform; ACT is the associated learning method that predicts chunks of actions. The authors demonstrate learned precision skills such as cup opening and battery insertion. Their page usefully separates autonomous policy results from human teleoperation and includes failures under disturbances. This distinction matters when interpreting impressive robot videos. [ACT and ALOHA, 2023](https://tonyzhaozh.github.io/aloha/).

Diffusion Policy generates candidate action sequences through a denoising process, executes part of a sequence and replans after observing again. It can represent different plausible actions without simply averaging them together. Its demonstrations include pouring, mug flipping and pushing. These are advances in learning continuous motor behaviour. The reported task-performance evaluations do not establish a general safety guarantee; the examples and training distribution still matter. [Diffusion Policy, 2023/2024](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/).

**3. Vision-language-action models broaden what a shared policy can be asked to do.** A VLA connects visual observations and language instructions to robot actions. OpenVLA demonstrates reusable pretrained policies and adaptation to new robot setups. Its project distinguishes zero-shot results from tasks requiring fine-tuning. The original 2024 paper also explicitly acknowledges that typical task success remains below 90% in its evaluations. Those results concern that model and those tasks, and should not be assigned to every later derivative. [OpenVLA project](https://openvla.github.io/); [original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246v3).

The 2026 frontier extends task composition and the combination of learning signals. Physical Intelligence's April 2026 pi0.7 report describes laundry, appliance use and other tasks, with controlled tests of instruction and cross-robot generalization. Its training combines demonstrations, autonomous experience, interventions and other sources, including behaviours from RL specialists. These are company-reported capabilities and evaluations. They establish neither independent field reliability nor universal household competence. [Pi0.7 research report](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi07); [technical paper](https://www.pi.website/download/pi07.pdf).

Google's July 2026 releases also make an architectural distinction visible. Gemini Robotics ER 2 reasons over observations and orchestrates tools; Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 outputs numerical robot actions. High-level task reasoning and motor execution remain different functions even when packaged within one robot system. The latter's model card reports limits on unfamiliar tasks and high-degree-of-freedom embodiments. [ER 2 release](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-robotics-er-2/); [On-Device 2 model card](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-robotics-on-device-2/).

**4. Reinforcement learning improves behaviour through outcomes and corrective experience.** HIL-SERL combines demonstrations, a learned reward classifier, human corrections and real-world RL. Its hardware tasks include assembly, handover and dynamic manipulation. Contact behaviour is mediated by an impedance controller with reference limiting, while people correct undesirable training behaviour. The trained policy is one component of that arrangement. Strong performance on a specific controlled task does not measure every possible injury, damage or sequencing hazard. [HIL-SERL, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21845v3).

Simulation provides another route to gathering experience. Dexplore uses human hand-object motion as flexible guidance for simulated RL, then transfers a distilled controller to a physical robot hand. It demonstrates dexterity while acknowledging difficulty with small or thin objects and limited training diversity. This illustrates why imitation, RL and simulation are overlapping dimensions. Where experience is collected also matters: physical correction, simulation fidelity and transfer each introduce different engineering requirements. [Dexplore, CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09671v1).

**5. Predictive world models support planning and intervention.** An action policy chooses what to do; an action-conditioned world model predicts what could follow. V-JEPA 2-AC illustrates the latter: video representations are extended with robot-action prediction, and a planner compares imagined outcomes against image goals. The reported robot experiments include reaching, grasping and pick-and-place in two laboratories without collecting training data in those deployment environments. The authors also report camera sensitivity, compounding prediction errors and difficulties with longer planning horizons. [V-JEPA 2, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1).

Prediction creates an opportunity to inspect consequences before execution, but its usefulness depends on what the model represents and how the planner uses it. The inference for our reading is that predictive accuracy, planning competence and safety need separate evidence. UNISafe uses a world model differently, combining uncertainty estimates and learned fallback behaviour to filter a task policy. It evaluates simulated block plucking and hardware Jenga. The paper explicitly limits its assurance: a statistical result for an uncertainty-detection component is not a system-level guarantee of zero failures. [UNISafe, CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v2).

**6. Touch and compliant hardware improve the physical information available to every learning family.** Manipulation involves forces, slip and contact that images alone may not reveal adequately. ViTaMIn combines tactile and visual observations with a compliant gripper and demonstration learning. It evaluates insertion, reorientation and other contact-rich tasks, and separately measures interaction forces. Its failure analysis still includes collisions, weak grasps and coordination failures. Better task performance and lower average force are useful results, but neither establishes an upper bound on every harmful interaction. [ViTaMIn, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06156v2).

FreeTacMan develops the infrastructure for this direction: wearable demonstration collection with visual and tactile sensing, together with a reusable contact-rich dataset. It illustrates progress in acquiring the experience from which policies learn, rather than only changing the optimizer. [FreeTacMan, ICRA 2026](https://opendrivelab.com/FreeTacMan). For our research reasoning, this is a reminder to distinguish a failure to observe contact from a failure to discover the right response.

**7. Explicit safety mechanisms constrain what the whole system can do.** OSCBF combines operational-space control with barrier-function constraints for joint limits, workspace restrictions, collisions and other properties. Its paper includes simulation and hardware evidence, but also discusses infeasibility when constraints conflict. Relaxing constraints can restore an executable solution while losing the strict safety guarantee. A method's name therefore cannot substitute for reading its assumptions and implementation. [OSCBF, IROS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06736v3).

Commercial robot hardware supplies another layer. Universal Robots documents configurable force, power, momentum, speed and stopping limits for the UR10e, while explicitly making application safety dependent on the tool, obstacles and surrounding machinery. A collaborative robot is not automatically safe for every process. [Manufacturer documentation](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_26/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR10e/H_g5_sections/safety_g5/risk_assessment.htm). At the foundation-model level, Google's robotics safety description likewise combines semantic, physical and operational safeguards, and identifies its human-distance stopping behaviour as research rather than a guaranteed safety-rated system. [Google robotics safety](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-robotics/responsibly-advancing-ai-and-robotics/).

The answer to “what is safe?” therefore needs a bounded statement: a particular mechanism protects against specified hazards under stated conditions, supported by particular evidence. Observed collisions are evidence of failure in those trials. Missing safety measurements leave a claim unestablished. Neither author demonstrations nor manufacturer protection features alone establish unrestricted safe autonomy. This distinction lets us acknowledge substantial capability progress without assigning an unsupported safe or unsafe label to an entire method family.

For a broad foundation, use Russ Tedrake's MIT *Robotic Manipulation: Perception, Planning, and Control* notes, updated for Fall 2026. Begin with the introduction, basic pick and place, manipulator control, and tactile sensing. [MIT course notes](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/). Then compare the autonomous sections of [ALOHA](https://tonyzhaozh.github.io/aloha/), the contact skills in [HIL-SERL](https://hil-serl.github.io/), and the constraint demonstrations in [OSCBF](https://stanfordasl.github.io/oscbf/). Ask of each: **which part of the successful behaviour was learned, which part was supplied by the surrounding system, and which safety claim does the evidence actually support?**
