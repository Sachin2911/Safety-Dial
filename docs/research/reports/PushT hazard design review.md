# Place hazards around feasible pushing routes

For PushT, I recommend starting with **one compact virtual disk on a task-relevant pushing route**, followed by a short rounded bar and, later, a generous offset gate. The initial discussion considered protecting both bodies. For the first engineering smoke test on 29 October, Sachin chose **pusher-only safety**, covering the full pusher disk; the T-block remains part of task completion but is excluded from the hazard predicate. The broader object-safety discussion below describes a possible later extension. Our existing midpoint band obstructs a geometric pusher shortcut, but does not establish a feasible safe manipulation strategy. The reviewed sources support simple geometric constraints, explicit predicates and contact-aware planning. They do not establish a universal PushT hazard layout or validate our proposed dimensions. The small pusher-only pilot is specified separately in `configs/pusht_smoke_20261029/protocol.json`.

## Published precedents use simple regions with different meanings

**Original PushT contains arena boundaries, a circular pusher, a T-block and a goal, without internal hazard regions.** Its conventional success criterion is over 95% target coverage. Our pinned LeWM experiment instead uses joint pusher/block position and block-angle error, so a new hazard study should retain that criterion or explicitly declare a separate task variant. ([Original PushT environment](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/main/diffusion_policy/env/pusht/pusht_env.py))

Fiedler, Vahl and Zhang provide a close precedent in *Dynamically Constraining Diffusion Policies During Inference*: they impose central rectangular regions and left workspace strips on a pretrained PushT policy without retraining. Their metrics concern task score and action samples inside restricted regions. These results demonstrate inference-time workspace restrictions, but do not establish dense executed-path safety or protection of the T-block footprint. The distinction matters when choosing our claims. ([Paper](https://tams.informatik.uni-hamburg.de/people/fiedler/publications/constrained_diffusion.pdf))

Safety-Gymnasium uses **noncolliding disk-shaped hazards** alongside solid cylindrical pillars. Its pushing tasks move a box, but the hazard implementation checks the agent's position rather than the box footprint. Thus pusher-only avoidance has a benchmark precedent, while object-safe manipulation requires an additional definition. These mobile-robot tasks supply useful conventions, not a validated tabletop PushT protocol. ([Hazard implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PKU-Alignment/safety-gymnasium/main/safety_gymnasium/assets/geoms/hazards.py), [Push tasks](https://safety-gymnasium.readthedocs.io/en/stable/environments/safe_navigation/push.html))

Neve et al.'s slider-pusher planner uses circular or ellipsoidal obstacles, incorporates slider geometry, and plans changes of pushing direction. This supports simple shapes while showing why a clear point-agent path is insufficient: the object must move through contact-constrained dynamics. Their simplified slider model and collocation constraints do not certify our T-block trajectories. ([ECC 2024 paper](https://paperhost.org/proceedings/controls/ECC24/files/0245.pdf))

## Our proposed sequence separates detours from doorway difficulty

The first family should be a **round island** crossing an interior segment of a successful reference object's swept route, clear of both initial and final footprints. Leave room on both sides for transport, pusher repositioning and recontact. This makes the intended behavior legible: the object visibly goes around the forbidden region while reaching the same goal. Use a successful reference trajectory rather than assuming the straight pusher line is an optimal manipulation path. Our old 20 px minimum object displacement can admit pairs with insufficient separation for this construction. Select sufficient clearance geometrically; declare any new goal-separation or horizon rule explicitly.

The second family should be a **short rounded bar** across the reference transport route. Its finite ends provide understandable alternatives; varying orientation and aspect ratio can test sustained clearance without immediately requiring precise doorway alignment. A **wide offset gate** can then test transport and orientation through a passage. Choose its opening through full-body engineering, not by retaining the old 60 px value. Avoid L/U traps and near-goal pockets initially because they combine escape, rotation, recontact and safety into difficult-to-interpret failures. These are our proposed stages, not published benchmark standards.

Keep the regions virtual initially. Solid obstacles change contact dynamics; painting new hazards into LeWM's input changes its visual distribution. Human replay overlays can show the constraints while preserving existing model observations.

## Full-body safety changes both geometry and checking

For the pusher disk, account for its physical radius before adding clearance. The pinned radius is 15 px, so the old 60 px opening with 12 px extra clearance leaves a centre interval of **only 6 px**. For the rotating T-block, use its transformed component polygons or a documented conservative approximation. Configuration-space inflation depends on body shape and orientation; a thin doorway's feasibility cannot be inferred solely from an unrotated bounding-box width. ([Pinned runtime](../../../experiments/helpers/lewm18_runtime.py), [configuration-space construction](https://msl.cs.illinois.edu/~lavalle/planning/node161.html))

Our current analytic checker predicts pusher motion only. Object-safe candidate screening needs a specified object-pose predictor with uncertainty treatment, or simulator previews whose interactions and compute are counted and matched across methods. An executed object monitor alone does not supply that planning capability. Check motion between states as well as endpoints, including rotation; physics-substep sampling alone is not a continuous-time certificate. ([Motion validation](https://msl.cs.illinois.edu/~lavalle/planning/node217.html))

Before freezing each case, record an independent successful safe pushing witness under its exact predicate, largest margin and intended action budget. Count failed attempts and rejected scenes. Freeze construction rules before evaluated-method outcomes, and report reference-conflict cases separately from no-hazard and nonconflicting controls. Report completion, incursions by each body, unfinished episodes and meaningful object engagement together. This would turn a visually plausible hazard layout into a testable manipulation challenge.

The subsequent [pusher-only engineering smoke test](../../../progress/receipts/20261029-pusht-hazard-smoke.json) used one scene and one seed. Island, rounded-bar and wide-gate runs reached the current goal criterion in 26, 40 and 40 actions, maintaining the requested 6 px full-pusher clearance at recorded physics steps. The shared unchecked reference reached the goal in 17 actions but breached all three regions when rescored. These are working examples, not a population success-rate estimate. The T-block was excluded from the hazard predicate.
