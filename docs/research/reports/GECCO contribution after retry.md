# Earn the evolutionary claim through execution

24 October 2026. Research recommendation after task 3, with a $50 to $100 next-phase compute budget. The designs below are **proposals, not a frozen protocol or an instruction to rent compute**.

**Stay with Jar, test one execution lever, and make the optimizer comparison conditional on a useful effect.** The strongest affordable direction is a compact controller that changes how a frozen VLA's joint targets are executed. Its scientific question is whether that controller improves safe requested completion on tasks withheld from intervention development, and whether evolution discovers better controllers than fixed settings, random search and ordinary learning under the same interaction ceiling. A scalar sweep alone would establish engineering leverage, not an evolutionary contribution. The completed retry experiment supplies valuable mechanism evidence and evaluation controls, but does not yet support a GECCO main-paper claim. At the lean budget, a narrow empirical study is more credible than simultaneously pursuing recurrent control, Pareto optimization, a world model and cross-family transfer.

## The retry result narrows the mechanism, not the project

The reproduced Jar baseline has **24/104 benchmark safe successes and 43/104 violations**, with 103 engaged episodes. Only 23 of those 24 safe successes satisfy the separate closure-before-carry rule. Outcomes are compatible with the published row, but a nonsignificant difference is not a statistical demonstration of equivalence. The diagnosis concentrates losses at the lid push and regrasp: 19 episodes tip after closure while still on the table, nine tip before closure, and 36 stall with the lid open or closed. These observations motivate execution changes, without proving that slower commands prevent tipping. ([Replication](../research_notes/Jar%20replication%20and%20noise%20floor.md), [failure diagnosis](../research_notes/Where%20the%20leverage%20is.md))

The retry gate produced **17/96 native versus 16/96 retry safe successes**, 48 versus 47 violations, and 31 versus 33 safe unfinished episodes. Every episode engaged. The detector missed visibly parked behavior because joint jitter exceeded its 0.01-radian threshold. Retreats fired in 21 episodes and none subsequently succeeded, although the arm returned cleanly and the policy resumed. That subgroup is selected by the treatment trajectory: it does not estimate what retreat would cause in an otherwise identical stalled state. The supported statement is no detected improvement from this frozen detector and retreat, rather than retry cannot work or the knob changes nothing. ([Results](../../../progress/receipts/03-results.md), [smoke diagnosis](../../../progress/receipts/03-smoke.md))

The identity check also changes the noise story. Identical requests with fresh sampling keys produce identical policy chunks, but separate simulator launches already differ in rendered observations at the first frame. Contact can amplify those differences; physics alone is not established as their source. Nevertheless, all 96 paired outcomes have positive correlations, about 0.38 for safe success and 0.31 for violations. Task-mean-adjusted correlations remain about 0.30. Keep task/seed blocking and lane/time alternation, while recognizing that matching seeds does not construct identical counterfactual trajectories. The observed covariance is not guaranteed to transfer to another lever. ([Identity receipt](../../../progress/receipts/03-identity.md), [calculations and limitations](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

## Contract targets first, with one bounded fallback

The recommended first lever is **arm-only, query-state-anchored target contraction**. For current arm joints q and each predicted absolute target a, execute q + alpha*(a-q); retain the gripper output and the native eight-action prefix. Provisional settings are alpha = 1, 0.8 and 0.6. Multiplying absolute targets directly by alpha would pull toward the robot's zero configuration, a different intervention. Query-state anchoring avoids silently changing the query schedule or adding a new phase detector. This is a target transformation, not exact time scaling, a velocity cap or a guarantee of smaller impact forces. Log measured joint motion, descent, contact, tip timing and timeouts to establish what physically changes. Global contraction could delay the approach or weaken the lid push, so even a well-implemented treatment can fail. ([Implementation assessment](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/mechanisms.md))

First validate alpha=1 identity for identical requests, unchanged gripper behavior and proposed, filtered and executed action records. Then use a small prospectively fixed development diagnostic set to check the physical effect; count those interactions, and exclude episodes used to revise implementation from confirmatory inference. The initial rate gate can allocate six development tasks, 16 new seeds and three arms, **288 episodes in total**. Native must run contemporaneously on the same host. This is a screen for a large useful effect, not adequate proof of a ten-point benefit or safety noninferiority. Freeze the minimum useful effect, two native comparisons, multiplicity handling and a possible continuation before reading outcomes. A larger 576-episode version is an option only under that predefined rule. ([Evaluation proposal](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

The scientific gate is improvement in safe requested completion, with violations and unfinished episodes assessed alongside it. A movement change only passes the implementation check. A wide null interval is inconclusive; an interval excluding the target gain supports stopping. Any safety noninferiority claim needs a declared margin and an upper confidence bound, not a nonsignificant rise. Continue to optimization only if an effect survives fresh development-seed confirmation and candidate rankings are stable enough to guide selection. Otherwise use **one bounded fallback: native prefix eight versus prefix four**, in a separate study. A shorter prefix increases feedback opportunities but can increase discontinuities and approximately doubles the maximum policy queries from 250 to 500 at a 2,000-step horizon. Fix which budget is matched and disclose the other resource. Do not combine contraction and shorter prefixes before knowing which has leverage. ([Execution trade-offs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339), [budget accounting](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

Phase targeting is a later refinement. The current proxy receives joints, gripper state and the proposed chunk; it does not receive true closure, jar tilt or finger contact. A true-closure gate would add privileged simulator information, and closure already flickers in 19 baseline episodes. A gripper-event gate is observable but is not automatically a regrasp detector. A clearly labelled oracle diagnostic can test whether ideal timing helps; a deployable result requires validating its observable replacement. ([Mechanism and observability assessment](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/mechanisms.md))

## The contribution needs more than an evolutionary optimizer

The closest literature rules out the broad novelty claims in the earlier research report. VLA-Pilot already evolves proposals from frozen VLAs; APEX places a feedback execution layer below black-box policies; evolving cache schedules already tunes frozen-policy inference with evolution; and VICS-G reranks frozen-VLA candidates for safe completion. DSRL demonstrates ordinary RL adaptation through a frozen policy's noise input. These are related mechanisms, not evidence that their implementations transfer to Jar. They mean that evolution around a frozen model, an execution wrapper, and a runtime safety objective are individually established ideas. ([VLA-Pilot](https://rip4kobe.github.io/vla-pilot/), [APEX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16504), [cache schedules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20293), [VICS-G](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05166), [DSRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15799))

The defensible opening is a controlled empirical result: a small execution controller improves temporal-specification-grounded safe completion, transfers to the frozen held-out Jar panel, and has its optimization cost and uncertainty measured honestly. A useful fixed scalar is the first rung. A justified conditional controller outperforming that scalar is a representation result. Replicated superiority over equally informed alternatives is an evolutionary result. A validated allocation rule that improves noisy selection is a methodological result. None follows automatically from the preceding rung, and no targeted literature search establishes a global first-to claim.

After leverage and evaluation calibration, freeze the smallest supported representation, initially one or two parameters and only then additional conditioning if justified. A later illustrative gate could use two to four weights on a bias, gripper aperture and recent arm-motion norm to choose bounded contraction; that architecture still needs validation. Use one simple evolutionary optimizer, such as a small real-valued mutation-and-selection strategy, alongside random search, native and the best fixed setting. The repository also requires an ordinary-learning comparison before an evolutionary claim, despite the older plan deferring it. A small policy-gradient-trained gate choosing among the same execution settings, with the same observable features and outcomes, is a plausible comparison. Its decision frequency, training reward, deployment rule and tuning allowance need specification; feasibility is unproved. Bayesian optimization can compare black-box optimizers but does not replace this learning control. Do not make the learner deliberately weak or add controller dimensions merely to disadvantage random search. ([Project controls](../../../AGENTS.md), [benchmarking guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03488))

If evolution ties random search but both beat native, report an execution-controller improvement and a negative finding about evolutionary advantage. A meaningful empirical application can still exist; its strength depends on transfer, mechanism and effect size. A safety-completion plot is useful reporting, but NSGA-II and a plotted Pareto front do not alone create an EMO contribution. Several front points also multiply final validation costs. Start with one primary safe-completion objective and report the safety trade-off explicitly.

The alternatives remain available in a deliberate order. Repair the retry detector offline against annotated development stalls before buying another recovery study; detector accuracy and recovery effectiveness are separate questions. A contact-stop filter needs phase discrimination because contact is also necessary for the task. Latent-noise steering has a legitimate mechanism and strong prior learning work, but the local evidence does not identify it as the next best purchase. A critic or world model introduces labels, predictive validation and extra proposals; a frozen-feature risk probe should pass a leakage-controlled offline test first. A large recurrent controller or adapter combines too many unmeasured assumptions; full policy-weight adaptation also changes the frozen-policy question. Stack remains the predeclared backup, rather than an immediate escape from a replicated Jar setup. These are options for later evidence, not parallel work packages inside the lean budget. ([Option assessment](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/mechanisms.md), [latent steering precedent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15799))

## Spend the budget on evidence before breadth

Historical throughput is better than the older per-GPU estimates implied. The last gate completed 192 episodes in 7 hours 11 minutes across two GPUs. Whole-host prices in the receipts were approximately $0.50 to $0.61 per hour. Thus a 288-episode gate costs approximately **$5 to $8 raw compute**; allow roughly $10 to $15 for that stage and operational contingency before committing more. These are historical scenarios, not current offers. Slower treatments, setup, failed starts, uploads and recorder storage add cost. ([Run accounting](../../../progress/receipts/03-run.md), [historical preservation receipt](../../../progress/receipts/03-preserve.md), [cost calculations](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

At **$50**, prioritize a 1,500 to 2,000-episode development pilot, and preserve held-out intervention tasks if a credible final comparison cannot fit. At **$100**, a narrow complete pilot is plausible under favorable throughput. One illustrative envelope is 288 gate episodes, three methods with three independent searches of 192 training episodes each, and 1,620 final episodes. The final allocation tests all nine search winners on nine held-out tasks with 12 fresh seeds each, plus native and fixed controls matched within the three replicate blocks. Total: **3,636 episodes**, about $68 raw at the last gate's performance, or $93 at the slower historical scenario, before overhead. This is an optimistic feasibility calculation, not an endorsed power calculation: additional diagnostic episodes, fresh development confirmation and ranking calibration are not fully allocated in that envelope. ([Detailed envelope](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

That envelope exposes the main constraint: 192 training episodes buy only 16 candidates at 12 repeats, before reevaluation. At a 23% success rate, a 12-episode estimate has standard error about 12 percentage points. A ten-point gain requires approximately 316 independent episodes per arm for 80% power under a standard planning calculation; useful pairing can reduce that, but its covariance is lever-dependent. Three optimizer runs show variability descriptively, not a robust distribution of optimizer performance. If the gate finds only a small effect or unstable ranks, spend on replication or retain a fixed-setting result instead of conducting a nominally complete optimizer contest. ([Uncertainty calculations](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md), [few-run evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264))

Before further GPU spend, reconstruct closure-before-carry scores from preserved sidecars, retain the benchmark scores, and reconcile all interactions including identity, smoke and failed starts. Freeze actual simulator-step ceilings for searches, counting discarded candidates and learner tuning. Equal episode allocation is not exact interaction matching: the last equal-size arms consumed different steps because successful episodes ended early. Report model queries and wall time separately. Final evaluation must retain full benchmark horizons and all outcome classes. Keep the nine held-out tasks untouched by new interventions until every controller and analysis choice is frozen; evaluate all independent search winners rather than the luckiest. Treat task transfer and optimizer variability as separate levels of uncertainty. ([Accounting and scoring requirements](../research_notes/GECCO%20contribution%20after%20retry/evaluation.md))

## Match the paper route to the evidence

A compact learned execution controller fits EML most naturally. A substantive, independently confirmed trade-off front could fit EMO. A noise-focused BBSR contribution would require actual search decisions, winner's-curse or ranking measurements, and a tested remedy such as structured replication or elite reevaluation, compared at equal cost. Binary-response resampling already has precedent, so renderer nondeterminism alone is not methodological novelty. A reusable measured candidate bank could make allocation experiments cheap, but replay claims remain conditional on that bank and need an independent online check. ([EML scope](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/Track?itemId=53), [EMO scope](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/Track?itemId=54), [BBSR scope](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/Track?itemId=2672), [binary-response resampling precedent](https://jkimst.org/upload/pdf/KIMST-2022-25-4-412.pdf))

If bounded, mechanism-validated levers stay flat, a diagnostic or negative-result companion submission is the responsible fallback. It still needs a clear EC connection beyond reporting one robotics null. The student workshop is a legitimate smaller route based on the 2026 call, which admitted Bachelor students, not a guaranteed outcome. Official GECCO 2027 pages confirm 12 to 16 July in Krakow, but the fetched call still lists January 2026 submission dates; **2027 deadlines remain unconfirmed**. Use late January as an internal planning assumption and verify finalized instructions before submission. No route guarantees acceptance. ([2026 student workshop](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Call%2Bfor%2BStudent%2BWorkshop), [2027 homepage](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/HomePage), [fetched call](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/Call%2Bfor%2BPapers))

The next commitment should therefore be small and decisive: complete offline scoring, freeze a contraction gate and its one fallback, and reserve most of the budget until leverage and ranking stability are measured. That preserves the possibility of an evolutionary contribution without making a favorable evolutionary result a requirement for telling the truth about the experiment.
