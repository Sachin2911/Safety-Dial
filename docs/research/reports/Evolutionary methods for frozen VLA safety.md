# Evolve the supervisor, not the sampler

The one direction that is at once simple, inside a 1,000–2,000-episode budget, and absent from the fetched literature is an evolutionary search (CMA-ES or NSGA-II) over **four to six execution and supervisor parameters** wrapped around the frozen pi0.5 — the executed prefix in free space versus around gripper events, a post-contact action scale, a stall patience and a scripted retry — scored directly against ManiGuard's LTLf monitor as a **two-objective front of safe-success against violation rate**. The six notes converge on this for three reasons. Everything that acts on the sampler is either already published or measured flat for contact-decided failures: openpi's flow sampler is a deterministic Euler integration whose only randomness is the initial noise, random best-of-N adds about one point, fixed "golden" noises fail on precision-contact tasks, and raising sampler noise drops pi0 from 62% to 9% ([openpi pi0.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/models/pi0.py); [DiVeR](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04933v1); [Golden Ticket](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15757); [pi-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25889v3)). The gradient and population alternatives are either out of budget by two to three orders of magnitude (ES fine-tuning at 10^4–10^6 rollouts, residual RL at 10^5–10^8 steps, quality diversity at 10^5–10^6 evaluations) or already demonstrated (DSRL steers a frozen pi0 in 65–80 real episodes), so sample efficiency cannot be the claim ([Qiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372); [ResiP](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677); [MOME](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03057v2); [DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)). And every execution-level and supervisor-level knob in the literature — executed horizon, speed, stall and retry, slow-or-stop filters — has been measured only against success, never against a safety specification, and never tuned by any black-box search; the closest precedents, CMA-ES over six merge coefficients of a frozen LLM and NSGA-II over a small controller head with a collision objective, both ran on about 1,000 evaluations and both fit the budget ([Akiba et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187); [NeuroAction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1)). The mandatory comparison set is the untouched policy, random search and a Latin-hypercube one-shot at identical episode budget and seed panels, a scalarised single-objective run, and a Bayesian-optimisation arm or a sentence excluding it; the statistics are Wilson intervals, McNemar on seed-paired held-out episodes, a pre-registered ten-point minimum effect (roughly 220 paired or 300 unpaired episodes per arm, far more than the 104 measured so far), three to five EA runs with every front shown, and hypervolume bootstrapped over held-out episodes ([Bartz-Beielstein et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.03488); [Brown, Cai, DasGupta](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ss/1009213286)). Two cautions govern every "first to" statement below: the researchers' shared web-search budget ran out part-way through, so "no example found" means none in the fetched papers, their reference lists and the GECCO 2023–2026 main-track lists rather than a database sweep; and GECCO's RWA track excludes public benchmarks, so EMO or NE is the natural home ([RWA track](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Track?itemId=60)).

## Budget arithmetic eliminates most of the published winners

The evolutionary-computation literature's headline results around frozen large models are text-domain and expensive. Full-parameter evolution strategies now fine-tune billion-parameter LLMs with **population 30 for 500 to 1,000 iterations** and beat GRPO on Countdown (Qwen-2.5-7B: ES 66.8 versus RL 52.8–57.5), but the paper mentions no robotics, control, diffusion or flow policy anywhere ([Qiu et al., arXiv 2509.24372](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372)). EGGROLL's low-rank perturbations reach 1,024 to 8,192 parallel generations per update over 260 to 915 updates, and its only control policies are 3-layer MLPs trained from scratch on Brax and Kinetix ([EGGROLL](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652)). Even the smallest black-box LLM tuning on record, CMA-ES over a 500-dimensional random subspace of a frozen RoBERTa, needed **8,000 inference calls on a noiseless classifier** ([BBT](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03514)). The note's search of these full texts, their bibliographies, and the GECCO 2023–2026 and EvoStar 2025–2026 title lists found no ES, GA or neuroevolution fine-tuning of a VLA or a diffusion/flow robot policy, with the explicit caveat that arXiv postings after June 2026 could not be searched ([evolution note, KQ1 gaps](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652)).

The residual and adapter line is mature and gradient-based. ResiP wraps a frozen chunked diffusion policy with a PPO residual on 1,024 parallel environments under a **500M environment-step cap**; Policy Decorator bounds a SAC residual to (−alpha, alpha) with alpha hand-set per task between 0.03 and 0.8 and switches it on over 1M to 8M steps; ResFiT reaches 64% from 14–23% in 134 to 343 real episodes with an off-policy actor-critic ([ResiP](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677); [Policy Decorator](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630); [ResFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301)). DSRL is the result a reviewer will hold up: SAC over the frozen policy's initial noise takes a real pi0-DROID toaster task from **5/20 to 18/20 in 80 episodes**, needs only forward passes, and reports that ResiP-style residual RL and V-GPS "don't significantly improve pi0" while warning that "a highly concentrated action distribution may offer too few options for steering" ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)). Two published facts therefore pin the design space: RL wrappers already work in 40 to 350 episodes, so an evolutionary wrapper cannot claim sample efficiency, and per-step residual RL on a 3B flow policy is not a free win, so the gradient baseline a reviewer demands is not guaranteed to beat the untouched policy either.

Quality diversity and tiny-controller neuroevolution set the other cost scale. Cully's hexapod repertoire took **40 million evaluations over two weeks** before the robot adapted in 3–16 physical trials; the Doncieux group's QD-for-grasping study runs 100k to 400k evaluations per domain; MOME and its successors use 1,000,000 to 1,024,000 evaluations on Brax ([Cully et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501); [Huber et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.05483v2); [MOME-P2C](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12433v2)). Even the celebrated small controllers are not cheap per generation: World Models' 867-parameter controller used **population 64 with 16 rollouts each**, 1,024 episodes per generation, and Tang et al.'s 3,667-parameter attention agent used population 256 with 16 rollouts ([World Models](https://worldmodels.github.io/); [Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.08165v2)). The entries that do fit the budget share one property: the search space is tiny and the base model is untouched. Akiba et al. ran CMA-ES over about six TIES/DARE merge coefficients of frozen LLMs for 1,000 trials and lifted MGSM-JA from 30.0 to 52.0 ([Akiba et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187)); NeuroAction evolved a 2×32 head with NSGA-II at population 50 for **1,000 function evaluations of 2 episodes each**, six runs, and selected deployment policies "directly from the learned Pareto set without retraining" ([NeuroAction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1)); a cobot PD-tuning study found about 30 individuals best for NSGA-II with a torque-smoothness "safety" objective ([Navarro-Cabrera et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08988)); Mason and Hauert evolved a 6-5-2 network with objective weights as inputs in 20,000 evaluations so one run yields the whole preference sweep ([Mason & Hauert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14237)). The notes' own arithmetic makes the constraint concrete: evaluating every individual on the full 26-task Jar panel buys only 38 distinct individuals at 1,000 episodes or 76 at 2,000, whereas CMA-ES at its default λ = 8 for five parameters with k = 8 episodes per individual gives 31 generations at 2,000 episodes, and k = 4 gives 62 ([Hansen tutorial](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.00772)). The design decision is forced before any method is chosen: a stratified sub-panel per individual, a search space of at most a handful of scalars or a 10^2-parameter net, and 600–1,000 episodes reserved for confirmation.

## Sampler-side interventions are published or flat for contact-decided failures

The notes resolve the local observation that same-seed repeats are frame-identical until contact. openpi's `sample_actions` is a **deterministic 10-step forward-Euler integration** from unit-variance Gaussian noise; the step function uses no RNG, there is no temperature or noise-scale parameter, and `Policy.infer()` accepts an explicit `noise` array, so "given a fixed noise, the output is fixed" ([openpi pi0.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/models/pi0.py); [openpi policy.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/policies/policy.py)). Divergence after contact is therefore the simulator, not the sampler, and ManiGuard itself reports that two identically seeded runs differed in 39% of overview pixels, changing episode lengths ([ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)). The policy already runs at the minimum stochasticity its sampler allows, which removes "lower the temperature" from the list of cheap interventions; raising it is measured harmful, since converting pi0's ODE to an SDE drops LIBERO-Spatial from about 65% deterministic to **62.3 / 56.0 / 46.6 / 9.4%** as the noise scale rises from 0.2 to 1.0, and genetic denoising likewise found a per-step noise scale of 0.2 decisive ([pi-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25889v3); [Genetic denoising](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21991)). Denoising-step count is on a plateau in every ablation found: Dita peaks at 10–20 steps, DPPO is robust above four, and DSRL reports step count "barely affects" it ([Dita](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19757); [DPPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00588v3); [DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)).

Noise search is the direction the literature both motivates and undercuts. Golden Ticket replaces per-step noise with a single constant vector found by random search or CEM and improves 46 of 51 tasks — SimplerEnv GR00T N1.5 63.0% to 93.4%, real Franka average 57.5% to 88.5% — but its named failures are precisely the precision-contact tasks: robomimic **Square fell from 52.2% to 42.6% with "no golden ticket found"** and DexMimicGen Threading moved 62% to 60%; the authors leave the mechanism to future work and never decompose outcome variance into noise versus physics ([Golden Ticket](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15757)). The fraction-of-tickets statistic is the cheap diagnostic the paper uses — 2.5% of random noises beat the base on Can with a 28-D MLP versus over 50% for pi0 on a LIBERO-90 task — and the local finding that noise evolution found nothing on Jar is the ManiGuard analogue of Square and Threading. The nuance the inference-time note insists on is that the same-seed evidence shows the physics is stochastic, not that the chunk choice is irrelevant to the odds, because holding the chunk fixed cannot test the second claim; nothing in the literature changes physics after contact, so every inference-time method is a bet that the failure is already decided by the chunk chosen before contact.

Best-of-N without a learned scorer is flat and with one is in budget but silent on safety. DiVeR's control is the cleanest number: among 16 pi0.5 candidates on RoboCasa, **random selection gains +1.0 point (43.1 to 44.1) while the trained verifier gains +11.6 (to 54.7)**; the verifier is a two-layer, 64-unit MLP on the frozen action expert's representation trained on 160 to 540 self-rollouts with episode-level labels and scores a chunk in under 0.5 ms ([DiVeR](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04933v1)). MG-Select saturates at N = 4 but needs a token distribution the flow head does not produce and dropout at training time ([MG-Select](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05681)); V-GPS and RoboMonkey need a Cal-QL critic trained on roughly 175K episodes or a LLaVA-7B reward model on 20M synthetic pairs, and V-GPS degrades past K = 50 ([V-GPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816v2); [RoboMonkey](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811v2)). No best-of-N paper reports a constraint-violation, drop or tip-over rate, and the one safety-relevant selection datapoint is negative: on Robomimic Can Transport with a "keep the can upright" specification, **sample-and-rank left violations at 82% (base 84%) while world-model gradient guidance cut them to 4% at unchanged 100% success** ([Temporal Logic Guidance](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22729)). That guidance result is the only measured safety effect in the whole inference-time family and the closest analogue to "keep the jar upright", but it is a three-page workshop paper with the guidance scale, step count and compute unreported, no independent replication, and it requires a differentiable next-state model trained on the policy's data plus a gradient path into the denoiser; the one contact-rich guidance datapoint elsewhere, Latent Policy Barrier's belt assembly (0.55 to 0.75), cost a 24-hour dynamics-model training run and "only corrects short-term, local deviations" ([LPB](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05941v2)). Naive objective gradients without annealing push samples off-manifold, raising Maze2D collisions from 0.01 to 0.06 and dropping block-stacking constraint satisfaction to 15% ([ITPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16627v2)).

Prompt evolution is a control, not a result. EvoPrompt gains up to 25% on BIG-Bench Hard over human prompts ([EvoPrompt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532)), but LIBERO-Plus shows pi0 falling from 94.2% to 61.0% under language perturbation while a blank instruction left OpenVLA-OFT "largely unchanged" and a swapped target still executed the original, and LIBERO-PRO finds pi0.5's outputs "remain unchanged even when given corrupted instructions or even messy tokens" ([LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626); [LIBERO-PRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03827)). On ManiGuard's own Jar family, adding a natural-language or LTLf safety specification to the prompt moved pi0.5-SFT safe-success from **26.92% to 24.36% and 14.10%**, and SafeManip's safety prompts cut GR00T success from 43.9% to 6.9% while violations fell only from 71.8% to 65.1% ([ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386); [SafeManip](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12386)). The only sampler-side item that is both in budget and unoccupied is a violation-aware verifier trained on the policy's own LTLf-labelled rollouts and used for best-of-N; the inference-time note states that no paper combines a safety verifier with best-of-N on a VLA, but it is not an evolutionary method and the upright-constraint datapoint predicts a small effect for contact-decided outcomes ([inference-time note gaps](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22729)).

## Execution and supervisor knobs are measured for success, never for safety, never by search

ManiGuard fixes the execution protocol without explanation and defers every runtime intervention. pi0.5 predicts 16-step chunks and every policy executes the first 8 before re-inferring, joint-position control at 20 Hz with 120 Hz physics, Jar horizon 2,000 steps; **no ablation of chunk length, executed actions, speed or re-planning appears anywhere**, and no rationale is given for 16 versus 50 or for executing 8 ([ManiGuard appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)). The monitor "observes and never intervenes", there is "no idle timeout or stall penalty", violations count only after first whole-arm contact with a task object, a rollout ends when the goal predicate holds for ten consecutive steps, and the paper lists "runtime shields, action filters, and monitor-based rewards" as not tested ([ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)). Three scoring facts shape any wrapper. Violations on Jar come early — median time-to-violation for pi0.5-SFT is 14% of the post-engagement window in distribution — which matches the local lid-push and regrasp-descent diagnosis. A robot that never engages is "vacuous-safe", so the safe-unsuccess class (27 of 78 in the published Jar table, the "third of episodes stall") is inflated by disengagement and engagement rate must be reported alongside safety. And because the monitor never aborts, a supervisor that retries after a violation cannot recover that episode's verdict, whereas a supervisor that stalls to stay safe is scored safe-unsuccess rather than penalised ([ManiGuard Table 13 and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)).

The executed-prefix knob is the best-measured execution lever on pi0.5-class policies, and its sim effect at ManiGuard's chunk length is small. For 50-step chunks the cliff is steep: pi0.5 on LIBERO-Spatial reads **94.9% executing 10 of 50 versus 71.5% executing all 50**, and real Franka pick-and-place swung from 0.0% at 5 of 50 to 81.5% at 10 of 50 on one task ([AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445)). For a 16-step chunk, the only sweep available (GR00T N1.5 on LIBERO) is non-monotone and within about 5 points from e = 4 to e = 16, so ManiGuard's 8 of 16 sits inside a plateau and the action-chunk note infers that this knob alone "is unlikely to move success by tens of points for a 16-step pi0.5" in simulation ([AutoHorizon Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445)). Fully closed-loop execution is a cliff in the other direction — Diffusion Policy at one action per query scores 0.0% on every FurnitureBench task versus 63.8% executing 8, and a real pi0-class arm gave 27.5% at 5 of the chunk versus 79.2% at 15 ([TAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421); [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567)). What the five 2025–2026 adaptive-horizon papers agree on qualitatively is the mechanism an evolved dial would exploit: the right horizon is **short around grasping, placing and flipping and long in free space**, the committed prefix shrinks "during contact-critical phases", long fixed horizons "cause object drops" and moderate ones make the robot "overreach or collide", yet not one of them measures drops, tips or collisions ([AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445); [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567); [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434); [Knowing When to Stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00908)). Where the chunk boundary falls matters on its own — the best versus worst re-planning phase spans 11.3 points on RoboTwin — and the closest thing to black-box tuning of an execution knob is Bernoulli-Continuation's GRPO over a continue/replan head on a frozen VLA, rewarded on success with a replanning-efficiency term and no safety term ([Continue or Replan?](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03483)).

Speed, smoothing and compliance are measured for success only, and slowing down has one negative datapoint. Executing a chunked policy 2× faster at test time costs 8–10 points on average and far more on insertion; the only sub-1× result, ACT-A at half speed, sits **16 points below its scripted expert** in simulation, so time-scaling a chunk moves it off distribution even when slower is physically safer ([DemoSpeedup](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05064); [Temporal robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01453)). Every speed paper converged on phase-dependent slowing keyed to gripper open/close events, which SAIL implements as a binary flag — a trigger directly reusable as a dial input ([SAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11948)). Temporal ensembling hurt pi0 in its authors' hands and, on a real pi0.5 under +100 ms latency, oscillated hard enough to trigger the protective stop, so it is not a cheap smoother for a flow VLA ([pi0](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164); [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)). Compliance has the largest measured real-robot effect of any execution knob (stiff 14%, fixed-compliant 23%, adaptive 96% on flipping, and the stiff policy broke its tool), but OmniGibson's joint-position controller does not expose stiffness ([ACP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09309)). The knobs with no published measurement for either success or safety on a learned policy are exactly the ones the Jar diagnosis points at: a descent-velocity cap near the table, a gripper closing-rate limit, post-contact delta scaling, a scripted lift-and-retry, and proprioceptive stall detection with re-query ([action-chunk note, ranked list](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445)).

Supervisors and retries have cheap published instances, all hand-tuned. The pattern across deployable filters is that intervention style matters more than certificate machinery: PACS, which slows or stops along the policy's own path rather than steering away, keeps real-robot success at **0.79 to 0.80 while the violation fraction falls from 0.56 to 0.00**, whereas a per-step CBF projection collapsed simulated success to 0.04 and real success to 0.43 ([PACS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06385)). The simplest retry that needs no training is VLA-Corrector's rule-triggered "discard the pending chunk and re-prompt the same frozen pi0.5 with a recovery directive", **70.9% to 82.6% on LIBERO**, with thresholds (tau = 0.40, four consecutive positives, 10-step cooldown) set by hand ([VLA-Corrector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06508)). Proprioception-only triggers work but lose part of the gain — B2FF's online trigger recovers 64.5% against 74.0% with oracle timing and 56.3% without, so about 46% of the oracle benefit survives — and AFI's proprioceptively detected "memory traps" with repositioning report +20.2% on LIBERO-Pro for pi0/pi0.5 ([B2FF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09258); [AFI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07472)). Retreating beats stopping as a fallback (X-Safe 66.4% versus 44% success at zero collisions), but recovery motions themselves fail often (FLARE's reset skills succeed 20–88% of the time) and no paper scores retreat or reset motions against a temporal-logic monitor ([X-Safe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22278); [FLARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26645)). The supervisors note states the gap plainly: no paper tunes a runtime supervisor over a learned manipulation policy or VLA by evolutionary or other black-box search; the only trade-off evidence is one-dimensional hand sweeps, such as iKinQP-RL's n in {6, 7, 8} giving 84/88/93% success, and the latent safety filter's SQRL threshold trading **60 points of completion for 6 points of violations** between epsilon 0.1 and 0.05 ([iKinQP-RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08233); [Latent safety filter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)). CMA-ES does appear nearby — tuning impedance-controller gains "because its objective is only available through rollouts", and as "CMA-ES for Safe Optimization" at GECCO 2024, which is about avoiding unsafe evaluations during search rather than tuning a supervisor ([Deliberate Practice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13415); [Uchida et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10534)).

## The unoccupied cell is a multi-objective evolved wrapper, and GECCO has never seen a VLA

Each note independently reports the same empty cell. The evolution note concludes that "frozen VLA + evolutionary search over a low-dimensional wrapper (execution knobs, supervisor rules, or a tiny modulator) + multi-objective safety/speed front" collides with no fetched paper, the nearest collisions being DSRL (frozen pi0, black-box, but RL over a 1,600-dimensional latent) and Akiba et al. (CMA-ES over six knobs, but LLM merging) ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799); [Akiba et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187)). The multi-objective note searched for NSGA-II or any MOEA over a wrapper around a frozen VLA or diffusion policy and found none; its nearest neighbours are gradient-trained residuals, reward-parameter evolution outside an RL learner, and MOAE's LLM-agent archive that "applies constraint-aware selection only at deployment" ([MOAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05992)). Neither note found a neuroevolved gating or modulating network over a frozen learned manipulation or locomotion policy since 2020; the hierarchical examples either evolve both levels or, as in Chalumeau et al., train the meta-controller with PPO for 200 million interactions over an evolved repertoire, and the evolved-supervisor precedents use scripted skills as leaves (GP-evolved behaviour trees that transfer to a real assembly robot) ([Chalumeau et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.03516v4); [Styrud et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09036)). The structural idea — a tiny network from cheap features to a few execution knobs over a frozen skill — therefore exists with scripted leaves and with gradients, and the combination with a frozen learned VLA as the leaf appears unoccupied in the fetched literature.

The methodological framing is established even though the application is not. In Hayes et al.'s MORL taxonomy the proposal is "multi-policy MORL with a posteriori (decision-support) preference articulation", where a set of policies is presented and the user selects by preference ([Hayes et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.09568v1)); Deb's constrained domination handles infeasibility inside NSGA-II without penalty coefficients ([Deb et al.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/996017)); and SNES is the least-machinery precedent for an ES with a probabilistic safety constraint — population 20, a 32-unit hidden layer, a Beta(s+1, v+1) posterior over the satisfaction probability setting the Lagrangian weight automatically, with a maximum-likelihood variant that "over-satisfies the constraint and obtains lower returns" ([SNES](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03898v2)). Reviewers from EMO will expect the first framing and reviewers from safe RL will ask why not the second; a one-paragraph mapping between them pre-empts both. The deployment story also has a template: NeuroAction's knee-point or scenario-specific point chosen from the front without retraining, or Mason and Hauert's preference-as-input network that traces the front by sweeping its weight inputs ([NeuroAction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1); [Mason & Hauert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14237)).

GECCO's own record makes the venue risk relevance rather than novelty. Across the 2023–2026 main tracks the foundation-model content is LLMs as search operators, LLM-driven algorithm design, prompt spaces and, in 2025, EC guiding a diffusion image model (ImageBreeder); the robotics items are evolutionary MORL for MuJoCo (2023), search-based repair of DNN controllers guided by system-level specifications (2024 SBSE), neuroevolution versus RL in transfer (2025), QD and uncertain-QD methodology throughout, and **no GECCO 2026 title mentions MORL, manipulation, or a VLA or foundation robot policy** ([GECCO 2023](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html); [GECCO 2024](https://gecco-2024.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers); [GECCO 2025](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers); [GECCO 2026](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)). The likely reviewer pool is Complex Systems (QD and uncertain QD), EMO (constrained and chance-constrained MOEAs dominate 2024–2026), Neuroevolution and RWA; a "probability of violation" objective will be read through the constrained-MOEA lens, robotics budgets at GECCO are stated as 10^6 evaluations or GPU minutes, and a 1,000–2,000-episode study is unusual enough that it should be framed as an application constraint rather than a methods contribution ([multi-objective note, KQ6 inferences](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)).

The coverage caveats must travel with every "no example found". The evolution note's web-search budget was exhausted before it started, so all findings come from directly fetched arXiv pages, conference accepted-paper lists and one DOI; DBLP and the ACM Digital Library blocked automated access, so GECCO abstracts and the Companion volumes (workshops, posters, late-breaking) were never read; ALIFE, PPSN, CoRL, RSS and ICRA programmes were not scanned; and arXiv postings after June 2026 could not be discovered ([evolution note, method](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372)). The inference-time, action-chunk and supervisors notes report the same cut-off, with Semantic Scholar rate-limited for the supervisors search; the multi-objective note's 16 searches ran before the budget ended, after which dblp, ACM DL, ScienceDirect, Springer and IEEE Xplore were blocked, so PPSN, EvoStar, ALIFE and TELO tables of contents could not be read and "any claim about those venues is absent, not negative"; the noisy-fitness note's quota ran out twice and several classics are confirmed only by bibliographic records ([multi-objective note, KQ6 gaps](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)). The evolution note's own recommendation stands: a follow-up search over arXiv cs.RO and cs.NE for "evolution strategies" with "vision-language-action", and over the GECCO 2026 Companion, is needed before the related-work section claims priority.

The protocol that makes rank 1 credible is assembled entirely from cited practice, and it starts by partitioning seeds before any run into a search pool used for every selection decision and a held-out pool the optimiser never sees, because agents trained on fixed level sets overfit "even with 16,000 training levels" and the benchmarking guidelines require that conclusions "not depend on the precise seeds" ([Cobbe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02341); [López-Ibáñez et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.03380)). Within each generation every individual is evaluated on the same stratified panel of k = 4–8 (task, seed) pairs drawn fresh from the search pool — common random numbers, which are "paired samples, use the same seeds across algorithms" in the guidelines, gave "by far most of the improvement" on a real population-based problem but were harmful under some artificial noise models, so a one-paragraph check that pairing helped on the baseline is cheap insurance ([Bartz-Beielstein et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.03488); [Decock et al.](https://scholars.ln.edu.hk/en/publications/variance-reduction-in-population-based-optimization-application-t/)). The optimiser is pycma with `noise_handler=True` at λ = 8–12 for a scalarised run, whose rank-based uncertainty handling is "independent of the uncertainty distribution" and "requires only a small number of additional function evaluations", or NSGA-II at population 16–24 seeded from a Latin-hypercube design for the front ([Hansen et al. 2009](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/a-method-for-handling-uncertainty-in-evolutionary-optimization-wi/); [pycma](https://github.com/CMA-ES/pycma)). Static resampling with elites re-evaluated each generation is defensible against any demand for an adaptive scheme, since the 2025 noisy-NSGA-II comparison found "a one-shot static strategy" was "the only strategy that outperformed ARB across all settings", and Qian et al. prove re-evaluation does not uniformly help ([ARB](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21495v2); [Qian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.4987)). Stopping a search episode at the first violation is exact for both headline labels, because "violated" is observed directly and "safe success" requires no violation; only the unsafe-success versus unsafe-failure split and the violating episode's completion time are censored, so confirmation episodes run to the full horizon, and a calibration figure of early-stopped against full-horizon estimates on the same seeds is the empirical check of Hyperband's convergence assumption ([Hyperband](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06560)). No fetched paper studies noisy NSGA-II or UH-CMA-ES on Bernoulli fitness — every benchmark uses Gaussian or chi-squared noise — which the paper can state as a gap rather than hide ([noisy-fitness note, KQ1 gaps](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21495v2)).

The statistics are dictated by the fact that the unit of inference is the held-out episode, not the EA run. Wald intervals "cannot be trusted"; Wilson or Jeffreys are recommended for small n, and on the local scale the notes' own computation gives **24/104 = 0.231 with Wilson interval [0.160, 0.320] and 43/104 = 0.413 with [0.324, 0.510]**, intervals wide enough to swallow any ten-point change ([Brown, Cai, DasGupta](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ss/1009213286)). The notes' two-proportion power arithmetic (alpha 0.05 two-sided, 80% power) puts a ten-point gain from 0.23 at 316 episodes per arm unpaired, falling to 220 paired at a CRN correlation of 0.3 and 158 at 0.5, with power at 104 per arm only 0.32–0.36 — and ManiGuard's 39% pixel divergence between identically seeded runs says the pairing correlation is modest, so expect a 20–30% saving from pairing, not a free lunch. Each selected front point versus the baseline is then a McNemar test on seed-paired held-out outcomes (Fisher's exact if unpaired), paired with a pre-specified minimum effect and its power, which satisfies the guidelines' "pair every 'A outperforms B' claim with suitable statistical test results" and "report confidence intervals" ([Bartz-Beielstein et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.03488); [López-Ibáñez et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.03380)). Comparisons across the 26 Jar tasks or across more than two methods use Friedman with Holm-type post-hoc correction, not repeated pairwise tests ([Derrac et al.](https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/files/TematicWebSites/sicidm/2011-Derrac-SWEVO.pdf)). Thirty EA replications are infeasible (the notes estimate 2,000–4,000 GPU-hours), so the honest pattern is three to five independent runs with every per-run front shown and an attainment-surface summary, following the GECCO 2026 Companion precedent of five runs with 20 re-tests and Mann–Whitney with Holm–Bonferroni, and the 2026 EvoGym preprint's explicit statement that three-seed results are "descriptive comparisons under a fixed evaluation protocol" ([Leopardi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24191v1); [EvoGym co-design](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25832); [eaf](https://mlopez-ibanez.github.io/eaf/)). Hypervolume with a fixed, stated reference point is the headline front metric in every fetched MOEA-over-controller paper, reported as median and IQR with Wilcoxon tests; with only three to five runs the well-defined interval is a bootstrap over held-out episodes of the confirmed front, and uncertain-QD reviewers will expect the final front to be re-evaluated as a "corrected" front with the re-test count stated ([NeuroAction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1); [Flageat & Cully](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00463v2)).

The baselines are what the guidelines list and what reviewers habitually miss. Pure random search and a Latin-hypercube one-shot design at identical episode budget and seed panels are the floor — random search is the comparator most often omitted, it reuses the harness for free, and it is "competitive" with model-free RL on MuJoCo with linear policies, which doubles as the "why not RL" answer alongside the frozen policy and the 4–6-parameter space ([Bartz-Beielstein et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.03488); [Mania et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07055)). The scalarised single-objective run (CMA-ES on a weighted sum, swept over a few weights) is the "do you need multi-objective at all" control that MOME and NeuroAction both include ([MOME](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03057v2)). The "why not BO" question will come from EMO and ENUM reviewers because "surrogate-assisted algorithms tend to be algorithms of choice for small budgets" and Bayesian optimisation gives "the best trade-off" for high-DOF robots with low-dimensional policies; the answer is a matched-budget BO arm or one sentence noting Bernoulli, task-stratified, two-objective outcomes ([Bartz-Beielstein et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.03488); [Chatzilygeroudis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02303)). Sim-only is routine in evolutionary robotics and should be stated as the scope with the note that seeds reproduce layouts exactly ([Mouret & Chatzilygeroudis](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01518764)). The venue rules, using GECCO 2026 as the proxy for 2027, are **8 pages excluding references, double-blind, a ≤10 MB anonymised supplement with Zenodo recommended, and "whether there is enough information to permit replication" as a review criterion**; 2026's deadlines were abstract 19 January and paper 26 January, non-extensible, and GECCO 2027 is Kraków, 12–16 July, in person only, with no CFP yet posted ([GECCO 2026 CFP](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Call-for-Papers); [GECCO 2027](https://gecco-2027.sigevo.org/)). Because the RWA track "will not consider articles that use previously existing, publicly available real-world benchmark problems", ManiGuard's public status pushes the paper to EMO (Pareto dial, noisy objectives), NE (if the tiny controller is the contribution) or ENUM (a CMA-ES-centred methodological framing), and the problem statement should be written in that track's vocabulary ([RWA track](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Track?itemId=60); [GECCO 2026 tracks](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Tracks)).

## Conclusion

The contribution these notes support is a measurement that does not exist rather than a method that does: the first front of completion against specification violation traced through the execution and supervisor parameters of a frozen VLA, found by the plainest rank-based search the field has, under a noisy-Bernoulli protocol the field has never written down. The literature's own structure explains why that cell is empty — the robotics papers that own the knobs report success only and hand-set everything, the EC papers that own the methodology have never touched a diffusion or VLA policy, and the safety benchmarks test only data-side interventions and defer runtime layers to future work — and it also predicts where the effect will and will not come from. The free-space executed prefix of a 16-step chunk sits on a measured plateau, so it is not where the points are; the contact-phase prefix, a post-contact action scale, a descent cap and a stall-triggered retry are where the adaptive-horizon consensus, PACS's slow-on-path result and the rule-triggered re-prompt point, and nobody has measured any of them against a tip or drop. The Jar diagnosis (violations at a median 14% of the post-engagement window, a third of episodes stalled) says the dial should be keyed to gripper events and proprioceptive motion, not to time.

Two consequences follow for the next spend. The 104-episode baseline has a power of about one third for a ten-point effect, so the first purchase is not an EA run but a seed-paired, held-out baseline of about 200 episodes plus a four-point hand sweep of one contact-phase knob, which together calibrate the pairing correlation, the effect size, and whether early stopping preserves rank — the three numbers that decide whether 1,200 search episodes can find anything a reviewer will believe. And before any sentence claims priority, the search pass the notes themselves could not run — arXiv cs.RO and cs.NE for "evolution strategies" with "vision-language-action", and the GECCO 2026 Companion — must be done, because every "no example found" above is a statement about fetched sources, not about the field.

The researchers' shared web-search budget ran out, so the coordinator ran the arXiv API queries the notes asked for ("evolution strategies", "evolutionary", "CMA-ES", "genetic algorithm", "neuroevolution", "Pareto" and "black-box" each combined with "vision-language-action"; "evolutionary" with "diffusion policy"). Three papers the notes missed sit closer to the proposed direction than anything they cite, and none of them occupies it:

- **VLA-Pilot** ([arXiv 2511.14178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14178), submitted to RA-L): inference-time steering of a frozen diffusion VLA (DiVLA, RDT-1B) by evolving a population of 32 candidate action chunks for 10 steps per decision, mutation by partial forward-diffusion and re-denoising, selection by a GPT-4o-written keypoint reward grounded with DINO and SAM. Real-robot average success 0.31 to 0.62; 2.41 s per steering step; no safety or violation metric; needs a multimodal LLM in the loop. This is "evolution plus frozen VLA", so it must be cited and distinguished: per-decision search over chunks with an LLM reward, versus a once-evolved execution and supervisor wrapper scored against a specification monitor.
- **Online-ES for flow-matching VLAs** ([arXiv 2609.38855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38855)): perturbs sampled trajectories and converts the evolution direction into a weight update of GR00T N1.5's action expert; LIBERO multi-task 0.948 to 0.972; no safety metric. Weights are updated, so it is not a frozen-policy method, but it refutes the notes' "no ES on any VLA" as a literal statement.
- **Evolving cache schedules for diffusion-policy inference** ([arXiv 2607.20293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20293), PRCV 2026): evolutionary search over inference-time cache-refresh schedules of a frozen diffusion policy for speed (up to 8.05x), success "near-full", no safety metric. The nearest precedent for evolving inference-time knobs of a frozen policy, in the speed dimension only.

Also surfaced: **APEX** ([arXiv 2606.16504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16504)), an execution layer between a black-box policy and the controller that improves success by 4.8 to 25.8 points with no safety metric and no search over its parameters. The cell named in the ranking table, black-box search over execution and supervisor parameters of a frozen VLA scored as a completion-versus-violation front, remains unoccupied after this check.
