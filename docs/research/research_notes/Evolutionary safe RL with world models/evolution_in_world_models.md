# Evolutionary policy search inside learned world models ("evolving in imagination") and optimisers exploiting world-model errors

Research date: 3 October 2026. Status labels used throughout: **[peer-reviewed: venue]**, **[preprint]**, **[workshop]**. Where a detail comes from my prior knowledge rather than a page fetched in this session it is marked *(not re-fetched)*.

## Q1. Lineage from Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) through Risi & Stanley (GECCO 2019) to 2020-2026: who evolves policies inside learned models, with what models, and how is transfer measured?

### Takeaway
The "evolve a controller inside the dream" result (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018) has had surprisingly few direct follow-ups. The lineage split into four branches: (a) evolving the world model itself end to end, in the real environment (Risi & Stanley 2019; DIP 2021); (b) evolutionary or CEM *planning* (MPC) in a latent model, which is how every JEPA-style model (LeWM, V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM) is used today; (c) evolutionary policy search on learned or probabilistic models in data-efficient robotics and industrial batch RL (Black-DROPS 2017, GPRL 2018); and (d) model-based quality-diversity in imagination (DA-QD 2022, reset-free QD 2023, GuSS 2022/2024). I found **no 2023-2026 paper that evolves a standalone policy purely inside a pixel-trained JEPA or latent world model and reports how it transfers to the real environment**. Ha's table of virtual versus actual scores across temperatures is still the canonical way to measure the exploitation gap.

### Cited Findings
**Foundational (2018-2019)**
- Ha & Schmidhuber, "World Models", arXiv 1803.10122 (Mar 2018) **[preprint + interactive article]**, and "Recurrent World Models Facilitate Policy Evolution", **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2018]**, arXiv 1809.01999. The architecture is V (a VAE), M (an MDN-RNN) and C (a linear controller, `a_t = W_c[z_t h_t] + b_c`). C has only 867 parameters in CarRacing and is trained with CMA-ES: "population size of 64 ... each agent perform[s] the task 16 times with different initial random seeds", and fitness is the mean of those 16 rollouts Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/); [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/file/2de5d16682c3c35007e4e92982f1a2ba-Paper.pdf); [arXiv 1809.01999](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01999)
- In CarRacing, C was trained in the *real* environment on world-model features (z and h). It scored 906 ± 21 over 100 trials, where 900 counts as solved; a V-only agent scored 632 ± 251 Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- In VizDoom Take Cover, C was trained *entirely inside the MDN-RNN's dream* and then transferred to the actual environment. Fitness was survival time. Results by temperature τ (dream score / actual score): 0.10: 2086±140 / 193±58; 0.50: 2060±277 / 196±50; 1.00: 1145±690 / 868±511; **1.15: 918±546 / 1092±556 (best)**; 1.30: 732±269 / 753±139. A random policy scores 210±108 and the solve threshold is 750 Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- In the same paper, Ha & Schmidhuber proposed (but did not demonstrate on a hard task) an iterative loop: roll out in the real environment, train M, train C inside M, and repeat. They also suggested flipping the sign of M's loss in the real environment to drive curiosity Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- Risi & Stanley, "Deep Neuroevolution of Recurrent and Discrete World Models", **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2019, Prague, pp. 456-462]**, arXiv 1906.08857. A genetic algorithm trains all world-model components (vision, memory, controller) end to end and matches the original world model's performance on CarRacing. The evolved representations resemble those learned by gradient descent. Discrete latent variables "open[] opportunities for classical planning in latent space" Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08857); [GECCO listing](https://pure.itu.dk/en/publications/deep-neuroevolution-of-recurrent-and-discrete-world-models/). The abstract does not say fitness was computed inside a dream, and my reading is that fitness came from real-environment rollouts (see Gaps).
- Freeman, Metz & Ha, "Learning to Predict Without Looking Ahead: World Models Without Forward Prediction", **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2019]**, arXiv 1910.13038. "Observational dropout" withholds real observations at some timesteps, which forces a world model to emerge that is useful for control without any forward-prediction loss Source: [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/15cf76466b97264765356fcc56d801d1-Abstract.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.13038)
- Risi & Stanley, "Deep Innovation Protection", **[peer-reviewed: AAAI 2021]**, arXiv 2001.01683. Multi-objective optimisation temporarily reduces selection pressure on components that have just changed, which allows world models to be evolved end to end in 3D environments. The evolved models learn to predict survival-relevant properties without a forward-prediction loss Source: [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17470); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01683)

**Evolutionary planning in a latent model (2020)**
- Olesen, Nguyen, Palm & Risi, "Evolutionary Planning in Latent Space", arXiv 2011.11293 (23 Nov 2020) **[preprint on arXiv; venue not verified]**. The model is a VAE plus a mixture-density RNN, and Random Mutation Hill Climbing searches action sequences in latent space. The model is bootstrapped from random-policy rollouts and refined iteratively with rollouts from the improving planner, i.e. Ha's proposed loop applied to planning. It beats model-free baselines on a driving task Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.11293v1)

**Evolution against learned or probabilistic models in data-efficient robotics and industry**
- Chatzilygeroudis et al., "Black-Box Data-efficient Policy Search for Robotics" (Black-DROPS), arXiv 1703.07261 (Mar 2017) (IROS 2017 *(not re-fetched)*). After each episode it learns a dynamics model (a Gaussian process) and then optimises the policy against the model *and its uncertainties* with a parallel, rank-based black-box optimiser (CMA-ES), "where only the ranking of potential solutions matters". It was tested on two simulated benchmarks and a real low-cost manipulator Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07261)
- Hein, Udluft & Runkler, "Interpretable Policies for Reinforcement Learning by Genetic Programming" (GPRL), **[peer-reviewed: Engineering Applications of AI, Nov 2018]**, arXiv 1712.04170. A population of algebraic policy equations is evaluated by Monte Carlo rollouts on a world model learned from batch data (offline). Benchmarks are mountain car, cart-pole and the industrial benchmark Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.04170)

**Model-based quality-diversity in imagination (2021-2024)**
- Lim, Grillotti, Bernasconi & Cully, "Dynamics-Aware Quality-Diversity for Efficient Learning of Skill Repertoires" (DA-QD), **[peer-reviewed: ICRA 2022]**, arXiv 2109.08522. It runs MAP-Elites-style quality-diversity search "in imagination" using an ensemble of probabilistic neural networks, building an *imagined* repertoire, and then selects skills for real evaluation. It is about 20× more sample-efficient than earlier QD methods and can learn new repertoires zero-shot in imagination. The authors note that "due to the inaccuracies of the model, the final skill space coverage of DA-QD is lower than its imagined repertoire size." For zero/few-shot acquisition they "select 20 skills with the lowest model disagreement" for real evaluation Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08522); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522)
- Smith, Lim, Janmohamed & Cully, "Quality-Diversity Optimisation on a Physical Robot Through Dynamics-Aware and Reset-Free Learning", **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2023 Companion]**, arXiv 2304.12080. A learned dynamics model predicts the robot's behaviour, and a behaviour-selection policy "filters out uninteresting or unsafe policies predicted by the model". A physical quadruped learned a repertoire in two hours Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12080). An earlier version is "Learning to Walk Autonomously via Reset-Free Quality-Diversity", arXiv 2204.03655 Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03655) (venue not verified).
- Guided Safe Shooting (GuSS), arXiv 2206.09743 (2022/2024), uses MAP-Elites as a planner inside a learned model with safety costs. It is covered in detail under Q2 Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09743)

**CEM-style evolutionary planning is how JEPA and latent world models are used now (planning, not policy evolution)**
- LeWM, arXiv 2603.19312 (Mar-Jun 2026) **[preprint]**, plans with CEM in an MPC loop over a horizon of 5 steps at frameskip 5 (25 environment steps), using 300 samples, 30 iterations on PushT and 10 elsewhere, keeping the top 30 as elites Source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)
- V-JEPA 2-AC, arXiv 2506.09985 (Jun 2025) **[preprint]**, plans with CEM on an L1 energy between predicted and goal representations, with planning horizon T=1 for most tasks Source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- Unrelated despite the name: "Evolutionary Policy Optimization" (Wang, Su, Gupta, Pathak; arXiv 2503.19037, Mar 2025) combines a genetic algorithm over latent agent embeddings with policy gradients in the *real* environment. It does not evolve policies inside a world model Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19037)

**How transfer was measured**
- Ha & Schmidhuber: paired dream versus actual scores for each temperature (table above) Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- DA-QD: real repertoire coverage compared with the size of the imagined repertoire Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522)
- Black-DROPS: return on the real robot after each model-based optimisation episode Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07261)

### Inferences
- Ha's VizDoom result is still the only well-known case where a *policy* evolved purely in a learned pixel-based dream transferred successfully. It worked because a stochastic model with a temperature knob was available. LeWM's predictor is a deterministic next-embedding regressor (MSE loss), so the temperature trick does not apply directly; something like latent noise injection or an ensemble would be needed.
- The student's planned setup (ES, CMA-ES or QD evolving a compact policy on a 192-d JEPA latent, with exact MuJoCo snapshot branching as ground truth) sits where branches (a) and (d) meet, and I found no existing paper there. The obvious baseline is LeWM's own CEM-MPC planner (branch b).
- Model types used so far: MDN-RNN (Ha; Olesen), GA-evolved VAE/RNN (Risi & Stanley), Gaussian process (Black-DROPS), probabilistic NN ensembles (DA-QD), deterministic and mixture-density NNs (GuSS), and ViT/transformer JEPAs (planning only). I found no RSSM/Dreamer or transformer world model being used as an evolutionary *policy* training ground.

### Gaps
- Despite at least six differently phrased searches, I found no 2023-2026 paper that trains a standalone policy by ES, GA or CMA-ES purely inside a learned pixel world model (RSSM, transformer or JEPA) and reports real transfer. This may be a real gap or a search limitation; Semantic Scholar's "cited by" list for arXiv 1809.01999 was not crawled.
- Not verified: Olesen et al.'s peer-reviewed venue (possibly EvoApplications 2021); Black-DROPS' IROS 2017 venue; whether Freeman et al. used CMA-ES specifically; whether Risi & Stanley ever ran dream-only training (the abstract suggests not).

## Q2. Has anyone combined evolution in imagination with safety constraints or cost signals (safe RL in a learned model using ES, CMA-ES, GA or QD)?

### Takeaway
Very little work does this. The closest is **GuSS** (Paolo et al., arXiv 2022/2024, preprint). It uses MAP-Elites as a *decision-time planner* inside a learned dynamics model, with lexicographic cost-first archive replacement, tested on low-dimensional states (Safe Pendulum, Safe Acrobot, Safety-Gym SafeCar-Goal). It uses no model uncertainty and openly concedes that a wrong model "could easily lead the agent to unsafe states". Other safety work that uses world models relies on CEM *planning* (SafeDreamer's CCEM, RCE) or gradient actor-critics (SafeDreamer, LAMBDA, SMBPO). Evolutionary *constrained* RL (ECRL, ACERL, AutoCost) runs in the real environment with no world model. **I found no paper that evolves a deployable safe policy inside a pixel-trained latent world model and measures the gap between imagined and real constraint violations.**

### Cited Findings
**Closest prior work: GuSS**
- Paolo, Gonzalez-Billandon, Thomas & Kégl (Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, France), "Guided Safe Shooting: model based reinforcement learning with safety constraints", arXiv 2206.09743, v1 20 Jun 2022, v2 12 Sep 2024 **[preprint; no venue found]** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09743). Details below were read from the v2 PDF ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2)):
  - It calls itself "the first MBRL approach that combines QD and safety objectives in a principled way". MAP-Elites runs as an MPC-style planner on a learned model, either a deterministic NN or an autoregressive mixture-density net, with planning horizon H (H=50 and N=500 plans in the toy task).
  - Cost is an indicator (C=1 in an unsafe state). The archive's STORE rule replaces an elite only if the new candidate has lower cost, or equal cost and higher reward. SELECT draws parents only from zero-cost elites and pads with random policies when there are too few. The executed action comes from the highest-reward plan among the lowest-cost pool.
  - Environments are Safe Pendulum, Safe Acrobot and Safety-Gym SafeCar-Goal. Baselines are CEM, RCEM, safe random shooting, Safe-MBPO, CPO and PPO-Lagrangian. The authors report a favourable reward/safety trade-off and say GuSS is "the only method that manage[s] to solve" SafeCar-Goal.
  - The stated limitation, verbatim: "the performance of GuSS is dependent on the accuracy of the model used. If the model is wrong, it could easily lead the agent to unsafe states. In future work, we will work on incorporating model uncertainty with QD..."

**CEM-style constrained planning in learned models (not policy evolution)**
- Huang et al., "SafeDreamer: Safe Reinforcement Learning with World Models", **[peer-reviewed: ICLR 2024]**, arXiv 2307.07176. It adds Lagrangian methods to Dreamer and introduces online safety-reward planning (OSRP) with a Constrained Cross-Entropy Method (CCEM) over trajectories imagined in the world model and scored by learned reward and cost models. It reports near-zero cost on Safety-Gymnasium, including vision-only tasks. Its abstract attributes earlier failures to "model inaccuracies" Source: [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07176)
- Liu, Zhou, Chen, Zhong, Hebert & Zhao, "Constrained Model-based Reinforcement Learning with Robust Cross-Entropy Method" (RCE), arXiv 2010.07968 (Oct 2020) **[preprint; venue not verified]**. It estimates uncertainty with an NN ensemble inside an MPC loop, and a robust CEM optimises action sequences against both model uncertainty and constraints. It reports far fewer violations than baselines Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07968)
- Wen & Topcu, "Constrained Cross-Entropy Method for Safe Reinforcement Learning", **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2018]**. This is model-free CE policy search with constraints and the ancestor of SafeDreamer's CCEM Source: [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/34ffeb359a192eb8174b6854643cc046-Abstract.html)

**Evolved diverse repertoires plus learned safety models (sim-to-real rather than a learned world model)**
- Kaushik, Arndt & Kyrki, "SafeAPT: Safe Simulation-to-Real Robot Learning using Diverse Policies Learned in Simulation", **[peer-reviewed: IEEE RA-L, Jul 2022]**, arXiv 2201.13248. A repertoire of diverse policies is evolved in a *simulator*. Probabilistic reward and safety models are then learned from real observations, with simulator priors, and Bayesian optimisation over the repertoire respects safety constraints Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13248)
- Najib, Depeweg & Swazinna, "Iterative Batch Reinforcement Learning via Safe Diversified Model-based Policy Search", **[workshop: CoRL 2024 SAFE-ROL]**, arXiv 2411.09722 (14 Nov 2024). This is ensemble-based model-based policy search for industrial control with a safety term (staying within the data's support) plus a diversity criterion. The abstract does not say the search is evolutionary Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09722)

**Evolutionary constrained RL without a world model**
- Hu, Pei, Liu & Yao, "Evolving Constrained Reinforcement Learning Policy" (ECRL), **[peer-reviewed: IJCNN 2023]**, arXiv 2304.09869. Evolutionary RL that balances reward and constraint violation with **stochastic ranking** and keeps a buffer of Lagrange coefficients, evaluated on robotic control benchmarks Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09869)
- Hu et al., "Robust Dynamic Material Handling via Adaptive Constrained Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning" (ACERL), arXiv 2506.16795 (20 Jun 2025) **[preprint]**. A population of actors with constraint-violation restrictions, and no world model Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16795)
- "AutoCost: Evolving Intrinsic Cost for Zero-violation Reinforcement Learning", arXiv 2301.10339 (Jan 2023). Evolutionary search over intrinsic cost functions; each candidate trains CPO or PPO-Lagrangian, and the population converges to zero-violation policies (venue, possibly AAAI 2023, *not re-fetched*) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10339)
- "Safe Neural Evolutionary Strategies" (SNES), in "Synthesizing safe policies under probabilistic constraints with reinforcement learning and Bayesian model checking", **[peer-reviewed: Science of Computer Programming, 2021]**. It uses online Bayesian model checking to estimate the probability that constraints are satisfied. It has no learned world model, and I did not verify the authors Source: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167642321000137)

**Gradient-based safe RL with world models (context: same problem, different optimiser)**
- Thomas, Luo & Ma, "Safe Reinforcement Learning by Imagining the Near Future" (SMBPO), **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2021]**, arXiv 2202.07789. It assumes unsafe states can be avoided by planning a short time ahead, heavily penalises unsafe imagined trajectories, and gives guarantees under model-accuracy assumptions Source: [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/73b277c11266681122132d024f53a75b-Abstract.html)
- As, Usmanova, Curi & Krause, "Constrained Policy Optimization via Bayesian World Models" (LAMBDA), arXiv 2201.09802 (ICLR 2022 *(not re-fetched)*). It uses a Bayesian world model with posterior sampling, optimistic for reward and pessimistic for cost (my recollection, not re-fetched) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09802)
- "Learn With Imagination: Safe Set Guided State-wise Constrained Policy Optimization" (S-3PO), arXiv 2308.13140. A safety monitor over black-box dynamics plus an "imaginary" cost achieves zero training violations. Listed in PMLR v283 (2025); one search summary said AAAI 2024, so the venue is conflicting Source: [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/sun25a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13140)
- From search snippets only: PIGDreamer (ICML 2025) and FOSP (ICLR 2025) are further safe-RL methods built on Dreamer-style world models Source: [search hit: safe RL list](https://github.com/bitzhangcy/Safe-Deep-Reinforcement-Learning)

### Inferences
- GuSS establishes that **feasibility-first (lexicographic) selection inside a model-based QD archive** is workable. It also states that adding model uncertainty to that archive is open future work, which a GECCO paper could take up directly.
- The supervisor's brief is "no hand-crafted reward penalties or cost functions". Lexicographic or stochastic-ranking selection (GuSS's STORE rule, ECRL's stochastic ranking, CCEM's constrained elite selection) fits that brief better than Lagrangian penalties: the constraint becomes a feasibility predicate (the health rule decoded by probes) rather than a weighted penalty.
- GuSS differs from the student's setting in four ways: (i) low-dimensional states, not pixels or JEPA latents; (ii) per-timestep planning, not an amortised policy; (iii) no uncertainty; (iv) no ground-truth branching to measure false-safe rates. Each difference is a point of novelty for the student.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023-2026 paper titled or described as safe ES, CMA-ES, GA or QD "in imagination" with a latent or pixel world model. Searches covering safety + evolution + world model, safety + QD + archive, and evolutionary constrained RL turned up only the papers above.
- PIGDreamer and FOSP were seen only in snippets; I did not check what they do.

## Q3. Model exploitation by optimisers: what is known, and which mitigations fit evolutionary search?

### Takeaway
Optimisers exploiting model error is well documented in model-based RL (ME-TRPO 2018; MBPO 2019; MOPO and MOReL 2020), in evolutionary robotics (the "reality gap" and the transferability approach), and in evolution generally ("Surprising Creativity of Digital Evolution"). Ha & Schmidhuber's τ table is direct quantitative evidence that *CMA-ES specifically* exploits a learned dream: at low temperature, dream scores near 2,000 collapse to below random (~195) in reality. Most mitigations carry over naturally to black-box evolution because they act on the fitness value or on rollout termination, not on gradients.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence of exploitation**
- Ha & Schmidhuber found that the dream-trained Doom agent "discovered an adversarial policy to move around in such a way so that the monsters in this virtual environment governed by M never shoots a single fireball during some rollouts", because M "will occasionally generate trajectories that do not follow the laws governing the actual environment". Raising τ to 1.15 traded dream score (918) for real score (1092); at τ=0.1 the dream score was 2086 but the real score only 193 Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- Kurutach et al., "Model-Ensemble Trust-Region Policy Optimization" (ME-TRPO), **[peer-reviewed: ICLR 2018]**, arXiv 1802.10592. "The learned policy tends to exploit regions where insufficient data is available for the model to be learned, causing instability in training"; an ensemble keeps track of model uncertainty and regularises learning Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10592v2); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.10592)
- Koos, Mouret & Doncieux, the transferability approach (IEEE TEVC 2013; short introduction arXiv 1307.1870). "The most efficient solutions in simulation often exploit badly modeled phenomena to achieve high fitness values with unrealistic behaviors." Their remedy learns, by regression from a few real trials, a *transferability function* that scores how far simulation can be trusted for each behaviour descriptor, and adds it as an objective. They report 11 tests on the physical robot against 200,000 simulated evaluations Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1870)
- Lehman et al., "The Surprising Creativity of Digital Evolution", **[peer-reviewed: Artificial Life 2020]**, arXiv 1803.03453. It collects 27 anecdotes in which evolution exploited loopholes in fitness measures ("selection gone wild") and exposed simulator bugs ("unintended debugging") Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03453)
- Dharna, Lu, Sullivan, Lehman, Krakovna & Clune, "AI Finds A Way", arXiv 2608.23875 (24 Aug 2026) **[preprint]**. It gives 26 first-hand anecdotes of RL and foundation-model systems exploiting reward loopholes or unstated constraints Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23875)
- Wang et al., surrogate-assisted ERL (Information Sciences, 2022). The explicit design goal is to "prevent the optimization process from being misled by the possible false minima introduced by the surrogate" Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00129)
- GuSS: "If the model is wrong, it could easily lead the agent to unsafe states" Source: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2)

**Mitigations in the MBRL literature**
- **Short rollouts branched from real states.** MBPO (Janner, Fu, Zhang & Levine, **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2019]**, arXiv 1906.08253) shows that "short model-generated rollouts branched from real data" give the benefits of model-based methods "without the usual pitfalls" and "scale[] to horizons that cause other model-based methods to fail entirely" Source: [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/hash/5faf461eff3099671ad63c6f3f094f7f-Abstract.html)
- **Ensembles and probabilistic models with uncertainty propagated into the planner.** PETS (Chua, Calandra, McAllister & Levine, NeurIPS 2018, arXiv 1805.12114) uses probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling and CEM-MPC *(not re-fetched; cited as the base of uncertainty-guided CEM in the GuSS related-work section)* Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114); [GuSS PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2). RCE handles uncertainty and constraints inside a robust CEM Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07968)
- **Pessimistic halting.** MOReL (Kidambi, Rajeswaran, Netrapalli & Joachims, **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2020]**, arXiv 2005.05951) builds a pessimistic MDP. An "unknown state-action detector" thresholds ensemble discrepancy, and states above the threshold become an absorbing HALT state with a penalty, so real performance is approximately lower-bounded by P-MDP performance Source: [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=701686); [secondary summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00133)
- **Uncertainty-penalised reward.** MOPO (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2005.13239) applies a soft reward penalty from an estimate of model error, i.e. it trains in an uncertainty-penalised MDP *(described here from a secondary summary; primary not re-fetched)* Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13239); [secondary summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00133)
- **Uncertainty-weighted imagined data.** MEEE (arXiv 2107.01825) gives imagined transitions discounted weights according to model uncertainty Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.01825)
- **Rank-based black-box optimisation under model uncertainty.** Black-DROPS treats the expected return under the uncertain GP model as a noisy objective and uses rank-based CMA-ES Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07261)

**Mitigations specific to evolution and QD**
- **Noise or temperature in the dream** (Ha & Schmidhuber's τ) Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- **Filtering by disagreement plus real re-evaluation before entering the real archive.** DA-QD picks the 20 lowest-disagreement imagined skills for real evaluation and adds real transitions to the replay buffer Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522). Reset-free QD uses a behaviour-selection policy that filters policies predicted to be unsafe, plus a recovery policy Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12080)
- **Transferability as an extra objective** (Koos et al.) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1870)
- **Noisy and uncertain QD.** Flageat & Cully, "Uncertain Quality-Diversity", arXiv 2302.00463 (Feb 2023; venue not verified), treat fitness and descriptors as distributions, propose archive-sampling, parallel-adaptive-sampling and deep-grid-sampling, and introduce a per-generation sampling budget for evaluation. This is relevant because archives keep "lucky" elites, which is analogous to elites that only look good because of model error Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463v2)

### Inferences (how each mitigation maps onto evolution in a LeWM)
1. **Branched short-horizon fitness (MBPO-style).** Score each candidate policy as the mean or worst case over K imagined 10-block (0.8 s) segments started from *real* latent states, which the student can produce exactly via MuJoCo snapshots. This keeps fitness inside the validated horizon. Cost: the evaluation no longer captures delayed consequences, so whole-episode safety needs a terminal estimate (e.g. a probe-decoded margin at segment end).
2. **Pessimistic cost from disagreement (MOPO / LAMBDA-style).** Feasibility can be defined as max over ensemble members (or probe heads) of the predicted violation. A cheap ensemble: several probe heads, several predictor fine-tunes, or MC-dropout on the LeWM predictor.
3. **MOReL-style halting as a constraint.** When disagreement crosses a threshold, end the imagined rollout and *count it as infeasible*. Under a feasibility-first rule this converts "the model doesn't know" into "unsafe" without any penalty weight.
4. **Temperature analogue.** The LeWM predictor is deterministic, so inject Gaussian noise into predicted latents, scaled to the measured one-step residual. This recreates Ha's τ experiment for a JEPA model.
5. **Real re-evaluation budget for elites (transferability approach / DA-QD).** Snapshot branching makes ground-truth evaluation of elites cheap. Re-evaluate the top-k or all archive elites each generation and (a) correct their fitness and feasibility, and (b) feed the transitions back into world-model fine-tuning (Ha's iterative loop, Dyna-style).
6. **Conservative margin.** Tighten the health-rule thresholds decoded from probes by δ. The student's measured ~45% false-safe rate at zero margin is a calibration curve for choosing δ. Under selection pressure the false-safe rate among *elites* will probably exceed this unconditional rate (an optimiser's-curse effect; my inference, not sourced).
7. **Rank-based selection** (CMA-ES, MAP-Elites replacement) is unaffected by monotone distortions of fitness but not by error that differs from candidate to candidate, which is exactly what exploitation produces.

### Gaps
- I could not find a paper that measures, for an evolutionary optimiser, how the imagined-versus-real gap *grows with optimisation pressure* (generations or population size). Ha's τ sweep is the closest, but it varies the model, not the pressure.
- Jakobi's 1995 "Noise and the reality gap" (noise injection in evolutionary robotics) is the classic ancestor of the τ trick, but I did not verify it in this session.
- The details of COMBO and MOPO were not re-fetched.

## Q4. Surrogate-assisted evolutionary RL and neuroevolution: how do they handle surrogate error, and when does it hurt?

### Takeaway
Surrogate-assisted neuroevolution and ERL use *policy-space* surrogates: kernel or Kriging models over genotype or behavioural distance (Gaier 2018; Stork 2019), or learned classifiers over policy embeddings (PE-SAERL 2023; AE+HNN 2025). The surrogate pre-screens offspring and the real environment evaluates the survivors. Error is managed mainly by *always* confirming promising candidates with real evaluations and by controlling how often the surrogate is trusted. Explicit reports of the surrogate making things worse are rare. The 2022 SAC-ERL paper is the one that frames "false minima introduced by the surrogate" as the central risk. A world model is a *state-space* surrogate: it generalises across policies through dynamics but can be exploited along trajectories.

### Cited Findings
- Gaier, Asteroth & Mouret, "Data-efficient Neuroevolution with Kernel-Based Surrogate Models", **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2018, Kyoto; Best Paper Award]**, arXiv 1804.05364. A kernel surrogate uses NEAT's compatibility distance, so it works across variable topologies. On cart-pole swing-up and half-cheetah it reached the same or better results with several times fewer evaluations than NEAT Source: [H-BRS record](https://pub.h-brs.de/frontdoor/index/index/year/2018/docId/3646); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05364)
- Stork, Zaefferer, Bartz-Beielstein & Eiben, "Surrogate Models for Enhancing the Efficiency of Neuroevolution in Reinforcement Learning", **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2019]**, arXiv 1907.09300. It combines CGP-ANN under a (1+4)-ES with Kriging over a *phenotypic distance*: the difference between networks' outputs on a set of input states. On CartPole-v1 and MountainCar-v0 it used "about 70% (median) or 80% (mean) fewer function evaluations". Results were "fairly robust" to how the input states were chosen. The stated limitations are that computing the network outputs on those states becomes expensive for complex networks, and "we do not know which length of state vector is required" Source: [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3321707.3321829); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.09300)
- Stork, Zaefferer & Bartz-Beielstein, "Improving NeuroEvolution Efficiency by Surrogate Model-based Optimization with Phenotypic Distance Kernels", **[peer-reviewed: EvoApplications 2019]**, arXiv 1902.03419 Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.03419)
- Tang, Li, Zhang, Li, Yang & Tang, "Enabling surrogate-assisted evolutionary reinforcement learning via policy embedding" (PE-SAERL), arXiv 2301.13374 (31 Jan 2023) **[peer-reviewed: BIC-TA 2022 per the arXiv comments]**. Policy embeddings make the surrogate tractable for DNN policies with millions of weights. It reports up to 7× speedup on 5 Atari games Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13374)
- Wang, Zhang, Chang, Liang, Wang & Yuan, "A Surrogate-Assisted Controller for Expensive Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning", **[peer-reviewed: Information Sciences, DOI 10.1016/j.ins.2022.10.134]**, arXiv 2201.00129. The surrogate partly replaces real evaluations in hybrid RL-EA frameworks, and two control strategies stop the search from being "misled by the possible false minima introduced by the surrogate". Evaluated on six Gym continuous-control tasks Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00129)
- Li, Jiang, Qian, Tang, Zhou & Yang, "Surrogate-Assisted Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning Based on Autoencoder and Hyperbolic Neural Network", arXiv 2505.19423 (26 May 2025) **[preprint]**. An autoencoder compresses policies, and a hyperbolic-NN *classification* surrogate pre-selects sampled policies "without real evaluations". Evaluated on 10 Atari and 4 MuJoCo tasks Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19423)
- Bhatt, Tjanaka, Fontaine & Nikolaidis, "Deep Surrogate Assisted Generation of Environments" (DSAGE), **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2022]**, arXiv 2206.04199. A deep surrogate predicts agent behaviour in generated environments, and QD runs on the surrogate. Domains are Maze (with an ACCEL agent) and Mario (with an A* agent) Source: [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/f649556471416b35e60ae0de7c1e3619-Abstract.html)
- GPRL and Black-DROPS (Q1) are the "world model as surrogate" end of this spectrum: fitness is a Monte Carlo return under a learned dynamics model Source: [GPRL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.04170); [Black-DROPS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07261)

### Inferences
- Stork's phenotypic distance (outputs on a fixed set of states) is effectively a "behavioural probe" surrogate. The student's latent-state probes could serve the same role, comparing candidate policies by their actions on a bank of real latent states.
- The policy-space surrogate literature always keeps a real evaluation in the loop for the candidates it promotes. That is the same pattern as "re-evaluate elites in reality", and for the student it costs almost nothing thanks to snapshot branching.
- A GECCO angle: framing a LeWM as a *dynamics surrogate* for constrained neuroevolution links the student's work to an established GECCO line (Gaier 2018 best paper; Stork 2019; reset-free QD 2023 Companion).

### Gaps
- I found no surrogate-assisted ERL paper that systematically reports *when* the surrogate hurts (for example, rank correlation between surrogate and real fitness over generations, or failure cases). SAC-ERL names the risk, but the abstract gives no degradation statistics.
- I did not fetch SAIL (Gaier, Asteroth & Mouret, surrogate-assisted illumination, ~2018). The user's "Gaier et al. 2018" is taken to be the GECCO 2018 neuroevolution paper above.

## Q5. Long-horizon imagination error in latent and JEPA world models (LeWM, DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC, PLDM, etc.) and its effect on policy optimisation versus short-horizon MPC

### Takeaway
Every recent JEPA-style world model is deployed with **short-horizon, replanning MPC** (CEM horizon 1 for V-JEPA 2-AC; 5 steps at frameskip 5 for LeWM). Their own authors name autoregressive error accumulation as the limit on long horizons, and hierarchical models are the current response (HWM, Apr 2026; Hi-LeWM, Jul 2026). A 2026 workshop paper on DreamerV3 *walker-walk* finds imagination is "kinematic, not dynamic": imagined rollouts stay plausible-looking even after the real dynamics have changed. That matters directly for a Walker2d health rule. Evolving an amortised policy whose fitness comes from long open-loop imagination is the regime most exposed to these errors, so short branched evaluation plus closed-loop correction is what the evidence supports.

### Cited Findings
- Maes, Le Lidec, Scieur, LeCun & Balestriero, "LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels", arXiv 2603.19312 (v1 13 Mar 2026; v3 3 Jun 2026) **[preprint]**. About 15M parameters (ViT-Tiny encoder ~5M, 6-layer transformer predictor ~10M), latent dimension 192, losses are next-embedding MSE plus SIGReg. Planning is CEM-MPC with horizon 5 (25 environment steps). Environments are PushT, OGBench-Cube, TwoRoom and Reacher. Verbatim: "Planning remains restricted to short horizons, motivating hierarchical world modeling for long-horizon reasoning" and "auto-regressive rollouts accumulate prediction errors as the horizon grows, which can deteriorate the quality of the optimized action sequence." Probes recover positions and angles from the latents, and surprise is higher for physically implausible frames Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)
- Caselli et al., "Mind the Gap: Promises and Pitfalls of Hierarchical Planning in LeWorldModel", arXiv 2607.12547 (14 Jul 2026) **[workshop]**. Hierarchy on a frozen LeWM helps only "when high-level search remains compatible with the low-level controller": +11.3 points at medium horizons and +14.7 points at the longest PushT horizon. "High-level subgoal generation is the main bottleneck" Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12547)
- Zhang et al. (FAIR/NYU/Mila/Brown), "Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models", arXiv 2604.03208 (3 Apr 2026) **[preprint]**. "Learned world models often struggle with long-horizon control due to the accumulation of prediction errors." Multi-timescale latent world models reach 70% success on real pick-and-place against 0% for a single-level model, with up to 3× less planning compute Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03208)
- Assran et al., "V-JEPA 2", arXiv 2506.09985 (Jun 2025) **[preprint]**. V-JEPA 2-AC is trained on 62 h of DROID robot video and plans with CEM over horizon T=1 for most tasks. Verbatim: "autoregressive prediction suffers from error accumulation: the accuracy of the representation-space predictions decreases with longer autoregressive rollouts". It is also sensitive to camera pose because it has no calibration Source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- Sobal, Zhang, Cho, Balestriero, Rudner & LeCun, "Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models" (PLDM), **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2025]**, arXiv 2502.14819. Planning with a JEPA latent dynamics model generalises better to unseen layouts and is more data-efficient than model-free goal-conditioned or zero-shot RL; model-free RL wins only with large amounts of high-quality data Source: [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116649); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14819)
- Zhou, Pan, LeCun & Pinto, "DINO-WM", arXiv 2411.04983 (Nov 2024; v2 Feb 2025) **[preprint per the arXiv listing; later venue not verified]**. It predicts frozen DINOv2 patch features and optimises actions at test time without rewards or demonstrations Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983)
- Schäfer et al., "Imagined Rollouts are Kinematic, Not Dynamic: A Diagnosis of Long-Horizon World-Model Failure", arXiv 2607.05966 (7 Jul 2026) **[workshop: RSS 2026 Robot World Model Workshop]**. Using a DreamerV3 checkpoint on DMC walker-walk, the imagined kinematic-consistency error "runs roughly two orders of magnitude above that of matched real-physics rollouts". Across a friction sweep that crosses the boundary where the gait collapses, "the model's iKCE stays statistically flat even as the trained policy's reward collapses". The failures appear at horizons longer than the gait period Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05966)
- Raghavan & Singh, "Do Better Imagined Rollouts Mean Better Robot Control?", arXiv 2609.02811 (2 Sep 2026) **[preprint]**. On a differential-drive tracking task, open-loop rollout error ranks models worse (Spearman ρ=0.774 with closed-loop performance) than replay error does (ρ=0.923). Rollout-based ranking chose a suboptimal estimator in 18 of 24 conditions. "Long rollouts without correction can produce rankings that differ substantially from closed-loop behavior", while regular corrections keep the rankings predictive Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02811)
- Timor, Shwartz-Ziv, Goldblum, LeCun & Harel, "On Training in Imagination", arXiv 2605.06732 (May 2026) **[preprint]**. Theory of return error when training policies on imagined rollouts: lower Lipschitz constants of the dynamics, reward and policy models tighten the bounds, and it analyses how to split samples between dynamics and reward data Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06732)
- MBPO's short branched rollouts "scale[] to horizons that cause other model-based methods to fail entirely" Source: [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/hash/5faf461eff3099671ad63c6f3f094f7f-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- The student's measurements (prediction errors that grow with horizon, a 0.8 s / 10-block validated horizon, ~45% false-safe at zero margin) match the field's own reported limits. With frameskip 10, the student's horizon (10 blocks) is about twice LeWM's own planning horizon of 5 blocks at frameskip 5, measured in environment steps.
- The "kinematic not dynamic" finding on walker-walk suggests that predicted torso height and pitch may *look* smooth while missing the dynamic onset of a fall. That is a mechanism for false-safe errors and is worth testing: are false-safe cases concentrated at gait-phase transitions or high angular velocities?
- MPC replans from the true state every step, so an exploited error lasts only one step. An evolved policy scored by an imagined episode accumulates exploitation over the whole rollout. Comparing ES or CMA-ES policies against LeWM's CEM-MPC on the same safety metric would be a clean, publishable contrast.
- The Raghavan & Singh result implies that model selection or early stopping for evolution-in-imagination should use branched-from-real (replay-like) error, not long open-loop error.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports quantitative error-versus-horizon curves for LeWM specifically on locomotion, or any JEPA-model study of *policy* optimisation, as opposed to MPC, in imagination.
- DINO-WM's open-loop versus MPC comparison was not verified (the abstract did not include it).

## Q6. Concrete open gaps a 4-month project could fill, given the student's assets

### Takeaway
The most defensible GECCO-2027 contribution is an **empirical study of how evolution exploits safety errors in a JEPA world model, plus a comparison of evolution-native mitigations**, measured against exact MuJoCo ground truth. I found no prior work combining (i) evolution of deployable policies, (ii) a pixel-trained latent world model, (iii) a safety constraint with feasibility-first handling instead of penalties, and (iv) systematic measurement of the gap between imagined and real constraint violations. GuSS explicitly lists "incorporating model uncertainty with QD" for safety as future work.

### Cited Findings
- Closest prior systems and what each lacks: GuSS (QD planner with cost-first archive, low-dimensional, no uncertainty, preprint) Source: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2); DA-QD (uncertainty-filtered QD in imagination, no safety) Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522); SafeDreamer (constrained CEM planning plus gradient actor in an RSSM, not evolution) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07176); SafeAPT (evolved repertoire plus safety model, but repertoire from a simulator, not a learned model) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13248); ECRL (stochastic-ranking constrained ERL in the real environment) Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09869)
- The canonical measurement template is Ha & Schmidhuber's paired virtual/actual table across a model-uncertainty knob Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- Short horizons are the accepted operating regime for JEPA world models Source: [LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3); [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1); [HWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03208)
- GECCO has published this lineage: Gaier et al. 2018 (best paper), Risi & Stanley 2019, Stork et al. 2019, Smith et al. 2023 Companion Source: [Gaier](https://pub.h-brs.de/frontdoor/index/index/year/2018/docId/3646); [Risi](https://pure.itu.dk/en/publications/deep-neuroevolution-of-recurrent-and-discrete-world-models/); [Stork](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3321707.3321829); [Smith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12080)

### Inferences (candidate contributions, ordered from most to least feasible)
1. **A safety "τ table" for JEPA world models (the exploitation curve).** Evolve compact policies (linear or small MLP on the 192-d latent, Ha-style) with CMA-ES under a feasibility-first rule on the probe-decoded health rule. Plot imagined against real violation rates (via snapshot branching) as functions of (a) generations or population size (optimisation pressure), (b) imagined horizon from 1 to 10 blocks, (c) safety margin δ, and (d) injected latent noise σ (the analogue of τ). Expected finding to test: the false-safe rate among *selected* elites exceeds the 45% base rate and grows with pressure. This needs only the existing assets and inference on one RTX 5090.
2. **Head-to-head comparison of evolution-native mitigations** at a matched real-evaluation budget: margin δ; noise injection; ensemble or probe-head disagreement as pessimistic cost (MOPO-style); MOReL-style "uncertain → infeasible" halting; MBPO-style branched 10-block fitness from real start states; and top-k elite re-evaluation in MuJoCo (transferability / DA-QD-style), optionally with world-model fine-tuning on the corrected data (Ha's iterative loop). Report real return, real violations, and the gap between imagined and real cost.
3. **A safety-indexed QD archive.** A MAP-Elites archive with one descriptor axis set to predicted safety margin (or model uncertainty) and another to behaviour (e.g. speed or gait). The archive then *maps where the model's safety judgements fail*: re-evaluated cells show where false-safe errors cluster. This extends GuSS (cost-first replacement) with uncertainty, which is their stated future work, and uses ideas from Uncertain QD (archive re-sampling).
4. **Amortised evolved policy versus CEM-MPC in the same world model.** Does evolving a policy exploit safety errors more than per-step CEM planning with replanning? This is a clean experiment because LeWM ships a CEM planner.
5. **Testing the kinematic-imagination diagnosis on Walker2d.** Check whether false-safe predictions cluster near gait-phase or fall-onset dynamics, linking the student's probe measurements to the RSS 2026 workshop diagnosis.

**Feasibility notes (inference):**
- The world model was trained mostly on competent PPO and PPO-Lagrangian data, so falling or near-unsafe states are under-represented. Evolution will push policies into exactly those states, which makes out-of-distribution exploitation likely, and that is the phenomenon to measure. It also argues for including disagreement-based infeasibility from the start.
- The "no hand-crafted penalty or cost function" brief is best met by feasibility-first or lexicographic ranking on a *rule* (health thresholds) rather than a weighted cost. The probes become the constraint oracle, and their calibration error is part of what is studied.
- Compute is modest (LeWM ~15-18M parameters, inference only, batched across population × start states), so 4 months is plausible for contributions 1-2, with 3 as a stretch goal.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence was found on how much real-evaluation budget is needed to close the safety exploitation gap in an evolutionary setting. The transferability approach's 11 real tests are for a different (simulator) setting.
- I found no prior benchmark of safety-constrained evolution on Safety-Gymnasium velocity or health tasks inside a learned model, so baseline numbers would have to come from the student's own PPO and PPO-Lagrangian runs.
- The GECCO 2027 track structure and deadline were not verified here.
