## Key question 1 — NSGA-II / MOEA/D / SMS-EMOA tuning controller or supervisor parameters with a safety objective against a performance objective (2018–2026): knob counts, population sizes, budgets, and how the front is used at deployment

### Takeaway
Pareto-based tuning of a small set of controller/supervisor parameters against a "safety-like" objective exists but is sparse and mostly application-flavoured; NSGA-II is the default algorithm, populations are 20–60, budgets run from ~1,000 evaluations (NeuroAction) to 10^6 (QD-style papers), and the front is used at deployment by picking a knee point or a scenario-specific point without retraining. No fetched paper used SMS-EMOA; MOEA/D appears only as a baseline (PGMORL, NeuroAction).

### Cited Findings
- **Cobot PD-gain tuning with "safety" as an objective (closest controller-knob example).** Navarro-Cabrera, Luque, Ros, "Multi-objective tuning for torque PD controllers of cobots", arXiv:2309.08988 (accepted CPS workshop 2023). Tunes PD gains ("adjusting only two parameters per robotic joint", Kp and Kd) for a Kuka iiwa LBR 7-DoF arm in ROS2/Gazebo at 500 Hz, with NSGA-II; accuracy = "mean Euclidean distance between the end effector and the desired Cartesian coordinates"; the "safety" objective is a torque-smoothness term f_t(U) = (1/T) Σ(u_i − u_{i−1})². Population sizes were compared and "around 30 individuals" gave the best results, larger populations giving similar results at much longer runtime; each experiment repeated 5 times; the hypervolume indicator with a reference point set to the maximum values across tested controllers is used to compare fronts; the paper does not state how a final gain set is chosen from the front. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08988); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08988)
- **Evolved neuro-controllers with an obstacle-avoidance ("safety") vs target-reaching trade-off.** Moshaiov & Zadok, "On the Generalization Capability of Evolved Counter-propagation Neuro-controllers for Robot Navigation", arXiv:2004.12188 (2020). Bi-objective NSGA-II with F1 = "fast and straight motions with obstacle avoidance without any specific destination" and F2 = reaching targets; "we use the trade-off between safety and the attraction to targets to produce a [diverse set]"; population N = 56 divided into four groups of 14, 50 generations per group then 250 generations (300 total); controller genome of 163 components (162 connection weights). The authors note that "NSGA-II may not be the most optimal search [mechanism]... more advanced algorithms, such as the MO-CMA-ES, may be better. Yet NSGA-II [was used]". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12188)
- **Autonomous driving: NSGA-II over policy weights with a collision objective, front used as a deployment menu.** Aboyeji, Ajani, Fenyom, Mallipeddi, "NeuroAction: a neuroevolutionary approach to reinforcement learning for autonomous vehicles", Scientific Reports 16:7403 (5 Feb 2026), DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-38269-1. Policy = "a DNN with 2 fully connected layers with 32 units each" on top of the Stable-Baselines3 default feature extractor; 2053 decision variables evolved by NSGA-II, NSGA-III, MOEA/D, MOEA/DD and HypE in highway-env; objectives f1 speed (reward 0–0.4 over 20–30 m/s), f2 right-lane preference (0.1 per step), f3 collision avoidance (−1 penalty); population N = 50, maximum 1000 function evaluations, N_ep = 2 episodes per evaluation, 6 independent runs; hypervolume with reference point [−5,−5,−5], median and IQR, Wilcoxon rank-sum at 0.05. Deployment: "selection can be made directly from the learned Pareto set without retraining"; a knee-point policy is suggested for general driving; example vectors [7.60, 487, 0] (right-lane priority) and [52.80, 463.50, 0] (balanced). NSGA-II median HV 1.12×10^5 vs the scalarised policy-gradient baseline MORL-PG 1.91×10^3. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1)
- **UAV navigation: MOEA evolves reward-function parameters outside an RL learner.** An, Wu, Shen, Shang, Ishibuchi, "Evolutionary Multi-Objective Deep Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous UAV Navigation in Large-Scale Complex Environments", GECCO 2023 (EMO session): "MOEAs generate sets of non-dominated reward-function parameters for RL"; reported to beat traditional RL on UAV navigation; no budgets in the abstract. — [GECCO 2023 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html)
- **A "robustness dial" by Pareto front in a control application.** Edo, Miyauchi, Maki, Akimoto, "Trade-off Between Robustness and Worst-Case Performance in Min-Max Optimization", GECCO 2023 (Real World Applications): computes the Pareto front between uncertainty-set size and worst-case performance, applied to automatic ship berthing. — [GECCO 2023 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html)
- **NSGA-II used to search for unsafe behaviour (testing, not tuning).** Nylænder, Arrieta, Ali, Arcaini, "Search-based Generation of Waypoints for Triggering Self-Adaptations in Maritime Autonomous Vessels" (WPgen), GECCO 2025 (RWA): NSGA-II makes small waypoint changes that cause unsafe navigation; three vessels; Random Search is the baseline; three seeding strategies. — [GECCO 2025 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)
- An autonomous-driving testing paper states that evolutionary methods are established for testing ADS and that NSGA-II "is a state-of-the-art solution for optimization problems with up to three objectives" (snippet only). — [arXiv 2204.00480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00480)
- **AEB tuning with TTC vs jerk (snippet only; page returned 403).** A Brazilian conference paper uses NSGA-II in a CarMaker software-in-the-loop setup to trade time-to-collision against jerk for Autonomous Emergency Braking, compared against parameter sweeping. — [Blucher proceedings](https://www.proceedings.blucher.com.br/article-details/otimizao-multiobjetivo-eficiente-de-sistemas-aeb-um-estudo-comparativo-entre-sweeping-e-nsga-ii-40308)
- **Swarm parameter tuning with decision-making on the front (snippet only; page returned 403).** "Simulation-based multi-objective optimization for mission specific tuning of swarming robots" (Swarm and Evolutionary Computation, 2025): "NSGA2-MCM approach permits decision-makers to balance competing objectives by selecting optimal swarm parameters for specific mission requirements". — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2210650225003724)
- **Mobile-robot navigation with collision risk as one of five objectives (path planning, not gain tuning).** "Adaptive A*/NSGA-II framework for multi-goal navigation of mobile robots" optimises path length, wheel effort, curvature, obstacle clearance and collision risk over B-spline paths (snippet only). — [IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6501/ae9209)
- **Weights-as-input alternative to a Pareto population (one network, dial as input).** Mason & Hauert, "Evolving Multi-Objective Neural Network Controllers for Robot Swarms", arXiv:2307.14237 (ARMS workshop at AAMAS 2023): a single network with "6 input nodes (4 sensor inputs and 2 objective weights), 1 hidden layer with 5 nodes, and an outputs layer with 2 nodes" is evolved with Natural Evolution Strategies "over 20,000 evaluations"; objectives are minimise distance to the arena origin vs maximise velocity; the "Pareto front" (their Fig. 5) is traced at deployment by sweeping the weight inputs, and "No retraining is required for different objectives". The authors explicitly distinguish this from "utilizing a MO-EA, e.g. NSGA-II, to evolve multiple sets of network parameters for different objective weightings". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14237)
- **Scale of MOEA-over-weights with GPU tensorisation.** Liang, Li, Yu, Sun, Cheng, "Bridging Evolutionary Multiobjective Optimization and GPU Acceleration via Tensorization", arXiv:2503.20286 (accepted IEEE TEVC): tensorised NSGA-III, MOEA/D and HypE, "up to 1113x" speed-up over CPU, "scaling population sizes to hundreds of thousands", and "a multiobjective robot control benchmark using a GPU-accelerated physics engine"; the GECCO 2024 precursor applied tensorised RVEA "to multiobjective neuroevolution for robotic control tasks". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20286); [GECCO 2024 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2024/toc.html)
- **Survey view of what MORL has used.** Bai, Cheng, Jin, "Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning: A Survey", Intelligent Computing (2023): "EC has a wide range of MOEAs such as Pareto-based, decomposition-based, and indicator-based methods, but MORL has mainly used NSGA-II and the HV metric." — [arXiv 2303.04150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04150)

### Inferences
- Across the fetched papers, the practical recipe is uniform: NSGA-II, population 30–60, a few hundred to a few thousand evaluations when each evaluation is a simulated episode, and HV with a fixed reference point as the headline metric. A 4–6 knob wrapper is at the low end of the knob counts seen (2 per joint in the cobot paper; 163–2053 for weight evolution), which favours small populations and few generations.
- "Front used at deployment" is treated informally: NeuroAction picks a knee point or scenario-specific point; the swarm paper adds an explicit multi-criteria decision step; Mason & Hauert make the preference an input. None of the fetched robotics papers call it a "dial"; the term in the MORL literature is "decision support"/"a posteriori preference" (see question 3).
- Process-control examples were not found in this session (see Gaps), so claims about MPC tuning should not be made from these notes.

### Gaps
- No fetched example tunes a *supervisor/wrapper* (horizon, retry threshold, retreat) around a controller with NSGA-II; all examples tune gains, trajectories/paths, reward parameters, or network weights.
- Process-control (MPC) multi-objective tuning papers and UAV gain-tuning papers with explicit crash objectives could not be retrieved because the search budget ran out; the AEB and swarm papers could not be opened (403).
- MO-CMA-ES (Igel et al.) and SMS-EMOA were not found applied to controller tuning with a safety objective; MO-CMA-ES appears only as a recommendation (Moshaiov) and a baseline for MO-PI²-CMA (snippet only, [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10015-025-01027-z)).

## Key question 2 — Multi-objective evolution of a wrapper around a frozen/learned policy (as opposed to evolving the policy)

### Takeaway
No paper was found in which a frozen neural (or VLA) policy is wrapped and only the wrapper is evolved multi-objectively; the nearest neighbours are (i) residual/steering layers around frozen BC/VLA policies trained by RL or applied at inference, (ii) evolution of reward parameters or network weights *outside* an RL learner, and (iii) ES fine-tuning of billion-parameter LLMs, which shows the black-box route scales but is single-objective.

### Cited Findings
- **Residual RL around a frozen chunked BC policy.** Ankile, Simeonov, Shenfeld, Torne, Agrawal, "From Imitation to Refinement – Residual RL for Precise Assembly", arXiv:2407.16677 (2024): "ResiP augments a frozen, chunked BC model with a fully closed-loop residual policy trained with reinforcement learning (RL)", motivated by distribution shift and the open-loop nature of action chunks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16677)
- **Model-agnostic residual around large policy models.** Yuan, Mu, Tao, Fang, Zhang, Su, "Policy Decorator: Model-Agnostic Online Refinement for Large Policy Model", arXiv:2412.13630 (2024): residual policy plus "controlled exploration strategies" refines Behavior Transformer and Diffusion Policy on eight ManiSkill/Adroit tasks; it "preserves the smooth motion of imitation learning models" while avoiding "the erratic behaviors of pure RL policies". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13630)
- **Residual RL around frozen generalist VLAs (2026).** OGPO notes that residual RL methods "improve pretrained VLA policies by learning lightweight corrective policies around frozen generalist models" (snippet only). A 2026 method keeps the base policy frozen and trains an online residual on a bottleneck latent with SAC (snippet only). — [OGPO arXiv 2610.12914](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12914); [arXiv 2605.19919](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19919)
- **Training-free, gradient-free steering of frozen diffusion/flow policies at inference.** VLS, arXiv:2602.03973 (2026), "treats adaptation as an inference-time control problem" by steering the sampler of a frozen policy with a VLM (snippet only). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03973)
- **Execution-horizon / action-chunk knob exists and is studied at test time.** Liu, Hamid, Xie, Lee, Du, Finn, "Bidirectional Decoding: Improving Action Chunking via Guided Test-Time Sampling", arXiv:2408.17355 (2024): action chunking "allows the learner to better capture the temporal dependencies in demonstrations" but "at the cost of reduced reactivity to unexpected states"; BID is "a test-time inference algorithm" that samples candidate chunks and selects by backward coherence and forward contrast, evaluated on seven simulation benchmarks and two real tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- **Black-box ES does scale to frozen-weight-sized models (but single-objective).** Qiu, Gan, Hayes, Liang, Xu, Dailey, Meyerson, Hodjat, Miikkulainen, "Evolution Strategies at Scale: LLM Fine-Tuning Beyond Reinforcement Learning", arXiv:2509.24372, ICML 2026: simplified NES with Gaussian perturbations, population N = 30, σ = 0.001, on Qwen-2.5 0.5B–7B and Llama 1B–8B; on Countdown, Qwen-2.5-3B goes from 10.0% (original) to 60.5% with ES vs 43.8% for the best RL variant; "ES received no KL feedback and showed no hacking" while GRPO with small KL coefficients hacked the conciseness reward; ES reached a better reward-vs-KL Pareto front than GRPO. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372v3)
- **Evolution applied to a learned controller for safety (SBSE flavour).** Lyu, Zhang, Arcaini, Ishikawa, Laurent, Zhao, "Search-Based Repair of DNN Controllers of AI-Enabled Cyber-Physical Systems Guided by System-Level Specifications", GECCO 2024 (Search-Based Software Engineering): searches alternative weights for faulty DNN controllers "guided by system-level specifications instead of ground-truth labels" so failing tests pass without breaking passing ones. — [GECCO 2024 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2024/toc.html)
- **Evolution outside the RL learner (reward parameters).** An et al., GECCO 2023 (see question 1): MOEAs produce non-dominated reward-function parameter sets for an RL UAV navigator. — [GECCO 2023 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html)
- **Hierarchy over learned/evolved skills, but the top level is RL not evolution.** Chalumeau et al., "Neuroevolution is a Competitive Alternative to Reinforcement Learning for Skill Discovery", ICLR 2023 (spotlight), arXiv:2210.03516: for hierarchical planning on halfcheetah-hurdles "a PPO agent, trained for 200 million policy interactions (20 million meta-controller actions)... picks a skill, which then runs for the next ten consecutive timesteps"; QD repertoires have 1024 cells; meta-controllers over PGA-MAP-Elites and DIAYN+reward primitives "consistently jump over multiple consecutive hurdles". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.03516v4)
- **Neuroevolution vs RL transfer (GECCO 2025, Benchmarking track).** Nisioti, Pedersen, Plantec, Montero, Risi, "When Does Neuroevolution Outcompete Reinforcement Learning in Transfer Learning Tasks?", arXiv:2505.22696: NE "frequently outperforms RL baselines" on stepping-gates and Ecorobot (Brax with obstacles and switchable morphologies) curricula; "scaling NE to complex, real-world problems remains a challenge". — [arXiv API record](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22neuroevolution%20outcompete%22&max_results=5)
- **Pareto-based selection in a few-episode policy search.** Kaushik, Chatzilygeroudis, Mouret, "Multi-objective Model-based Policy Search for Data-efficient Learning with Sparse Rewards" (Multi-DEX), CoRL 2018, PMLR 87:839–855: three objectives (novel state trajectories, cumulative reward, keeping to regions where the model is accurate) "optimized with a Pareto-based multi-objective optimization algorithm"; solves sparse-reward tasks on a simulated arm "in a few episodes", with lower interaction time than VIME, TRPO, GEP-PG, CMA-ES and Black-DROPS. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v87/kaushik18a.html)
- **LLM-agent analogue of "preserve the Pareto archive, apply the preference at deployment".** Jiang, Cai, Luo, Tang, "MOAE: Multi-Objective Agent Evolution with Pareto-Preserving Search", arXiv:2609.05992 (Sep 2026): objectives are task performance, trajectory quality and safety; it "maintains an empirical archive of non-dominated candidates" and "applies constraint-aware selection only at deployment, separating candidate preservation from the final preference"; LLM agents (TravelPlanner, AgentDojo), no parameter updates. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05992)

### Inferences
- The "wrapper around a frozen policy" idea is established in the RL community (ResiP, Policy Decorator, OGPO-cited residual methods) and in inference-time steering (VLS, BID), but all are single-objective and gradient- or sampler-based. Evolving a small wrapper multi-objectively would be novel in the combination, not in any component.
- The knobs the project proposes (execution horizon, post-contact scaling, retry) are exactly the variables these RL/inference-time papers hold fixed or hand-tune (BID studies chunk reactivity; ResiP addresses open-loop chunks), which supports framing them as an optimisation target.

### Gaps
- Where I looked: web searches on "evolutionary/evolved wrapper frozen pretrained policy VLA residual", "NSGA-II + frozen policy", GECCO 2023–2026 TOCs and accepted lists, the residual-RL-on-VLA papers surfaced by OGPO, and arXiv title lookups. None returned an NSGA-II (or any MOEA) over a wrapper around a frozen VLA or diffusion policy. Explicit statement: no such example was found.
- No example was found of a meta-controller over a frozen policy that is *evolved* (Chalumeau's is PPO-trained).

## Key question 3 — Evolutionary safe RL: constrained EAs, lexicase with constraints, "safe evolution", multi-objective RL via evolution (PGMORL, MO-CMA-ES), Pareto-optimal safe policies; what the supervisor's framing maps onto

### Takeaway
The supervisor's framing ("black-box optimisation balancing reward against probability of violation without hand-tuned penalties") maps onto three established strands: (a) multi-policy MORL with a posteriori preference selection (Hayes et al.'s "decision support" scenario; PGMORL-style evolutionary MORL), (b) chance/probabilistic constraints handled by sampling or Bayesian confidence inside an ES (SNES; chance-constrained EMO), and (c) safe optimisation where unsafe evaluations themselves are avoided (Safe CMA-ES). Lexicase with explicit constraints has no established robotics result.

### Cited Findings
- **Probability-of-violation as a constraint inside an ES, no hand-tuned penalty.** Belzner & Wirsing, "Synthesizing Safe Policies under Probabilistic Constraints with Reinforcement Learning and Bayesian Model Checking", arXiv:2005.03898 (2020/21; also Science of Computer Programming). SNES maximises E(R) subject to P(cost = 0) ≥ p_req with confidence c_req; each iteration counts satisfying (s) and violating (v) episodes, models satisfaction probability as Beta(s+1, v+1), confidence c_sat = 1 − Beta(s+1,v+1).cdf(p_req), and sets the Lagrangian weight λ = max(0, c_sat − c_req)/(1 − c_req) (return weighted αλ, cost α(1−λ)). Domains: Particle Dance (continuous; p_req/c_req 0.85/0.98; net 8/32/2; 60,000 episodes) and Obstacle Run (discrete; 0.9/0.98; net 4/32/5; 20,000 episodes); episode length 50, population N = 20, α = 0.01, σ = 0.1; verification every 1000 episodes for up to 1000 episodes; 5 repeats. "Return and constraint define a Pareto front in our domain"; a maximum-likelihood variant "over-satisfies the constraint and obtains lower returns than SNES". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03898); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03898v2)
- **Safe optimisation (avoid unsafe evaluations during search).** Uchida, Hamano, Nomura, Saito, Shirakawa, "CMA-ES for Safe Optimization", arXiv:2405.10534, "accepted as a full paper at GECCO2024": "Safe optimization requires performing efficient optimization without evaluating unsafe solutions"; the method estimates "the Lipschitz constants of safety functions transformed with the distribution parameters" and projects samples onto the estimated safe region; on benchmark functions it "successfully performs optimization, suppressing the unsafe evaluations" where existing methods "struggle". A binary-space follow-up, "Adaptive Stochastic Natural Gradient Method for Safe Optimization on Binary Space" (Uchida, Hamano, Nomura, Shirakawa), is accepted at GECCO 2026 (ECOM). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10534); [GECCO 2026 accepted papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)
- **Chance constraints handled by sampling inside Pareto optimisation (GECCO EMO/ECOM).** Yan, A. Neumann, F. Neumann, "Sampling-based Pareto Optimization for Chance-constrained Monotone Submodular Problems", GECCO 2024: directly samples chance-constraint satisfaction in ASW-GSEMO and outperforms surrogate-based variants; related GECCO 2024 papers use 2- and 3-objective MOEA/D and 3-objective EAs for chance-constrained knapsack (Pathiranage, Neumann, Antipov, Neumann). — [GECCO 2024 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2024/toc.html)
- **Constrained multi-objective EAs as a mature sub-field.** GECCO 2024 EMO includes "Analysis of Real-World Constrained Multi-Objective Problems and Performance Comparison of Multi-Objective Algorithms" (Nan, Ishibuchi, Shu, Shang) and "Extending Pareto Dominance for Multi-Constraints Satisfaction..." (Yu, Chen, Zhou; 23 CMOPs); GECCO 2025 EMO/EML includes "Constrained Multi-objective Optimization with Search Direction Learning", "Addressing Heterogeneous Evaluation Times in Constrained Multi-Objective Optimization Using a Mixed-Fidelity Evaluation Technique" (Santoshkumar, Deb) and "Machine Learning-Assisted Constraint Handling Under Variable Uncertainty for Preference-based Multi-Objective Optimization" (Yadav, Ramu, Deb). — [GECCO 2024 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2024/toc.html); [GECCO 2025 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)
- **The canonical constraint handling without penalties is Deb's constrained-domination inside NSGA-II.** Deb, Pratap, Agarwal, Meyarivan, "A fast and elitist multiobjective genetic algorithm: NSGA-II", IEEE TEVC 6(2):182–197, 2002 (feasibility-first domination; no penalty coefficients). — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/996017)
- **Evolutionary MORL for continuous robot control (the PGMORL line).** Xu, Tian, Ma, Rus, Sueda, Matusik, "Prediction-Guided Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Robot Control", ICML 2020: a population of PPO-trained policies, warm-up then evolutionary stage with a prediction model selecting (policy, weight) tasks, then Pareto analysis/interpolation; seven environments — HalfCheetah-v2 (forward speed, energy efficiency), Hopper-v2 (speed, jumping height), Swimmer-v2, Ant-v2 (x-speed, y-speed), Walker2d-v2, Humanoid-v2 (speed, energy; S⊆R^376, A⊆R^17), Hopper-v3 (three objectives: speed, jumping height, energy); baselines RA, PFA, MOEA/D, RANDOM, META, all "with same amount of simulation steps"; metrics hypervolume and sparsity, "6 runs"; e.g. HalfCheetah-v2 HV 5.77×10^6 (ours) vs 5.66 (RA), 5.75 (PFA), 5.61 (MOEA/D), 5.69 (RANDOM), with sparsity 0.44×10^3 vs 15.87/3.81/16.96/1.09. — [Project page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jiex/papers/PGMORL/index.html); [paper PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jiex/papers/PGMORL/paper.pdf)
- **GECCO's own evolutionary-MORL for robots.** Tran, Doan, Luong, Binh, "A Two-Stage Multi-Objective Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning Framework for Continuous Robot Control" (MOPDERL), GECCO 2023 (EML): warm-up stage optimises policies separately per objective, then a multi-objective evolution stage shares that knowledge; "six modified MuJoCo locomotion tasks against PGMORL", better fronts "in five of six environments". — [GECCO 2023 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html)
- **csPGMORL and PA2D-MORL follow-ups (snippets only).** A 2025 journal paper proposes csPGMORL (corner-solution search, five robot locomotion problems); a 2026 overview of PA2D-MORL says PGMORL "can suffer from performance instability and computational cost due to predictor uncertainty". — [KNU listing](https://research.knu.ac.kr/en/publications/prediction-guided-multi-objective-reinforcement-learning-with-cor/); [alphaXiv 2603.19579](https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/overview/2603.19579)
- **MO-CMA-ES in MORL.** A 2020 Applied Intelligence paper combines a gradient method with an evolution strategy for MORL, using MO-CMA-ES to fine-tune "policy-independent parameters" so the front is estimated "densely and uniformly" on Deep Sea Treasure, Adaptive Streaming and Super Mario Bros (snippet only). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10489-020-01702-7)
- **Reference implementations.** MORL-Baselines ("A Toolkit for Reliable Benchmarking and Research in Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning", NeurIPS 2023) implements PGMORL alongside GPI-LS, MORL/D, Envelope Q-Learning, CAPQL, PCN, OLS, IPRO, etc., all following the MO-Gymnasium API. — [GitHub](https://github.com/LucasAlegre/morl-baselines)
- **What the "balance reward vs violation without penalties" framing is called in MORL.** Hayes et al., "A Practical Guide to Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning and Planning", AAMAS journal 36:26 (2022), arXiv:2103.09568: the utility-based approach is to "derive the optimal solution set from the available knowledge about the utility function of the user"; in the unknown-utility scenario "a priori scalarisation is undesirable as the utility function is unknown" so a coverage set is computed; in the decision-support scenario "a set of policies is presented to a user who selects a policy based on their preferences"; the Pareto front is "the set of non-dominated policies"; a "review and adjust" scenario lets the user switch policies when preferences change. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.09568v1)
- **Lexicographic safety-first as the alternative to Pareto.** Skalse, Hammond, Griffin, Abate, "Lexicographic Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning", IJCAI 2022 pp. 3430–3436, arXiv:2212.13769: rewards ranked by priority; "imposing safety constraints on an agent's behaviour" is the motivating application; new action-value and policy-gradient algorithms with proven convergence to lexicographically optimal policies. — [arXiv API record](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Lexicographic%20Multi-Objective%20Reinforcement%20Learning%22&max_results=5)
- **Lexicase: theory on conflicting objectives, no constraint handling.** Shahbandegan & Dolson, "On the robustness of lexicase selection to contradictory objectives", GECCO 2024: lexicase and epsilon-lexicase "each fail in one region of parameter space and perform well elsewhere" under maximally conflicting objectives, with guidelines for parameter choice. — [GP bibliography](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/shahbandegan_2024_GECCO.html); [GECCO 2024 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2024/toc.html)
- **Lexicase in an evolutionary-robotics task (snippet only; repository returned 403).** "The Limits of Lexicase Selection in an Evolutionary Robotics Task" (Keele eprint 6710): quadruped wall climbing with walls of varying height; lexicase "remained effective even when the researchers limited which environments it sampled". — [Keele eprints](https://eprints.keele.ac.uk/6710/)
- **"Safe mutation" in the EvoRL survey means operator safety, not physical safety.** Bai et al. 2023: "Lehman et al. propose a family of safe mutations" (output-gradient-scaled mutation) and "safe mutation for safe exploration, which uses visited unsafe states to explore safer actions" (as paraphrased in the survey); "NSGA-II or MCMA (a multi-objective variant of CMA-ES)" was applied in a cited MORL work. — [arXiv 2303.04150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04150)
- **Expectation-based safe RL misses tail events (gradient-based, for contrast).** Extreme Value Policy Optimization (ICML 2025) argues expectation-based constraints "overlook rare but high-impact extreme value events" and achieves lower violation probability (snippet only). — [papernotes](https://en.papernotes.org/ICML2025/reinforcement_learning/extreme_value_policy_optimization_for_safe_reinforcement_learning/)

### Inferences
- The supervisor's phrase corresponds to "multi-policy MORL with a posteriori (decision-support) preference articulation" in Hayes et al.'s taxonomy, with the probability of violation treated either as a second objective (NeuroAction, PGMORL-style) or as a chance constraint with Beta-posterior confidence (SNES). Reviewers from the EMO community will expect the first framing; reviewers from safe-RL will ask why not the second.
- SNES is the closest "least-machinery" precedent for an ES with a probabilistic safety constraint: population 20, a 32-unit hidden layer, and a Beta posterior — no penalty coefficient to tune.
- "Lexicase with constraints" is not an established tool in this literature; using it would need its own justification and baselines.

### Gaps
- No evolutionary paper was found that reports "Pareto-optimal safe policies" for manipulation; the MORL robot benchmarks are all locomotion (MuJoCo/Brax).
- Igel et al.'s MO-CMA-ES original paper and Lehman et al.'s safe-mutation paper were not fetched (budget); claims about them are via the Bai survey and Moshaiov.
- The Keele lexicase paper's authors, year and numbers could not be read (403).

## Key question 4 — Quality diversity in robotics: MAP-Elites/CVT descendants, QD for grasping and manipulation (2020–2026), QD under noisy fitness, QD for robustness/safety, descriptor choices for manipulation

### Takeaway
The QD canon for robots is MAP-Elites repertoires built in simulation (Cully 2015: 36 controller parameters, 6-D descriptor at 5 bins, ~13,000 gaits, 40M evaluations) and then used for fast adaptation; for grasping the 2023–2026 Doncieux-group line shows a *plain* MAP-Elites variant that preferentially re-selects successful parents (ME-scs) beats 14 other QD methods at 100k–400k evaluations, with the end-effector position at first contact as the descriptor; for noisy fitness the Flageat–Cully "uncertain QD" line gives the evaluation protocol (corrected archive by 512 re-evaluations, reproducibility score) and shows that naive re-sampling is dominated by Deep-Grid/archive-sampling at equal budget.

### Cited Findings
- **The canonical repertoire paper.** Cully, Clune, Tarapore, Mouret, "Robots that can adapt like animals", Nature 521:503–507 (2015), arXiv:1407.3501. Hexapod controller "parametrized by 36 real-valued parameters" (6 per leg); "the behavior space is six-dimensional: the portion of time that each leg is in contact with the ground", "discretized at five values for each dimension"; "the created behavior-performance map contains approximately 13,000 different gaits"; "creating one map involved 40 million iterations... which lasted roughly two weeks on one multi-core computer"; adaptation via map-based Bayesian optimisation lets the robot "adapt to damage in less than two minutes" (e.g. four damage scenarios: 66 [24;134] s, 8 [3;16] trials); it also tests a robotic arm with joints broken in 14 ways. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501)
- **Scaling the archive.** Vassiliades, Chatzilygeroudis, Mouret, "Using Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations to Scale Up the Multi-dimensional Archive of Phenotypic Elites Algorithm", arXiv:1610.05729 (IEEE TEVC 2018): "a constant, pre-defined number of regions irrespective of the dimensionality of the feature space"; compared against MAP-Elites on maze navigation and hexapod locomotion. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05729)
- **Repertoire for recovery without resets.** Chatzilygeroudis, Vassiliades, Mouret, "Reset-free Trial-and-Error Learning for Robot Damage Recovery", Robotics and Autonomous Systems (2018), arXiv:1610.04213: pre-generates "hundreds of possible behaviors" with a simulator of the intact robot; simulated wheeled and six-legged robots and a real six-legged robot damaged in several ways recover "most of their locomotion abilities" without human intervention. — [arXiv API record](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Reset-free%20Trial-and-Error%22&max_results=5)

- **Stability-aware QD for robot controllers (GECCO 2026).** Pex, Marzougui, wyffels, "Stability-Driven CPG Design for More Effective Quality-Diversity in Evolutionary Robotics", GECCO 2026 (CS), title only. — [GECCO 2026 accepted papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)

### Inferences
- Manipulation descriptors in the published QD-grasping work are geometric and robot-observable (end-effector position at first contact, 3-D); multi-descriptor variants (MES multi-BD) are the 2026 extension. No published manipulation QD paper uses "strategy" descriptors like retry count or approach height, so that choice would be new but is in the same spirit.
- With 1,000–2,000 episodes per experiment, the 100k–1M evaluation budgets of these papers are two to three orders of magnitude out of reach; the Cully 2015 repertoire was built once in simulation with 40M evaluations and then exploited with 3–16 physical trials, which is the only QD pattern compatible with such a small budget (archive built offline, selection online).
- For noisy episode outcomes, the community expectation (Flageat & Cully) is a corrected archive via re-evaluation and a reproducibility statistic, not just the raw archive.

### Gaps
- Real-robot transfer numbers for Huber et al. were not in the fetched text; the ECJ page was not opened.
- No QD paper was found that uses safety violations as the *objective* and strategy as the descriptor; QD-for-robustness appears as damage recovery (Cully, RTE), sensor damage (Nadizar) and reproducibility (Grillotti), not as constraint satisfaction.

## Key question 5 — Neuroevolution of small gating/supervisory controllers: NEAT, CMA-ES on tiny MLPs, evolved gating/switching, hierarchical controllers where evolution tunes the top level; parameter counts and episode budgets

### Takeaway
Tiny evolved controllers are well documented: 867 parameters (World Models, CMA-ES, population 64 × 16 rollouts), 3,667 (Tang et al. GECCO 2020, CMA-ES population 256 × 16 rollouts), 32-hidden-unit nets with population 20 (SNES, 20k–60k episodes), a 6-5-2 network in 20,000 evaluations (Mason & Hauert), 163 weights with NSGA-II population 56 × 300 generations (Moshaiov), and 2,053 weights with population 50 × 1,000 evaluations (NeuroAction). No modern paper was found that evolves a gating/switching network *on top of a large pretrained policy*; the hierarchical examples either evolve both levels or train the top level with RL.

### Cited Findings
- **CMA-ES on an 867-parameter controller.** Ha & Schmidhuber, "World Models" (NeurIPS 2018): controller C has 867 parameters (Car Racing) / 1,088 (Doom), "trained with CMA-ES", "population size: 64 agents per generation", each agent evaluated over 16 rollouts with fitness the mean; V has 4,348,547 and M 422,368 parameters (Car Racing); CarRacing-v0 score 906 ± 21 over 100 trials; 900.46 over 1024 rollouts after 1800 generations. — [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- **CMA-ES on a 3,667-parameter attention agent (GECCO 2020).** Tang, Nguyen, Ha, "Neuroevolution of Self-Interpretable Agents", GECCO 2020, arXiv:2003.08165: 3,667 learnable parameters (query FC 592, key FC 592, LSTM-16 2,483) vs World Model/GA 4,733,485 and PPO 445,955; CMA-ES via pycma, "population size: 256", initial σ 0.1, 16 rollouts per fitness evaluation for CarRacing and 5 for DoomTakeCover; agents "generalize to environments where task irrelevant elements are modified while conventional methods fail". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.08165v2)
- **ES as a scalable alternative (reference point for budgets).** Salimans, Ho, Chen, Sidor, Sutskever, "Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning", arXiv:1703.03864 (2017): "solve 3D humanoid walking in 10 minutes", "competitive results on most Atari games after one hour of training", "invariant to action frequency and delayed rewards", "tolerant of extremely long horizons", scales "to over a thousand parallel workers". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864)
- **Small ES with a safety constraint.** SNES (Belzner & Wirsing): networks 8/32/2 and 4/32/5, population 20, 60,000 and 20,000 episodes (see question 3). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03898v2)
- **Preference-as-input network with NES.** Mason & Hauert 2023: 6-5-2 network, NES, 20,000 evaluations in a low-fidelity Python simulator, transferred to Webots and to larger swarms "without further retraining" (see question 1). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14237)

- **NSGA-II over 2,053 weights with 1,000 evaluations.** NeuroAction 2026 (see question 1). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1)
- **Linear GP teams for continuous control (GECCO 2025, GP).** Vacher et al., "MAPLE: Multi-Action Programs through Linear Evolution for Continuous Multi-Action RL": one linear GP program per action on MuJoCo, "beats state-of-the-art GP and a small deep RL model, and matches a larger deep RL network in low-dimensional environments with lower complexity". — [GECCO 2025 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)
- **Interpretable evolved visual control (GECCO 2025, EML).** De La Torre, Nadizar, Lavinas, Luga, Wilson, Cussat-Blanc, "Evolution of Inherently Interpretable Visual Control Policies": graph-based GP policies "motivated by reliability in safety-critical settings". — [GECCO 2025 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)

### Inferences
- Episode budgets for the tiny-controller successes are large by the project's standard: World Models 64 × 16 = 1,024 rollouts per generation; Tang 256 × 16 = 4,096 per generation. Only NeuroAction (1,000 evaluations × 2 episodes) and SNES (20k–60k episodes) are in the low thousands, and NeuroAction got there by starting from an SB3-initialised network and evolving only a 2×32 head.
- A "tiny neuroevolved network from robot-observable features" with 4–6 outputs would have on the order of 10²–10³ parameters, in line with World Models' 867 and SNES's 8-32-2, so CMA-ES/NES with population 20–64 is the documented tool; NEAT is not used in any fetched 2023–2026 control paper except as TensorNEAT.

### Gaps
- Evolved arbitrator/gating over a repertoire: the EvoRBC line (Duarte, Gomes, Oliveira, Christensen, "Evolution of Repertoire-Based Control for Robots With Complex Locomotor Systems", IEEE TEVC 2018, DOI 10.1109/TEVC.2017.2722101) is believed to evolve a high-level neural controller over a MAP-Elites repertoire, but neither the IEEE page nor arXiv returned content, so it is unverified here.
- No paper was found evolving a gating/supervisory network on top of a pretrained *large* policy (VLA, diffusion policy); the search for "evolved gating network switching between controllers" returned only gradient-trained mixtures and neuroscience models.

## Key question 6 — GECCO 2023–2026 (and PPSN, EvoStar, ALIFE, ECJ, TELO) papers touching evolutionary robotics with safety, multi-objective controller tuning, QD for manipulation, or neuroevolution around learned policies: track, claim, budget, baselines

### Takeaway
GECCO's robotics-adjacent output is concentrated in Complex Systems (QD, uncertain QD, MOQD), Neuroevolution (ES/QD scaling, NEAT on GPU), EML (evolutionary MORL, coevolution), RWA/SBSE (NSGA-II for safety testing and controller repair) and EMO (constrained/chance-constrained MOEAs); safety appears as a *testing* or *constraint-handling* theme, never as a manipulation-controller tuning paper. ## Key question 7 — Baselines these communities treat as mandatory for a multi-objective or QD claim, and expected hypervolume / IGD / coverage reporting

### Takeaway
Fetched papers converge on: random search or random selection as a floor; the single-objective counterpart (MAP-Elites on summed objectives, or a scalarised RL policy gradient) as the "do you need MO at all" control; NSGA-II and SPEA2 (plus MOEA/D for MORL) as MOEA references; equal evaluation budgets; 6–20 seeds; hypervolume with a stated reference point as the headline, plus sparsity/spacing or coverage, median + IQR and Wilcoxon tests; QD adds QD-score, coverage (filled cells), and in noisy domains a corrected archive built by re-evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- **MOQD papers' baseline set and statistics.** MOME (GECCO 2022): NSGA-II, SPEA2, and MAP-Elites with fitness = sum of objectives, all scored through a passive archive; 20 runs on Brax; Wilcoxon signed-rank with p-values reported; metrics MOQD score, global hypervolume, coverage, max sum. MOME-P2C: mome-pgx, mome, pga-me, nsga-ii, spea2 (plus "small" variants), 20 seeds, metrics including sparsity, equal 1,024,000-evaluation budgets; it reports failing to run pg-morl rather than omitting it silently. — [MOME HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03057v2); [MOME-P2C HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12433v2)
- **Evolutionary MORL baselines.** PGMORL: RA, PFA, MOEA/D, RANDOM, META "with same amount of simulation steps"; "6 runs"; hypervolume and sparsity ("a desired Pareto set approximation is expected to have high hypervolume metric and low sparsity metric"). MOPDERL (GECCO 2023) uses PGMORL as the baseline. — [PGMORL PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jiex/papers/PGMORL/paper.pdf); [GECCO 2023 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2023/toc.html)
- **Applied MOEA-over-policy paper (2026).** NeuroAction: five MOEAs compared, a scalarised policy-gradient baseline (MORL-PG), hypervolume with reference point [−5,−5,−5], "median HV and interquartile range across runs", Wilcoxon rank-sum at 0.05, 6 runs. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1)
- **QD-grasping baselines.** Huber et al.: Random sampling is included among the 15 methods and used to express sample-efficiency gains ("more than 60 times"); 10 seeds; coverage of the success archive is the primary metric. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.05483v2)
- **Noisy-domain reporting.** Flageat & Cully: Corrected Archive with M = 512 re-evaluations (median), Corrected QD-Score, QD-Score loss, Reproducibility-Score, comparisons at equal per-generation sampling size, 10 replications. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00463v2)
- **SBSE/RWA baseline.** WPgen (GECCO 2025): "Random Search as the baseline". — [GECCO 2025 TOC](https://www.sigevo.org/gecco-2025/toc.html)
- **Controller-tuning paper's HV usage.** Navarro-Cabrera et al.: hypervolume with reference point = maximum values across tested controllers; 5 repeats; population-size sweep reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08988)
- **Community benchmarking guidance.** Bartz-Beielstein et al., "Benchmarking in Optimization: Best Practice and Open Issues", arXiv:2007.03488 (2020): lists "one-shot optimization algorithms (e.g., pure random search, Latin Hypercube Design...)" and "greedy local search algorithms (e.g., randomized local search, BFGS..., Nelder-Mead)" among algorithms to include; gives a pipeline for choosing statistical tests — "the t test is the appropriate parametric one, while its relevant non-parametric version is the Wilcoxon signed rank test" for paired data, Mann-Whitney U (Wilcoxon rank-sum) for unpaired, with post-hoc procedures "Bonferroni, Holm, Hochberg, Hommel, Holland, Rom, Finner, and Li" for multiple algorithms; recommends design of experiments to decide "which and how many algorithm runs should be performed". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03488)
- **Indicator definitions and the reference-point requirement.** pymoo provides GD, GD+, IGD, IGD+ and Hypervolume; "For Hypervolume only a reference point needs to be provided" (the others need a reference front); "IGD+ is weakly Pareto compliant whereas the original IGD is not". — [pymoo indicators](https://pymoo.org/misc/indicators.html)
- **QD metrics.** Chatzilygeroudis et al.: performance plus "the coverage of the behavior space"; "number of filled bins, or the QD score". — [arXiv 2012.04322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04322)
- **What MORL has historically reported.** Bai et al. 2023: "MORL has mainly used NSGA-II and the HV metric", with spread and cardinality indicators also used. — [arXiv 2303.04150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04150)

### Inferences
- For a 2-objective front with a handful of knobs, the minimal credible comparison set implied by these papers is: random search at equal episode budget; a grid/sweep (the AEB paper's "sweeping" baseline, snippet only) or hand-tuned default; a scalarised single-objective run (CMA-ES or (1+λ)-ES on a weighted sum, swept over a few weights); and NSGA-II itself. Reporting HV with a fixed, stated reference point, median/IQR over ≥5–10 seeds, and a non-parametric test is the norm; IGD needs a reference front and is therefore rarely used in robot-control papers (none of the fetched ones used it).
- Because evaluations are noisy episodes, reviewers familiar with uncertain QD will expect re-evaluation of the final front (a "corrected front") and a statement of how many episodes each point on the front was re-tested with.

- No GECCO track CFP or reviewer-guideline text was fetched; the "mandatory" list is inferred from what accepted papers did, not from stated policy.
- Ishibuchi et al.'s guidance on choosing the hypervolume reference point (ECJ 2018) was not fetched.

## Key question 8 — "Simplest things that worked": methods ranked by machinery needed versus demonstrated result

### Takeaway
The least machinery with a published, quantitative result is plain NSGA-II (population 30–50, ~1,000 evaluations) over a small parameter vector with HV reporting and knee-point selection; next is a (1+λ)/NES-style ES with a Beta-posterior safety confidence (SNES); plain MAP-Elites with success-biased parent selection is the winner in QD-for-grasping; everything with policy gradients, archives of Pareto fronts, or predictor models bought data efficiency only at 10^5–10^6 evaluations.

### Cited Findings (ranked, least machinery first)
1. **NSGA-II over a few parameters/weights, HV + knee point.** NeuroAction: population 50, 1,000 evaluations × 2 episodes, 6 runs, NSGA-II median HV 1.12×10^5 vs scalarised PG 1.91×10^3; deployment "directly from the learned Pareto set without retraining". Cobot PD tuning: NSGA-II, ~30 individuals best, 5 repeats. Moshaiov: NSGA-II, N = 56, 300 generations. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38269-1); [arXiv 2309.08988](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08988); [arXiv 2004.12188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12188)
2. **Preference-as-input network with NES (one run gives the whole dial).** Mason & Hauert: 6-5-2 network, 20,000 evaluations, no retraining across preferences, transfers to Webots and larger swarms. — [arXiv 2307.14237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14237)
3. **ES with a probabilistic safety constraint and no penalty coefficient.** SNES: population 20, one 32-unit hidden layer, Beta(s+1, v+1) confidence sets λ automatically; 20k–60k episodes; meets p_req 0.85–0.9 at c_req 0.98 while a max-likelihood variant "over-satisfies the constraint and obtains lower returns". — [arXiv 2005.03898](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03898v2)
4. **CMA-ES on a tiny controller.** World Models: 867 parameters, population 64, 16 rollouts per candidate, CarRacing 906 ± 21; Tang et al.: 3,667 parameters, population 256, 16 rollouts. — [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/); [arXiv 2003.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.08165v2)
5. **Plain MAP-Elites with success-biased selection (QD for grasping).** ME-scs beats 14 methods including CMA-ME/CMA-MAE/NSLC at 100k–400k evaluations; "a simple variant of MAP-Elites consistently dominates state-of-the-art QD methods". — [arXiv 2308.05483](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.05483v2)
6. **Deep-Grid for noisy fitness instead of re-sampling.** Flageat & Cully: Deep-Grid (depth 32) best on Arm, Hexapod, Walker "whatever the sampling-size", while MAP-Elites-sampling is dominated by plain MAP-Elites at sampling size 256. — [arXiv 2302.00463](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00463v2)
7. **Offline repertoire + online few-trial selection.** Cully 2015: 40M simulated evaluations once, then 3–16 physical trials; RTE: "hundreds" of pre-generated behaviours. — [arXiv 1407.3501](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501); [RTE](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Reset-free%20Trial-and-Error%22&max_results=5)
8. **MOME / MOME-PGX / MOME-P2C (Pareto front per cell).** Best MOQD scores but 1–1.024 million evaluations, 2×64–2×128 policies, and no significant global-HV gain over NSGA-II on Brax in the original MOME. — [arXiv 2202.03057](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03057v2); [arXiv 2411.12433](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12433v2)
9. **PGMORL and successors (population of PPO policies + predictor).** Better HV/sparsity than RA/PFA/MOEA/D at equal simulation steps, but the HalfCheetah HV margin is 5.77 vs 5.75 (PFA) ×10^6 and later work reports "performance instability and computational cost due to predictor uncertainty" (snippet only). — [PGMORL PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jiex/papers/PGMORL/paper.pdf); [alphaXiv 2603.19579](https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/overview/2603.19579)
10. **Residual RL around a frozen policy (not evolutionary).** ResiP, Policy Decorator and the 2026 VLA residual methods improve frozen BC/VLA policies but require online RL machinery (SAC/PPO, replay, exploration control). — [arXiv 2407.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16677); [arXiv 2412.13630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13630)

### Inferences
- Items 1–3 are the only entries whose demonstrated budgets (≈1,000–60,000 episodes) are within an order of magnitude of 1,000–2,000 simulator episodes, and all three are "a population of 20–50, a few dozen generations, two objectives or one objective plus a Beta-confidence constraint".
- Items 5–8 are the standard QD/MOQD references a Complex-Systems reviewer will expect to see cited and, if a MAP-Elites variant is proposed, compared against at equal budget.

- The ranking omits Bayesian optimisation and bandit-style selection (out of scope here) and omits EvoRBC and the swarm NSGA2-MCM decision-making step because their pages could not be fetched.
