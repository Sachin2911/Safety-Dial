# Execution of action chunks from learned manipulation policies: what the knobs do to success and safety (as of October 2026)

Scope: execution-level knobs on a frozen chunked policy (ACT, Diffusion Policy, pi0/pi0.5-class VLAs): chunk size, execution horizon (how many of the predicted actions are executed), temporal ensembling and cross-chunk smoothing, latency-aware chunking, speed/time scaling, compliance, stall/retry, and open-loop vs closed-loop re-querying. Simulated and real-robot results are labelled [SIM] and [REAL] throughout. "Safety" below means collisions, drops, tipping, force violations, protective stops, or specification violations; most papers measure only success.

Method note: the session's web-search budget ran out after the first three searches, so the remaining evidence came from direct fetches of arXiv HTML/PDF, GitHub source, and project pages for papers whose identifiers were already known or surfaced by those searches. Papers I could not reach are listed under Gaps rather than paraphrased from memory.

## Key question 1: Chunk size and execution horizon (ACT, Diffusion Policy, pi0/pi0.5, Bidirectional Decoding, 2025-2026 adaptive-horizon work)

### Takeaway
Chunk size is the single largest known execution-level lever on success (ACT: 1% to 44% going from k=1 to k=100), but the *executed fraction* of a chunk has a broad plateau in quasi-static simulation (typically 20-60% of the chunk, within a few points) and a steep cliff when the whole chunk is executed open-loop (pi0.5 on LIBERO: 94.9% at 10 of 50 executed vs 71.5% at 50 of 50). On real robots the sweep is much more consequential (0% to 81.5% between executing 5 and 10 of 50 on one task), and very short execution horizons are consistently bad on hardware because each re-query adds a pause or a mode jump. No paper in this set measured the executed-fraction knob against a safety metric.

### Cited Findings

**ACT (Zhao, Kumar, Levine, Finn; RSS 2023; arXiv 2304.13705)** [SIM for ablations; REAL for main tasks]
- Chunk-size sweep with temporal ensembling disabled, averaged over 4 settings (2 simulated tasks x scripted/human demos): success "improves drastically from 1% at k = 1 to 44% at k = 100, then slightly tapers down with higher k" (k up to 400); k=1 is "fully-closed-loop", k=episode length is "fully-open-loop" — [ACT paper, Sec. VI-A and Fig. 8(a)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- Temporal ensembling (TE) ablation, Fig. 8(b): the figure annotations read +3.3% for ACT, +4% for BC-ConvMLP, and -20% for VINN; caption: "Temporal Ensemble (TE) improves our method and BC-ConvMLP, while hurting VINN." Note the paper states the with/without-TE runs were "separately tuned" — [ACT paper, Fig. 8(b)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- TE mechanism: query the policy at every timestep and average overlapping predictions with weights w_i = exp(-m*i); original code uses m = 0.01 (lerobot comment) — [ACT paper, Alg. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705); [lerobot configuration_act.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/act/configuration_act.py)
- Default hyperparameters: chunk size 100 at 50 Hz (2 s of actions); real-world evaluation is one seed, 25 trials per task — [ACT paper, Table III and Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- Stated rationale: chunking reduces the effective horizon k-fold to mitigate compounding error and handles non-Markovian (paused) human demonstrations — [ACT paper, Sec. IV-A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- Independent reimplementation (one seed, lower confidence): on robomimic Can, TE vs chunked execution: 88% vs 80% at chunk 20, 64% vs 38% at chunk 50, tied 74% at chunk 10; on ALOHA human demos at chunk 100, 50% chunked vs 74% with TE — [YahuanShi/act-reimplementation](https://github.com/YahuanShi/act-reimplementation)
- A 2026 forensic re-run of the ACT CVAE ablation reports that temporal ensembling raises mean success without the encoder by 15.6 points — [arXiv 2609.16745](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16745)

**Diffusion Policy (Chi et al.; RSS 2023, IJRR 2024; arXiv 2303.04137)** [SIM ablations; REAL deployment details]
- Defaults: observation horizon To=2, action execution horizon Ta=8, prediction horizon Tp=16 (CNN) for most tasks; real Push-T uses Ta=6, Tp=16 — [DP paper, Tables 7-8](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- "found the action horizon of 8 steps to be optimal for most tasks that we tested"; "too long a horizon reduces performance due to slow reaction time"; a horizon >1 "helps the policy predict consistent actions and compensate for idle portions of the demonstration"; "single-step policies can easily overfit to this pausing behavior" — [DP paper, Secs. 4.3 and 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- Latency: "Diffusion Policy is able to maintain peak performance with latency up to 4 steps" (latency = steps between last observation and first executable action); velocity control is more latency-sensitive — [DP paper, Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- Position control "consistently outperforms" velocity control for DP — [DP paper, Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- Real UR5 Push-T execution: 10 Hz policy commands linearly interpolated to 125 Hz; end-effector velocity capped at 0.43 m/s and position kept at least 1 cm above the table (execution-level safety caps used but not ablated) — [DP paper, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- lerobot Diffusion defaults as of Oct 2026 main: horizon=64, n_action_steps=32, n_obs_steps=2 — [lerobot configuration_diffusion.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/diffusion/configuration_diffusion.py)

**pi0 / pi0.5 / openpi / lerobot actual execution settings** [REAL]
- pi0: H=50 action chunk; 50 Hz on most platforms, 20 Hz on UR5e and Franka; 10 Euler integration steps; actions executed open-loop "with no temporal ensembling or action aggregation" — the authors tried TE early, found it hurt, and dropped it; on 20 Hz robots inference runs every 0.8 s after 16 actions, on 50 Hz robots every 0.5 s after 25 actions; inference 73 ms on an RTX 4090 — [pi0 paper, arXiv 2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)
- pi0.5: base velocity targets at 50 Hz with action chunking; 10 denoising steps; targets tracked by PD controllers "without any additional trajectory planning or collision detection"; no execution ablation — [pi0.5 paper, arXiv 2504.16054](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054)
- openpi LIBERO evaluation: `replan_steps = 5` of the predicted chunk, executed open-loop then re-infer; images 256 rendered, resized to 224 — [openpi examples/libero/main.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/libero/main.py)
- openpi DROID evaluation: `open_loop_horizon = 8` of a 10-step chunk at 15 Hz, with the comment "8 is usually a good default (equals 0.5 seconds of action execution)"; gripper binarised at 0.5; actions clipped to [-1, 1] — [openpi examples/droid/main.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/droid/main.py)
- lerobot pi0.5: chunk_size=50, n_action_steps=50 (execute the whole chunk), 10 flow steps; RTC config present but off by default — [lerobot configuration_pi05.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/pi05/configuration_pi05.py)
- lerobot ACT: chunk_size=100, n_action_steps=100, temporal ensembling off by default and only allowed with n_action_steps=1 — [lerobot configuration_act.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/act/configuration_act.py)
- OpenVLA-OFT: LIBERO chunk K=8 executed in full before re-planning ("improves both speed and performance"); ALOHA K=25 executed in full; chunking + parallel decoding lifted LIBERO average from 76.5% to 90.2% and LIBERO-Long from 53.7% to 86.5% — [OpenVLA-OFT, arXiv 2502.19645](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19645)
- BEHAVIOR Challenge 2026 baselines: pi0.5 trained with a 32-step horizon and served with `--action_horizon 16 --control_mode receding_horizon`; GR00T N1.7 uses 16-step chunks with temporal ensembling on by default; no success rates or execution ablations published — [BEHAVIOR baselines page](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/baselines.html)
- Liang et al. (CVPR 2026) summarise field defaults: GR00T N1.5 chunk 16 on RoboCasa; pi0 16 or 25 depending on benchmark; SmolVLA 10 on LIBERO — [Liang et al., CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Liang_Adaptive_Action_Chunking_at_Inference-time_for_Vision-Language-Action_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)

**ManiGuard protocol (arXiv 2608.17386, Peng et al., Aug 2026)** [SIM; some REAL]
- Chunks: pi0.5 16-step, pi0 50-step, GR00T N1.6 16-step, SmolVLA 50-step; every policy executes the first 8 actions of each chunk then re-infers; joint-position control in 8-D; action/render rate 20 Hz (rendered as "2020 Hz" in the HTML), physics 120 Hz; 256x256 images; Jar horizon 2,000 steps; monitor scores every step but never aborts — [ManiGuard, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- No ablation of chunk length, executed actions, speed, or re-planning appears anywhere in the paper; no rationale is given for 16 vs 50 or for executing 8 — [ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Jar family, in-distribution (78 rollouts per cell): pi0.5-SFT safe-success 26.92%, 30 violations, engaged-violation rate 38.97%; pi0-SFT 14.10%, 31 violations, EVR 40.41%; GR00T N1.6-SFT 0.00%; SmolVLA-SFT 0.00%. OOD (312 rollouts): pi0.5-SFT SSR 6.41%, 137 violations, EVR 54.59% — [ManiGuard, Table 3 and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Jar specification: (on(jar, table) W closed(jar)) AND G intact(jar); intact = not dropped and upright, tilt threshold 30 deg; median time-to-violation for pi0.5-SFT on Jar is 14% (ID) / 18% (OOD) of the post-engagement window, i.e. violations come early after first contact — [ManiGuard, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)

**Bidirectional Decoding (Liu, Hamid, Xie, Lee, Du, Finn; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2408.17355)** [SIM + REAL]
- Theory: no universally optimal action horizon; longer horizons win in deterministic, long-idle settings, shorter in stochastic ones. 1-D toy: deterministic (delta=0) favours h=10; delta=0.4 favours h=1; delta=0.2 is non-monotone with h=5 best — [BID, Sec. 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- VQ-BeT on Push-T under action noise 0 / 1.0 / 1.5: vanilla open-loop 64.0 / 26.9 / 13.0; vanilla closed-loop 48.9 / 38.3 / 29.5; EMA closed-loop 52.6 / 35.7 / 18.4; BID closed-loop 54.4 / 45.3 / 31.7. Open-loop is best with no noise and collapses under noise; closed-loop is worse clean but degrades more gracefully — [BID, Tables 1 and 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- Closed-loop DP on 7 sim tasks: BID gives 32% relative improvement over vanilla closed-loop; EMA "exhibits performance drops in 2 tasks"; Warmstart degrades 3 of 7; BID+EMA 46% relative — [BID, Fig. 6-7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- EMA/temporal-ensemble decay rate: "the optimal choices differ significantly" across tasks (Fig. 13); averaging chunks that follow different latent strategies "may not yield a plausible strategy" — [BID, App. A.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- Extending the action horizon h on Push-T "consistently improves performance" while extending the context length hurts (Fig. 11) — [BID, App.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- [REAL] Dynamic cup picking (UR5, 20 episodes): BID achieves "2x higher success rate than all other baselines"; vanilla open/closed and EMA "degrade substantially under slow cup movements" (bars only, no table) — [BID, Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)

### Inferences
- For a 16-step chunk, executing 8 (ManiGuard's choice) sits inside the plateau measured for GR00T's 16-step chunk on LIBERO (e=4..16 within ~5 points); the large sim cliffs appear only when 50-step chunks are executed almost fully. So in simulation the executed-fraction knob alone is unlikely to move success by tens of points for a 16-step pi0.5, but the real-robot sweeps (A3, PAV, AutoHorizon) show 30-80 point swings, driven mainly by very short horizons with synchronous inference (pauses, oscillation) and very long ones (missed corrections).
- Every adaptive-horizon paper agrees qualitatively that the right horizon is short around contact (grasp, place, flip, sub-centimetre alignment) and long in free space; this is the closest the literature comes to supporting a contact-aware execution dial, but none of them measured drops, tipping, or collisions.
- Re-planning phase (where the chunk boundary falls) alone shifts success by up to 11 points on RoboTwin, which means an evolutionary search over execution parameters will see real but noisy signal from the boundary placement, not only from the executed count.

### Gaps
- No study measures safety violations (drop, tip, collision) as a function of executed fraction; AutoHorizon's collision/drop remarks are qualitative.
- The Diffusion Policy Fig. 5 success-vs-Ta numbers are only in a plot; the text gives "8 optimal" and "latency up to 4 steps" without values.
- ManiGuard provides no execution ablation and no rationale for 8 of 16, so there is no evidence specific to the Jar family on this knob.
- Liang et al.'s full fixed-chunk table could not be extracted from the CVF PDF beyond the stated best sizes (4 for Spatial, 16 for Goal).

## Key question 2: Real-Time Chunking, latency-aware execution, smoothing and blending between chunks

### Takeaway
Temporal ensembling is the simplest smoother and it is unreliable: it helps ACT-style CVAE policies by a few points, hurts pi0 (dropped by its authors), is decay-rate sensitive, and on a real pi0.5 bimanual robot with +100 ms injected latency it oscillated hard enough to trigger the protective stop. Real-Time Chunking (inpainting the next chunk onto the frozen prefix of the current one) removed the latency penalty entirely up to +200 ms and improved the most precision-sensitive task; its main safety-relevant finding is that naive asynchronous switching produces out-of-distribution accelerations.

### Cited Findings
- RTC (Black, Galliker, Levine; Physical Intelligence 2025; arXiv 2506.07339): "Chunking sacrifices the reactivity of a system ... and also introduces discontinuities in the transition points between chunks"; "A long execution horizon reduces a policy's responsiveness to new information, while a short one increases the likelihood of mode-jumping, jerky behavior"; typical practice is s ~ H/2 — [RTC, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- RTC: naive asynchronous switching leads to "jerky behavior that is worsened dramatically with higher delays"; temporal ensembling "reduces the acceleration but produces poor actions"; the blog describes naive async as causing "discontinuities and unsafe accelerations" — [RTC, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339); [PI blog](https://www.pi.website/research/real_time_chunking)
- [SIM] Kinetix dynamic benchmark (H=8, delays 0-4): TE "performs poorly across the board, even with an inference delay of d = 0"; RTC and BID are the only methods whose success is "strictly increasing ... with decreasing execution horizon"; naive async degrades with delay — [RTC, Sec. 4.1 and Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- [REAL] Setup: pi0.5, H=50, 20 ms control period (50 Hz), 5 denoising steps, 76 ms model latency (97 ms for RTC), +10-20 ms LAN, so d ~ 6; injected +100 ms and +200 ms give d ~ 11 and 16. Synchronous baseline "executes s = 25 actions and then pauses". 6 tasks x 10 trials x 4 methods x delays = 480 episodes, 28 robot-hours — [RTC, Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- [REAL] Results: RTC "is completely robust to injected delay, showing no degradation, whereas synchronous degrades linearly and both TE variants do not run at all due to causing such high oscillations that the robot's protective stop is triggered" (at +100 and +200 ms). On light-candle, "the most precision-sensitive task and also the only one without retrying, RTC shows a large advantage in final score" — [RTC, Sec. 4.2 and Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- [REAL] "All tasks, except for light candle, allow for retrying until the time limit (and pi0.5 does, in general, exhibit robust retrying behavior)"; synchronous often reaches a similar final score but later, "reflecting fewer mistakes and less retrying" for RTC — [RTC, Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- RTC execution horizon is adaptive by construction: s = max(d, s_min), constrained d <= s <= H - d — [RTC, Alg. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- BID vs RTC compute: BID with batch 16 on pi0.5 has 2.3x the latency of RTC — [RTC, App. A.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- pi0 dropped temporal ensembling because it "hurt performance" in early experiments; execution is open-loop with no aggregation — [pi0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)
- [SIM] TAS (arXiv 2511.04421): EMA vs open-loop execution of 8: Push-T DP 74.1 vs 72.6; FurnitureBench One Leg DP 53.0 vs 63.8 (EMA worse); Lamp DP 10.5 vs 11.6; EMA "can improve motion smoothness" but "disrupts the multimodal action distributions" — [TAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421)
- [SIM] Median temporal ensembling (arXiv 2609.27167): replacing the exponential mean with a coordinate-wise median over the same overlapping candidates raises robomimic Can (h=2, burst-corrupted observations) from 0.600 to 0.900 and is never significantly worse on clean data; ablation on Can: latest-prediction-only 0.480, exponential mean 0.600, median 0.880; no smoothness or safety metric — [Median TE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27167)
- [REAL] Reactive Diffusion Policy (Xue et al., RSS 2025, arXiv 2503.02881), wiping with perturbation after contact (grasp rate / score): chunk 8: 100% / 0.15; chunk 2: 20% / 0.10; TE tau=0.2: 30% / 0.05; tau=0.5: 0% / 0.00; tau=0.8: 100% / 0.15; RDP (closed-loop tactile fast loop) 100% / 0.50. Heavy ensembling "over-smooths behavior and hurts reactivity"; low tau behaves like a small chunk — [RDP, ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- Interpolation as the standard smoother: ACT interpolates and sends positions at 50 Hz; DP interpolates 10 Hz commands to 125 Hz (UR5) or via a ~1 kHz mid-level controller (Franka); RDP interpolates to >500 Hz — [ACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705); [DP](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5); [RDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- [REAL] GR00T N1.7 BEHAVIOR baseline serves with temporal ensembling on by default — [BEHAVIOR baselines](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/baselines.html)

### Inferences
- The only quantified *safety* event tied to a smoothing choice in this literature is RTC's protective-stop result for temporal ensembling under latency; this argues against adding TE to a flow-matching VLA as a "cheap smoother" without checking the oscillation it induces.
- In a synchronous simulator with no inference latency (ManiGuard's setting), RTC's benefit does not apply; its sim result does say that continuity-preserving methods are the ones that profit from shorter execution horizons, which bears on whether dropping from 8 to 4 executed actions can help a frozen pi0.5.

### Gaps
- No low-pass-filter or chunk-blending study on contact-rich manipulation reports collision, force, or drop statistics; the smoothing literature reports success only.
- RTC's per-task throughput numbers are in bar charts without labelled values.

## Key question 3: Speed and gain scaling at execution (time-scaling, compliance, descent caps, gripper rates)

### Takeaway
Uniformly speeding up a chunked policy at test time costs 8-10 success points on average in simulation (2x downsampling) and more on precision tasks; a sim study on a humanoid parcel task shows ACT degrades 2-3x faster than its own scripted expert when sped up and is also worse than the expert when slowed to 0.5x. Nobody has published a clean "slow the learned policy down and measure success or safety" sweep below 1x. Stiffness is a known large lever on real contact tasks (fixed-compliant 23% vs stiff 14% vs adaptive 96% on flipping; a stiff policy broke its tool). Descent-velocity caps and gripper closing-rate limits appear only as unablated deployment safeguards.

### Cited Findings

**Uniform time-scaling of a frozen policy**
- [SIM] DemoSpeedup (arXiv 2506.05064): executing the original-speed policy with chunks downsampled 2x at test time drops average success ACT 77% -> 69% (1.7x faster) and DP 55% -> 45% (1.6x faster) over 11 ALOHA/BiGym tasks; insertion ACT 21 -> 13; Saucepan-to-Hob DP 79 -> 41; "causes an average performance drop of over 8%"; precision phases (picking, aligning, withdrawing from an inserted plate) must stay at normal speed; gripper controllers may "fail to track high speed and cause failures"; no safety metric — [DemoSpeedup](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05064)
- [SIM] SAIL (arXiv 2506.11948), RoboMimic/MimicGen, DP vs DP-Fast (uniform speedup with a low-gain controller): Lift 1.00 -> 0.95 at 1.59x; Can 0.97 -> 0.87 at 2.48x; Square 0.83 -> 0.55 at 2.20x; Stack 1.00 -> 0.98 at 2.28x; Mug 0.68 -> 0.56 at 1.74x. SAIL's adaptive speed modulation uses a binary critical-action flag (gripper open/close events) to switch between c_slow and c_fast — [SAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11948)
- [REAL] SAIL at 5x, DP-Fast -> SAIL: Stacking Cups 0.10 -> 0.40; Wiping Board 0.90 -> 0.70 (sustained contact, worse); Baking 0.90 -> 1.00; Folding Cloth 0.10 -> 0.30; Plate Fruits 0.60 -> 0.80; Pack Chicken 0.40 -> 0.90; Bimanual Serve 0.40 -> 0.70. Failures at speed: imprecise grasping, low-fidelity tracking, jerky motion; the paper "does not explicitly handle robot-object dynamics" — [SAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11948)
- [SIM] Enwerem, Baras, Belta (arXiv 2609.01453, Sept 2026), ParcelStow in Isaac Lab on a fixed-base Unitree G1 at 50 Hz, ACT chunk 100 with TE, speed factor r applied to post-acquisition phases, demos span r in [0.5, 2]; success over 100 episodes at r = 1 / 1.5 / 2: scripted expert 1.00 / 0.99 / 0.84; ACT-A 1.00 / 0.91 / 0.53; ACT-B 0.70 / 0.58 / 0.36; ACT-C 0.62 / 0.44 / 0.14; Diffusion Policy 0.68 / 0.73 / 0.54 (non-monotone). ACT-A "differs at both slower and faster demonstrated speeds, including a difference of 0.16 at r = 0.5"; 35 of ACT-A's 47 failures at r=2 are insertion misalignments; 0 of 414 acquisitions without force closure completed; "Our experiments do not vary chunk size" — [Temporal robustness study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01453)
- [SIM+REAL] SpeedTuning (arXiv 2608.09138): ACT on ALOHA at 50 Hz; a DQN speed policy time-warps each chunk by linear interpolation to a discrete speed v; >2.4x speedup over six tasks; learned slow-downs during grasping a swinging tea bag and during pouring "to prevent spilling"; baseline uniform-speed curves exist only in figures; no sub-1x speeds reported; no safety statement — [SpeedTuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09138)
- [SIM+REAL] SpeedAug (arXiv 2512.00062): naive acceleration "degrades the success rate" because "certain task segments require slower and more precise motion" (approach for grasp; corners); real episodes are terminated on "a hard collision with the table, objects, or the box", but collision counts are not reported by speed — [SpeedAug](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00062)

**Compliance / stiffness at execution**
- [REAL] Adaptive Compliance Policy (Hou et al., arXiv 2410.09309), UR5e with F/T sensor, same data and epochs for every variant. Item flipping (100 tests): ACP 96%, fixed-compliant policy (k = 500 N/m) 23%, stiff position policy 14%. Vase wiping (16 tests): ACP 93.75%, compliant 43.75%; the stiff policy is excluded from the table because "Position noise causes excessive force that breaks the tool" (tool broke on test four); the compliant policy was safe in contact but lost tracking under friction — [ACP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09309)
- [REAL] RDP: pure-visual DP "frequently predicts inaccurate trajectories and results in large contact forces", so evaluators moved their hands to prevent sensor damage; no force numbers for policy evaluation — [RDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- Diffusion Policy deployment caps: end-effector velocity 0.43 m/s and >=1 cm table clearance on the UR5 station (not ablated) — [DP appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- SparkVLA real runs: "robot speed and gripper force are capped" and operate "independently of SparkVLA's task-state estimation" (not ablated) — [SparkVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16172)
- openpi DROID: gripper binarised at 0.5 (no closing-rate control) — [openpi droid/main.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/droid/main.py)

### Inferences
- The one sub-1x data point (ACT-A at r=0.5, 16 points below the expert, in sim) suggests that slowing a chunked policy is not free: chunks encode timing, and time-scaling them moves the policy off its training distribution even when slower would be physically safer. The effect size is single-task and single-policy, so it is a caution, not a law.
- Phase-dependent slowing (around gripper events) is what every speed paper converged on; the slow-phase trigger used in practice is trivial (gripper open/close events in SAIL), which is directly reusable as a dial input.
- Compliance has the largest measured real-robot effect of any execution knob in this survey (14% -> 96%), but ManiGuard's joint-position controller in OmniGibson does not expose stiffness, so this lever may not be available in the user's simulator.

### Gaps
- No paper measures success or collision/drop rates when a learned policy is executed *slower* than demonstration speed as a deliberate intervention.
- No paper studies a descent-velocity cap near the table or a gripper closing-rate limit as an execution-time intervention on a learned policy, for success or for safety.
- No paper scales action deltas after contact detection (post-contact gain scaling) on a frozen VLA.

## Key question 4: Stall, hesitation and retry at execution level

### Takeaway
Stalling is documented as a failure signature (OpenVLA "freezes or shakes", DP "gets stuck" at demonstration pauses, FurnitureBench closed-loop DP at 0%), and failure detectors reach 72-89% balanced accuracy / ROC-AUC, but none of the detectors use proprioception-only stall rules and none trigger an automatic retry; retry is reported only qualitatively ("pi0.5 exhibits robust retrying behavior"). There is no measurement of how often a scripted lift-and-retry helps or harms a VLA.

### Cited Findings
- [SIM] Chunk length controls idle reproduction: in BID's 1-D model "Long action horizons lead to idle distributions closer to the long-idle expert in low-noise environments, whereas shorter action horizons align more closely with the short-idle expert in high-noise environments" — [BID, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- [SIM] DP: "single-step policies can easily overfit to this pausing behavior"; a horizon >1 compensates for idle demo portions — [DP, Sec. 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5)
- [SIM] TAS: fully closed-loop execution (1 action per query) of DP gives 0.0% on all FurnitureBench tasks vs 63.8% (One Leg), 11.6% (Lamp), 60.3% (Round Table) when 8 actions are executed open-loop; Push-T DP 64.9% closed vs 72.6% open — [TAS, Tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421)
- [REAL] RDP: DP with tactile embeddings "may get stuck" at contact and switch repeatedly between contact states; smaller chunks "make the policy sensitive to pauses in human demonstrations, causing it to get stuck before grasping" — [RDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- [REAL] "Knowing When to Stop": a multi-sampling baseline showed "intermittent pauses during movement" that hurt success; short horizons "may induce oscillatory behaviors" — [Knowing When to Stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00908)
- [SIM+REAL] SAFE (Gu et al., arXiv 2506.09937): OpenVLA on LIBERO "often freezes or shakes mid-task in failed rollouts"; in one real case the failure flag is raised after the policy has frozen; pi0-FAST shows oscillatory motion; detection ROC-AUC 81-89 (seen) using the VLA's internal features; proprioception not evaluated; action-variance baselines exist but are mixed; after a flag the options are abort or hand over to a human, no automatic retry — [SAFE](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09937)
- [SIM+REAL] FAIL-Detect (Xu et al., arXiv 2503.08558): best balanced accuracy ~78% sim, ~72% hardware; the SPARC action-smoothness score (jitter) is fastest on 4/6 hardware cases but inaccurate; OOD trajectories "can be slow or jittery and score worse even when they succeed"; no recovery is triggered — [FAIL-Detect](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08558)
- [SIM] ManiGuard "vacuous-safe" inaction: zero-shot engagement rates 41.33% (pi0.5), 56.33% (pi0), 33.00% (SmolVLA); fine-tuned 83-96%; "a falling engagement rate can lower the violation rate without any gain in safety"; on Jar ID, pi0.5-SFT engaged 77/78 — [ManiGuard, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- [REAL] Retry as policy behaviour: pi0.5 "does, in general, exhibit robust retrying behavior" and most RTC tasks allow retrying until the time limit; RTC completes more of each task earlier, "reflecting fewer mistakes and less retrying" — [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- [REAL] pi0 says training only on high-quality data gives a brittle model that "does not reliably recover from mistakes"; recovery is attributed to diverse pre-training, not to an execution mechanism — [pi0](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)
- [REAL] pi*0.6 / RECAP (Physical Intelligence, 2025): RL from on-robot experience "can decrease failure rates by 2x or more" and "more than doubles the throughput on some of the hardest tasks"; edge cases (boxes sticking together, refolding after mistakes) are handled by the policy; no retry counts, stall metrics, or execution settings reported — [pi*0.6 blog](https://www.pi.website/blog/pistar06)
- [SIM] ACT's own failure analysis names premature gripper closure ("the right arm closes its gripper too early") as a dominant real-world failure on Thread Velcro (92% stage-1 -> 20% final) — [ACT, Sec. V-C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)

### Inferences
- Stalls are strongly coupled to the chunk/execution-horizon knob: shorter horizons and every-step re-querying reproduce demonstration pauses, longer horizons push through them. For a frozen policy, lengthening the executed prefix when proprioception shows zero motion is the only stall intervention with indirect support in the literature; it has not been tested as such.
- Failure detection from internal features works but is heavier than a proprioceptive stall rule; a simple "no joint motion for N steps" trigger has no published evaluation, positive or negative.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates a scripted recovery (lift, back off, re-query) for a chunked VLA, nor reports how often retries convert failures vs cause new violations.
- No proprioception-only stall detector for VLAs was found; SAFE and FAIL-Detect use learned scores.
- Retry statistics in deployments (RTC, pi*0.6) are not published.

## Key question 5: Open-loop vs closed-loop re-querying and reaction to contact events

### Takeaway
More frequent re-querying helps only when the policy can react to what it sees: it rescues tasks with external perturbation or stochastic dynamics (Push-T under noise: closed-loop 29.5% vs open-loop 13.0%; peeling after contact loss: 0.19 open-loop DP vs 0.88 with a 20-30 Hz tactile fast loop) and hurts clean quasi-static tasks (72.6% -> 64.9% on Push-T, 63.8% -> 0% on FurnitureBench). For purely visual policies at 256x256, contact events such as a jar tipping are only visible after the fact, which is why the contact-rich papers add force/tactile feedback rather than just re-querying faster.

### Cited Findings
- [SIM] BID, VQ-BeT Push-T: open-loop 64.0 -> 13.0 as action noise rises 0 -> 1.5; closed-loop 48.9 -> 29.5; "the vanilla open-loop approach exhibits a rapid performance decline as the environment becomes increasingly stochastic. Even in closed-loop operations, the vanilla baseline still experiences a significant performance drop" — [BID, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- [SIM] TAS: closed-loop (1 action per query) vs open-loop (8 per query) for DP: Push-T 64.9 vs 72.6; Image Push-T 48.3 vs 64.9; FurnitureBench 0.0 vs 63.8 / 11.6 / 60.3; under added noise, One Leg DP open-loop falls 63.8 -> 14.1 and closed-loop stays 0.0; TAS (selecting among cached candidates) reaches 78.1 / 63.4 (noise) — [TAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421)
- [REAL] RDP, perturbation applied *after* contact: peeling DP 0.19 vs RDP-force 0.88; wiping DP 0.25 vs RDP-force 0.80; bimanual cup lifting DP 0% / 0% vs RDP-force 100% clamp / 90% lift (soft cup); DP "will execute the remaining trajectory in the action chunk in an open-loop manner" after losing contact. Slow loop 1-2 Hz (12 FPS chunks), fast loop 20-30 Hz (24 FPS) — [RDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- [REAL] RDP chunk-size ablation under after-contact perturbation shows that shrinking the vision chunk from 8 to 2 does *not* rescue wiping (score 0.15 -> 0.10, grasp 100% -> 20%); only the tactile fast loop does (0.50) — [RDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881)
- [REAL] BID dynamic picking: re-sampling with coherence (BID) doubles success over vanilla open- and closed-loop and EMA when the cup moves — [BID, Fig. 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- [SIM] RTC Kinetix (dynamic, no hold-position): with continuity preserved, success rises monotonically as the execution horizon shrinks; without it (naive async) it does not — [RTC, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- [REAL] AutoHorizon: executing 5 of 50 (near-closed-loop) gave 0.0% on Cube->Plate vs 81.5% at 10 of 50 — [AutoHorizon, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445)
- [REAL] A3: executing 5 of the chunk gave 27.5% with 91.5 inference calls per episode vs 79.2% at 15 — [A3, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567)
- ManiGuard: policies "re-infer after every 8 executed actions"; the pi models see a third-person overview and one wrist view at 256x256; no proprioceptive force input; violations on Jar occur at a median 14-18% of the post-engagement window for pi0.5-SFT — [ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)

### Inferences
- Re-querying more often than every 8 steps is unlikely to fix tip-on-lid-push failures by itself: the evidence says closed-loop helps when the new observation carries the information needed to correct (moving target, tactile contact loss) and hurts when it mostly adds mode-jumping. A jar starting to tip is visible in the wrist camera only after the fingers have already loaded the rim.
- Conversely, lengthening the executed prefix from 8 toward 16 is supported in free-space transport phases by every adaptive-horizon paper and by the GR00T 16-step sweep (e=16 best on LIBERO-Long).

### Gaps
- No study isolates re-query frequency against contact-event outcomes (slip, tip) for a visual-only VLA; the contact-rich comparisons all add a sensor.

## Key question 6: Benchmarks and deployment reports where execution parameters were varied

### Takeaway
LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0 now have multiple published executed-horizon sweeps for pi0/pi0.5 (summarised in Q1); ManiSkill/MetaWorld have one (A3, flat); RoboCasa has one chunk-size sweep (Liang et al., only best sizes extractable); OmniGibson/BEHAVIOR and ManiGuard have none. Real-robot sweeps exist in four 2026 papers and consistently show 30-80 point ranges across fixed horizons.

### Cited Findings
- LIBERO: pi0.5 50-step chunk executed 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 -> averages roughly 94 / 91.5 / 86.8 / 81.2 / 70.6 across suites (from AutoHorizon Table 1); pi0 50-step executed 5 / 10 / 30 / 50 -> 92.10 / 93.05 / 90.15 / 76.05 (PAV); GR00T 16-step executed 1..16 within ~5 points (AutoHorizon Table 2); fixed-chunk best differs by suite, 4 for Spatial vs 16 for Goal (Liang) — [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445); [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434); [Liang et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Liang_Adaptive_Action_Chunking_at_Inference-time_for_Vision-Language-Action_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- RoboTwin 2.0: pi0.5 H=32 executing 4 / 8 / 20 / 32 -> 27.40 / 55.76 / 55.16 / 43.64 (PAV, all 50 tasks); pi0.5 H=50 executing 10 / 15 / 20 / 25 -> 61.6 / 58.0 / 53.8 / 52.2 (Knowing When to Stop); X-VLA H=30 the reverse (43.5 at 15 -> 76.1 at 30) — [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434); [Knowing When to Stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00908)
- ManiSkill / MetaWorld: best fixed horizon vs adaptive differ by about one point (A3) — [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567)
- RoboCasa: GR00T N1.5 success "highly related to the action chunk size" (Fig. 1); AAC +2.3% average over 24 tasks — [Liang et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Liang_Adaptive_Action_Chunking_at_Inference-time_for_Vision-Language-Action_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- OmniGibson / BEHAVIOR: the 2025 challenge page gives no baseline numbers or execution settings; 2026 baselines serve pi0.5 with action_horizon 16 in receding-horizon mode and GR00T N1.7 with temporal ensembling; no ablation — [BEHAVIOR archive](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/archive/index.html); [BEHAVIOR baselines](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/baselines.html)
- ManiGuard (OmniGibson): fixed 8 of 16 / 8 of 50, no sweep; 21,000 sim rollouts cost 1,326 RTX 4090 GPU-hours; two identically seeded runs differed in 39% of overview pixels, changing episode lengths — [ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Real pi0.5/pi0-class sweeps: AutoHorizon (Franka, 3 tasks), PAV (Franka, 3 tasks x 30), A3 (Piper, 4 tasks), Knowing When to Stop (dual-arm, 3 tasks x 20): see Q1 numbers — [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445); [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434); [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567); [Knowing When to Stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00908)
- Real pi0.5 latency sweep (RTC): synchronous degrades linearly with +0/+100/+200 ms; RTC flat — [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)

### Inferences
- The ManiGuard non-determinism note (39% pixel difference between identically seeded runs) means that small execution-parameter effects (<5 points) will be inside the noise of a 104-episode evaluation; only the large real-robot-scale effects are detectable at that budget.

### Gaps
- No OmniGibson or BEHAVIOR report varies execution parameters; the 2025 BEHAVIOR leaderboard page and winners' reports were not reachable.

## Key question 7: Execution parameters framed as a tunable dial against speed or safety

### Takeaway
Speed-vs-success is framed explicitly as a Pareto trade-off (SpeedTuning, SAIL, SpeedAug, DemoSpeedup); horizon-vs-reactivity is framed as a trade-off in every chunking paper; nobody frames any execution parameter as a dial against *safety* (violations, drops, collisions), and nobody tunes one by evolution or any black-box search on a frozen VLA.

### Cited Findings
- SpeedTuning positions the learned speed policy "beyond the Pareto curve" of uniform-speed baselines (speed vs success) — [SpeedTuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09138)
- SAIL defines throughput and speedup-over-demonstration as the axes traded against success — [SAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11948)
- RTC: "Chunked execution ensures temporal consistency at the expense of reactivity"; execution horizon s is "a hyperparameter constrained by d <= s <= H - d" — [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- SparkVLA and MoH describe the fixed horizon as a "tradeoff between smoothness and reactivity" / consistency vs reactivity — [SparkVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16172); [MoH](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19433)
- Bernoulli-Continuation uses RL (GRPO) over a frozen VLA to learn continue/replan, the closest thing to black-box tuning of an execution knob; the reward is success-gated with a replanning-efficiency term, not a safety term — [Continue or Replan?](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03483)
- Adaptive-horizon papers uniformly report no safety analysis: AutoHorizon ("does not present a safety analysis"), A3, PAV, Knowing When to Stop, MoH, TAS (safety mentioned only as a reason to train in sim) — [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445); [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567); [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434); [TAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421)

### Inferences
- The "execution dial vs safety" framing is open: the pieces (horizon sweeps, phase-dependent slowing, gripper-event triggers, compliance) each exist with success numbers, but no one has reported violation rates against any of them.

### Gaps
- No paper found that optimises execution-time parameters of a frozen policy for a safety specification.

## Simplest things that worked (ranked by cost of the intervention, with the measured effect)

1. **Choose the executed prefix in the 20-60% band of the chunk instead of executing the whole chunk** (one integer). pi0.5 on LIBERO: 94.9% at 10 of 50 vs 71.5% at 50 of 50 [SIM]; real Franka: 81.5% at 10 of 50 vs 0.0% at 5 of 50 and 51.5% at 50 of 50 on one task [REAL]. For a 16-step chunk the band 4-16 is flat within ~5 points [SIM]. Sources: [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445), [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434), [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567).
2. **Do not run fully closed-loop (1 action per query) on a quasi-static task** (one integer). DP: 72.6% -> 64.9% on Push-T, 63.8% -> 0.0% on FurnitureBench One Leg [SIM]; real pi0-class: 27.5% at 5 of chunk vs 79.2% at 15 [REAL]. Sources: [TAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04421), [A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11567).
3. **Shorten the executed prefix around gripper events and lengthen it in free space** (a two-value schedule keyed on gripper open/close). Not ablated in isolation, but it is what every adaptive-horizon method converged on and what SAIL's speed flag uses; adaptive versions gain +10 to +33 points over the best fixed horizon on real robots [REAL]. Sources: [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21445), [Knowing When to Stop](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00908), [PAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21434), [SAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11948).
4. **Replace the exponential temporal-ensemble mean with a median** (one line) if ensembling is used at all: 0.600 -> 0.900 on corrupted Can, never significantly worse clean [SIM]. Source: [Median TE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27167).
5. **Do not add temporal ensembling to a flow-matching VLA by default**: pi0's authors found it hurt; on real pi0.5 under +100 ms latency it oscillated into the protective stop [REAL]. Sources: [pi0](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164), [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339).
6. **Lower stiffness / use compliance in contact** if the controller exposes it: 14% (stiff) -> 23% (fixed compliant) -> 96% (adaptive) on flipping; stiff execution broke the tool [REAL]. Source: [ACP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09309).
7. **Do not uniformly speed up**: 2x test-time downsampling costs ~8-10 points on average, far more on insertion [SIM]; ACT loses 47 points from 1x to 2x where its expert loses 16 [SIM]. Sources: [DemoSpeedup](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05064), [Temporal robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01453).
8. **Uniformly slowing down has one data point and it is negative**: ACT-A is 16 points below its expert at 0.5x [SIM]. Source: [Temporal robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01453).
9. **Deployment caps that everyone uses and no one ablates**: end-effector velocity cap (0.43 m/s) and table clearance (1 cm) in Diffusion Policy's real setup; speed and gripper-force caps in SparkVLA; gripper binarisation in openpi DROID. No measured effect. Sources: [DP](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5), [SparkVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16172), [openpi](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/droid/main.py).

Knobs with no published measurement for either success or safety on a learned policy: descent-velocity cap near the table, gripper closing-rate limit, post-contact delta scaling, scripted lift-and-retry, proprioceptive stall detection with re-query, and any execution parameter evaluated against specification violations (ManiGuard-style) rather than success.
