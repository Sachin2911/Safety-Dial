# Runtime safety supervisors, shields, failure detectors and recovery behaviours for learned manipulation policies and VLAs (as of October 2026)

Scope note. Web search was cut off part-way through this task (shared session budget), so after the first round every source below was fetched directly from arXiv / the arXiv API / project pages, and three PDFs (SafeDiffuser ICLR 2025, SafeManip, the VLA-safety survey) were text-extracted locally. Numbers are quoted as the fetched text reported them; where a paper's prose and tables disagree, both are given. "Privileged" means the layer reads simulator state (object poses, contacts) that a real robot would not have; "deployable" means it reads only what the robot observes (images, proprioception, F/T, the policy's own internals).

## Q1. Shields and safety filters for learned policies (CBF filters, predictive filters, SafeDiffuser, SafeVLA/ISA, AEGIS, RoboGuard, Safety-Chip, Code-as-Monitor, X-Safe, latent filters)

### Takeaway
Every deployable filter that reports before/after numbers on a learned manipulation policy drives geometric violations (collisions) to near zero, but the ones that *steer* the arm away (per-step CBF-QP) cost a lot of task success, while the ones that *slow or stop along the policy's own path* (PACS) or *contract the action toward the current configuration* (X-Safe) cost 0 to 8 points. No published filter targets the ManiGuard-type constraints (object tipping, dropping) on a VLA; the closest are the latent safety filter (spilling) and SafeDiffuser (joint limits in a planner).

### Cited Findings

**Per-step CBF / QP projection layers**
- VLSA / AEGIS (Hu et al., arXiv 2512.11891, Dec 2025): a plug-and-play CBF-QP "safety constraint layer" after the VLA output, solving min ||a − u_vla||² s.t. ḣ ≥ −α(h), "activates only when potential safety violations are detected"; action space [v, ω, g]. Inputs are *deployable*: GLM-4.5V names the critical obstacle from the agent-view RGB, GroundingDINO localises it, agent-view and back-view depth fused to a point cloud, plus end-effector pose with a fixed ellipsoid gripper model. On SafeLIBERO (16 tasks, 2 obstacle levels, 1,600 episodes), translational setting: π0.5 collision-avoidance rate (CAR) 18.7% / task success (TSR) 50.9% → AEGIS CAR 77.9% / TSR 68.1%; full 6-D setting: π0.5 CAR 17.1% / TSR 57.8% → AEGIS CAR 68.9% / TSR 67.5%; OpenVLA-OFT baseline CAR 15.1% / TSR 22.8% (translational). α(h)=10h and k=10 are fixed "with no rationale"; "no sweep over α, safety margin, or other parameters is reported"; real-robot (π0.5-DROID, Franka) results are qualitative only. The authors note qualitatively that "safety-induced distribution shift can cause task failure after obstacles are avoided". — [arXiv 2512.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11891)
- PACS, "From Demonstrations to Safe Deployment: Path-Consistent Safety Filtering for Diffusion Policies" (Römer, Balletshofer, Thumm, Pavone, Schoellig, Althoff; arXiv 2511.06385, v2 Mar 2026): the action chunk is integrated into a time-optimal intended trajectory (Ruckig), set-based reachability checks it each safety step, and on failure the filter "switches to the last verified failsafe stopping trajectory", i.e. it slows or stops *along the intended path rather than steering away*. Inputs: joint state with bounded tracking error, measured object positions with bounded error and velocity/acceleration bounds, sensor delay (sim uses object poses directly). Simulation (Robomimic Lift/Can/Square + dynamic sphere, 100 rollouts, avg success): unfiltered joint-space policy 0.70; CBF filter [13] 0.04; single-action SSM/PFL 0.41/0.44; PACS SSM/PFL 0.69/0.72. Real Franka FR3 (30 rollouts): average success OFF 0.79 vs PACS 0.80; safe-success OFF 0.00 vs PACS 0.80; safety-violation fraction of timesteps OFF 0.56±0.21 vs PACS 0.00. SmolVLA on Handover: OFF success 0.77 / viol 0.41±0.15 → PACS 0.80 / 0.00. Real CBF comparison (Sorting with human): CBF success 0.43 vs PACS 0.80. Energy thresholds T_safe were "formally identified in prior work" (0.000 J Sorting, 0.014/0.265 J Handover, 0.001 J Feeding); CBF α and margins are not reported. — [arXiv 2511.06385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06385)
- iKinQP-RL, "Towards Online Safety Corrections for Robotic Manipulation Policies" (Spalter, Roberts, Hiatt, NRL; arXiv 2409.08233, Sep 2024): a lightweight inverse-kinematics QP (a "simplified, lightweight version of MPC") checks and corrects each SAC action for obstacle, self-collision and joint limits, requires "a given model of the robot arm and environment (including collision obstacles)" (sim only; obstacle poses' source not stated), with a failsafe to one of three predetermined retreat positions when inside the collision buffer. Kinova Gen3 peg-carry with a static block: collision baseline 100% / 86% / 32% (Middle / Partial / Far) → 0% in all; task success 100% baseline → 83% / 100% / 100%. Parameters hand-fixed (buffer 0.015 m, Δt 0.05 s); a sweep over n (points executed per query) at n=6/7/8 gave success 84%/88%/93%, episode time 30.65/24.88/21.88 s, all 0% collision; n=7 chosen by hand. — [arXiv 2409.08233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08233)
- "Towards Safe Robot Foundation Models Using Inductive Biases" (Tölle et al., arXiv 2505.10219): ATACOM constraint-manifold safety layer "placed after the foundation policy" for manipulation (avoid irrelevant objects) and air hockey; abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2505.10219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10219)

**Sampling-based action masking**
- X-Safe, "Embodiment-Agnostic Safeguarding for Robot Arm Manipulation Policies via Action Masking" (Beaudin, Krasowski, Nagpal, Seshia, Arcak, Mehr, UC Berkeley; arXiv 2606.22278, v2 Oct 2026): samples configurations to build a probabilistically collision-free polytope around the current configuration, "radially contracts a candidate action toward the current configuration until it lies inside the polytope", with a Chebyshev-centre fallback that moves away from collision. Needs a collision checker (MuJoCo), object poses and dimensions from perception, URDF/FK; no training data. Franka cluttered pick-and-place (200 sim episodes): diffusion policy success 73.3±1.8% / collision 31.3±4.8% → DP+X-Safe 66.4±1.3% / 0.04±0.02% (prose says 0.02%); RAIL baseline 53.2% / 0.8%. X-Safe intervenes on 54.2% of timesteps. Replacing the Chebyshev fallback with a stopping controller drops success to 44%. xArm7 hardware (25 runs): obstacle, no guard 52±9.9% success / 100% collision → X-Safe 44±9.9% / 0% collision; with a 2 mm-inflated obstacle and calibrated pose, 0 collisions in 20 runs. Parameters fixed by hand (M=1024, N_iter=12, δ=0.005, ε=0.02, τ=0.5); authors "recommend lowering δ first, then N_iter"; failure probability over n actions bounded by n·δ. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22278)

**Latent / world-model filters (deployable, observation-based)**
- "Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis" (Nakamura, Peters, Bajcsy; arXiv 2502.00935, v3 Apr 2025): world-model latent + failure classifier + discounted latent HJ backup; at deployment the task action runs unless the predicted next-state value ≤ threshold, then the safety policy overrides. Hardware inputs: two RGB cameras at 15 Hz, proprioception, actions (DINO-WM). Sim block manipulation ("red blocks must not topple", 50 rollouts; safe-success / violation / incompletion): Dreamer no filter 64/36/0; SQRL ε_risk=0.1 68/28/4; SQRL ε_risk=0.05 8/22/70; LatentSafe 80/20/0. Franka Skittles-bag "do not spill": weak IL policy spill rate 73.4% → "63.6% decrease in constraint violations" with filter (filter still fails 26.4%); strong IL policy: one violation with and without filter. Filter failed to prevent spills on OOD bags (Classic, Peanut M&Ms). Threshold ε=0.4 (sim) / 0.3 (hardware) with "no stated selection method"; SQRL's ε_risk "manually tuned". Dubins car privileged-vs-latent gap: F1 0.987 vs 0.984; safe states 98.4% vs 96%. — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)
- AnySafe (Agrawal, Seo, Nakamura, Tian, Bajcsy; ICRA 2026) adapts latent safety filters at runtime via constraint parameterisation; listed in the "No Free Checker" survey, no numbers retrieved. — [No Free Checker, arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

**Diffusion-planner-internal CBFs**
- SafeDiffuser (Xiao, Wang, Gan, Rus; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2306.00148): "finite-time diffusion invariance" via CBFs inside denoising. Manipulation (PyBullet, joint trajectories conditioned on object locations; specs are joint limits incl. speed-dependent ones; N=100 runs). Table 4: Diffuser S-spec −0.057 / C-spec −0.065 / reward 0.650±0.107 / 0.038 s; Truncation 1.6e−8 / × / 0.575±0.112; classifier guidance −0.050 / −0.053 / 0.800±0.328; RoS-diffuser 0.072 / 0.069 / 0.925±0.107 / 0.088 s; RoS-diffuser-CF 0.093 / 0.002 / 0.800±0.114 / 0.039 s (spec metric = min constraint value over runs; ≥0 means never violated). The authors note RoS-diffuser "can even improve the performance (reward)" here because joint-limit satisfaction avoids joint-space collisions. CBF class-K parameter choice not described. — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f95606d8e870020085990d9650b4f2a1-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2306.00148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00148)
- PC-Diffuser (Ku, Lyu; arXiv 2603.10330) argues SafeDiffuser's invariance "holds over the diffusion index, not rollout time, so the final executed trajectory carries no formal safety certificate"; its own numbers are driving (nuPlan): collision 100% → 10.29% on an all-collision set, vs Robust-Safe Diffuser 81.43%, MPC-CBF 79.29%. — [arXiv 2603.10330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10330)

**Training-time "safety alignment" (not a runtime layer, included for contrast)**
- SafeVLA / ISA (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight; arXiv 2503.03480): constrained-MDP post-training with a learned Lagrange multiplier; on Safety-CHORES "average 83.58% reduction in cumulative safety cost and a 3.85% higher success rate compared with FLaRe"; Safety-ObjNav success 0.865 / cost 1.854 vs FLaRe 0.822 / 12.356; "over 32x safer in extreme failure scenarios" (cost 2.20 vs 71.68). Needs a simulator cost signal at training time; nothing at runtime. SafeVLA-Bench notes its safety signal "is defined only for its own Safety-CHORES environment". — [arXiv 2503.03480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480); [project page](https://safevla.github.io/); [SafeVLA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00773v2)

**Semantic / plan-level guardrails (LLM-level, not low-level)**
- RoboGuard, "Safety Guardrails for LLM-Enabled Robots" (arXiv 2503.07885, Mar 2025): a root-of-trust LLM turns rules into temporal-logic constraints and control synthesis reconciles them with the plan; "unsafe-plan execution fell from 92% to below 2.5%" under jailbreaks "without hurting performance on safe plans". Plan level, not motion level. — [arXiv 2503.07885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07885v1)
- "Plug in the Safety Chip" (Yang, Raman, Shah, Tellex; arXiv 2309.09919, 2023): LTL constraint module that prunes unsafe actions for LLM-driven agents (VirtualHome + real robot); abstract gives no violation or success numbers. — [arXiv 2309.09919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09919)
- HazardArena (Chen et al., arXiv 2604.12447, 2026) wraps a frozen VLA with a VLM judge (Qwen3-VL-32B, refreshed every ~20 control steps, 3.04 s/query) that replaces the action with a "refusal-mode hold action (zero motion, optional gripper-open)"; only judge metrics reported (recall 0.77, FPR 0.10, F1 0.76; property recall 0.00), not the wrapper's effect on policy outcomes. Refusal-augmented fine-tuning (training-time) cut π0 unsafe-success 0.33 → 0.04 in sim and 39.6% → 0.4% on a real Franka, with benign success 46.0% → 47.4%. — [arXiv 2604.12447](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12447)
- RoboSafe (arXiv 2512.21220) is a Gemini-2.5-flash executable-safety-logic guardrail on SafeAgentBench; unguarded agents "average ESR 84.11%". Plan level. — [arXiv 2512.21220](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21220v1)
- The VLA-safety survey (Li et al., arXiv 2604.23775, §5.2) frames all of these as a "dual-loop" architecture — a ~100 Hz "Fast Reflexes" loop (CBFs, kinematic projection, impedance) and a ~1 Hz "Slow Reasoning" loop (VLM monitors, LLM→STL translation) — and states the two structural costs: "excessive latency in the defense pipeline can degrade control frequency, potentially inducing the very collisions it is designed to prevent", and "coercive geometric projections and accumulated corrective actions can gradually push the VLA's trajectory out of its training data distribution", so CBFs' "excessive conservatism often degrades the base model's nominal task success rate". — [arXiv 2604.23775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23775)

### Inferences
- Across AEGIS, PACS, X-Safe and iKinQP-RL the pattern is consistent: geometric violations → ~0; success cost ranges from +17 pp (AEGIS, where the unfiltered policy was crashing) through ≈0 (PACS) to −7/−8 pp (X-Safe) to −66 pp (per-step CBF steering in PACS's sim comparison). The intervention *style* (stop/slow along path, contract toward current pose) matters more than the certificate machinery.
- None of these filters has been evaluated against object-state constraints of the ManiGuard Jar type (upright, not dropped); the only object-state filters with numbers are the latent filter (toppling blocks, spilling) and SafeDiffuser (joint limits).
- All low-level filters need some geometric scene model (obstacle poses, collision checker); the latent filter is the only one that is purely observation-based, and it degrades on OOD objects.

### Gaps
- No shield/filter paper reports a run on π0.5 under LTLf-scored contact-rich tasks; AEGIS's π0.5 results are collision-only on SafeLIBERO.
- SafeDiffuser is a planner (Diffuser), not a wrapper on an imitation policy, so its numbers do not transfer to a frozen VLA.
- ATACOM-inductive-bias and CompliantVLA numbers were not retrievable (abstract only).

## Q2. Runtime monitoring with temporal logic (LTL/STL): scoring versus intervening

### Takeaway
In the manipulation-VLA literature, LTLf/STL monitors are used almost exclusively to *score* (ManiGuard, SafeManip, SafeVLA-Bench, SENTINEL); the only STL monitor that *intervenes* on a learned manipulation policy is STeP (robustness below threshold → switch to MPC or replan), and the classic LTL shield (Alshiekh et al. 2018) has not been applied to a VLA's continuous actions.

### Cited Findings
- ManiGuard (Peng et al., arXiv 2608.17386): LTLf specs compiled to DFAs with Spot before any policy step; the monitor "observes and never intervenes"; "the runtime monitor scores φ at every step but never aborts a rollout". Runtime shielding, action filters and monitor-based rewards "appear only as related work or future directions". — [arXiv 2608.17386 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- SafeManip (Huang, Huynh, Zhang, Elbaum, Kira, Feng; arXiv 2605.12386): LTLf properties compiled to DFA monitors over RoboCasa365 (50 tasks) and LIBERO (40 tasks), with persistence/grace/timeout windows to suppress transient noise, and a "recovery formula that resets them after a violation" (tracking only). "No shield, filter, stopping, or replanning mechanism is tested." Future work: ground predicates from onboard sensors and "study how monitor reliability degrades under perception error". — [arXiv 2605.12386](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12386)
- SafeVLA-Bench (Fan, Xu, Wang, Kong, Sokolsky, Lee; arXiv 2606.00773): post-hoc STL clauses over simulator state on LIBERO and RoboCasa-365; lists "direct use of STL robustness for post-training or test-time guidance" as future work. — [arXiv 2606.00773](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00773v2)
- SENTINEL (Zhan et al., arXiv 2510.12985, 2025): "multi-level formal framework for safety evaluation of foundation model-based embodied agents" at semantic, planning and trajectory levels — evaluation, per ManiGuard's related work. — [cited in ManiGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- STeP (Torshizi, Singh, Mathur, Habib, Du, Tokekar, UMD; arXiv 2607.18580, Jul 2026): "STL monitor evaluates the robustness of the active subtask formula" on z_t = ψ(s_t, o_t); real UR3e with object poses from a single RGB-D capture + SAM + VLM labelling. On threshold crossing, System 1 recalls System 2 to repair the plan or reassigns the subtask from the BC policy (ResNet, 30 demos) to MPC. Switching experiment (30 trials): collision detected in 29, rerouted via MPC in 25, grasp completed in 22; BC alone collided in 28/30; MPC alone failed the grasp in 24/30. Replanning (20 trials, "tight failure margin"): first attempt knocked the stack over in 16/20; 12 of 16 corrected on the second attempt, 14 within three. No numeric threshold is given; no monitor on/off violation counts. — [arXiv 2607.18580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18580)
- Shielding (Alshiekh, Bloem, Ehlers, Könighofer, Niekum, Topcu; AAAI 2018; arXiv 1708.08611): a shield synthesised from a temporal-logic spec either supplies the list of safe actions before the learner chooses or "monitors the chosen action and corrects it only if that action would violate the specification". — [arXiv 1708.08611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611). iKinQP-RL characterises such shields as encoding specs "in temporal logic, though that makes it harder to add new rules at runtime" and does not compare against one. — [arXiv 2409.08233](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08233)
- RoboGuard uses temporal-logic control synthesis at the *plan* level (92% → <2.5% unsafe-plan execution). — [arXiv 2503.07885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07885v1)
- "No Free Checker" (arXiv 2609.09250) classifies temporal-logic scoring as a rule-based full-trajectory verifier and notes that goal predicates "need object pose, which a physics engine reports exactly but perception only estimates, so they are confined to simulation". — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

### Inferences
- The ManiGuard monitor is already a DFA over per-step predicates, so the same automaton could in principle emit an "about-to-violate" edge, but no paper reports doing this for a VLA.
- STeP is the existence proof that STL robustness can gate a learned policy on a real arm; its numbers show replanning retries succeed most of the time (12/16 on the second attempt) but each retry after a knock-over is itself a prior violation.

### Gaps
- No work applies an LTL/STL shield to a VLA's continuous action stream; no work uses an LTLf monitor to trigger retreat or slowing on a VLA.
- STeP's switching ablation lacks a monitor-off violation count, so the monitor's isolated contribution is not quantified.

## Q3. Failure detection for VLAs and imitation policies (SAFE, FAIL-Detect, Sentinel, Hide-and-Seek, AHA, Code-as-Monitor, RC-NF, uncertainty stop rules)

### Takeaway
Failure detectors on VLA internals (SAFE, Hide-and-Seek) reach ROC-AUC/balanced accuracy ≈0.77–0.97 at sub-millisecond cost, detectors from success-only data (FAIL-Detect, Sentinel STAC) reach ≈72–96% balanced accuracy, and all of them stop at raising a flag: none of the detection papers measures the effect of acting on the flag.

### Cited Findings
- SAFE (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2506.09937): one scalar failure score from the VLA's last-layer hidden state (pre-decoding for OpenVLA/π0-FAST, pre-velocity-projection for π0); SAFE-LSTM (≈2.3 M params, +0.73 ms vs π0's 149 ms) or SAFE-MLP; threshold from functional conformal prediction on successful rollouts, α=0.15 (0.05–0.2 "performed similarly"). ROC-AUC (seen/unseen): OpenVLA+LIBERO 72.68/73.47 (MLP); π0-FAST+LIBERO 92.98/84.48 (LSTM); π0+LIBERO 76.98/71.09; π0+SimplerEnv 89.50/84.82; average LSTM 82.26/77.04 vs best baseline cosine-kNN 73.93 unseen. Real π0-FAST Franka: 86.76/64.16; real OpenVLA WidowX 89.11/88.42. Baseline groups: token-uncertainty ≈48–62, sample-consistency ≈60–71 (needs multiple samples; π0-FAST is "about 221% slower to generate 10 samples than 1"), STAC-Single 74.21/73.65, LogpZO 79.67/70.97. On flag: "the task is then aborted or a human monitor takes over control"; retry/recovery "not evaluated". — [arXiv 2506.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09937)
- FAIL-Detect (Xu et al., RSS 2025; arXiv 2503.08558): success-only training; scalar signals over observation embeddings (ResNet/CLIP + robot state, last two observation steps) and actions — logpZO (flow-matching latent-noise norm), RND, CFM, DER, NatPN, SPARC (action smoothness), STAC, PCA-kmeans; functional conformal band at α=0.05. Average best balanced accuracy "∼78%" sim (Robomimic Square/Transport/Can/Toolhang) and "∼72%" hardware (bimanual Franka FoldRedTowel, CleanUpSpill). logpZO top-1 in 10/16 sim and 8/12 hardware cases; compute 0.04 s/step vs STAC 1.45 s ("36–44 times slowdown"); STAC "usually slowest, often later than the average successful trajectory time". No intervention evaluated. — [arXiv 2503.08558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08558)
- Sentinel (Agia et al., CoRL 2024; arXiv 2410.04640): erratic failures via STAC (statistical temporal action consistency between consecutive diffusion-policy sample batches, B=256 PushT / 32 real, horizon 16, execution 4–8; gripper excluded) and task-progression (stall) failures via a VLM on a video of observations (GPT-4o / Claude; GPT-4o mean response 14.0 s). Thresholds at the 95th quantile of 50 sim / 10 real successful rollouts. Close Box: Sentinel TPR 1.00 / TNR 0.87 / acc 0.91; ID detection 5.47 s, OOD 14.25 s. Task-progression failures: "93% TPR whilst incurring only a 7% increase in FPR" (STAC alone 44% TPR). Real Push Chair: TPR 1.00 / TNR 0.90 / 9.60 s, "95% detection accuracy". "Execution stops if either detector raises a warning" but outcomes of stopping are not reported. — [arXiv 2410.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04640)
- Hide-and-Seek (Park et al., arXiv 2605.30834, 2026): contrastive discovery of failure-indicative steps from VLA action embeddings (OpenVLA layer-averaged; π0/π0.5 hidden states before the velocity head) using trajectory-level labels only. bACC seen/unseen: LIBERO-10 OpenVLA 0.852/0.834 vs SAFE-MLP 0.823/0.775; LIBERO-10 π0 0.885/0.892 vs 0.870/0.801; VLABench π0.5 0.856/0.713 vs 0.788/0.641; real CUBE π0.5 0.966/0.914 vs 0.992/0.797; real KITCHEN π0.5 0.968/0.972 vs 0.784/0.878. 0.001 s/step vs 2.343 s for a Qwen3-VL-8B monitor (0.843 vs 0.712 bACC). No intervention experiment in the visible text. — [arXiv 2605.30834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30834)
- AHA (Duan et al., arXiv 2410.00371; ICLR 2025 per the search result): VLM fine-tuned on FailGen perturbations; beats GPT-4o ICL by 10.3% and six models' average by 35.3% on failure reasoning; feeding its feedback into RL reward, TAMP and trajectory generation "raised task success rates by an average of 21.4%". — [arXiv 2410.00371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00371)
- Code-as-Monitor (Zhou et al., CVPR 2025; arXiv 2412.04455): GPT-4o writes subgoal constraints, a segmentation "painter" lifts them to tracked 3D constraint elements (two RGB-D views, CoTracker, end-effector from FK — no simulator state as input), GPT-4o writes boolean monitor code; "if the monitor returns False, the policy execution halts immediately" and the reason is fed to replanning. CLIPort Stack-in-Order under disturbance pp=0.3: 21.67% (no monitor) → 88.33% (CaM) vs DoReMi 76.67%; Sweep Half the Blocks 0.00% → 75.00%; real Pick&Place deformables DoReMi 83.33% vs CaM 96.67%; abstract "28.7% higher success rate and 31.8% lower execution time under severe disturbances". No false-alarm rate reported. — [arXiv 2412.04455](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04455v2)
- RC-NF (Zhou et al., CVPR 2026; arXiv 2603.11106): normalizing flow over robot state and task-relevant object trajectories trained on positive samples only, "response latency under 100 ms", plug-and-play on π0; the abstract reports no detection or success numbers. — [arXiv 2603.11106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11106)
- VLA-Corrector's learned verifier (Song, Qian, Feng, Song; arXiv 2609.06508): GRU over an 8-step window of two RGB views + 8-D proprioception + 7-D executed action; labels from teacher-defined failure events (positive within 10 steps of failure); accuracy 93.09%, precision 61.14%, recall 94.85%, AUROC 0.902, 0.457 ms p50 latency; gate τ=0.40, four consecutive positives, 10-step cooldown; gated trigger precision 79.2%. — [arXiv 2609.06508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06508)
- AEGIS backup reflex (Chen, arXiv 2606.06660, Jun 2026; distinct from VLSA-AEGIS): an activation probe on a weak policy's frozen activations (early-window AUROC 0.764) escalates to a stronger policy; on LIBERO-Spatial recovers 10.1% of failed trajectories vs 4.6% blind escalation and 5.1% random trigger; strong policy active on 38% of steps; no safety metrics. — [arXiv 2606.06660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06660)
- "No Free Checker" (arXiv 2609.09250) adds FIPER (OOD on observation embeddings + action-chunk uncertainty), Tri-Info (entropy/MI), Ward et al. (570 K-param calibrated latent-uncertainty detector, +3.8 points on a real bimanual cable task), and notes the conformal guarantee "holds only under the same distribution" and "weakens for a policy trained against the detector". — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)
- Guardian (arXiv 2512.01946) detects planning and execution errors with VLMs (surfaced by search; not fetched). — [arXiv 2512.01946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01946)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-accuracy signals for a π0.5-class policy are its own hidden states (SAFE/Hide-and-Seek, <1 ms); the cheapest *external* signals with measured accuracy are proprioception-plus-action windows (VLA-Corrector) and success-only density scores on observation embeddings (logpZO).
- Sample-consistency "hesitation" scores (STAC) are the most natural stall cue but are expensive on flow/AR VLAs (10 samples ≈ 3× inference cost) and are the slowest to fire.
- Detection timing is reported as seconds or as relative T-det curves; no paper gives detection lead-time in the ManiGuard sense (steps before an LTLf violation).

### Gaps
- No paper titled "Can VLAs know when they fail" was found (Semantic Scholar rate-limited; arXiv search budget exhausted); closest are SAFE, Hide-and-Seek and the AEGIS probe.
- No detector paper reports what happens to success or violation rates when the flag triggers a stop; Sentinel and SAFE state the stop rule but do not evaluate it.

## Q4. Recovery and retry (recovery policies, retry-from-failure, reset, scripted retreat, stall detection)

### Takeaway
Retry/recovery on top of a frozen VLA has been shown to add roughly +6 to +18 pp success (VLA-Corrector, B2FF, FLARE, AFI), with proprioception-only triggers working but losing about a third to half of the oracle-timed gain; evidence that the retreat/reset motion itself causes new violations is thin, but FLARE's reset skills fail 12–80% of the time and X-Safe shows "just stop" as a fallback loses 22 pp relative to a retreat.

### Cited Findings
- FLARE (Zhao et al., CVPR 2026; arXiv 2608.26645): π0.5 (LoRA) with (i) *retry* via demonstration augmentation — random perturbation segments (≤45°, ≤0.5 m) followed by bridging segments back to the valid pose — and (ii) *reset* via a library of object-centric reset skills (20 demos each, augmented to 500) selected by an online MLLM monitor. Simulation (9 RoboMimic tasks, mean): π0.5 72.2% → FLARE 84.0% (Coffee_D0 82→96, Coffee_D1 56→78, Threading_D0 42→72, ThreePiece_D1 46→58). Four-task ablation: w/o reset (retry-only) 70.0%, reset-only 65.5%, full 73.5%, Ours-Oracle (human instructions) 80.5%. Real Piper arm (40 trials): Stack Three Blocks 62.5% → 75.0%; Insert U-block 45.0% → 55.0%. Reset-skill success: lid 84%, pod 24%, T-block 88%, U-block 20%. No online monitor accuracy, false-trigger rate, or new-failure measurement. — [arXiv 2608.26645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26645)
- B2FF, "Back to the Familiar Future" (Shin et al., arXiv 2606.09258, 2026): frozen UD-VLA; a learned detector over *proprioceptive history* (trigger pool: trajectory deviation, gripper state, end-effector stall, task-progress consistency, motion consistency, state novelty, OR-combined; no camera) triggers recovery by selecting a pre-imagined future-image milestone as the visual goal for 5 chunks. Failure-injected LIBERO average: UD-VLA 56.3% → B2FF 74.0% with oracle-timed trigger → 64.5% with the online proprioceptive trigger; standard LIBERO 91.3 → 93.7 / 92.0. Failed-state re-planning baseline +12.3 pp below B2FF (LIBERO-Object: replanning 54.2, UD-VLA 52.5, B2FF 69.3). Real: 61.1% over 90 recovery trials. No scripted retreat baseline. — [arXiv 2606.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09258)
- VLA-Corrector (arXiv 2609.06508): frozen OpenPI pi05_libero; on trigger the pending chunk is discarded and the same policy is re-prompted with "Immediate recovery: " + a stage-specific directive, then the original instruction is restored. Standard LIBERO: No Assistance 70.9% (table 70.7%) → Rule+Prompt 82.6% → Learned+Prompt 80.2%; LIBERO-Plus difficulty 4: 73.7 → 79.7 / 79.0; difficulty 5: 66.7 → 73.6 / 72.6; real (target-object shift, 40 trials/task) 82.5% → 88.3%. Recovery success 77.0% (rule) / 75.0% (learned). Only baseline is No Assistance; no retry/reset/timeout baselines; new failures not reported. — [arXiv 2609.06508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06508)
- Affordance Field Intervention (Xu et al., arXiv 2512.07472, Dec 2025): "detects memory traps through proprioception" and "repositions the robot to recent high-affordance regions", anchoring VLA actions with affordance waypoints; π0 and π0.5; "average improvement of 23.5%" on real OOD scenarios and "20.2% on LIBERO-Pro" (abstract; absolute rates not retrieved). The VLA-safety survey describes it as rolling back "to a safe state upon detecting a physical stall". — [arXiv 2512.07472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07472); [survey §5.2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23775)
- RePO-VLA (Liufu et al., arXiv 2605.09410): trains π0.5 on success/failure/recovery rollouts with a value token; at inference "without online failure detectors or heuristic retries". Only retry evidence: "The heuristic retry baseline remains weaker than value guidance even when trained with the same recovery data" — no number. Real adversarial average π0.5 20.0 → 75 (4×). — [arXiv 2605.09410](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09410)
- Kintsugi-VLA (Snegirev et al., arXiv 2609.31048): recovery data from restored failed sim states; SmolVLA cube task; nominal-only recovery 12.5% → 34.6% (Protocol A); defines a deviation time as the earlier of leaving a nominal progress corridor and a progress plateau, signals unspecified. — [arXiv 2609.31048](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31048)
- FailSafe (Lin et al., arXiv 2510.01642): generates failure cases + executable recovery actions in sim and fine-tunes LLaVA-OneVision-7B as a recovery model (search-level only; numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 2510.01642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01642)
- Code-as-Monitor's halt-and-replan loop: 21.67% → 88.33% on disturbed Stack-in-Order, 0 → 75% on Sweep. — [arXiv 2412.04455](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04455v2)
- STeP retries: 12/16 knocked-over stacks corrected on the second attempt, 14/16 within three (VLM-MPC-Cost: 7/18 and 9/18). — [arXiv 2607.18580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18580)
- Fallback-motion evidence: X-Safe's Chebyshev-centre retreat gives 66.4% success vs 44% with a stopping controller, both at ≈0 collisions. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22278). iKinQP-RL falls back to one of three fixed retreat positions chosen by table region; 0% collisions, success 83–100%. — [arXiv 2409.08233](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08233)
- Human-gated retries: "No Free Checker" lists HG-DAgger, ThriftyDAgger (budget-aware novelty/risk gating), Sirius-Fleet (world-model failure prediction with thresholds "relaxed as policies improve"), HIL-SERL; it states an intervention verifier "must request help early enough to avoid failure but selectively enough to limit human effort" and gives few quantitative results. — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

### Inferences
- The simplest demonstrated retry that needs no training is VLA-Corrector's "discard chunk, re-prompt the same frozen π0.5 with a recovery directive" (+11.7 pp on LIBERO with a rule trigger).
- The oracle-vs-proxy trigger gap in B2FF (74.0 vs 64.5, base 56.3) means a proprioception-only trigger kept ≈46% of the oracle gain.
- FLARE's reset-skill success rates (20–88%) are the only quantitative hint that recovery motions themselves fail often; no paper scores recovery motions against safety monitors.

### Gaps
- No paper measures violations *caused by* retreat or reset motions under temporal-logic monitors.
- No scripted "retreat-then-retry" baseline with numbers on a VLA was found; the closest are STeP's replanning counts and VLA-Corrector's rule trigger.
- Stall detection from proprioception is used (AFI, B2FF, Kintsugi) but its detection accuracy is not reported separately.

## Q5. ManiGuard itself, its related work, and other manipulation safety benchmarks

### Takeaway
ManiGuard tests only data-side interventions (SFT on a safety-annotated suite, demo scaling, specification format) and explicitly defers runtime shielding to future work; for π0.5-SFT on Jar it reports 21/4/27/26 (safe-success / unsafe-success / safe-unsuccess / unsafe-unsuccess) in-distribution, i.e. SSR 26.92%, and the concurrent benchmarks (SafeManip, SafeVLA-Bench, HazardArena, LIBERO-Safety) likewise evaluate only prompting or post-training, never a runtime layer.

### Cited Findings
**ManiGuard (Peng et al., arXiv 2608.17386, Aug 2026; NU-IDEAS-Lab; BEHAVIOR-1K/OmniGibson)**
- 200 base tasks × (1 ID + 4 single-axis OOD: target appearance, language, location, environment) = 1,000 scenarios; LTLf monitors compiled with Spot; predicates grounded in "simulator state, including contacts, object poses, articulation states, and particle information"; "no learned classifier or language model is invoked during labeling"; hardware verdicts are operator-scored. — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386); [repo](https://github.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard)
- Jar family: 26 base tasks; clause "(jar_on_support) U (jar_closed) & G(!jar_dropped) & G(jar_upright)" (weak until); upright threshold 30° tilt; dropped = AABB below floor plane plus margin; horizon 2,000 steps at 20 Hz (1.5× longest scripted demo); policy re-infers after executing 8 actions of each chunk; rollout ends when the goal predicate holds for ten consecutive steps; violations only count after first whole-arm contact with a task object ("engagement"). — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- π0.5-SFT Jar ID (Table 13): 21 / 4 / 27 / 26 → SSR 26.92%; OOD pooled 20 / 19 / 155 / 118 → SSR 6.41%; per-axis SSR Target 16.67%, Language 6.41%, Location 0.00%, Environment 2.56%. π0-SFT Jar ID 11/8/36/23 (SSR 14.10%); GR00T N1.6-SFT 0/4/49/25; SmolVLA-SFT 0/1/27/50; zero-shot π0.5 0/2/34/42. Time-to-violation (Table 25, Jar): "14% (30) ID; 18% (137) OOD". — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Overall π0.5: zero-shot Success 1.83 / SSR 0.33 / Eng. 41.33; SFT (8,000 demos, 40/task) Success 31.83 / Safe 82.50 / SSR 29.83 / Succ.&Unsafe 2.00 / Unsucc.&Safe 52.67 / Eng. 83.67 / Eng.&Safe 66.17; "engaged violation rate falls from 54% to 21%" (intro; table 20.9); violation rate "22.3 → 17.5". π0: SSR 0.17 → 27.00; SmolVLA 0.00 → 7.50; GR00T N1.6 SSR 15.50. — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Interventions tested: SFT on the safety-annotated suite; demonstration scaling (8/20/32/40 demos on Clutter and Cabinet — Cabinet SSR "stays near zero and does not improve monotonically"); specification format (none / NL / LTLf). Jar, Table 27 (π0.5-SFT SSR): none 26.92%, NL 24.36%, LTLf 14.10% (engagement 98.72 / 96.15 / 96.15); a separate "Safe‖Eng" Jar metric reads none 61.03, NL 73.52 (+12.50), LTLf 69.24 (+8.22). "Not tested here: runtime shields, action filters, and monitor-based rewards." — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Idle/stall handling: "no idle timeout or stall penalty"; a robot that never engages is "vacuous-safe"; the safe-unsuccess class is "inflated by disengagement", hence engagement is reported alongside safety. — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Future directions: specification-aware data collection, monitor costs/rewards for policy optimisation, prioritising counterexamples, "runtime shielding", and safety-data composition. Limitations: "primarily simulation-based"; "safe" only means no encoded spec was violated. — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Related work as characterised by ManiGuard: SafeLIBERO, RedVLA (arXiv 2604.22591), VLA-Arena (arXiv 2512.22539) "rely mainly on per-state predicates"; HazardArena introduces safe/unsafe pairs and "training-free mitigation"; SafeManip applies LTLf to RoboCasa; OopsieVerse derives safety from damage signals; SENTINEL (arXiv 2510.12985) evaluates temporal-logic safety at three levels; ISBench/EARBench/ASIMOV judge via text or VLM. Runtime-verification refs: Bauer et al. (Boolean LTL monitors), Deshmukh et al. (STL robustness), Wu et al. "Do what you say" (arXiv 2510.16281). — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- Repo: LTL utilities, safety monitor, object states (Dropped, Upright), websocket policy server, SFT drivers for openpi / GR00T / SmolVLA; "no runtime shield, filter, intervention, or retry logic"; RL "under development". — [GitHub](https://github.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard)

**Concurrent / related benchmarks and the interventions they evaluated**
- SafeVLA-Bench (arXiv 2606.00773): LIBERO four-suite means (SR / Safety / SBU / VSI): π0.5 96.6 / 77.6 / 21.4 / 0.147; π0 93.9 / 77.5 / 19.2; OpenVLA-7B 69.4 / 77.4 / 10.8; VLA-Adapter 97.6 / 82.0 / 17.0; RoboCasa-365 π0.5 42.3 / 51.2 / 19.8 / 0.195; "fifteen policies above 90% SR still have 18–28% unsafe rollouts". Thresholds (200 N contact, 5 mm bystander displacement, 15° held-object tilt, 87 N·m) are "external anchors, not hardware certification thresholds". — [arXiv 2606.00773](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00773v2)
- SafeManip (arXiv 2605.12386): unsafe-rollout share 29–42% on RoboCasa365 and 3–27% on LIBERO; failed episodes carry 1.7–7× the per-trigger risk of successful ones; OpenVLA has the highest collision risk (0.128 per opportunity); "π0 and π0.5 concentrate their risk on containment"; per-trigger risk roughly doubles from atomic to long-horizon (≈0.03 → 0.075). - LIBERO-Safety (Cui et al., arXiv 2606.23686): 19,664 collision-free demos; eight VLAs; "high-diversity training produced safer trajectories, but task success remained limited" (no rates in abstract). — [arXiv 2606.23686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23686)
- OopsieVerse / DAMAGESIM (Balaji, Bahety, …, Martín-Martín; arXiv 2606.31993): continuous damage signals (mechanical/thermal/fluid) in OmniGibson and RoboCasa; uses include damage-conditioned IL/RL; no rates in abstract. — [arXiv 2606.31993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31993)
- SafeStage (arXiv 2609.21223): 97 risk scenarios before/during/after execution; no mitigation. — [arXiv 2609.21223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21223)
- SafeAgentBench (Yin et al., arXiv 2412.13178): 750 planning tasks, best baseline "only a 10% rejection rate" for detailed hazardous tasks — plan level. — [arXiv 2412.13178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13178)
- Safety-CHORES (SafeVLA): AI2THOR, 150k scenes; constrained-RL post-training only. — [SafeVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480)
- SafeMimic (Bahety et al., 2025) appears only as a bibliography entry in OopsieVerse, described as safe autonomous human-to-robot imitation for mobile manipulation; it is not a safety benchmark. — [OopsieVerse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31993)
- "No Free Checker" summarises the benchmark picture as "policies that complete more tasks are not safer". — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

### Inferences
- From ManiGuard's Jar ID counts, the violation rate for π0.5-SFT is (4+26)/78 = 38.5% and the safe-but-unsuccessful share is 27/78 = 34.6%; pooled OOD violation is (19+118)/312 = 43.9%. These are my arithmetic on the published counts.
- Because ManiGuard neither penalises idling nor aborts on violation, a supervisor that stalls to stay safe is scored "safe-unsuccess", and a supervisor that retries after a violation cannot recover the safety verdict for that episode.
- Across ManiGuard, SafeManip, SafeVLA-Bench and HazardArena, the only evaluated mitigations are data-side (SFT, DPO, refusal fine-tuning) or prompting; prompting is the one intervention that consistently loses more success than it saves in violations.

### Gaps
- ManiGuard's Jar per-axis violation counts beyond SSR, and its physical-platform Jar numbers, were not in the fetched text.
- The "Safe‖Eng" vs SSR discrepancy for the Jar specification-format study is unresolved in the fetched text (different metrics, opposite direction).

## Q6. Has anyone tuned a supervisor's parameters (patience, retreat height, speed limits) by black-box/evolutionary search, or reported a completion-vs-violation trade-off curve?

### Takeaway
No paper was found that tunes a runtime supervisor over a learned manipulation policy or VLA by evolutionary or other black-box search; the only trade-off evidence is hand-swept single parameters (iKinQP-RL's n, SQRL's ε_risk, SAFE/Sentinel's conformal α) and one Bayesian-optimisation paper for CBF parameters on toy control tasks.

### Cited Findings
- iKinQP-RL swept the number of executed corrected points n ∈ {6,7,8}: success 84/88/93%, episode time 30.65/24.88/21.88 s, collisions 0% throughout; n=7 chosen "as the best balance"; collisions appeared at higher n in a 20-episode pre-sweep. — [arXiv 2409.08233](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08233)
- Latent safety filter paper: SQRL's "manually tuned" ε_risk gives safe-success/violation/incompletion of 68/28/4 at 0.1 and 8/22/70 at 0.05 — tightening the threshold traded 60 pp of completion for 6 pp of violations; LatentSafe's own ε (0.4 sim, 0.3 hardware) has "no stated selection method". — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00935)
- X-Safe: parameters fixed (δ=0.005, N_iter=12, ε=0.02, M=1024); "ε had the least effect"; fallback ablation stop vs retreat 44% vs 66.4%. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22278)
- AEGIS/VLSA: α(h)=10h, k=10, "no sweep … is reported", "no quantitative success-versus-safety analysis". — [arXiv 2512.11891](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11891)
- SAFE: α=0.15, 0.05–0.2 "performed similarly"; balanced-accuracy vs detection-time curves across α (Fig. 4) are the trade-off curve, for detection only. — [arXiv 2506.09937](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09937). Sentinel: 95th-quantile threshold from 50/10 successful rollouts. — [arXiv 2410.04640](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04640v2). FAIL-Detect: α=0.05. — [arXiv 2503.08558](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08558v3)
- VLA-Corrector: τ=0.40, four consecutive positive windows, 10-step cooldown, stage-confidence 0.9625 — hand set. — [arXiv 2609.06508](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06508)
- HazardArena judge: refresh every ~20 steps, temperature 0, attempt margin 0.10 m — hand set. — [arXiv 2604.12447](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12447)
- PG-VP (Son, Zhang; arXiv 2610.07558, Oct 2026) reports one operating point for a perception-side wrapper on a frozen navigation VLA: low-risk guidance 84.9%/83.2% at a success cost of 6.8/7.9 points. — [arXiv 2610.07558](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22plug-and-play%22+AND+abs:safety+AND+abs:vision-language-action&max_results=8)
- "Optimal Parameter Adaptation for Safety-Critical Control via Safe Barrier Bayesian Optimization" (Wang et al., arXiv 2503.19349, Mar 2025) tunes CBF configurable parameters by safe BO under feasibility and safety constraints — evaluated on swing-up and adaptive cruise control, not manipulation, no numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2503.19349](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22Bayesian+optimization%22+AND+abs:%28%22control+barrier%22+OR+%22safety+filter%22+OR+%22safe+set%22%29+AND+abs:%28tuning+OR+parameters%29&max_results=15)
- "Deliberate Practice: Learning Robot Skills under a Budget" (arXiv 2608.13415, 2026) uses CMA-ES to tune impedance-controller parameters "because its objective is only available through rollouts" — controller gains, not a safety supervisor. — [arXiv 2608.13415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13415)
- "CMA-ES for Safe Optimization" (arXiv 2405.10534) exists; one traffic-control paper says CMA-ES's "erratic exploration makes it less suitable for safety-critical domains". — [arXiv 2405.10534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10534); [arXiv 1912.11023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.11023)
- An arXiv-API query for shield + (evolutionary | genetic | evolution strategy) + safety returned a single HVAC paper whose "deterministic safety shield" is not itself evolved. — [arXiv API result](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:shield+AND+abs:%28evolutionary+OR+%22genetic+algorithm%22+OR+%22evolution+strategy%22%29+AND+abs:%28robot+OR+%22reinforcement+learning%22%29+AND+abs:safety&max_results=15)
- The VLA-safety survey frames a "safety-performance trade-off characterized by the 'over-refusal' phenomenon" qualitatively, with no curves. — [arXiv 2604.23775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23775)
- "No Free Checker" calls ThriftyDAgger's intervention budget and Sirius-Fleet's relaxed thresholds "explicit trade-off knobs" and frames threshold choice as "not a solved setting". — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

### Inferences
- The literature is silent on evolved or black-box-tuned supervisors for VLAs: every runtime layer above fixes its thresholds by hand or by a conformal quantile on successful rollouts, and the only multi-point trade-off curves are one-dimensional sweeps (n in iKinQP-RL; ε_risk in SQRL).
- Conformal calibration on successful rollouts is the de-facto "principled" threshold rule, and it fixes the false-positive rate rather than optimising completion vs violation.

### Gaps
- Semantic Scholar was rate-limited and the web-search budget exhausted, so coverage of non-arXiv venues (e.g. GECCO, ALIFE) for evolved shields is incomplete.

## Q7. Oracle versus proxy gating: privileged simulator signals first, robot-observable proxies second

### Takeaway
Few papers run the same safety layer with both privileged and observable signals; where they do, the proxy keeps most but not all of the benefit (B2FF: oracle-timed trigger 74.0% vs proprioceptive trigger 64.5% vs none 56.3%; FLARE: oracle instructions 80.5% vs MLLM monitor 73.5%; latent vs privileged Dubins filter 96% vs 98.4% safe), and the benchmark papers acknowledge their monitors are privileged and hardware verdicts are coarser.

### Cited Findings
- B2FF: "main failure-injected results use controlled timing aligned with the injected perturbation, not the online trigger"; oracle-timed 74.0%, online proprioceptive trigger 64.5%, base 56.3%. — [arXiv 2606.09258](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09258)
- FLARE: Ours-Oracle (human instructions) 80.5% vs full FLARE 73.5% on the four-task ablation. — [arXiv 2608.26645](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26645)
- Latent safety filter: Dubins car monitor F1 0.984 (latent) vs 0.987 (privileged); safety policy 96% vs 98.4% of states; manipulation compared only against SQRL. — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00935)
- VLA-Corrector: Rule+Prompt 82.6% vs Learned-Binary+Prompt 80.2% (rule triggers derived from teacher-defined failure events; the learned verifier uses RGB+proprio+action). — [arXiv 2609.06508](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06508)
- X-Safe hardware: perception-derived object poses with 2 mm inflation gave 0 collisions in 20 runs; sim collision 0.04% vs hardware 0%. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22278)
- SafeVLA-Bench hardware pilot (SmolVLA on SO-101, 40 rollouts): success 26/40; non_target_max_disp violated 5/40; stable_grasp violated 6/28 lifts; SBU 4/40; "the video criteria are coarser than the simulator's 5 mm and 2 cm"; no VSI/robustness computed; force and torque families "not scored because the arm lacks torque or wrist force-torque sensing". — [arXiv 2606.00773](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00773v2)
- ManiGuard: monitors use simulator contacts/poses; "physical verdicts are scored by the operator rather than by the automaton monitor". — [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386)
- SafeManip: "currently grounds its predicates using privileged access to object poses, contact lists"; future work to ground from onboard sensors and study degradation under perception error. — [arXiv 2605.12386](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12386)
- Sentinel needs only the policy's own action samples (STAC) plus images for the VLM — fully deployable. — [arXiv 2410.04640](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04640v2). SAFE and Hide-and-Seek need only VLA hidden states. — [arXiv 2506.09937](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09937); [arXiv 2605.30834](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30834)
- "No Free Checker": goal predicates are "confined to simulation"; ConRFT replaces the predicate with a binary classifier trained on human labels; model-intrinsic signals are "the cheapest" but "a familiar-looking failure can still score highly". — [arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250v1)

### Inferences
- The two direct oracle→proxy comparisons on VLAs (B2FF, FLARE) both show a 7–10 pp success gap, which is the right order of magnitude to budget for when a privileged trigger is replaced by a proprioceptive or VLM trigger.
- Object-state predicates of the Jar type (upright, dropped) are the ones every benchmark says are hardest to proxy on hardware; wrist F/T and grasp-stability proxies are what SafeVLA-Bench's hardware pilot could and could not score.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a privileged-vs-proxy comparison for a *safety* gate on manipulation (the latent-filter comparison is on a Dubins car).

## Simplest things that worked (ranked by cost of signal and mechanism, with the measured effect)

### Takeaway
The cheapest demonstrated effects come from (1) slowing or stopping along the policy's own path, (2) re-prompting the frozen policy after a rule-based trigger, and (3) a proprioception-triggered reposition; everything that steers the arm away or needs a world model costs either success or a heavy stack.

### Cited Findings (ranked)
1. **Slow/stop along the commanded path, never steer** — PACS: real-robot success 0.79 → 0.80, violation fraction 0.56 → 0.00, incl. SmolVLA 0.77/0.41 → 0.80/0.00; the per-step CBF alternative collapsed success to 0.04 (sim) and 0.43 (real). Needs joint state + object positions with error bounds. — [arXiv 2511.06385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06385)
2. **Rule-triggered "discard chunk, re-prompt the same frozen π0.5 with a recovery directive"** — VLA-Corrector: LIBERO 70.9% → 82.6% (rule) / 80.2% (learned); real +5.8 pp. No training of the policy; trigger needs proprio + action history (+ RGB for the learned variant). — [arXiv 2609.06508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06508)
3. **Proprioception-detected stall → reposition to a recent high-affordance pose** — AFI: +23.5% real OOD, +20.2% LIBERO-Pro on π0/π0.5. — [arXiv 2512.07472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07472)
4. **Proprioceptive OR-rule trigger (deviation, gripper, EE stall, progress, novelty) → recovery toward a pre-imagined goal** — B2FF: 56.3% → 64.5% with the online trigger (74.0% oracle). — [arXiv 2606.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09258)
5. **Retreat rather than stop as the fallback** — X-Safe: 66.4% vs 44% success at ≈0 collisions; iKinQP-RL: three fixed retreat poses, 0% collisions. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22278); [arXiv 2409.08233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08233)
6. **Sampling-based contraction of the action toward the current configuration** — X-Safe: collision 31.3% → 0.04% for −7 pp success (sim), 100% → 0% for −8 pp (hardware). Needs object poses + collision checker. — [arXiv 2606.22278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22278)
7. **CBF-QP on the VLA's velocity command with perceived obstacle** — AEGIS: π0.5 collision-avoidance 18.7% → 77.9%, success 50.9% → 68.1%. Needs VLM + detector + depth pipeline. — [arXiv 2512.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11891)
8. **Halt-and-replan from a code monitor** — Code-as-Monitor: 21.67% → 88.33% under disturbance, but needs GPT-4o + segmentation + tracking. — [arXiv 2412.04455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04455)
9. **Retry via demonstration augmentation (training-time)** — FLARE retry-only 70.0% vs full 73.5% vs base π0.5 (9-task mean 72.2 → 84.0 full). — [arXiv 2608.26645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26645)
10. **Latent (observation-only) safety filter** — −63.6% violations on a weak policy, no gain on a strong one; fails OOD. — [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)
11. **Detection-only stop rules** (SAFE +0.73 ms, Hide-and-Seek 0.001 s/step, logpZO 0.04 s/step, STAC 1.45 s/step) — accurate, but no paper measured the outcome of stopping. — [arXiv 2506.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09937); [arXiv 2605.30834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30834); [arXiv 2503.08558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08558)
12. **What did not work cheaply**: safety prompting (SafeManip: success 43.9 → 26.4 → 6.9% for violations 71.8 → 69.4 → 65.1%; ManiGuard Jar SSR 26.92 → 24.36 (NL) → 14.10 (LTLf)); VLM judges at 3–14 s latency (HazardArena, Sentinel's GPT-4o); per-step CBF steering. — [arXiv 2605.12386](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12386); [arXiv 2608.17386](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386); [arXiv 2604.12447](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12447); [arXiv 2410.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04640)

### Inferences
- The three cheapest wins (1–3) all share one property: they never generate a new motion the policy has not seen — they slow the policy's own motion, re-prompt the policy, or move back to a pose the policy recently occupied.
- Execution slowing after contact specifically has only indirect evidence (PACS's slowing-on-path; CompliantVLA's impedance modulation and an uncertainty-aware shared-autonomy system that "slows down automatically when skill uncertainty is high" report no isolating numbers; ISO/TS 15066 speed-and-separation implementations are human-safety controllers with no productivity numbers in their abstracts). — [arXiv 2601.15541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15541); [arXiv 2312.02488](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22slow+down%22+AND+abs:uncertainty+AND+abs:%28%22diffusion+policy%22+OR+%22imitation+learning%22%29+AND+abs:robot&max_results=8); [arXiv 1904.07379](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22speed+and+separation+monitoring%22+AND+abs:robot&max_results=6)

### Gaps
- No measured effect exists for "slow down after contact" on a VLA's tipping/dropping violations; no evolved or black-box-tuned supervisor on any VLA; no retreat-and-retry baseline scored under temporal-logic monitors.
