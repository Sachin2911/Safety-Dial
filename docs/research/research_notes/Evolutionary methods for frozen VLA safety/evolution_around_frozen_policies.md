# Evolutionary and black-box optimisation around frozen pretrained policies and foundation models (2022 to October 2026)

Research notes, 23 October 2026. Scope: evolution strategies, genetic algorithms and
neuroevolution used to adapt, wrap or steer frozen pretrained policies and large models,
with robot manipulation (diffusion / flow / VLA policies) as the focus. Older classics are
marked as background.

How these notes were gathered, and what that limits. The session's web-search budget was
exhausted before this task started, so every finding below comes from directly fetched
primary pages (arXiv abstract and full-text HTML pages, conference accepted-paper lists,
one DOI). DBLP and the ACM Digital Library blocked automated access, so GECCO proceedings
were read from the conference websites' accepted-paper lists (main tracks only; the
Companion volumes were not reachable). Consequences: (1) coverage of arXiv postings after
June 2026 is weak, since nothing could be discovered by search; (2) "no example found"
below means none in the papers fetched, their related-work sections, or the GECCO 2023 to
2026 and EvoStar 2025 to 2026 accepted lists, not a full database sweep; (3) ALIFE, PPSN,
CoRL, RSS and ICRA programmes were not scanned. A follow-up search pass is recommended
before any "first to do X" claim is written into a paper.

## Key question 1: Evolution strategies as a fine-tuning method for large models (budgets, claims, any VLA or diffusion/flow policy case)

### Takeaway
By 2025 to 2026, ES fine-tunes billion-parameter LLMs end to end (population 30, 500 to
1,000 iterations) and competes with GRPO on reasoning tasks, and low-rank ES (EGGROLL,
ESSA) makes it cheap enough to run at hyperscale. No fetched paper applies any of this to
a VLA or to a diffusion / flow robot policy; the only ES-trained control policies in this
line are small MLPs trained from scratch on Brax/Kinetix.

### Cited Findings
- "Evolution Strategies at Scale: LLM Fine-Tuning Beyond Reinforcement Learning" (Qiu, Gan, Hayes, Liang, Xu, Dailey, Meyerson, Hodjat, Miikkulainen; arXiv 2509.24372, v1 29 Sep 2025, v3 14 Jul 2026, listed as ICML 2026). Claims the first full-parameter ES fine-tuning of LLMs at the billion-parameter scale without dimensionality reduction, with better tolerance of long-horizon and sparse rewards, less reward hacking and more stable training than RL — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24372)
- Same paper, configuration: a simplified OpenAI-ES / NES variant with fixed-covariance Gaussian noise, no rank transform, no mirrored sampling, no Adam; population N = 30 for all main runs (32 for Mini-Sudoku, 50 for ARC-AGI); sigma = 0.001, alpha = 5e-4 (Countdown); 500 iterations (Countdown), 1,000 (conciseness), 2,500 (Mini-Sudoku), 1,500 (ARC-AGI); models Qwen-2.5 0.5B to 7B Instruct, Llama-3.2 1B/3B, Llama-3.1 8B, Qwen-2.5-14B on ARC-AGI; only random seeds and scalar rewards are exchanged between machines, perturbations are regenerated from seeds layer by layer so each worker needs one extra layer-sized tensor — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372)
- Same paper, results: Countdown, Qwen-2.5-7B row reads 31.2 (base), RL columns 52.8 to 57.5, ES 66.8; Llama-3.1-8B base 8.1, RL 42.8 to 51.3, ES 61.2 (column-to-method mapping partially ambiguous in the HTML extraction, so treat the RL figures as a range). Conciseness: ES reward 0.889 +/- 0.004 at KL 0.274 versus best GRPO 0.911 +/- 0.038 at KL 1.591, with GRPO's run-to-run standard deviation about 15.5x higher. ARC-AGI 0.2% to 29.5%; Sudoku 2.5% to 69.5%. Cost analysis (Appendix A.8) is in FLOPs only: 2PL per ES sample versus 8PL per GRPO sample; no GPU-hours reported — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372)
- Same paper, prior ES / zeroth-order work it cites: BBT (ICML 2022) and BBTv2 (EMNLP 2022); Toledano-Lopez et al. 2022 "Fine-tuning mT5-based transformer via CMA-ES for sentiment analysis" (IberLEF 2022, CEUR vol. 3202); Zhao, Wang and Yang 2023 "Genetic prompt search via exploiting language model probabilities" (IJCAI-23); the surveys Wang et al. 2025 (Research 8, 0646) and Wu et al. 2025 (IEEE TEVC 29(2)). No robotics, control, diffusion or flow policy is mentioned anywhere in the paper — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372)
- "Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale" (EGGROLL: Evolution Guided General Optimisation via Low-rank Learning; Sarkar, Fellows, Duque, ..., Whiteson, Foerster; 20 authors; arXiv 2511.16652, v1 20 Nov 2025, v2 16 Feb 2026). Each perturbation is a rank-r matrix (works at r = 1; converges to full ES at O(1/r)); up to ~100x training speed-up for billion-parameter models at large populations, 91% of pure batch-inference throughput — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16652)
- EGGROLL numbers: Countdown on RWKV-7 1.5B, one GPU, 1,024 parallel generations per GPU, 618 updates, validation accuracy 35% versus GRPO 23% (64 generations, 915 updates); GSM8K on RWKV-7 7B, 8 GPUs, 8,192 parallel generations, 260 updates; DeepScaleR on RWKV-7 14B, 32 GPUs, 12 h, AIME24 13% to 30%, AIME25 7% to 33%, HMMT25 11% to 13%; pure int8 pretraining of a 6-layer character LM with populations from 2 to 2^20 (largest costs about 180x the backprop baseline's GPU-hours); a population of 2 (MeZO-like) is markedly worse. Tabula-rasa RL: 3-layer 256-unit MLP policies on 16 Navix / Craftax / Brax / Kinetix / Jumanji environments, 10 seeds, competitive with OpenES on 7, better on 7, worse on 2. No robotics or pretrained-policy adaptation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652)
- EGGROLL's related work lists ES on adapters: Jin, Liu and Tan 2024, "Derivative-free optimization for low-rank adaptation in large language models", IEEE/ACM TASLP 32, 4607-4616 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASLP.2024.3477330)); Korotyshova et al. 2025 ESSA (below); Malladi et al. 2023 MeZO; Zhang et al. 2024 "Revisiting zeroth-order optimization for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning: a benchmark" (arXiv 2402.11592); Yu et al. 2025 "Zeroth-order fine-tuning of LLMs in random subspaces" (ICCV 2025); and a GECCO item, Garbus and Pollack 2025, "Low rank factorizations are indirect encodings for deep neuroevolution", GECCO '25 Companion pp. 2371-2379 — [arXiv HTML, references](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652)
- "ESSA: Evolutionary Strategies for Scalable Alignment" (Korotyshova, Shaposhnikov, Malakhov, Khokhulin, Surnachev, Ovcharenko, Bredis, Gorbatovski, Sinii, Gavrilov; arXiv 2507.04453, v1 6 Jul 2025, v4 9 Sep 2026). After a short SFT warm-start, ES optimises only the singular values of LoRA factors; inference-only so works with INT4/INT8 weights; matches or exceeds LoRA-GRPO, beats Online DPO and PPO on GSM8K, reaches a fixed MATH500 accuracy up to 7.8x faster than LoRA-GRPO on Qwen2.5-32B with 128 GPUs; "practical even for 72B-parameter models". Population size and evaluation counts not on the abstract page — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04453)
- Background, black-box tuning of a frozen LM in a random subspace: "Black-Box Tuning for Language-Model-as-a-Service" (Sun, Shao, Qian, Huang, Qiu; ICML 2022; arXiv 2201.03514). CMA-ES over a d = 500 random projection of a 50-token continuous prompt (full space 50 x 1024) on RoBERTa-large, population 20, budget 8,000 inference calls; 16-shot average over 7 tasks: black-box tuning 73.01, prompt tuning 63.46, full model tuning 78.88 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03514); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03514)
- Background, zeroth-order gradient estimate: "Fine-Tuning Language Models with Just Forward Passes" (MeZO; Malladi, Gao, Nichani, Damian, Lee, Chen, Arora; NeurIPS 2023 oral; arXiv 2305.17333). Up to 12x memory reduction, trains a 30B model on one A100-80GB where backprop fits 2.7B, scales to 66B, works with LoRA and prefix tuning and non-differentiable objectives (accuracy, F1) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)
- Background, evolution over a frozen model's mixing coefficients: "Evolutionary Optimization of Model Merging Recipes" (Akiba, Shing, Tang, Sun, Ha; arXiv 2403.13187; Nature Machine Intelligence, 27 Jan 2025). Parameter-space merge: CMA-ES (Optuna defaults, init 0.5, sigma 1/6) over about 6 TIES/DARE parameters, 1,000 trials, fitness = accuracy on 1,069 translated GSM8K items; MGSM-JA rises from 30.0 (best source 7B model) to 52.0, and 55.2 with the additional data-flow-space search (CMA-ES in EvoJAX, population 128, 100 generations, about 12,800 evaluations, over a 2^192 binary path and a 64x64 scaling matrix) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13187); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187)
- Gradient-based RL fine-tuning of flow / diffusion policies that an ES paper on a VLA would have to beat (what reviewers know): DPPO (Ren et al.; arXiv 2409.00588) fine-tunes diffusion policies with policy gradients over the denoising chain, with sim-to-real on a multi-stage manipulation task — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00588); ReinFlow (Zhang, Yu, Su, Wang; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2505.22094) injects learnable noise into the flow path and reports +135% reward on locomotion and +40% success on manipulation at 1 to 4 denoising steps, 83% less wall-time than DPPO — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22094); pi_RL (below, Key question 2) does the same for pi0 and pi0.5.

### Inferences
- The ES-for-LLM line (Qiu et al., EGGROLL, ESSA) is entirely text-domain; its budgets are 10^4 to 10^6 full rollouts (30 x 500 = 15,000 population evaluations at minimum in Qiu et al., each evaluating a batch of prompts; 10^5 to 10^6 generations in EGGROLL). At 1,000 to 2,000 simulator episodes, full-parameter ES over a 3B VLA is out of reach by two to three orders of magnitude; ESSA-style search over LoRA singular values is the only ES variant whose dimensionality is plausibly in the hundreds, and even BBT needed 8,000 evaluations for d = 500 on a noiseless classifier.
- The strongest "already known" result for a GECCO reviewer is Akiba et al.: CMA-ES over about six scalar knobs of a frozen model, 1,000 evaluations, large measured gain. A paper that evolves a handful of execution knobs around a frozen VLA will be read as the robotics analogue of that recipe.
- Reviewers will also expect any ES-on-policy claim to be compared against DSRL (Key question 2), which adapts a frozen pi0 at 65 to 80 real episodes with RL, not ES.

### Gaps
- No published example found of ES, GA or neuroevolution fine-tuning a VLA or a diffusion / flow robot policy (checked: Qiu et al. full text, EGGROLL full text and reference list, ESSA abstract, GECCO 2023 to 2026 main-track titles, EvoStar 2025 to 2026 titles). Posts after June 2026 could not be searched.
- Jin, Liu and Tan 2024 (ES over LoRA in LLMs) was identified only through EGGROLL's bibliography; its budgets and results were not fetched.
- ESSA's population size, number of evaluations and the dimensionality of the singular-value search were not on the abstract page.
- Qiu et al. give no wall-clock or GPU-hour figures, only a FLOP ratio.

## Key question 2: Residual and adapter approaches around a frozen base policy (sizes, inputs, episode budgets; any evolved residual)

### Takeaway
Residual RL on top of frozen chunked / diffusion policies is mature (ResiP 2024, Policy Decorator 2024, ResFiT 2025) and the latent-noise variant DSRL (2025) adapts a frozen pi0 as a black box in 65 to 80 real episodes; every residual or adapter in this literature is trained by gradient RL (PPO, SAC, DDPG-style), and no evolved residual around a frozen diffusion / VLA policy was found.

### Cited Findings
- Background: "Residual Policy Learning" (Silver, Allen, Tenenbaum, Kaelbling; arXiv 1812.06298, Dec 2018). Model-free deep RL learns an additive residual on a non-differentiable hand-designed or MPC controller; six MuJoCo manipulation tasks with partial observability, sensor noise, model misspecification and miscalibration; residual learning solves long-horizon sparse-reward tasks RL alone cannot — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06298)
- Background: "Residual Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control" (Johannink, Bahl, Nair, Luo, Kumar, Loskyll, Aparicio Ojea, Solowjow, Levine; arXiv 1812.03201, Dec 2018). Conventional feedback controller plus RL residual, final policy a superposition of both, demonstrated on a real block-assembly task with contacts and unstable objects — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03201)
- "From Imitation to Refinement: Residual RL for Precise Assembly" (ResiP; Ankile, Simeonov, Shenfeld, Torne, Agrawal; arXiv 2407.16677, v1 Jul 2024, v4 Dec 2024). Frozen chunked Diffusion Policy (about 66M parameters, predicts 32 actions, executes 8, 50 demos per task) plus a per-step Gaussian MLP residual taking full simulation state concatenated with the base action, residual scale 0.1, trained with PPO on 1,024 parallel environments with a cap of 500M environment steps. FurnitureBench low randomisation: one_leg 54% to 98%, round_table 12% to 94%, lamp 7% to 97%; medium randomisation one_leg 26% to 76%; mug-rack 26% to 88%, peg-in-hole 5% to 99%, biman-insert 33% to 93%. Real robot: the residual is not deployed directly; a distilled image-based diffusion student reaches 5/10 to 6/10 versus 2/10 to 3/10 for real-only BC — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16677); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677)
- "Policy Decorator: Model-Agnostic Online Refinement for Large Policy Model" (Yuan, Mu, Tao, Fang, Zhang, Su; arXiv 2412.13630, Dec 2024). Small Gaussian MLP/CNN residual whose input is the observation only (feeding the base action in was worse, Appendix E.1); output added to the frozen base action; SAC backbone (PPO also works); critic takes base + residual. Controlled exploration: residual bounded to (-alpha, alpha) via scaled tanh, and the residual is switched on with probability epsilon rising linearly from 0 to 1 over H steps; per-task alpha 0.03 to 0.8 and H 1M to 8M environment steps (Table 8), evaluation every 50k steps over ten seeds. Base models: Behavior Transformer (about 1M parameters) and U-Net Diffusion Policy (about 4M, horizon 16). Eight tasks (ManiSkill StackCube, PegInsertionSide, TurnFaucet, PushChair with 1,000 demos each; Adroit Door, Pen, Hammer, Relocate with 25 demos). Near-perfect success on all state-observation tasks; direct fine-tuning baselines with LoRA (SAC, DIPO, RLPD, ROT, Cal-QL) failed, DIPO on all tasks, which the authors attribute to randomly initialised critics and long horizons. Non-fine-tuning baselines: JSRL, vanilla residual RL, FISH. No real-robot results — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13630); details from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630)
- "Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning Behavior Cloning Policies" (ResFiT; Ankile, Jiang, Duan, Shi, Abbeel, Nagabandi; arXiv 2509.19301, Sep 2025). Frozen chunked BC base treated as a black box (ACT on the real robot); residual policy takes the observation and the base action; DDPG-style off-policy actor-critic with TD3 tricks, randomised ensembled double-Q, layer-norm critic, n = 3 returns, UTD > 1, 50/50 demo/online sampling; sparse binary reward. Real world (humanoid with dexterous hands, claimed first real-world RL on such a platform): WoollyBallPnP 134 RL rollouts (about 15 min) 14% to 64%; PackageHandover 343 episodes (about 76 min) 23% to 64%. Simulation: near-perfect on Can at about 75k steps, BoxCleanup converges at about 200k steps. Baselines: PPO residual (ResiP-style), tuned RLPD, IBRL, filtered BC — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19301); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301)
- "Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning" (DSRL; Wagenmaker, Nakamoto, Zhang, Park, Yagoub, Nagabandi, Gupta, Levine; arXiv 2506.15799, Jun 2025). RL (DSRL-Na noise-aliased actor-critic, or plain SAC) acts in the base policy's initial-noise space; the base policy is frozen and needs only forward passes from noise to action ("obviating the need to modify the weights of the base policy at all"); latent actions clipped to [-b_W, b_W] with b_W 1 to 3 online. Simulated online: at least 5 to 10x the sample efficiency of DPPO, IDQL, DQL, DIPO, QSM. Real Franka pick-and-place: base 2/10, RLPD 0/10, RLPD + interventions 0/10, DSRL 9/10 after 3,500 online steps (about 40 episodes, 10 demos, sparse 0/1 reward). WidowX multi-task (Bridge V2 DiT base): gains in 100 to 150 episodes, RLPD fails. pi0 (3.3B, flow head) on LIBERO: about 20% to about 100% after about 10,000 online samples, while ResiP-style residual RL and V-GPS "don't significantly improve pi0". pi0-DROID real Franka: toaster 5/20 to 18/20 over 80 episodes (about 11,000 steps), spoon-on-plate 15/20 to 19/20 over 65 episodes (about 10,000 steps). Stated limits: exploration is bounded by the base policy; narrow, concentrated action distributions may offer too few options to steer; no way to estimate steerability in advance — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15799); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)
- DSRL appendix, the pi0 configuration most comparable to a pi0.5 LIBERO-style setup: noise latent 32 per step, chunk 50, so a 1,600-dim latent, 10 flow steps; LIBERO executes 20 of 50 actions per chunk, AlohaTransferCube executes 50, real-world chunk 10 executes 10; actor/critic MLP hidden 128, 3 layers, 10 critics (LIBERO) or hidden 1024, 2 critics (real); b_W 1.0 (LIBERO), 2.0 (Aloha), 2.5 (real); 64x64 images for the critic in sim; 20 to 30 gradient steps per update; batch 256 — [arXiv HTML, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)
- RL fine-tuning of pi0 / pi0.5 themselves (gradient, not black-box): "pi_RL: Online RL Fine-tuning for Flow-based Vision-Language-Action Models" (Chen, Liu, Zhang, ..., Yu; arXiv 2510.25889, v1 29 Oct 2025, v3 29 Jan 2026). Two methods: Flow-Noise (learnable per-step noise network, discarded after training) and Flow-SDE (ODE-to-SDE conversion preserving the marginal, noise level a hyperparameter: too little limits gains, too much distorts the flow). Only the about 300M-parameter action expert is trained, the VLM is frozen. Few-shot SFT starting points: pi0 58 trajectories (208 for Long), pi0.5 40. LIBERO average: pi0 57.6% to 96.1 / 97.6%; pi0.5 77.1% to 97.9 / 98.3% (full-data SFT baselines 94.2 and 96.9); pi0.5 Long 43.9% to 94.0%. ManiSkill: pi0 38.4% to 78.8%, pi0.5 40.1% to 90.9%. Hardware 8 x H100-80GB; episode counts not in the visible text — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25889); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25889)
- Other VLA RL post-training (all gradient-based, listed so the reviewer's "already known" set is complete): SimpleVLA-RL (Li et al., 21 authors; arXiv 2509.09674, Sep 2025) on OpenVLA-OFT, veRL-based, SoTA on LIBERO, beats pi0 on RoboTwin 1.0/2.0, reports a "pushcut" behaviour not in the demos — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09674); VLA-RL (Lu et al.; arXiv 2505.18719, May 2025) on OpenVLA-7B, 40 LIBERO tasks, +4.5% over the strongest fine-tuned baseline, matches pi0-FAST, VLM process-reward model — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18719); RIPT-VLA (Tan, Dou, Zhao, Kraehenbuehl; arXiv 2505.17016, May 2025), sparse binary reward, leave-one-out advantages, OpenVLA-OFT to 97.5%, QueST +21.2%, one-demo SFT model from 4% to 97% within 15 iterations — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17016); ConRFT (Chen et al.; arXiv 2502.05450, Feb 2025), consistency-policy offline+online fine-tuning with human interventions, eight real tasks, 96.3% average after 45 to 90 min online, +144% over supervised fine-tuning, 1.9x shorter episodes — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05450); RLinf-VLA (Zang et al.; arXiv 2510.06710, v1 Oct 2025, v3 12 Aug 2026), unified RL framework, 98.11% over 130 LIBERO tasks, 97.66% over 25 ManiSkill tasks, 84.63% over 6 RoboTwin tasks — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06710); "pi*0.6: a VLA That Learns From Experience" (Physical Intelligence; arXiv 2511.14759, 18 Nov 2025), RECAP = offline RL pre-training plus advantage-conditioned policy with demonstrations, on-policy data and teleoperated corrections, more than doubles throughput and roughly halves failure rate on the hardest tasks (laundry, box assembly, espresso) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759)
- Prior art for safety-specific adaptation of VLAs, gradient-based: "SafeVLA: Towards Safety Alignment of Vision-Language-Action Model via Constrained Learning" (Zhang et al.; arXiv 2503.03480; NeurIPS 2025 spotlight). CMDP min-max safe RL on long-horizon mobile manipulation; cumulative safety-violation cost down 83.58% and task success up 3.85% versus the prior state of the art — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480)

### Inferences
- Design facts that recur across the residual papers: residual networks are small MLPs; the residual magnitude is bounded by a hand-set scalar (ResiP 0.1, Policy Decorator alpha 0.03 to 0.8, DSRL b_W 1.0 to 2.5) and that bound is tuned per task by hand in every paper; residual inputs are either observation only (Policy Decorator, better in its ablation) or observation plus base action (ResiP, ResFiT).
- Budgets split into two regimes: simulator residual RL at 10^5 to 10^8 environment steps (ResiP, Policy Decorator), and real-world off-policy or latent RL at 40 to 350 episodes (DSRL, ResFiT, ConRFT). The second regime is inside a 1,000 to 2,000-episode budget, so an evolutionary wrapper cannot claim sample efficiency as its novelty; its distinct claims would have to be elsewhere (no critic, no reward shaping beyond an episode score, multi-objective fronts, rank-based robustness to noisy fitness, or a search space RL does not express such as discrete execution rules).
- DSRL's own limitation statement (narrow action distributions give too few options to steer) plus its finding that ResiP-style residual RL did not significantly improve pi0 on LIBERO are the two most relevant published facts for a pi0.5 wrapper study: steering through the sampler needs a base policy that is stochastic enough, and per-step residual RL on a 3B flow policy is not a free win.

### Gaps
- No published example found of a residual, adapter or latent-noise steering network around a frozen diffusion / flow / VLA policy that was found by evolution or any other gradient-free search (checked the residual papers above and their baselines, GECCO 2023 to 2026 and EvoStar 2025 to 2026 titles).
- DSRL's pi0 LIBERO run reports "about 10,000 online samples"; whether that is environment steps or chunks, and the episode count, were not in the fetched text.
- pi_RL's per-task episode counts and GPU-hours sit in an appendix that was not retrieved.
- Whether anyone has applied DSRL or a residual to pi0.5 specifically (rather than pi0) was not established.

## Key question 3: Neuroevolution of small controllers that gate, scale or modulate a frozen large policy ("evolved supervisors", switching controllers), 2020 onward

### Takeaway
No published example was found of a neuroevolved (NEAT, CMA-ES, GA) network that gates, scales or modulates a frozen learned manipulation or locomotion policy; the closest published relatives are gradient-trained residual MLPs (Key question 2), genetic programming of behaviour trees whose leaves are hand-written skills (Iovino 2020, Styrud 2021), and GECCO 2024's search-based repair of DNN controllers.

### Cited Findings
- GECCO 2024 main track lists "Search-Based Repair of DNN Controllers of AI-Enabled Cyber-Physical Systems Guided by System-Level Specifications" (Lyu, Zhang, Arcaini, Ishikawa, Laurent, Zhao; SBSE track): search used to repair a trained DNN controller against system-level specifications — [GECCO 2024 accepted papers](https://gecco-2024.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)
- GECCO 2025 main track lists "When Does Neuroevolution Outcompete Reinforcement Learning in Transfer Learning Tasks?" (Nisioti, Plantec, Llera Montero, Pedersen, Risi; BBSR track) and "Evolution of Inherently Interpretable Visual Control Policies" (De La Torre, Nadizar, Lavinas, Luga, Wilson, Cussat-Blanc; EML) — [GECCO 2025 accepted papers](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)
- GECCO 2023 lists "Using a Variational Autoencoder to Learn Valid Search Spaces of Safely Monitored Autonomous Robots for Last-Mile Delivery" (Bentley, Lim, Arcaini, Ishikawa; RWA) and "MAP-Elites with Descriptor-Conditioned Gradients and Archive Distillation into a Single Policy" (Faldor, Chalumeau, Flageat, Cully; CS) — [GECCO 2023 accepted papers](https://gecco-2023.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers)
- Evolved supervisors with hand-written leaves: "Learning Behavior Trees with Genetic Programming in Unpredictable Environments" (Iovino, Styrud, Falco, Smith; arXiv 2011.03252, Nov 2020). GP learns the BT structure for a robotic task in a simple simulator; the learned tree solves the task in a more realistic simulator and tolerates faults — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03252)
- "Combining Planning and Learning of Behavior Trees for Robotic Assembly" (Styrud, Iovino, Norrloef, Bjoerkman, Smith; arXiv 2103.09036, Mar 2021, submitted to IROS 2021). GP over BTs seeded with a planner's output outperforms either alone on a variety of assembly problems; the learned high-level trees transfer to a real system without further training — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09036)
- Population-based but not frozen-base: "Evolutionary Policy Optimization" (Wang, Su, Gupta, Pathak; arXiv 2503.19037, v1 Mar 2025, v3 Nov 2025). A population of agents conditioned on latent variables shares actor-critic parameters and aggregates experience into a master agent; dexterous manipulation, legged locomotion, classic control; claims better sample efficiency and scalability than on-policy RL — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19037)
- The gradient-trained analogue of an "evolved gate": Policy Decorator's progressive schedule switches the residual on with probability epsilon over H steps and bounds it by alpha (Key question 2) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630)
- The smallest learned modulator of a frozen 3B policy on record is DSRL's pi0 LIBERO actor: MLP hidden 128, 3 layers, outputs a 1,600-dim noise vector, trained by SAC-style RL — [arXiv HTML, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)

### Inferences
- The structural idea (tiny network from cheap features to a few execution knobs over a frozen skill) exists in the BT-evolution literature with scripted skills as leaves, and in residual RL with gradients; the combination (evolved modulator, frozen learned VLA as the leaf) appears to be unoccupied in the fetched literature.
- A reviewer would ask for the gradient baseline (Policy Decorator-style SAC residual with a bounded alpha) at matched episode budget, because that is the known way to get the same effect.

### Gaps
- No published example found of NEAT / CMA-ES / GA evolving a gating or modulating network over a frozen learned policy in manipulation or locomotion since 2020 (search was limited to fetched papers and conference title lists; abstracts of the GECCO items above were not retrievable, so their exact setups are unverified).
- The GECCO 2024 "search-based repair of DNN controllers" paper's method, controller size and budget were not retrievable (ACM DL blocked).

## Key question 4: Evolutionary prompt optimisation and VLA instruction sensitivity

### Takeaway
Evolutionary prompt optimisation is well established for LLMs (EvoPrompt up to +25% on BBH; Promptbreeder; GECCO 2024 and EvoStar 2026 papers), but no application to a VLA's language instruction was found, and the 2025 robustness benchmarks report that LIBERO-fine-tuned VLAs largely ignore the instruction (near-identical trajectories under corrupted or blank instructions), which caps what prompt evolution could move.

### Cited Findings
- "EvoPrompt: Connecting LLMs with Evolutionary Algorithms Yields Powerful Prompt Optimizers" (Guo, Wang, Guo, Li, Song, Tan, Liu, Bian, Yang; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2309.08532). ### Inferences
- For a pi0.5 checkpoint fine-tuned on one task family, the published evidence points both ways but to the same conclusion: paraphrase can hurt (pi0 94.2 to 61.0 under LIBERO-Plus language perturbation) yet the model does not act on instruction semantics (goal swap executes the original target; blank instruction changes little). Prompt evolution would therefore be searching for a phrasing the fine-tuned policy happens to tolerate, with no documented mechanism by which wording changes contact-phase behaviour.
- The nearest "already known" work for a reviewer is EvoPrompt plus LIBERO-Plus; a prompt-evolution arm reported as a negative control would be read as confirming LIBERO-Plus, not as a contribution.

## Key question 5: Evolution or black-box tuning of execution / controller parameters for a learned policy (gains, speed scaling, horizon), as opposed to evolving the policy

### Takeaway
This simplest class is empirically empty in the fetched literature: every residual, steering and VLA paper hand-sets its execution knobs (chunk size, executed horizon, residual bound, latent clip, flow steps), and the only black-box tuning of controller parameters with a measured effect is on hand-designed controllers (SafeOpt on a real quadrotor, 2016). Test-time selection among sampled action chunks with a learned verifier (V-GPS, RoboMonkey, BID) is the closest published "wrapper that changes execution without touching the policy".

### Cited Findings
- Hand-set execution knobs in the VLA literature: OpenVLA-OFT (Kim, Finn, Liang; arXiv 2502.19645) uses chunk K = 8 on LIBERO and K = 25 on ALOHA and states that executing full chunks before replanning "improves both speed and performance" without reporting the comparison; action chunking plus parallel decoding raised LIBERO average by about 14 points over autoregressive OpenVLA (76.5 to 95.3 / 97.1) and throughput from 4.2 Hz to 71.4 to 109.7 Hz — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19645)
- DSRL's pi0 configurations execute 20 of 50 predicted actions on LIBERO, all 50 on AlohaTransferCube and 10 of 10 on the real robot, with latent clip b_W 1.0 / 2.0 / 2.5 and 10 flow steps, all set by hand per task (Table 11) — [arXiv HTML, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)
- Policy Decorator sets the residual bound alpha (0.03 to 0.8) and schedule length H (1M to 8M steps) per task by hand (Table 8) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630); ResiP sets the residual scale to 0.1 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677)
- Test-time execution wrappers that leave the policy frozen: "Steering Your Generalists: Improving Robotic Foundation Models via Value Guidance" (V-GPS; Nakamoto, Mees, Kumar, Levine; CoRL 2024; arXiv 2410.13816). Re-ranks K = 50 sampled actions (10 or 50 in sim) with a Cal-QL value function trained on Bridge V2 + Fractal; treats the policy as a black box needing only multiple action samples; SIMPLER averages: Octo-small 0.27 to 0.34, Octo-base 0.17 to 0.18, Octo-small-1.5 0.02 to 0.18, RT-1-X 0.12 to 0.14, OpenVLA 0.24 to 0.27; real WidowX with Octo-small-1.5: 0.24 to 0.44 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13816); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816)
- "RoboMonkey: Scaling Test-Time Sampling and Verification for Vision-Language-Action Models" (Kwok, Agia, Sinha, Foutter, Li, Stoica, Mirhoseini, Pavone; arXiv 2506.17811, Jun 2025). Samples 5 actions from OpenVLA, fits a Gaussian to translation/rotation, majority-votes the gripper, draws 16 candidates, scores them with a LLaVA-7B verifier trained on 20M synthetic comparisons from Bridge V2; action error falls as an exponentiated power law in the number of samples; real-world OOD 60% versus OpenVLA 35% and V-GPS 30%; SIMPLER +9 points (47.5%); LIBERO-Long 56.5% versus 49.8% (with OpenVLA also fine-tuned); 16 candidates verified in about 650 ms on one H100 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17811); numbers from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811)
- "Bidirectional Decoding: Improving Action Chunking via Guided Test-Time Sampling" (Liu, Hamid, Xie, Lee, Du, Finn; arXiv 2408.17355, v4 Apr 2025). Samples several candidate chunks per step and selects by backward coherence with previous decisions and forward contrast; improves two generative policies on seven simulated benchmarks and two real tasks (numbers not on the abstract page) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- "Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies" (RTC; Black, Galliker, Levine; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2506.07339). Inference-time, no retraining: freezes actions already committed and inpaints the rest of the next chunk so consecutive chunks stay consistent under latency; 12 Kinetix tasks and 6 real bimanual tasks, improved throughput and high success on precise tasks such as lighting a match — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339)
- Background, safe black-box tuning of controller parameters on hardware: "Safe Controller Optimization for Quadrotors with Gaussian Processes" (Berkenkamp, Schoellig, Krause; ICRA 2016; arXiv 1509.01066). SafeOpt tunes control-law parameters from a low-performance initial controller, exploring only parameters the GP predicts to stay above a safe performance threshold with high probability, without human intervention — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01066)
- Background, search over initial noise with a verifier for diffusion models (image domain): "Inference-Time Scaling for Diffusion Models beyond Scaling Denoising Steps" (Ma et al.; arXiv 2501.09732, Jan 2025) frames the extra compute as a search over initial noise guided by verifiers — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09732)

### Inferences
- Because no paper tunes these knobs by search, the knobs' effect sizes are unknown in the literature; the only measured, policy-frozen execution effects are the best-of-N verifier gains (V-GPS +7 to +16 points in sim, +20 points real; RoboMonkey +9 sim, +25 real OOD), and they require a trained value function or verifier plus 5 to 50 forward passes per step.
- RTC and OpenVLA-OFT show that execution-level changes (how much of a chunk is executed, how chunks are stitched) measurably change success and speed, which is the mechanism an evolved execution dial would rely on; neither paper quantifies the executed-horizon trade-off.

### Gaps
- No published example found of CMA-ES, GA or Bayesian optimisation over the execution parameters (executed horizon, action scale, speed cap, gripper rate, flow steps, latent clip) of a frozen learned manipulation policy.
- Calandra et al. 2016 (Bayesian optimisation of gait parameters on a real biped) could not be fetched (Springer login wall), so it is not cited here.
- OpenVLA-OFT's appendix, where an executed-horizon ablation might sit, was not retrieved (text truncated).

## Key question 7: For each cluster, what claim, what baseline, what a reviewer treats as known, and the simplest instance that worked

### Takeaway
Each cluster has a simplest published instance with a measured effect: six-parameter CMA-ES merging (frozen LLM), a 3-layer MLP over the noise latent trained by RL in 40 to 80 real episodes (frozen diffusion / pi0), value-based re-ranking of 50 sampled actions (frozen generalist, no training of the policy), and LLM-operator prompt evolution; the evolutionary-wrapper-around-a-frozen-VLA cell is empty.

### Cited Findings
- ES fine-tuning of LLMs. Claim: full-parameter ES beats GRPO/PPO on Countdown and conciseness with 30 individuals (Qiu et al.) and runs 100x faster with low-rank perturbations (EGGROLL). Baselines: PPO, GRPO, Dr.GRPO. Simplest instance with an effect: Qiu et al., N = 30, sigma = 0.001, 500 iterations — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372). Simplest low-dimensional instance: ESSA, ES over LoRA singular values only — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04453). Known to reviewers: BBT (CMA-ES, d = 500, 8,000 calls) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03514) and MeZO — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333).
- Evolution of a few scalars around a frozen model. Claim: CMA-ES over about six merge coefficients, 1,000 evaluations, MGSM-JA 30.0 to 52.0 with no training (Akiba et al.) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187).
- Residual / adapter around a frozen policy. Claim: a small residual beats direct fine-tuning of the large policy at the same online budget (Policy Decorator: all LoRA fine-tuning baselines failed, residual reaches near-perfect) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630); off-policy residual reaches 64% from 14 to 23% in 134 to 343 real episodes (ResFiT) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301). Baselines: RLPD, Cal-QL, DPPO, IDQL, JSRL, vanilla residual RL, PPO residual. Known: residual RL since 2018 (Silver; Johannink).
- Latent-noise steering of a frozen diffusion / flow policy. Claim: black-box access suffices; 9/10 from 2/10 in about 40 real episodes; pi0-DROID 18/20 from 5/20 in 80 episodes; 5 to 10x the sample efficiency of DPPO-class methods (DSRL) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799). Simplest instance: DSRL-SAC with a 3-layer MLP actor.
- Test-time action selection with a frozen policy. Claim: re-ranking K = 50 samples with one offline value function improves five different generalists, real-world 0.24 to 0.44 (V-GPS) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816); sampling + Gaussian proposal + verifier gives +25 points OOD real-world (RoboMonkey) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811). Baselines: the base policy, each other. Known: best-of-N with verifiers from LLM inference-scaling; noise search for image diffusion — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09732).
- Prompt evolution. Claim: up to +25% on BBH over human prompts (EvoPrompt) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532). Baselines: human prompts, APE-style automatic prompting. Known for VLAs: instruction is largely ignored (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03827).
- Evolved supervisors. Claim: GP-evolved behaviour trees seeded by a planner solve assembly tasks and transfer to a real robot without further training (Styrud et al.) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09036). Known: BT evolution since 2020 (Iovino et al.) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03252).
- Execution-parameter tuning. No search-based instance found; measured hand-set effects only (OpenVLA-OFT full-chunk execution, RTC inpainting) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19645), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339); safe black-box gain tuning exists for hand-designed controllers (SafeOpt) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01066).

### Inferences
- The combination "frozen VLA + evolutionary search over a low-dimensional wrapper (execution knobs, supervisor rules, or a tiny modulator) + multi-objective safety/speed front" does not collide with any fetched paper. The nearest collisions are DSRL (frozen pi0, black-box, but RL over a 1,600-dim latent) and Akiba et al. (CMA-ES over six knobs of a frozen model, but LLM merging).
- What a reviewer will say is already known: (a) small wrappers beat direct fine-tuning at small budgets (Policy Decorator, DSRL); (b) RL wrappers already work in 40 to 350 real episodes, so evolution must justify itself by something other than sample count; (c) fine-tuned VLAs ignore instructions, so prompt evolution is a control, not a result; (d) best-of-N with a verifier is the standard training-free way to improve a frozen policy and will be requested as a baseline if the wrapper selects among chunks.

### Gaps
- Because GECCO abstracts and all post-June-2026 arXiv postings were unreachable, "does not collide" is provisional; a search pass over arXiv cs.RO / cs.NE for "evolution strategies" + "vision-language-action" and over the GECCO 2026 Companion is needed before the related-work section is final.
- ManiGuard (arXiv 2608.17386) and any follow-up work on it were deliberately not researched here, per the brief.

## Simplest things that worked (cheapest demonstrated effects, ranked by cost of the intervention)

### Takeaway
Ranked from cheapest to most expensive, the demonstrated effects are: re-ranking sampled actions with an existing value function (no training of the policy, but a value function is needed), CMA-ES over about six scalars of a frozen model, RL over a tiny latent-noise MLP in tens of real episodes, off-policy residual RL in hundreds of episodes, ES over LoRA singular values, then full-parameter ES at 10^4 to 10^6 rollouts.

### Cited Findings
1. Best-of-N action re-ranking with a frozen policy: V-GPS, K = 50 candidates, one Cal-QL value function reused across five policies, real-world 0.24 to 0.44, sim +1 to +16 points — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816); RoboMonkey, 5 + 16 candidates, real OOD 35% to 60%, SIMPLER +9 points, 650 ms per step on one H100 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17811). Cost: a trained verifier (Cal-QL on Bridge+Fractal; or 20M synthetic comparisons) and 5 to 50 policy forward passes per step.
2. CMA-ES over about six scalar knobs of a frozen model: Akiba et al., 1,000 evaluations, MGSM-JA 30.0 to 52.0 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187). Cost: 1,000 evaluations on a 1,069-item fitness set.
3. RL over a tiny MLP in the frozen policy's noise space: DSRL, 3-layer MLP, Franka 2/10 to 9/10 in about 40 episodes; pi0-DROID 5/20 to 18/20 in 80 episodes; pi0 LIBERO about 20% to about 100% in about 10,000 online samples — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799). Cost: a critic on 64x64 images, resets, sparse reward.
4. Off-policy residual RL on a frozen chunked BC policy: ResFiT, 134 to 343 real episodes, 14 to 23% up to 64% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301).
5. Bounded residual with progressive switch-on (Policy Decorator): near-perfect success but 10^6 to 10^7 simulator steps — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13630). ResiP: 10^8-step cap on 1,024 parallel environments — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677).
6. Black-box CMA-ES in a 500-dim random subspace of a frozen LM: BBT, population 20, 8,000 calls, 73.0 versus 63.5 for prompt tuning — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03514).
7. ES over LoRA singular values after SFT: ESSA, up to 7.8x faster than LoRA-GRPO to a fixed MATH500 accuracy at 32B — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04453).
8. Full-parameter ES on a 7B LLM: Qiu et al., N = 30, 500 to 1,000 iterations, Countdown 66.8 versus RL 52.8 to 57.5 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372); EGGROLL, 1,024 to 8,192 generations per update, 260 to 915 updates — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16652).
9. Evolved prompt populations: EvoPrompt up to +25% on BBH (budget not extracted) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532).
10. Background: GP-evolved behaviour trees transferring to a real assembly robot (Styrud et al.) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09036); SafeOpt tuning controller parameters safely on a real quadrotor — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01066).

### Inferences
- Within a 1,000 to 2,000-episode budget and a noise floor of about 200 episodes per condition, the only entries above whose evaluation counts fit are items 2 (1,000 evaluations over about six knobs) and 3 to 4 (tens to hundreds of episodes, but those are RL with critics); everything from item 5 onward is one to three orders of magnitude over budget.
- The literature's cheapest effects all share one property: the search space is tiny (six merge weights; a bounded residual; one value function reused) and the base model is never touched. That is also the property a GECCO reviewer will accept as "simple and effective" rather than "elaborate".

### Gaps
- No entry in this ranking is an evolutionary method applied to a robot policy; the ranking therefore gives the cost scale, not a direct precedent, for an evolved wrapper around a frozen VLA.
