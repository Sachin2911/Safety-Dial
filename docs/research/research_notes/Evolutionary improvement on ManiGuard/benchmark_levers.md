# ManiGuard interfaces, behavioural levers and a defensible improvement claim

Research date: 19 October 2026. Read-only evidence review; no simulator, rental, installation, checkpoint update or experiment was performed. Recommendation confidence: moderate for interface feasibility, low for an eventual performance gain. These notes nominate a testable bet, not a discovered winner.

## What does ManiGuard actually expose, and where is the headroom?

### Takeaway

The released policy already exposes a substantially stronger adaptation point than target caps: its initial flow noise. Text is also an input, but successful task decomposition is not a documented skill interface. Most existing local controls alter speed or timing; noise steering could alter the decoded behaviour without training the VLA or a safety predictor.

### Cited Findings

- The official paper is arXiv v1, submitted 18 August 2026. It defines 200 base tasks and 1,000 ID/OOD scenarios, six family-specific SFT checkpoints per model, and three sampling seeds. Native ID safe-success for pi0.5-SFT is 80.00% Clutter, 10.00% Lid, 0.00% Cabinet, 26.92% Jar, 20.24% Stack, and 0.00% Dusty. No policy has a Clutter violation in the paper's main simulation runs. The monitor observes without changing actions. The prompt-format study fine-tunes separate None/NL/LTL checkpoints and tests each on its training representation. Those results do not establish inference-only prompt steering of the mainline frozen checkpoint. Released ordering requirements use weak-until semantics; no eventuality is enforced. [Official paper, especially Sections 3, 5 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17386v1).
- Native input comprises an overview image, wrist image, joint/gripper state and task prompt. The current evaluator passes the prompt at every query, supports per-episode `prompt_template` and `prompt_condition` changes, validates chunk shape, and can explicitly convert EEF deltas to joint commands. Dynamic prompt switching is not implemented by those episode-level hooks. [Current evaluator source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard/main/maniguard/eval/benchmark.py).
- The openpi data contract is eight physical coordinates: seven arm joints plus gripper. Data are absolute joint targets, transformed to joint deltas for the model and reconstructed to absolute targets at inference. pi0.5 uses 16 predicted actions, pi0 uses 50. Neither path uses EEF/IK for these released checkpoints. Overview selection and camera mapping must match training. [Official openpi integration documentation](https://nu-ideas-lab.github.io/ManiGuard/docs/fine_tuning/openpi/).
- Critically, the model configuration has `action_dim=32` and `action_horizon=16` for the mainline pi0.5 Jar and other family configurations. The physical eight-coordinate output is not the full latent noise width. [Current ManiGuard training configuration source, Jar block near lines 184-205](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard/main/maniguard/openpi_sft/train_configs.py).
- At our pinned openpi revision, `Policy.infer(obs, *, noise=None)` accepts an explicit noise tensor, adds a batch dimension to rank-two noise, calls the model sampler, and then applies output transforms. It advances the JAX sampling RNG even when explicit noise is passed. [Pinned openpi policy source, lines 62-93](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479/src/openpi/policies/policy.py).
- The same pinned `pi0.sample_actions` accepts `noise` and defaults to a Gaussian tensor of shape `(batch_size, action_horizon, action_dim)` when noise is absent. Default flow integration has ten steps. Image/language preprocessing and the conditioned flow decoder remain active for an explicit noise tensor. [Pinned sampler source, lines 199-219](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479/src/openpi/models/pi0.py).
- ManiGuard's native websocket server currently calls `self._policy.infer(obs)` without forwarding an explicit noise argument. It resets the policy sampling RNG on a changed episode seed and advertises checkpoint/configuration metadata. Thus noise control needs an explicit server wrapper or extension; it is not already a supported evaluator request field. [Native server source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard/main/maniguard/serve/openpi_native.py).
- The current README describes model-agnostic SFT and policy evaluation, and says RL training is under development. I found no official ready-made trainable residual or noise-space adaptation baseline in the material inspected. This is an absence-of-found-evidence statement, not an exhaustive repository audit. [Official README](https://github.com/NU-IDEAS-Lab/ManiGuard).
- Current documentation defaults to 640x640 high-fidelity DLAA rendering, with `--source-profile legacy` needed for the previous condition. It warns that the long-finger robot asset is separate, that short chunks are valid, and that failed attempts have unavailable outcome verdicts. [Official benchmark execution guide](https://nu-ideas-lab.github.io/ManiGuard/docs/evaluation/run_benchmark/).
- Benchmark counted violations are gated from first contact with a relevant object; raw violation state, engagement, and counted violation are distinct. A policy can be vacuously safe by not engaging. [Official engagement definition](https://nu-ideas-lab.github.io/ManiGuard/docs/evaluation/engagement_metric/).
- Our six-hypothesis study recorded physically effective retreats and many guard/release activations but no repeatable repair. Some benchmark-labelled Jar completions did not establish jar closure. Local caps, query timing and release rules already have substantial adverse/inconclusive evidence. [Local synthesis](/home/sachin/Desktop/Research/Safety-Dial/docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20six-hypothesis%20study.md).

### Inferences

- **Jar is the stronger first scientific target than Clutter for a safety contribution.** Its pretrained policy has demonstrated task competence and substantial safety/completion headroom, while its 2,000-action horizon is shorter than Stack's. Clutter remains useful for cheap engineering and regression tests, but its published simulation safety is already saturated. Improvement there primarily addresses competence and must avoid introducing new violations. Cabinet/Dusty have more nominal room but far weaker evidence that a small adaptation can reach useful competence.
- **Text support is syntactic, competence is empirical.** Replacing the prompt is easy; whether a full-task family fine-tune responds to “close”, “lift”, or a changed safety instruction as a reliable primitive must be established physically. The official prompt study cannot be cited as proof. A subgoal sequence also needs a causal transition detector, potentially requiring new perception or privileged simulator state.
- **The missing physical lever is not an evidence gap that another optimizer fixes.** Existing motion caps can preserve a poor action's direction while doing little to correct an incorrect approach, target, contact mode or task stage. A generative-policy interface can in principle change those choices, although it is not guaranteed to do so.
- **Scope separation:** using safety labels as training fitness is appropriate benchmark supervision on development scenes. Passing the live monitor's object predicates or automaton state to the deployed actor changes its information. That can be a separate privileged track; it cannot silently enter a camera/proprioception comparison.

### Gaps

- The remote requests for our pinned ManiGuard `6bf4c61...` evaluator/configuration returned errors. Its checkout is absent at local `third_party/ManiGuard`. Current-source details above therefore require comparison against the actual run machine before implementation. The noise API was verified directly at the pinned openpi revision. The current full Jar noise shape is source-backed, but actual loaded model shape, dtype and transforms must be asserted at runtime.
- Current official source/doc pages have no stable publication date in the retrieved content. Their retrieval date is 19 October 2026. Do not silently replace the experiment's pinned source with live `main`.
- No inspected source proves that a low-dimensional perturbation improves Jar, that it spans relevant recovery behaviours, or that proprioception-only conditioning is sufficient. No new physical evidence is supplied here.

## Which new lever is the best bet, and how do alternatives compare?

### Takeaway

**Provisional winner: reusable bounded low-rank steering of the frozen flow policy's sampling noise, trained using actual safe completion and actual monitor outcomes.** Start with native execution timing and no learned safety evaluator. Retain a small action-residual/program alternative only if the decoder-response probe shows that useful behaviours cannot be elicited through noise.

### Cited Findings

- DSRL already adapts a frozen diffusion policy through a learned noise-space policy, including generalist policies, using black-box access. This is CoRL 2025 work with published proceedings, not a novel interface we can claim. [DSRL proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/wagenmaker25a.html); [official project](https://diffusion-steering.github.io/).
- UniSteer, submitted 11 May 2026, combines human corrective supervision, approximate action-to-noise inversion and noise-space RL while freezing the flow decoder. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10821); [official Microsoft code](https://github.com/microsoft/UniSteer).
- Principal Steering Subspaces, submitted 27 September 2026, constructs a fixed low-dimensional basis from finite-difference decoder responses and applies SAC, retaining Gaussian noise outside the basis. Low-rank response-aware steering itself is therefore close prior work. Its reported large-VLA deployment is exploratory, not ManiGuard safety evidence. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33765).
- LP-DS, submitted 31 May 2026, learns a compact perturbation to frozen generative-policy noise with a trust-region objective. RL2-VLA, submitted 29 July 2026, instead composes an RL flow with frozen VLA flow and activates steering using predicted failures. Both narrow broad claims about compact or adaptive steering being new. [LP-DS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01151); [RL2-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26991).
- The parent's parallel review verified frozen-pi0.5 support in Flow Reversal Steering and a low-dimensional noise actor whose physical coordinates are repeated temporally while padded model coordinates receive independent Gaussian noise. This is transferred literature evidence, not an independently fetched source in this sub-review. Its seven-dimensional embodiment is not ManiGuard's eight-coordinate joint/gripper contract. [FRS, Appendix E and Appendix C.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13675).
- An anonymous OpenReview preliminary manuscript studies optimizing the language interface of a frozen VLA using sparse task reward. It is relevant to priority checking, but venue acceptance and released implementation were not established. [OpenReview manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf/f95d37ad87525e5d7b59737e0d73f7ae31a25a5b.pdf).
- The parent's parallel literature review additionally identified Online-ES, arXiv:2609.38855, as especially close evolutionary prior art. I did not independently fetch it in this assigned evidence review. Any final novelty judgment must incorporate that reviewed paper and distinguish trajectory search/distillation from a frozen reusable controller. [Record to consult](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38855).

### Inferences

**Concrete candidate, not yet a frozen protocol.** Let `z` have the model's full native shape, expected `[16,32]` for the chosen pi0.5 config. Draw the normal native Gaussian noise `epsilon`; apply a fixed orthonormal low-rank basis `B` and a bounded coefficient controller, `z = epsilon + B u_theta(o, history)`. The decoder stays frozen. Reconstruct absolute physical joint targets through the existing output transforms and execute the native first eight actions. At zero strength use the original noise distribution and native sampling stream. Do not pass a tensor of zeros as identity: that replaces stochastic sampling by a different decoder input.

For a first probe use four continuous coefficients or a tiny coefficient policy using current joints/gripper and causal previous executed commands. The VLA itself still receives the native cameras and instruction. A recurrent actor or frozen visual features can follow if conditioning proves inadequate. Do not take a new visual encoder, model predictions or privileged object identities as prerequisites. A four-coefficient episode-global perturbation is an intentionally weak initial case; passing it is not evidence that a reusable feedback actor has been solved.

Audit which model coordinates map to the physical eight-coordinate action before constructing `B`. Do not assume an existing seven-dimensional Cartesian steering implementation fits joint-native ManiGuard. A random basis across all 32 model coordinates could waste adaptation capacity on padding. Prefer an initial basis supported on the audited active coordinates across the full native 16-action horizon, with independent native Gaussian noise retained on padded coordinates. Start with arm-only steering and document whether gripper is included as a separate comparison. Whether padded coordinates affect active outputs is an empirical decoder question; FRS's weak padding effect does not establish invariance here. Construct a fixed random basis initially, with response-aware bases as a separately costed option. PSS already studies response geometry, so it is a strong comparator rather than our novelty. Build bases using development observations only; reserve unused physical scenes for checking. Keep Gaussian components outside the controlled basis. Record perturbation norms and decoded command effects, including gripper changes; a flow decoder does not guarantee physically safe outputs or prevent out-of-distribution latents.

**Why this bet leads:** it uses a source-confirmed API, retains the policy's trained camera/action conventions, needs one decoder call per native query once trained, and can generate a different behaviour instead of restraining the same behaviour. Search can use completed rollout outcomes directly. No learned evaluator is required, avoiding Parts 3-4's failed dependency. It is plausible to learn a reusable controller rather than perform costly online population rollout search at every action. Its scientific contribution would need to concern constrained evolutionary adaptation, behavioural structure or transfer under budget, not the existence of noise steering or a substituted optimizer.

Other candidate levers, ordered by what would be needed:

- **Small joint-action residual:** directly feasible in our execution wrapper. A bounded residual can add missing motion, unlike scaling. A linear/recurrent actor using native observations offers matched EA/RL/RS comparisons. Main risks are destroying coordinated motion, sparse credit and a conventional residual-learning contribution. A meaningful baseline if noise steering shows decoded actions remain locked to the same failure mode. New perception is optional, not implicit.
- **Robot-frame/Cartesian residual:** physically interpretable retreat, lift, orientation or approach correction is possible only after validating forward kinematics, Jacobian frame conventions, joint limits and execution semantics. Do not reinterpret a joint-trained checkpoint's eight outputs as EEF motion or switch its controller convention. Compose a Cartesian residual through audited differential IK while preserving the native joint command path. This needs significant implementation and physical validation. End-effector orientation does not determine a held object's tilt or obstacle collisions.
- **Recurrent residual/FSM/GP controller:** a promising later representation when the desired action changes with grasp/task phase. Gives evolution a structural search problem and an interpretable program. But neither mode labels nor reliable transition tests appear automatically. Proprioceptive stall and gripper heuristics have false positives/misses locally; object-stage reasoning may require visual perception. Compare against an ordinary learner on the same representation and against a fixed sensible program. More complexity alone is not novelty.
- **Prompt evolution or skill sequencing:** lowest API cost for constant paraphrases, high uncertainty in effect. Repeated task instructions and OOD-language performance do not prove subskill compositionality. A dynamic sequence needs switching logic and risks changing the requested task or identifying task IDs. First show causal, reproducible differences on held-out instructions and physical stage execution. Avoid it as the main first bet.
- **Chunk selection / adaptive replanning:** chunks are available, and our AAC support can sample several proposals. Replanning changes when the policy sees feedback; it cannot by itself supply an absent skill. Best-of-N selection needs either a hand-designed proxy, a trained evaluator or charged physical lookahead. Existing short/long horizon work is inconclusive and offline re-query opportunities are sparse. Hold it fixed in the first noise-steering comparison to make attribution possible.
- **Monitor-aware or privileged-object control:** easiest way to build task-stage signals, but changes observations. Use only as a clearly labelled upper-bound/diagnostic track and supply the same information to RL and hand-designed baselines. A categorical monitor describes already-observed events; it is not a predictive shield.
- **Curriculum/counterexample acquisition:** potentially a strong EA application for finding physical failures, but it optimizes the training distribution rather than directly improving a frozen policy. Requires valid scenario generation, matched acquisition costs and a repair consumer that succeeds. Local Part 4 lacks an adequate predictor premise and event diversity. Do not re-enter this chain before the direct adaptation lever is established.
- **VLA LoRA/fine-tuning:** supported by the project but would become policy learning, add training cost and substantially change the current frozen-policy question. Use later as a properly costed adaptation comparison, not an unannounced component.

### Gaps

- No claim that EA will outperform noise-space RL is supported. DSRL/PSS are particularly important baselines. A generic CMA-ES/ES applied to this interface without a mechanism or robust budget advantage could remain an optimizer-substitution paper.
- Source-level support was checked for pi0/pi0.5 through openpi, not GR00T/SmolVLA noise interfaces. Cross-model claims need distinct validated integration paths.
- Low-dimensional noise might influence only sampling jitter, fail to change task stages, amplify instability or require too many coefficients for affordable evolution. Those outcomes would disconfirm this recommendation rather than justify adding a predictor automatically.

## What would a decisive inexpensive test and a valid benchmark win look like?

### Takeaway

Use one small allocation to test behavioural authority and one real evolutionary transition, then compare frozen finalists with a native reference on unused cases. A complete GECCO claim requires repeated optimizers, matched ordinary learning and held-out benchmark evaluation beyond that first allocation. A larger percentage on selected exposed scenes is not “beating ManiGuard”.

### Cited Findings

- The 48-episode local Jar repeat batch recorded 12,967.83 summed process seconds, about 270 seconds per episode under that historical two-worker setup. This arithmetic is not a single-4090 throughput measurement or a guarantee. [Local Jar repeat report](/home/sachin/Desktop/Research/Safety-Dial/docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20Jar%20guard%20and%20motion%20repeats.md).
- The active plan requires the first useful experiment to fit one RTX 4090/5090 and eight allocated hours including setup, smoke checks, exploration and evaluation. It distinguishes raw physical outcomes from intended requested completion and requires all interactions, queries and elapsed allocation to be counted. The current recovery work is planning authority, not permission for another rental. [Active plan](../../history/20261019/phase3.md).
- The local failure investigation found changing first camera inputs/proposals even before controller intervention, and too few candidate observations for stable selection. Its held-state detector and coordinatewise caps also had measurable limitations. Same nominal seed does not establish identical-state physical counterfactuals. [Local Part 1 diagnostic](/home/sachin/Desktop/Research/Safety-Dial/docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20Part1%20failure%20investigation.md).

### Inferences

**Cheap decisive precursor:** before renting, implement and review the server-only noise hook and ledger changes against the actual pinned files; prepare an explicit offline diagnostic over saved development observations. On the machine, assert loaded model shape/dtype, demonstrate that zero-strength uses the native distribution and exact intended RNG stream, and check bounded coefficient changes produce reproducible, structured changes in decoded joint/gripper chunks. Compare response magnitudes and directional changes against ordinary native noise resampling. No simulator is needed for this decoder check; its policy queries and GPU time still count. A saved-input response audit cannot demonstrate a physical gain.

An explicit small response-opportunity budget is 16 prospectively selected development observations from at least four tasks, two native noise draws, and baseline plus positive/negative perturbation along four fixed basis directions. That is 288 decoder calls. Repeating each baseline once for reproducibility adds 32 calls, for **320 calls maximum**. Keep same-noise contrasts distinct from between-noise variability. Include approach, contact, transport and stalled observations through a fixed sampling rule, not only hand-picked failures. Check the executed first-eight decoded actions, full returned chunk, active-coordinate mapping, perturbation response magnitude, direction, joint-limit saturation and gripper flips. Record source observation hashes, sampling keys and noise hashes. This can decide whether the lever changes meaningful proposed behaviour at a plausible amplitude; it cannot tell which alternative chunk is physically safer. Measure those calls' actual latency before deciding the physical allocation.

**Illustrative first physical allocation, dependent on conservative timing:** two methods, EA and uniform random search, four candidates, two evaluated populations, two development episodes per candidate gives 32 training episodes including fresh elite evaluations. That contains one genuine evolutionary transition per run. Freeze each method's finalist before checking it. Compare the two finalists and native identity on four independently specified checking cases each: 12 checking episodes. Total: 44 full Jar episodes, at most 88,000 policy control actions plus initialization, rejected work and short checks. At the historical average this is about 3.3 hours of summed episode process time, leaving nominal room for setup and checking within eight hours, but changed hardware/source/timing may invalidate it. Decide a smaller allocation from throughput before viewing comparative results; do not shorten task horizons. One optimizer seed and four checking episodes per condition are exploratory, not evidence of optimizer superiority.

If additional ordinary-learning integration does not fit the first allocation, do not claim evolutionary advantage. The next full study must compare the same parameterization under EA, RS and suitable noise-space learning, along with identity, a simple fixed perturbation and strong execution controls. Match physical steps, scene exposure and information; also report decoder queries and training time. Require independent optimizer seeds and scene-clustered uncertainty, with method selection before reserved evaluation. Search-budget sensitivity matters: 16 or 32 evaluations could underpower either optimizer.

**Decision logic:**

1. No meaningful decoder authority within bounded noise, or only erratic decoded changes: end this lever and test an audited small action residual. Avoid spending physical search on a frozen decoder that cannot produce useful alternatives.
2. Decoder authority but physical candidates only create waiting, supported-goal loopholes or additional violations: report the failed physical mechanism. This does not justify a larger EA merely because its offline chunks look different.
3. Physical behavioural changes and promising but uncertain safe-completion variation: retain a bounded search and perform independent checking. A hand-written repair need not win first. Do not stop a legitimate comparison because EA is behind RS.
4. Frozen finalist improves on identity but EA ties RS/RL: the adaptation interface helped; no evolutionary superiority. Either develop and test a scientifically motivated evolutionary mechanism or report the limited result.
5. Fresh gains persist across optimizer seeds, new base tasks, and OOD variants: expand family coverage. Jar then Lid/Stack is a coherent competence progression; all-six-family aggregate is a separate, expensive commitment.

**Defensible “beat the benchmark” contract:**

- First claim: improve a named released family checkpoint on prospectively held-out ManiGuard tasks under the same simulator/assets, prompts, difficulty, grasp mode, native horizons, policy checkpoint and outcome definitions. Re-run the native policy contemporaneously. Compare selected subset results only with that subset's reference, never directly with the paper's overall 29.83% ID aggregate.
- Keep all variants of a base task in the same adaptation/development/evaluation partition. Every previously inspected scene is exposed for method development. A fresh rollout of that scene supplies repeatability evidence, not a new unseen-scene claim. Adaptation on reserved scenes is test-time training and requires a distinct protocol; it cannot be described as frozen generalization.
- The original VLA was itself SFT-trained from benchmark-family demonstrations. State this inherited overlap and hold it constant. Our claim concerns additional adaptation exposure and reserved performance, not unseen-from-pretraining tasks unless that can actually be established.
- Report native success, native counted SSR, raw monitor violation counts, engagement and all four success/safety outcomes together. Also report requested completion with explicitly attained prerequisites, preserving the local Jar semantics correction. Neither moving an open supported jar into a goal region nor refusing to engage should become the sought scientific contribution.
- Separate improving the original checkpoint, outperforming the strongest policy on the same family, improving overall suite aggregates, and proving an EA-specific advantage. These are four different achievements. A Jar result can be a credible paper without a claim to dominate every family.
- A whole-suite “beats the benchmark” claim needs a suite-wide, fixed aggregation and matching evaluation conditions with uncertainty, not selected family wins. Visual profile changes, policy-chunk changes and extra privileged observations require their own ablations, not silent integration.

### Gaps

- The exact new train/check/reserved allocation and final statistical threshold are not frozen. The proposed arithmetic is an actionable capacity estimate, not launch approval or a required episode count regardless of timing.
- We do not know cold-start stack readiness, peak memory, rendering reproducibility or actual single-GPU Jar throughput on a new machine. Complete preparation and use conservative preflight timing within the same allocation clock.
- Final novelty is conditional on the parallel evolutionary literature review, including Online-ES. The present review establishes a credible behavioural adaptation interface and a falsifiable test, not a GECCO acceptance prediction.
