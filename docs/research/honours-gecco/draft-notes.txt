Evidence and framing
Task 16 establishes fixed configuration benefits on familiar layouts, with a
separate fresh-case comparison that does not establish a feedback gain. Task 18
extends the matched comparison to PushT and original Cube object moving: at the
largest margins Original SafeCEM versus Tuned fixed is 2/36 versus 19/36 in
PushT and 0/36 versus 12/36 in Cube, with no observed breaches by checked
planners; Feedback (20/36, 12/36) adds no established benefit. No tuned
native-planning ablation exists, so the gain cannot be credited to handling the
safety check rather than better planning in general. Task 17 is the main
evolutionary comparison and has a negative frozen claim gate; eight of ten
evolution selections were shared presets, and on the primary Reacher test the
one evolved controller scored 19/24 against 20/24 for its paired comparators.
Task 19 (ManiGuard Jar, frozen pi0.5): on 107 matched scene-seed pairs a
hand-designed tip shield, whose two numbers CMA-ES left near their start, cuts
violation attempts from 37 to 21 (one-sided Fisher p=0.010, Barnard p=0.008,
Newcombe -15.0 [-26.3, -3.0]) while strict safe completion stays at 32/107 in
both arms (primary endpoint, 0.0 [-12.1, 12.1]). Violations were the key
secondary endpoint (amendment 5), with no multiplicity correction fixed in
advance; the design inspected five native confirmation violations, and the
cleanest post hoc subset gives p=0.075. The Barnard values were corrected on
27 October (arms belong in columns); the receipt records the correction.
Task 15's confirmation was lost with two rented hosts and is not used.
The UNISafe grasp-sensing ablation is confirmed within its study; the archived
16 October UNISafe optimizer comparison found no evolution advantage either.
The UNISafe actor is Dreamer-derived, not a VLA. Zero observed breaches is not
a guarantee, and rates are never pooled across studies.
