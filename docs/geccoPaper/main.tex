\begin{abstract}
Evolutionary search in a learned world model can select policies whose imagined
safety does not survive execution. We audit this effect in Walker2d by evolving
residual policies inside a frozen visual latent model and replaying selected
policies from identical saved simulator states. A prospectively locked study
crosses latent noise and a return-range safety penalty over 20 paired search
seeds, three population sizes, and 320 fresh final source episodes. Increasing
search from population 16 at generation 1 to population 256 at generation 16
increases the control's real-minus-imagined failure gap by 10.39 percentage points
(adjusted 97.5\% interval: 6.70 to 14.25). Noise plus penalty reduces this
amplification by 7.22 points (2.49 to 12.18). At high search pressure, actual
failure falls from 29.56\% to 22.36\%, retaining 99.04\% of control progress.
However, the starting controller fails on only 16.88\% of the same episodes.
The result demonstrates partial mitigation of optimization-induced harm,
not a safe controller or an improvement over the starting policy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Related Work}
Learned world models make repeated policy evaluation inexpensive, but search
optimizes the learned evaluator rather than the physical system. Errors that
appear modest on ordinary trajectories can become consequential when selection
repeatedly rewards optimistic predictions. For safety, a policy may appear to
avoid failure while violating the constraint when executed.

This problem is not new. Ha and Schmidhuber evolved controllers in learned
dreams and used stochasticity to improve transfer~\citep{ha2018worldmodels}.
Recent action-sequence audits report that larger proposal pools can worsen
selection under latent prediction error~\citep{li2026asar}. Safety-aware
model-based planning also predates this study: Guided Safe Shooting uses
MAP-Elites planners~\citep{paolo2022guss}, while SafeDreamer combines world-model
planning and Lagrangian methods~\citep{huang2024safedreamer}. We do not claim
that noisy imagination, safe evolutionary planning, or return penalties are
new individually.

Our contribution is a controlled empirical audit of \emph{evolved policy}
selection in a frozen visual world model. We measure a predeclared change in
real-minus-imagined failure as search pressure increases, test a four-arm
noise/penalty combination, and separately check actual failure and useful
progress. The final comparison uses fresh source episodes and freezes every
selected policy before inspecting its simulator outcomes. The starting
controller remains an explicit reference: reducing the damage caused by search
is distinct from improving that controller.

\section{Policy Search and Safety Measurement}
\paragraph{Environment and frozen assets.}
We use the Walker2d simulator underlying \texttt{SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1},
with a \emph{health} constraint: torso height must remain strictly between
0.8 and 2.0\,m, and pitch between $-1$ and $1$ rad. This differs from the
benchmark's velocity-cost constraint. Actual failure means any violation at
one of the 100 simulator steps in a 0.8\,s branch. Termination is disabled for
branch measurement, retaining post-failure states and progress. ``Real'' below
means execution in MuJoCo, not on physical hardware.

A previously trained Walker2d LeWorldModel (LeWM)~\citep{maes2026lewm}, its
192-dimensional encoder, physical readouts, and six-member visual-history base
controller remain frozen. We evolve a linear residual with 1,020 parameters
($60\times17$) on a fixed 16-dimensional projection plus bias. Each visual
update produces ten sequential six-motor actions; actions are clipped to
$[-1,1]$. Visual feedback runs at 12.5\,Hz and actions at 125\,Hz. The base gain
is fixed at one. The residual starts at zero, with RMS-preconditioned coordinates
and initial search scale chosen for an expected unclipped action RMS of 0.05.

\paragraph{Noise and scoring.}
At each predicted block, we perturb the latent before readout and subsequent
policy feedback:
\begin{equation}
 z_{t+1}=M(z_{t-2:t},a_{t-2:t})+k\,\sigma\odot\epsilon_t,
 \qquad \epsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
The fixed per-coordinate scale $\sigma$ is the sample standard deviation of
one-step teacher-forced residuals on earlier development data. It is not a
state-dependent uncertainty estimate or a calibrated safety bound. We compare
$k\in\{0,1\}$. The noisy latent enters later history windows as well as the
policy and health readout.

Let $v$ indicate any imagined health violation and $u$ denote imagined forward
progress strictly before the first violating block, or the full horizon if
safe. The two fitness rules are
\begin{equation}
 F_0=\mathbb{E}[u], \qquad
 F_P=\mathbb{E}\left[u+v(V_{\min}-V_{\max})\right].
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Both rules truncate the scored task return identically; the control simply has
no additional violation penalty. $V_{\min}$ and $V_{\max}$ are cumulative
extrema of observed task returns from that search's fitness batches, updated
once per generation before ranking. This is a \rosarl{}-style adaptation of
the return-range penalty~\citep{tasse2026simple}, not the original algorithm
or a transfer of its safety guarantees. Rollout computation itself continues
after violation.

\paragraph{Evolution and selection.}
Separable CMA-ES~\citep{ros2008sep} searches with populations 16, 64, and 256
for 16 generations. Each generation uses 32 roots sampled without replacement
from 96 fitness episodes, shared across arms for a paired seed/generation.
Noisy fitness uses four samples per root. One nominee per generation is
rescored on 64 fixed selection roots, using 16 samples for $k=1$ and the
current fitness-only penalty bounds. At generations 1, 4, and 16, the best
of the baseline and all nominees so far is selected. Zero-noise queries use
one sample. Candidate noise streams are shared within each role, while fitness,
selection, and final evaluation use separate streams.



\section{Experimental Protocol}
The design in Figure~\ref{fig:protocol} uses twenty paired search seeds, giving $20\times4\times3=240$ searches and 720
selected policy slots. The model, controller, noise scale, sample size, endpoints,
and analysis were locked before collecting 480 fresh source episodes. A
four-seed development pilot informed a timing and precision review; the final
20-seed design fit a predeclared 12-hour cap. Earlier controller-readiness and
joint transfer screens had failed, and the continuation did not relabel them
as passed.

Episodes come from a fixed competent-actor pool with action-noise scales
0, 0.05, and 0.10. Collection stops at the first health violation or 1,000 steps.
For each sufficiently long episode, one root is sampled with a three-frame
history and room for a 100-step recorded continuation. This conditions the
source distribution on survival of the collection policy around the root;
it is not an arbitrary-start safety evaluation. The 96/64/320 fitness,
selection, and final roles have disjoint episode seeds. The final 320 episodes
are reused across policies for pairing, not treated as 6,400 independent
observations per method.

Every selected policy is executed from the same final snapshots used in its
imagined audit. It is audited at both $k=0$ and $k=1$, with 32 samples per root
for the latter. The declared primary gap uses each method's own noise setting:
\begin{equation}
 D_{m,p}=\overline{v}^{\,\mathrm{real}}_{m,p}
          -\overline{v}^{\,\mathrm{imag}}_{m,p},\qquad
 A_m=D_{m,H}-D_{m,L},
\end{equation}
where $L=(16,1)$ and $H=(256,16)$ specify population and generation. The two
co-primary contrasts are $A_C$ for control and $A_{NP}-A_C$ for noise plus
penalty versus control. Gap reduction alone could reflect increased imagined
failure, so actual failure and progress are assessed separately.

We use 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates, independently resampling search
seeds and source episodes with replacement, preserving method/pressure pairing.
The two primary intervals have 97.5\% coverage each (Bonferroni family coverage
95\%). Other intervals are descriptive 95\% intervals. Practical criteria
require a negative actual-failure difference and a lower progress-ratio bound
above 0.90. Inference is conditional on the frozen assets and shared
fitness/selection banks.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Both declared primary comparisons are supported.}
The control gap increases by 10.39 percentage points (pp), with adjusted
97.5\% interval $[6.70,14.25]$. Noise plus penalty reduces this amplification
by 7.22\,pp, with contrast interval $[-12.18,-2.49]$
(Figure~\ref{fig:primary}). These are contrasts between low and high pressure,
not the high-pressure gap itself. Figure~\ref{fig:pressure} shows all grid cells.

\paragraph{Actual failures fall relative to the evolved control.}
Figure~\ref{fig:walker} illustrates a missed violation. At high pressure, the combined method fails on 22.36\% of paired evaluations
versus 29.56\% for control (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The paired difference is
$-7.20$\,pp, descriptive 95\% interval $[-10.98,-3.70]$. Mean full-horizon
forward progress is 2.146 versus 2.167\,m: the ratio is 0.9904, interval
$[0.9824,0.9978]$, exceeding the 0.90 retention threshold. Both practical
criteria pass, with a small measured progress reduction of about 0.96\%.

The four arms separate noise and penalty descriptively. At high pressure,
adding the penalty without noise changes actual failure by $-2.09$\,pp
($[-5.39,0.59]$); with noise the change is $-3.30$\,pp
($[-6.16,-0.48]$). These secondary intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted
and do not establish an interaction. The noisy search also uses more model
samples, so a noise-specific effect at equal model-compute cost is not isolated.

\paragraph{The starting controller remains safer.}
The baseline fails on 54/320 episodes (16.88\%). The combined method increases
failure relative to it by 5.48\,pp ($[2.56,8.44]$). The result therefore supports
\emph{partial mitigation of harm from optimization}, not improvement over the
starting controller. Imagined failure is still much lower than actual failure:
4.95\% versus 22.36\% for the combined method. These imagined probabilities
are not calibrated risks, and the primary gap is not conditional false-safe
acceptance among accepted policies.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
This study covers one environment, one frozen model/controller combination,
and short branches on a survival-conditioned source distribution. Development
choices, including $k=1$, were informed by earlier observations, although final
episodes and analysis endpoints were separated prospectively. Model seeds,
fitness-bank variation, full episodes, other environments, and a real-fitness
search control are not tested. Baseline health failures and the failed earlier
transfer screens preclude a safe-controller claim.

Table~\ref{tab:costs} separates collection, search, and audit costs. The main run
completed in 6.819 hours with zero gradient updates.
The preserved evolution-stage ledger totals 26,838,972 simulator steps, with
3,600,058 additional shared original collection steps. Historical actor-training
and other upstream costs remain unquantified; these are not lifetime totals.
An independent audit checked 118 frozen source files and 11,322 numeric query
archives and reproduced the analysis from saved outputs. The exact main bundle
is privately archived; it is not yet an anonymous public replication package.

Evolutionary pressure measurably amplifies a safety-relevant model error.
Residual-scaled latent noise and a return-range penalty reduce that
amplification and actual failures against the evolved control, but do not undo
all damage from search. Independent simulator evaluation and comparison with
the starting policy are essential to interpreting the apparent improvement.
