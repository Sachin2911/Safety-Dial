# Evolving safe imagination across models

**The preferred idea is scientifically coherent, but its contribution remains unresolved.** Agents already learn which imagined states to expand, which actions to investigate, when to stop planning and when to act; recent work also uses predicted risk to allocate latent imagination. These precedents substantially narrow the original pitch. ([IBP](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06170), [Thinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14993), [RISE](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20430)) Evolutionary discovery of reusable planning rules also has a long history, and a verified GECCO 2026 paper already evolves RL algorithms that use latent planning during learning. ([Evolved MCTS](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cazenave/papers/evolveMC.pdf), [GECCO accepted papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted%2BPapers), [2026 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28416)) The strongest question to retain is **when a discovered choice of model queries improves actual safe decisions beyond what established planners achieve with the same information and computation**. Testing a frozen procedure with different world models could help explain whether it learned a useful planning strategy or relied on one model's peculiarities. That is a demanding scientific question, not yet a verified literature gap or a reason to begin implementation.

Research assessed on **7 October 2026**. This report develops the [preferred candidate](../research_notes/evolving-safe-imagination-20261007.md). It does not adopt a method, replace the current project plan, impose the November deadline or assume reuse of previous environments and infrastructure. No experiments or compatibility tests were performed for this review.

## Learning how to imagine is already established

The motivating idea remains clear: an agent should use an internal predictive model to examine consequences before acting. Here, an **imagination controller** means a decision procedure that chooses computations through that model, then selects a physical action. Its possible operations include extending a prediction, exploring another branch, revisiting an earlier imagined state and ending deliberation. This definition keeps the interesting object visible: the agent's allocation and use of predictive computation.

The closest early precedent is Pascanu and colleagues' 2017 preprint *Learning model-based planning from scratch*, usually called IBP. Its manager chooses whether to act or imagine and which previous real or imagined state to expand. A controller proposes actions, while memory aggregates planning information. It therefore already includes flexible branching, backtracking and execution decisions. ([IBP](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06170)) Hamrick and colleagues' *Metacontrol for Adaptive Imagination-Based Optimization*, also from 2017, learns how much internal optimisation to perform and which predictive expert to consult when experts differ in reliability and expense. Its contextual-bandit spaceship setting is narrower than sequential safe robot control, but the central allocation problem is explicit. ([Metacontrol](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.02670))

*Thinker*, published at NeurIPS 2023, makes model interactions part of an augmented action space. An RL agent chooses imaginary actions, resets search to its root and ultimately acts in the environment. Its actor receives search statistics and model-derived information, so it learns actual search behaviour through external rewards. ([Thinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14993)) *Dynamic Thinker*, an ALA 2025 workshop paper, adds variable stopping with a computation penalty. Its small knapsack experiments demonstrate context-dependent search allocation, although they do not establish robotic safety with learned latent dynamics. ([Dynamic Thinker](https://ala-workshop.github.io/papers/ALA2025_paper_44.pdf)) These comparisons matter because the proposed method would otherwise risk being evaluated only against fixed-depth planners that lack capabilities already demonstrated elsewhere.

The closest safety-specific overlap is **RISE**, an August 2026 preprint. It predicts risk and the benefit of further imagination, then chooses Roll or Stop using those estimates, depth and computation cost. Supervised gate targets come from evaluating available depths during training. Its driving experiments include integration with DAWN while leaving DAWN's predictor and planner unchanged. The manuscript does not clearly establish that the evaluator and gate also transfer without retraining. It supports safety-informed adaptive depth and another architecture, while frozen cross-family transfer remains unverified. ([RISE, sections 4.3, 4.4 and 5.4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20430))

Other neighbouring claims are occupied too. *Imagination-Augmented Agents* learns how to interpret imperfect predicted trajectories; *MCTSnets* learns search traversal, evaluation and backup operations. ([I2A](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06203), [MCTSnets](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/guez18a.html)) *When to Trust Imagination*, a May 2026 preprint, learns a verifier that decides whether to continue an action chunk or replan using predictions and current observations. *AdaReP*, accepted at ICANN 2026 according to its revised manuscript, uses prediction deviation and local sensitivity to adapt replanning across several model types; its transfer protocol permits backbone-level tuning. ([When to Trust Imagination](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06222), [AdaReP](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23079)) Learning to ignore unreliable predictions or wrapping several predictive backends therefore also requires a more precise claim.

The useful conceptual distinction is **danger versus the value of investigating further**. An obviously unsafe action can be discarded cheaply. An apparently good action can deserve more investigation if an additional prediction would change the choice. This is our interpretation of the candidate through the established value-of-computation literature, which evaluates computations by their expected improvement to the eventual decision. ([Selecting Computations](https://proceedings.mlr.press/r10/hay12a.html)) Predictive error adds another problem: deeper search can reveal consequences already encoded by a model, but cannot reliably recover a hazard that the model systematically omits. Theory about unfinished search with known dynamics should not be presented as a theorem about unknown model error. ([Static and Dynamic Values of Computation in MCTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.04335))

## Evolution needs to explain a discovered structure

Several different objects can be evolved: the next action sequence, a physical policy, numerical settings of an existing planner, reusable rules controlling search, or an algorithm that trains another agent. The preferred candidate concerns reusable runtime planning logic. That distinction helps position the work, but does not establish novelty.

*Evolutionary Planning in Latent Space* evolves the current action sequence through learned latent dynamics and carries part of the plan into the next decision. It is relevant evolutionary planning prior work, although the evolving object differs from the proposed reusable procedure. ([EPLS](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.11293)) At the latter level, EVOCK already evolved planning-control heuristics in 2001. Cazenave's 2007 manuscript evolved mathematical rules controlling Monte Carlo tree search and examined a simplified rule on larger boards and budgets. ([EVOCK](https://e-archivo.uc3m.es/rest/api/core/bitstreams/1c55d9d2-574e-42b3-a8f3-c41b6ded31b0/content), [Evolved MCTS](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cazenave/papers/evolveMC.pdf)) More recently, AAAI 2025's GP2S evolved node-scoring expressions inside branch and bound, including tests on larger held-out instances. Compact rules, controlled execution cost and transfer are established algorithm-discovery aims. ([GP2S](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09444))

The most consequential venue-specific finding is **Sygkounas, Loutfi and Persson's *Evolutionary Discovery of Reinforcement Learning Algorithms via Large Language Models***, accepted in GECCO 2026's EML track. The official programme verifies acceptance; the publisher DOI is 10.1145/3795095.3805180. ([Official programme](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted%2BPapers), [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1145/3795095.3805180)) Its examined March manuscript evolves executable learning rules using LLM mutation and crossover. Reported algorithms use learned latent dynamics, planning-derived training targets and confidence-weighted rollouts. Evaluation on additional environments includes post-evolution hyperparameter tuning and peak-checkpoint selection. This is substantial overlap with evolutionary discovery using imagination, although it does not establish a frozen runtime model-query controller transferring between world-model families. The final publisher text and an author code release were not retrieved, so method comparisons remain tied to that manuscript. ([Inspected manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28416))

The candidate thus needs a reason why evolution is useful at this level. One coherent possibility is **structural discovery**: evolution searches over different ways of arranging computations, and the resulting arrangement explains an observable improvement. A compact expression ranking branches exposes a narrow part of planning. A bounded program choosing between branch, extend and stop exposes more structure, while creating harder search and credit assignment. A recurrent controller offers flexible memory, but its closest comparison is an RL-trained imagination controller. None should be selected merely because the project must include an evolutionary algorithm.

The operation vocabulary itself contains assumptions. If one operation already means “investigate until delayed danger is resolved”, its designer has supplied the central behaviour. Even simple prediction operations depend on action proposals, cost readouts and memory supplied elsewhere. Typed programs and protected arithmetic can constrain execution, but do not establish safe behaviour. These are design questions about what evolution can actually discover.

Consequently, the meaningful comparisons extend beyond an unevolved baseline. Numerical configuration of an existing planner tests whether changing settings explains the gain. Random search over the same valid program space tests the value of the evolutionary search process. RL-trained metacontrol tests whether the same operations and observations suffice without evolutionary discovery. Structural ablations test whether the selected program's distinctive logic affects decisions. These are criteria for assessing a future claim, rather than an implementation prescription.

The ICLR 2021 work *Evolving Reinforcement Learning Algorithms* provides another useful methodological precedent: typed computation graphs, screening expensive candidates and evaluating discovered rules beyond search environments. Its learned object is an update rule. ([Evolving RL Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.03958)) The general lesson is to state exactly what transfers and what the search contributed. A credible GECCO contribution could concern a new discovery mechanism or an explanatory result about evolved planning structure. Changing the optimiser, adding a safety penalty or supporting another backend does not yet supply that result.

## Changing the model changes the information problem

LeWM and SafeDreamer are promising contrasting foundations, provided their roles are stated correctly. **SafeDreamer is a complete safe RL method**, not simply a predictive architecture. Its online variants use constrained CEM with actor and Gaussian proposals, reward and cost estimates, and critics. Its OSRP-Lag variant already addresses consequences beyond the explicit rollout horizon through long-term cost evaluation. It credits constrained CEM to Wen and Topcu. Its expected-cost constraint should not be interpreted as a guarantee of zero failures. ([SafeDreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3))

LeWM supplies an image encoder and action-conditioned latent prediction, with published planning based on latent goal distance. It does not assign an intrinsic safety meaning to its embeddings. Its predictor also uses task-dependent observation history. ([LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312)) Official code exposes useful encode, predict and rollout operations, but these are sequence operations rather than a validated generic safety-planning interface. Its inspected inference path produces point predictions; an ensemble or another uncertainty mechanism would be an additional component. ([LeWM predictor code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/main/jepa.py), [Model modules](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/main/module.py))

SafeDreamer's recurrent state needs different bookkeeping. Its RSSM contains deterministic and stochastic state, and observation assimilation uses previous state and action. Branching must preserve that context and define how stochastic samples are shared or resampled. ([Agent code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PKU-Alignment/SafeDreamer/main/SafeDreamer/agent.py), [RSSM code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PKU-Alignment/SafeDreamer/main/SafeDreamer/nets.py)) Its planner code also distinguishes learned cost prediction from cost calculated geometrically using reconstructed coordinates. Those variants have different supervision and information advantages. ([Planner code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PKU-Alignment/SafeDreamer/main/SafeDreamer/behaviors.py))

A plausible interface could keep model states opaque while exposing prediction operations, branch ancestry, action-specific progress and cost sequences, physical duration and remaining resources. This remains an untested design proposal. Raw latent coordinates have no shared meaning across these models. Nor does rescaling goal distance turn it into expected reward, or turn predicted cumulative cost into failure probability. State history, action duration, continuation and terminal-value conventions must be declared explicitly.

There is a tension between portability and information. Scalar final reward and cost can discard the temporal pattern that would make branching useful. Rich learned summaries can preserve it, but might themselves solve the difficult decision problem. A module predicting exactly which branch deserves investigation would move much of the intelligence out of the evolved controller. Likewise, a strong cost critic can expose a delayed hazard without an explicit deep rollout. A fair interpretation must identify whether improvement comes from the planner, its readouts, its critics or its action proposals.

Uncertainty also requires a declared meaning. A stochastic latent prediction is not automatically a calibrated measure of missing knowledge. UNISafe uses a separately trained ensemble of latent predictors; its appendix cautions against reading RSSM distribution variance as calibrated uncertainty. ([UNISafe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779)) Repeated samples can reduce sampling error under a particular model, while leaving systematic model bias intact. No uncertainty machinery, HJ filter or conformal method is adopted by this report.

Different portability results would support different conclusions:

| Evidence | What it establishes | What remains unestablished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One framework calls both backends | Interface compatibility | A useful transferable procedure |
| A new procedure is evolved for each backend | Applicability of the discovery method | Transfer of a frozen procedure |
| A fixed procedure handles held-out goals or layouts | Generalisation within the declared task distribution | New task-family or model-family transfer |
| A procedure trained using several model families handles new instances | Generalisation after joint exposure | Transfer to a previously unseen family |
| A frozen procedure works with an unseen model family | Transfer under the declared interface and adaptation rules | Independence from adapter learning, or universal planning ability |

For the final row, the program, selected constants and normalisation policy must have an explicit frozen boundary. Fitting a new readout or adapter can be legitimate, but changes the claim from transfer with no adaptation. Changing architecture on the same physical task would answer a different question from simultaneously changing sensors, actions, hazards and task family. Both can be useful; they should not be collapsed into one score.

Finally, query count is not a universal compute currency. Action repeats differ, a model step can advance a different amount of physical time, and sequential branch choices can lose the efficiency of batched rollouts. SafeDreamer's configurations already vary action repeat. ([Configuration source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PKU-Alignment/SafeDreamer/main/SafeDreamer/configs.yaml)) Comparable within-backend query budgets help isolate allocation, while actual latency and performance-versus-computation curves expose deployment tradeoffs. Discovery costs must also include candidate evaluations, model and readout training, discarded attempts and any backend adaptation.

## Two conditional questions survive the strongest objections

**First: under what conditions does discovering the structure of model queries improve safe decisions beyond configuring an established planner?** This formulation preserves the agent-focused interest and gives evolution a substantive object. The potential finding would connect a particular discovered rule to the consequences it reveals and the physical decisions it changes. For example, a useful rule could allocate depth differently when apparently successful plans conceal delayed consequences, or distribute search across alternatives when dangerous outcomes concentrate among the highest-scoring candidates. These are illustrative hypotheses, not proposed discoveries.

The motivating premise must be established: safety-relevant information is available in predictions, yet existing allocation strategies use it poorly at the relevant computation budget. Delayed hazards alone do not establish that premise, because longer horizons, terminal critics and existing adaptive methods already address them. If a simple tuned horizon or threshold captures the benefit, the result concerns that simpler rule. If an RL-trained controller discovers the same behaviour more efficiently, the rationale for evolution must rest on another demonstrated advantage, such as a useful representation or an inspectable structure.

What would weaken this question is as important as a favourable score. If the gain disappears after equalising action proposals, prediction work or runtime, the proposed explanation fails. If removing the distinctive branch-selection clause leaves actions and outcomes unchanged, that clause has not explained the improvement. If disrupting which prediction belongs to which candidate does not hurt performance, the agent may be relying on a reactive shortcut. Such interventions would connect an internal planning trace to a causal behavioural claim, rather than treating an attractive visualisation as evidence of reasoning.

Before adopting this version, conceptual clarification is needed about the particular planning bottleneck, the operations available to the controller, the safety event and the information needed to recognise unresolved consequences. It is premature to choose genetic programming, a neural controller or an environment before explaining that bottleneck. A small expressive language is a possible research instrument, not the present outcome of the review.

**Second: which parts of a discovered planning strategy remain useful when the predictive model changes?** This makes the user's multiple-model interest a way to challenge an explanation. A procedure evolved against one model might depend on its score scales, typical bias or confidence patterns. Keeping the procedure fixed while changing the backend could reveal whether it learned a more general relationship between query choice and decision quality. The informative result would identify what survives, why it survives, and where it stops helping.

This version requires more than showing that the same API works. Conventional black-box planners can already call different models. The key comparison is whether the discovered strategy retains a particular advantage over such planners through the same interface. A rule that always uses one fixed depth might transfer perfectly, while teaching little about adaptive imagination. Conversely, transfer failure caused by inconsistent cost units would diagnose the interface, not necessarily the planning rule. Any model-specific fitting therefore has to remain visible.

Model error gives this question an essential boundary. More depth can accumulate bias; more breadth can find predictions that look falsely favourable. Outer selection on actual simulator outcomes helps judge such behaviour, but can still reward memorisation of development conditions. The review's strongest information argument is simple: **when distinct physical situations requiring different safe actions look identical through every observation and model response available to the controller, querying that interface cannot distinguish them**. This is a limitation of the information supplied, not a claim that all world models suffer it. Real observations can add information, but then sensing, action commitment and the resulting interactions become part of the mechanism under study.

Before adopting this second version, the unresolved issues are the frozen boundary, the semantics of shared summaries, the chosen kind of shift and the proposed mechanism's predicted failure conditions. No common task/checkpoint pair, readout quality or branch-equivalence result has yet been established for LeWM and SafeDreamer. Source inspection supports plausibility, not demonstrated feasibility. Cross-model work should test a scientific explanation rather than become an integration exercise added to strengthen a thin novelty claim.

Both versions require safety and useful progress to remain visible separately. Lower violation counts obtained by refusing the task do not establish better safe decisions. Actual outcomes must remain distinct from imagined costs, and finite observations cannot certify zero failure probability. Search and evaluation also need separate cases: selecting the best of many noisy candidate estimates can favour lucky programs even when the world model is frozen. All real simulator interactions, including model-training data and unsuccessful outer-search evaluations, count toward the method's cost.

ROSARL offers an optional connection to the outer objective. The 2023 theory concerns a specified stochastic-shortest-path setting with unsafe absorbing states and particular policy assumptions; its safety criterion minimises unsafe-terminal probability and need not attain zero. Its guarantees do not transfer automatically to continuing costs, approximate evolution or erroneous predictions. The 2026 value-range formulation is a distinct source. ([ROSARL](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.00035), [2026 RLJ record](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.html)) Supervisor expertise can help clarify the scoring question, but the proposal need not combine every interest before its central mechanism is understood.

## Further reading should settle the remaining boundaries

A focused reading sequence can now replace broad idea collecting. Start with **IBP and Thinker**, comparing the operations they expose and what information their controllers receive. Together they establish the closest runtime-planning problem. ([IBP](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06170), [Thinker](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14993)) Read **RISE** next, especially its risk/benefit evaluator, supervision and DAWN protocol, to determine what a safety-sensitive extension would need to add. Its frozen-transfer ambiguity remains an open bibliographic question. ([RISE](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20430))

Then read **SafeDreamer**, concentrating on the different online and background variants, the information supplied by critics and the definition of cost feasibility. This prevents a proposed delayed-hazard mechanism from recreating an existing one. ([SafeDreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3)) Follow with **Selecting Computations**, which supplies the decision-theoretic distinction between concerning predictions and computations worth purchasing. ([Hay and colleagues](https://proceedings.mlr.press/r10/hay12a.html))

For the evolutionary contribution, read the **GECCO 2026 discovery paper**, then **Cazenave's evolved MCTS study** and **GP2S**. The first is the closest venue-specific overlap; the latter two clarify what it means to discover reusable search structure and demonstrate its usefulness outside the search cases. ([GECCO manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28416), [Evolved MCTS](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cazenave/papers/evolveMC.pdf), [GP2S](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09444)) These eight papers form a focused core. LeWM's paper and the two official repositories then answer interface questions once a precise claim emerges.

Coverage here is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review or a proof of absence. Methods were inspected for the closest imagination, safety and evolutionary-discovery papers; some neighbouring works received narrower screening. Searches covered learned imagination, metareasoning, evolutionary planner discovery, safe adaptive planning and cross-model integration, with backward references and selected forward searches. They did not exhaust citation indexes or all classical planning and hyper-heuristic literature. Recent preprints remain author-reported evidence, and the reviewed code was not executed.

Two concrete source uncertainties should remain visible: the final publisher version and implementation of the GECCO 2026 paper were unavailable, and RISE's manuscript and release do not resolve frozen evaluator/gate transfer. More broadly, the review has not established an environment in which the proposed allocation bottleneck is both present and inadequately handled by the strongest existing methods. That is the central conceptual question left open, rather than an invitation to claim an untouched intersection.

The supporting research records preserve detailed sources and search limits: [learned imagination and metareasoning](../research_notes/Evolving%20safe%20imagination%20across%20models/learned_imagination_and_metareasoning.md), [evolution and algorithm discovery](../research_notes/Evolving%20safe%20imagination%20across%20models/evolution_and_algorithm_discovery.md), and [safety and cross-model critique](../research_notes/Evolving%20safe%20imagination%20across%20models/safety_and_cross_model_critique.md). Earlier broad brainstorming does not override these narrower findings.

## Conclusion

The next intellectual step is to specify a failure of existing deliberation and explain what additional computation would change the decision. That would turn enthusiasm for an agent that imagines into a research question with a clear object of discovery. A successful outcome could be a compact rule with a demonstrated causal effect and well-explained limits; a useful negative outcome could show that simpler allocation already captures the available benefit. Neither requires presenting evolution as the invention of model-based reasoning.

Multiple world models are most valuable here as a stress test of the explanation. If a rule helps only because one model has a particular bias, that dependence should be visible. If it remains useful after a meaningful change in predictive architecture, information quality or task, that would strengthen the account of what it learned. The idea merits continued investigation on that basis, while the method, novelty claim and implementation commitment remain deliberately open.
