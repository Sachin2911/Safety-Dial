# Measure how evolution games imagined safety

The strongest bet for both the thesis and GECCO 2027 is to make world-model exploitation the contribution: evolve compact policies inside the LeWM's imagination with constraint handling that needs no penalty weight, use exact MuJoCo snapshot branching to measure how quickly selection pressure turns imagined-safe elites into real violations, and show which evolution-native mitigations close that gap at a matched real-evaluation budget. Repeated, differently phrased searches across five research threads found **no paper that evolves a deployable safe policy inside a pixel-trained latent world model and measures imagined against real constraint violations**; the nearest neighbours are GuSS (a 2022/2024 preprint that uses MAP-Elites as a cost-first planner on low-dimensional states and names model uncertainty as its future work), Ha & Schmidhuber's 2018 dream-versus-real table, and a July 2026 result that larger candidate pools in latent planners pick infeasible plans more often. Much of the surrounding ground is taken: feasibility-first CEM dates from 2018 and 2020 and reappeared at ICLR 2024, so the student's Safe CEM is a baseline rather than a contribution, conformal robust MPC on a LeWM variant (including Push-T) appeared in June 2026, and Dreamer-style Lagrangian agents and latent Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters are crowded. The lead study should be paired with a cheap real-environment benchmark of evolutionary constraint handling against PPO-Lagrangian on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1, which answers the supervisor's "no hand-crafted penalties" brief directly and exposes a testable bridge: under rank-based selection, ROSARL's minmax penalty reduces to Deb's feasibility rule. A safety-indexed MAP-Elites/CMA-MAE archive belongs in December and January as a stretch goal. The binding constraint is time, not compute: the 8 November results freeze is 36 days away, so the thesis core must be the exploitation curve plus one or two mitigations, with the full comparison built for a GECCO deadline that is unpublished but likely falls between 19 and 26 January 2027. Before committing, the student must run the outstanding novelty sweeps, settle the ROSARL citation (the evidence points to RLC 2026, not 2024), and measure local throughput.

## Every ingredient is published; the combination is not

The idea of evolving a controller inside a learned model's dream starts with Ha & Schmidhuber. They trained an **867-parameter linear controller with CMA-ES (population 64, 16 rollouts per candidate)** entirely inside an MDN-RNN dream of VizDoom and then transferred it. Transfer depended on a temperature knob. At τ=0.10 the agent scored **2086±140 in the dream but 193±58 in reality**, below the random policy's 210. At τ=1.15 it scored 918 in the dream and 1092 in reality, above the 750 solve threshold ([Ha & Schmidhuber](https://worldmodels.github.io/); [NeurIPS 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01999)). The lineage then split four ways. One branch evolved the world model itself against real-environment fitness ([Risi & Stanley, GECCO 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08857)). A second ran evolutionary or CEM *planning* in a latent model, which is how every JEPA model is used today; LeWM plans with CEM over 300 samples at horizon 5 ([EPLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11293); [LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)). A third used rank-based CMA-ES against uncertain Gaussian-process models in data-efficient robotics ([Black-DROPS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.07261)). The fourth ran quality-diversity in imagination: DA-QD was about **20× more sample-efficient** than model-free QD, but it conceded that real coverage falls below the imagined repertoire because of model error ([DA-QD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522)). The research found no 2023–2026 paper that trains a standalone policy by ES, GA or CMA-ES purely inside a pixel-trained RSSM, transformer or JEPA world model and reports real transfer.

Safety narrows the field further. GuSS runs MAP-Elites as a per-step MPC planner inside a learned dynamics model. Its archive replacement rule is lexicographic: lower cost wins, and ties go to higher reward. It was tested on Safe Pendulum, Safe Acrobot and Safety-Gym SafeCar-Goal against CEM, RCEM, Safe-MBPO, CPO and PPO-Lagrangian. Its authors state that "if the model is wrong, it could easily lead the agent to unsafe states" and list "incorporating model uncertainty with QD" as future work ([GuSS v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2)). GuSS differs from the student's setting in four ways, and each one is a novelty point: it uses low-dimensional states rather than pixel-trained latents, it plans per step rather than evolving an amortised policy, it has no uncertainty handling, and it has no ground truth for measuring false-safe rates. It also remains an unreviewed preprint.

Older work and the 2024–2026 surge have already claimed several things a naive proposal would otherwise list as contributions. **Feasibility-first elite selection is old.** Wen & Topcu's constrained cross-entropy method ranks feasible policies by objective and infeasible ones by constraint value ([CCE, NeurIPS 2018](https://papers.NeurIPS.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/34ffeb359a192eb8174b6854643cc046-Paper.pdf)). RCE applies the same rule to action sequences using worst-case ensemble cost ([RCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968)), and SafeDreamer's CCEM uses it inside a world model ([SafeDreamer, ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The student's Safe CEM therefore has to be presented as a credited baseline. **Conformal safe MPC on LeWM is also taken.** SLS² puts conformal dynamics-error sets, a conformal in-distribution support set and a conformalised constraint classifier into robust MPC on a LeWM variant, using a gradient-based SLS/iLQR optimiser, on Reacher, Cube, Push-T and rope ([SLS²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594)). Four more areas are crowded: Dreamer-family Lagrangian agents ([PIGDreamer, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02159); [Nightmare Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04686)), latent Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters ([LatentSafe, RSS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935); [UNISafe, CoRL 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/seo25a.html); [LatentCBF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606)), frozen-JEPA failure monitors ([FARM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)), and shields that filter sampled candidates for an action-conditioned JEPA ([JEPA shields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17496)).

The student's Push-T diagnosis is still useful, provided it is framed correctly. Attributing 75% of false-safe decisions to imagined dynamics maps directly onto Kim, Nakamura & Bajcsy's split between **prediction gaps and estimation gaps** ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)). Citing that split anchors the student's diagnostic in the latent-safety literature.

The opening is in what 2026's audits of latent world models have *not* measured. "Proposal overgeneration" shows that raising a latent planner's candidate pool from **72 to 288 cut the feasibility of minimum-cost selection from 0.375 to 0.062** on Cube, even though every larger pool contained a feasible sequence ([ASAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)). ARC-Bench finds that frozen JEPA world models rank candidate actions wrongly and that closed-loop replanning masks the defect ([ARC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)). The intervention-gap study finds that ensemble disagreement ranks out-of-distribution errors poorly ([Intervention gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)). Backdoored world models steer CEM and Dreamer-style actors to an attacker's chosen action ([Backdoors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15781)). All four results concern goal costs or action ranking. **None measures how a constraint predictor's false-safe rate scales with selection pressure**, and none tests mitigations that act on the selection operator rather than on the model. That gap belongs naturally to evolutionary computation.

## One factorial design serves thesis and paper

The cleanest design crosses two factors. The first is the **evaluation substrate**: real MuJoCo, LeWM imagination, and LeWM imagination with a mitigation. The second is the **constraint-handling rule**. The real-MuJoCo row supplies ground truth and the PPO-Lagrangian comparison (Direction 2). The imagination rows supply the exploitation study (Direction 1). Reading across the grid answers the question no prior paper has posed: do the penalty-free rules the brief favours survive a learned model?

### The lead experiment builds a safety τ table for a JEPA

The policy should follow Ha's design: a linear map from the 192-dimensional LeWM embedding to Walker2d's six actions ([PPGA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13795)). That is about 1,200 parameters, close to Ha's 867, which keeps full-covariance CMA-ES tractable. Larger MLP policies should switch to sep-CMA-ES, LM-MA-ES or OpenAI-ES, as JEDi did for Brax-sized genomes ([JEDi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04308)). If a single embedding does not carry velocity, the input should be [z_t, z_t − z_{t−1}], the Markovian latent that SLS² uses ([SLS²](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15594)). Initialising by regressing PPO-Lagrangian's actions onto encoded latents starts evolution from a walker rather than from noise. It also sharpens the scientific question to this: *does further evolution in imagination improve the real policy, or only exploit the model?*

Fitness should follow MBPO. Score each candidate as the mean (or worst case) over K imagined 10-block, 0.8 s segments started from real latent states drawn from a bank of MuJoCo snapshots. This keeps every evaluation inside the validated horizon, because short rollouts branched from real data "scale[] to horizons that cause other model-based methods to fail entirely" ([MBPO](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/hash/5faf461eff3099671ad63c6f3f094f7f-Abstract.html)). The proposed design uses probe-decoded forward velocity as reward, and the probe-decoded health and speed rules (with margin δ) as feasibility predicates. A probe-decoded margin at the end of each segment captures consequences that arrive after it. The start-state bank should be refreshed from elites' own real rollouts, which is the iterative loop Ha proposed but never ran on a hard task.

Ground truth comes from the student's branching. Every candidate that selection keeps is executed closed-loop in MuJoCo from the same snapshot for the same 0.8 s. That yields **paired imagined and real outcomes on identical start states**, which is a stronger design than any of the cited exploitation studies had.

The pressure factors are population size λ, generations, elite fraction, imagined horizon (1–10 blocks, plus longer horizons to locate the breakdown), margin δ, and latent noise σ. The LeWM predictor is a deterministic regressor, so Ha's temperature trick needs an analogue: Gaussian noise injected into predicted latents, scaled to the measured one-step residual.

The constraint-rule factor covers six options. Deb's feasibility-first rule, credited as CCE, is the default ([Deb 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7825(99)00389-8)). Stochastic ranking, which "was designed to deal with the inherent shortcomings of a penalty function", is the second ([Runarsson & Yao](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.873238)). The ε-constrained method with a decreasing schedule is the third ([Takahama & Sakai](https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2006.1688283)), and augmented-Lagrangian CMA-ES through pycma's `ConstrainedFitnessAL` is the fourth ([pycma](https://cma-es.github.io/apidocs-pycma/cma.constraints_handler.ConstrainedFitnessAL.html)). A fixed-penalty sweep and a ROSARL-style return-range penalty complete the set.

Three hypotheses organise the outputs.

**H1, the optimiser's curse for constraints.** The false-safe rate among *selected elites* exceeds the 45% unconditional rate and rises with λ and generations. This is the constraint analogue of ASAR's overgeneration result.

**H2, feasibility-first is the most exploitable rule.** Feasibility-first ranking concentrates selection on the optimistic tail of the constraint predictor. Stochastic ranking's randomness partly shields against this. This is an inference, not a published result.

**H3, amortised policies exploit more than replanning.** An amortised evolved policy exploits more than per-step CEM-MPC with replanning, because replanning from true states limits how long an exploited error lasts, and ARC-Bench shows that replanning masks ranking defects ([ARC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)).

The headline figure is the JEPA equivalent of Ha's table: imagined against real violation and return, by σ and by pressure. A second figure tracks the Spearman correlation between imagined and real fitness across generations. This matters because open-loop rollout error ranked models worse than replay error did (**ρ = 0.774 against 0.923**), choosing a suboptimal estimator in 18 of 24 conditions ([Raghavan & Singh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02811)).

### Mitigations compete at a matched real-evaluation budget

The second half of the lead paper compares remedies under one accounting rule: every arm spends the same number of real MuJoCo steps, and the steps used to train LeWM are counted. The candidates map onto known model-based RL ideas but act through the evolutionary loop. The two cheapest are a **margin δ** calibrated from the student's false-safe curve and **latent noise σ**, the analogue of Ha's τ. The third is **disagreement pessimism**: a cheap ensemble (several probe heads, several predictor fine-tunes, or MC dropout) supplies a pessimistic cost estimate, combined with MOReL-style halting, which thresholds ensemble discrepancy into an absorbing HALT state ([MOReL](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=701686)). Under feasibility-first ranking, halting converts "the model doesn't know" into "infeasible" with no penalty weight. The fourth is **top-k real re-evaluation of elites through snapshots**. This is the transferability approach, which needed 11 physical trials against 200,000 simulated evaluations ([Koos et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1870)), and it mirrors DA-QD's selection of the 20 lowest-disagreement skills. The fifth adds **LeWM fine-tuning on the corrected data** to that re-evaluation.

The arms report real return, real violation probability, the imagined–real gap, and real steps consumed. This comparison is where a GECCO paper earns its "remedy" half. Its likely winner, real-verified elite selection, is also the remedy the surrogate-assisted neuroevolution literature already trusts.

### The real-environment arm grounds the claims and carries the ROSARL bridge

Direction 2 runs ES and CMA-ES over linear or small MLP policies on the 17-dimensional state, through all six constraint rules. The comparison is OmniSafe PPO-Lagrangian, CPPOPID, CPO and PPO. The reference numbers on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1 at 10⁷ steps over 5 seeds are **PPO-Lagrangian 2982 ± 682 return and 13.5 ± 14.6 cost, and PPO 6240 ± 880 return and 903 ± 101 cost**, against a cost limit of 25 ([OmniSafe benchmark](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/blob/main/benchmarks/on-policy/README.md)). The fixed-penalty sweep must be included so that the "hand-tuned penalty" baseline is not a strawman. Budgets should be reported at 10⁷ steps (OmniSafe's) and 10⁸ steps (ES's natural regime), with the fraction of seeds that succeed.

For the health-rule arm, the Walker2d healthy bonus should be removed, as SMBPO did when it treated MuJoCo termination as the unsafe event. Otherwise reward implicitly encodes safety ([SMBPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789)). The official velocity cost (speed above **2.3415**) should be reported alongside the health rule, because the student's health rule is not the benchmark's cost ([Safety-Gymnasium velocity](https://safety-gymnasium.readthedocs.io/en/stable/environments/safe_velocity.html)).

The bridge to the supervisor's work is precise. Suppose each candidate is evaluated on a single episode and the episode terminates on violation. Then any penalty below every safe return ranks every violator below every safe candidate, which is exactly Deb's first rule. ROSARL's RLC 2026 version sets **R_unsafe = V_MIN − V_MAX** and terminates on violation ([ROSARL, RLJ 2026](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf)), so under rank-based selection it differs from Deb only in two places. The first is how it orders violators: by pre-violation return, roughly time-to-violation, rather than by violation magnitude. The second is how it aggregates rollouts. With n rollouts and mean fitness it becomes a probability-weighted penalty, (1−p)·E[R | safe] + p·R_unsafe, which is no longer lexicographic in p.

An ablation of mean-fitness ROSARL-ES against lexicographic (p̂, return) ranking and stochastic ranking therefore targets the brief's "probability of safety violations" directly. With an indicator cost at first violation and termination, E[episodic cost] equals P(violation), so "P(violation) ≤ δ" needs no separate chance-constraint machinery. That links the work to chance-constrained EC ([Neumann & Witt](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/665)). It also gives the user an interpretable δ to set in place of λ. That matters because ROSARL's own trade-off is steep: on PointGoal1 its cost fell to 0.08 against 0.62 for TRPO-Lagrangian, but its return fell to 1.87 against 10.17 ([RLJ 2026](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf)).

### The stretch archive must beat a conditioned policy, not just exist

If Gate 3 passes, the archive should be CMA-MAE in pyribs ([CMA-MAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10752v3); [pyribs](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs)). The 1-D descriptor is realised violation probability or episodic cost, in 10–20 bins, with an optional second axis for speed. Return is the fitness, and the archive is seeded from PPO and PPO-Lagrangian checkpoints. The budget should be 10⁴–10⁵ evaluations rather than the 10⁶ norm.

The results must use "corrected" archives: every elite is re-evaluated many times and placed back in its true cell, as PPGA does with **50 re-evaluations per agent** ([PPGA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.13795)). This matters more here than usual, because the descriptor is itself a noisy expectation and naive MAP-Elites fills cells with "lucky" elites ([Uncertain QD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463)).

The baselines are PPO-Lagrangian at k thresholds, NSGA-II compared on hypervolume, and a distilled cost-conditioned policy in the style of DCRL-ME or CCPO ([DCRL-ME](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3696426)), which answers the conditioned-policy critique head-on. Constrained MAP-Elites feasibility and GuSS-style lexicographic replacement complete the set, and comparing cost as a descriptor with cost as a constraint is itself a clean ablation.

The hook to the CMA-MEGA paper the supervisor suggested is untested. CMA-MEGA's measure gradient here is the gradient of expected cost, which a PPO-Lagrangian cost critic already estimates. A PPGA-style "safety-indexed CMA-MEGA" would therefore need no new estimator, but no paper has done this.

A cheaper diagnostic variant runs inside LeWM: archive by predicted margin × gait or speed descriptor, then re-evaluate each cell. This maps where false-safe errors cluster. It also tests whether they sit at gait-phase transitions, as the "kinematic, not dynamic" diagnosis of DreamerV3 walker-walk predicts ([Schäfer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05966)).

### Statistics follow the GECCO QD-RL norm

Recent GECCO NE, EML and CS papers on RL use 5–20 seeds, typically 10. They report medians with IQR or 95% confidence intervals, and they use Mann-Whitney U or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Bonferroni or Holm-Bonferroni correction. ASCII-ME used 20 seeds and compared methods at both equal runtime and equal evaluations ([ASCII-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18723)); MEMES reported wall-clock time on identical hardware ([MEMES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06137)). CRAX's seed study is a warning about small seed counts. The 95% CI half-width relative to the mean shrank from about 90% at 2 seeds to 22% at 5, and small seed counts sometimes produced misleadingly narrow intervals ([CRAX](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20376)). The plan should report the interquartile mean with bootstrap CIs ([rliable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)) and use Wilson or Clopper-Pearson intervals on every violation probability. Paired tests are appropriate on matched snapshots.

One arithmetic consequence deserves a sentence in the paper. With zero observed violations in n episodes, the 95% upper bound on P(violation) is about 3/n (the rule of three). Certifying a rate below 1% therefore needs roughly 300 clean episodes per final policy.

Budgets must appear on three axes: real steps (including LeWM's training data), imagined steps, and wall-clock time. Otherwise a reviewer will argue that the evolutionary advantage disappears once all samples are counted. Safety Gym's **cost rate over all of training** must count every real rollout of every population member ([Safety Gym](https://cdn.openai.com/safexp-short.pdf)). Imagined evaluation is where a world-model method can win this metric, and naive real-environment ES is where it can lose.

## Exploitation is the finding, and also the threat

The central risk is close to certain: evolution will exploit LeWM. Ha's agent learned to move so that the dream's monsters "never shoots a single fireball" ([Ha & Schmidhuber](https://worldmodels.github.io/)). Evolution's history is full of fitness loopholes ([Lehman et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03453)), and a 2026 backdoor study shows CEM re-discovering whatever the model's local dynamics reward ([Backdoors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15781)). The design answer is to make exploitation the measured quantity rather than a hidden confound. That only works if at least one remedy beats the baseline. Otherwise the paper is a negative result, which is harder to place.

There is a second-order risk that most proposals miss: **the mitigations themselves fail in the region evolution explores**. Ensemble disagreement ranks out-of-distribution errors poorly ([Intervention gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)), and UNISafe's authors concede that their ensemble "may not faithfully capture" true uncertainty ([UNISafe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1)). Conformal margins reduce false-safes at the cost of conservativeness ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)). This is why real re-evaluation through snapshots must be the backstop rather than one arm among equals.

Long-horizon error is the second structural risk. LeWM's authors state that "auto-regressive rollouts accumulate prediction errors as the horizon grows" and restrict planning to short horizons ([LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)). Hierarchy on a frozen LeWM helps only "when high-level search remains compatible with the low-level controller" ([Caselli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12547)). Most directly relevant to a Walker health rule, a DreamerV3 walker-walk model's imagined kinematic-consistency error **stayed flat while real reward collapsed** across a friction sweep, and failures appeared beyond the gait period ([Schäfer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05966)).

Coverage compounds both risks. If LeWM's data came mostly from competent PPO and PPO-Lagrangian rollouts, falls are under-represented, and evolution will push into exactly those states. Seeding the snapshot bank with perturbed and failing rollouts is cheap insurance. Theory supports keeping policies smooth: return-error bounds for training in imagination tighten as the Lipschitz constants of the dynamics, reward and policy shrink ([Timor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06732)). That favours linear policies with bounded weights.

## Conclusion

The research landscape turns the obvious proposal upside down. Building a safe planner on a JEPA world model is no longer open ground: SLS², SafeDreamer and the latent-filter papers occupy it, and the student's Safe CEM restates a rule published in 2018. What remains open is a question only an evolutionary-computation researcher with exact ground truth can answer cheaply. How much of a constraint predictor's error does selection pressure harvest, and which changes to the *selection operator*, rather than to the model, give it back? The student's prior results (a 45% base false-safe rate, a 75% dynamics attribution on Push-T, and fine-tuning that did not fix it) stop being limitations and become the calibration curve for that study.

The tension at the core of the supervisor's brief is also the paper's most interesting hypothesis. Penalty-free constraint handling (Deb rules, and ROSARL's minmax penalty under rank selection, which collapses into the same rule) is attractive because it is scale-free and needs no λ. But a rule that ranks every predicted-feasible candidate above every predicted-infeasible one is exactly the rule that rewards optimistic errors in the feasibility predictor. If H2 holds, the safest-looking choice for optimisation becomes the riskiest choice under a learned model, and the remedy has to come from evolution's own toolkit: re-evaluation, noise-aware ranking and pessimistic selection. That is a GECCO-native result whichever way the data falls.
