# Evolution finds where imagined safety breaks

As of 5 October 2026, the open problems where learned world models, evolutionary computation, ROSARL and safe RL meet reduce to one bottleneck: every component learns about danger from a distribution where data is cheap (competent-walking training data, the optimiser's starting search distribution, hand-set termination flags, logged one-step transitions), but every safety decision is taken on the tail that selection creates, and no published work measures or controls that tail against exact ground truth. The biggest change since the 3 October report is Wachi, Tanabe and Akimoto's "Safety Hacking" preprint of 24 August 2026, which proves that a learned safety filter followed by best-of-N selection picks an unsafe output with probability tending to one whenever unsafe candidates that pass the filter have heavier reward tails (in their toy problem, 2.3% rising to 99.96% as N reaches 8,192); it overturns the earlier claim that nobody had measured false-safe rates against selection pressure, but it explicitly leaves adaptive search open, and adaptive search is exactly what your locked 20-seed CMA-ES study measured (+10.39 percentage points, adjusted interval +6.70 to +14.25). Strong models offer no escape: in a 2026 benchmark from the lab behind SafeDreamer, failure preservation fell from 48.7 to 12.8 as a video world model grew from 2B to 14B parameters, and every larger-model test found, from KataGo to V-JEPA 2 at ViT-G scale, shows exploitation shrinking at best, never vanishing. ROSARL's theory exists only in the 2023 preprint and the non-archival RLC 2024 workshop paper; the archival RLJ 2026 version has no theorems, nobody has extended the method, and it presupposes a labelled absorbing failure set that, on Walker2d, fires in states a recovery search escapes 96% of the time. Nobody has formalised how many real failures a safety predictor needs, or measured a constraint exploitability gap on an optimiser's own population, which is why your repair (one-step failure detection from 11.3% to 67.7%) bought no measurably safer controller. The strongest GECCO 2027 full paper is an adaptive safety-hacking study that extends the contamination-and-amplification analysis to CMA-ES on the Walker2d LeWM, with selection pressure measured as the KL divergence of the search distribution, paired with a quality-diversity falsifier or a rank-managed safety surrogate as the remedy and judged by a fresh adversary. GECCO 2027 is confirmed for Kraków on 12 to 16 July 2027, but the 19 and 26 January dates on its call page are uncorrected 2026 copies, so 26 January remains a planning assumption.

## Safety is learned where data is cheap but decided where selection pushes

In MuJoCo a fall costs nothing, so "visit the dangerous part of the world as little as possible" has to be read through its physical analogue: every snapshot replay that ends in a fall stands for a fall a real robot would have taken, and every verification is a call to a budgeted oracle. The unifying question then becomes a budgeted-oracle problem, namely choosing the few real visits that most reduce the safety error that matters for the decision being taken. Pieces of an answer exist. Bayesian safety validation fits a surrogate iteratively and estimates the failure probability of a fixed system "with orders of magnitude fewer samples" ([Moss et al., AIAA JAIS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02449)). ActSafe explores optimistically about dynamics and pessimistically about constraints, with safety guarantees under regularity assumptions ([As et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09486)). PAINT finds the first irreversible state on a trajectory by binary search, needing O(N log |τ|) labels instead of O(N |τ|) ([Xie et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10765)). DSAGE runs quality diversity on a deep surrogate and spends ground truth only on a downsampled archive ([Bhatt et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04199)). None of these spends real visits to correct a world model's *safety predictions*, none uses evolution in that role, and none measures the error where the optimiser actually selects.

Your own locked study shows the whole chain. At the declared high-pressure setting (population 256, 16 generations), the control arm's selected controllers failed on **29.56%** of fresh real episodes while the model imagined failure on **0.47%**, and moving from low to high pressure widened the real-minus-imagined gap by **10.39 points**. A ROSARL-style penalty alone left real failures at 27.47% and latent noise alone gave 25.66%; noise plus the penalty gave 22.36%, cutting the pressure-driven amplification by 7.22 points while keeping 99.04% of forward progress, yet the combined arm still failed 5.48 points more often than the frozen starting controller, which failed on 16.88% (locked study, `docs/evoPlan/results/stage5-main/walker2d-evo-s5-main-20261004-1/README.md`). A separate predictor repair raised one-step failure-endpoint detection from 11.3% to 67.7%, but the controller the repaired model then chose failed on 3 of 24 audit episodes against 2 of 24 for the baseline (`docs/evoPlan/results/stage6-repair/walker2d-evo-s6-repair-20261005-1/INTERPRETATION.md`). Each of the five sub-problems shows up in one of those numbers.

| Number | Sub-problem | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 0.47% imagined failure | (a) | A model trained on competent walking imagines failures away |
| +10.39 points | (b) | Selection harvests exactly those errors |
| Small effect of the penalty alone | (c) | A minmax penalty cannot fire on failures the evaluator never registers |
| Privileged, after-the-fact failure labels in the repair | (d) | No theory says how many such labels are needed or which ones to buy |
| No safer controller after repair | (e) | A better one-step detector is not a better ranker of the candidates selection actually compares |

Evolution's distinctive contribution follows from that diagnosis. In gradient-based model-based RL, the distribution a policy optimiser pushes towards is implicit; in CMA-ES it is an explicit Gaussian whose mean, covariance, population size and generation count are experimental variables, whose divergence from the starting distribution has a closed form, and which shares one information-geometric family with the cross-entropy method ([Ollivier, Arnold, Auger and Hansen, JMLR 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3708)). A quality-diversity (QD) archive goes further and turns the distribution of tested behaviours into a map. Together these turn "where selection pushes" from a hidden confound into a measured, controlled quantity, and exact snapshot branching makes every point of it a counted oracle call. ## Each sub-problem has a solved shell and an open core

### (a) Models trained on success imagine failures away, and scale does not rescue them

Three independent 2026 lines of work point to a mechanism rather than a magnitude. Biased Dreams (RLC 2026 per arXiv) trained RSSMs on four DMC tasks including **Walker Run** and found that rollouts "converge to familiar latent regions, where uncertainty diminishes despite elevated proprioceptive discrepancy", that out-of-distribution uncertainty "rapidly converges to ID levels", and that returns are overestimated; the authors call this a structural limitation, not a training defect ([Berger, Frauenknecht, Trimpe and Leibe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25416)). On a released DreamerV3 walker, imagined kinematic-consistency error "remains statistically constant even as policy rewards collapse" when friction crosses the boundary where the gait collapses ([Schäfer et al., RSS 2026 Robot World Model Workshop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05966)). The Intervention Gap study (preprint, 30 August 2026) found that on Cheetah three **LeWorldModel** checkpoints produce imagined five-step effects "worse than predicting no effect", while DreamerV3 ensemble disagreement "ranks error only near training support" ([Vakalis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)). Two LeWM-specific results complete the picture: the corrective gradients of LeWM's SIGReg objective "rapidly vanish for isolated tail samples", leaving heavy-tailed deviations ([QQWorld, preprint, 30 Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28415)), and BasinLens found reproducible failure modes in LeWM on PushT and TwoRooms within 16 to 64 queries ([Shi et al., preprint, 23 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22421)). The prediction for your setting is direct: a LeWM trained on competent walking will imagine a stumbling walker re-stabilising, which is the 0.47% against 29.56%.

Scale does not help. MiraBench (preprint, 28 May 2026), from the PKU-Alignment group that built Safety-Gymnasium and SafeDreamer, scores whether action-conditioned world models still render failure when successful actions are perturbed. Failure preservation fell from **48.7 for DreamDojo-2B to 12.8 for DreamDojo-14B**, and training only on successes raised task completion from 14.0 to 92.0 while cutting failure preservation from 48.7 to 23.1; the authors conclude that "optimism bias is pervasive" ([MiraBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)). Scaling video models gives perfect in-distribution generalisation but failure out of distribution, with "case-based" mimicry of the nearest training example ([Kang et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kang25g.html)). Ctrl-World, trained on roughly 76,000 successful and 19,000 failed DROID trajectories, *underestimates* success ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)); set beside MiraBench, this suggests that the sign of the bias tracks the share of failures in the training data. That is an inference from two papers, not a controlled result, and it is cheap to test with snapshot branching.

The honest answer to "what if the model is really good, like SafeDreamer?" comes from SafeDreamer's own ablations. Safety improves "when the world model accurately predicts safety-related states", cost weighting matters because of "the unbalanced distribution of cost", and the cost threshold cannot be zero because of errors in the learned cost models ([Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3)). SafeDreamer also learns online from its own violations. The strong safe world models are the ones that saw their failures, and an offline JEPA trained on competent walking did not.

Uncertainty does not rescue a blind model either. In a single-author ICML 2026 RLxF workshop paper, dynamics-uncertainty penalties *raised* collision rates from 26% to 34%, model uncertainty and task risk correlated at r < 0.15 "because uncertainty lives in state-prediction space while risk lives at constraint boundaries", and outcome-based signals cut collisions to 1 to 14% ([Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16591)); treat it as suggestive. For latent Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters, ensemble disagreement and value-consistency residuals "can remain small even when the world model's predictions deviate from observations", and the fix is adaptive conformal inference on *observed* latent prediction error, with a finite-time coverage guarantee ([Cao and Bansal, preprint, 28 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)). UNISafe's authors concede that their ensemble "may not faithfully capture" true uncertainty ([Seo, Nakamura and Bajcsy, CoRL 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/seo25a.html)). The detector that works best is supervised: FARM's 33,985-parameter readout on frozen VLA-JEPA states reaches 85.68 pooled AUROC, but it needs failure labels ([Pei et al., preprint, Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)). A July 2026 security survey has already named your metric the "predicted-safe-but-actually-unsafe rate" and calls the phenomenon a "predictive safety illusion" ([Liu et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28226v1)); no benchmark yet reports it against exact counterfactual ground truth.

What is solved is showing that strong models fail out of distribution, building calibrated but conservative safety sets for latent models under exchangeability (SLS² on a LeWM variant, [Nath et al., preprint, Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594); UNISafe; conformal risk calibration, [Kim, Nakamura and Bajcsy, preprint, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)), and supervised failure readouts. Five things remain open. No paper measures a constraint predictor's false-safe rate as a function of distance from the training data for a JEPA on locomotion. No detector has been scored under optimisation pressure, where candidates are chosen *because* the model calls them safe. Nobody knows whether conformal guarantees survive that selection: marginal conformal sets can fail for selected units, and selection-conditional fixes exist ([Jin and Ren, JRSS-B 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03868)), while Cao and Bansal note that exchangeability can break under safety filtering, but no one has tested a learned safety head this way. "How good is good enough" has no answer, since validation loss and multi-step error "keep improving long after closed-loop performance has collapsed" ([Smolyanskiy, preprint, Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01736)), and a September perspective lists "determining when evidence is sufficient to act" as an open challenge ([Ma, Huang, Kim and Jang, preprint, revised 1 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03774)). Finally, no benchmark measures a world model's false-safe rate as a constraint checker in a constrained MDP, with exact replay, under optimisation pressure.

Evolution contributes in four ways. BasinLens tested LeWM against random search, GP-UCB and CEM only, and MiraBench hand-designs its six failure-inducing perturbations, so an evolutionary search over start states and perturbation schedules, with false-safe magnitude as fitness, is an untried baseline in a recognised problem. A MAP-Elites archive over gait and margin descriptors gives a map of where the model is blind, with one caveat that keeps the claims honest: archives can bound tail risk and average severity over their coverage, but "worst-case guarantees cannot be bounded by archive fitting error" ([Shihab and Afrin, preprint, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08008)). The cross-entropy method is the classical adaptive importance sampler for rare events, so using CMA-ES or CEM to propose branch start states, with likelihood-ratio weights, could give an unbiased false-safe estimate at a fraction of the cost of uniform sampling; this is an inference, as no 2025 or 2026 paper doing it was found. And evolution offers a native definition of sufficiency: rank-based selection is invariant to monotone miscalibration, so a model is "good enough" when the false-safe rate of the *selected* elites stays below a target at a given population size and selection intensity. No paper defines sufficiency this way.

### (b) Selection pressure turns small false-safe tails into near-certain failures

The theory arrived in August. Wachi, Tanabe and Akimoto define **safety hacking** as "selecting an output that passes the learned constraint but violates the true safety criterion", and split it into **contamination**, in which false positives enter the set the filter accepts, and **amplification**, in which reward maximisation favours them ([Safety Hacking in Constrained Best-of-N Inference-time Scaling, preprint, 24 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22915)). Their Theorem 4.2 shows that if unsafe-but-accepted outputs have heavier reward-proxy tails, the hacking probability tends to 1 as N grows, "even when false-positive mass and average safety- and reward-proxy errors are arbitrarily small". In a toy problem, hacking rose from **2.3% at N = 1 to 99.96% at N = 8,192**; in LLM tests with a Llama-Guard filter, swapping the reward proxy cut hacking from 13.4% to 6.8% at N = 256, so tail shape, not the filter, set the outcome. Their χ²-bounded "coverage control" gives a bound that does not depend on N but "cannot repair contamination introduced by an imperfect safety constraint", and the paper states that it "does not cover adaptive search, self-refinement, tree search, or agentic planning", with no evolutionary algorithms, world models or safe RL ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22915)). Youhei Akimoto is a CMA-ES theorist, which makes the paper legible to GECCO reviewers and makes his group the most likely to extend it to evolution.

Other formal accounts fill in the picture. The optimiser's curse guarantees post-decision disappointment whenever the best of many noisy estimates is chosen ([Smith and Winkler, Management Science 2006](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0451)). A world model is exploitable if it prefers one policy while the true dynamics prefer the other, exploitation "is essentially unavoidable on large policy sets", and a closed-form "safe horizon" bounds a relaxed version ([Bhamidipaty, Whitammer, Abel, Kochenderfer and Ramamoorthy, preprint, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)). Reward-model over-optimisation supplies a clean pressure axis: best-of-n moves the policy KL = log n − (n−1)/n from the base, gold reward follows d(α − βd) with d = √KL, and β falls with reward-model size without vanishing ([Gao, Schulman and Hilton, ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)). Worst-case and uncertainty-weighted ensemble objectives "practically eliminate" over-optimisation for best-of-n ([Coste et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02743)). Inference-time hacking is "an inevitable property" of best-of-n-like mechanisms, and choosing n is the remedy ([Khalaf et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19248)). A 29 September scaling law reduces reward optimisation to "choosing from a sequence of IID Gaussian random variables using noisy preference feedback" ([Aouad, El Gadarri and Farias, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38526)). For code world models, N random validation rollouts all miss a critical event of probability r with probability exactly (1−r)^N, and even an informative acceptance gate "covers about two percent of the exploited planner's queries" ([Aguilar Martín, preprint, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17956)).

Does exploitation persist for strong models? Every test found says it shrinks at best. Adversarial policies beat KataGo at superhuman settings more than 97% of the time ([Wang et al., ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00241)), and three defences all failed against "freshly trained adversaries" ([Tseng et al., AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12843)). ARC-Bench's ranking defect "persists when DINOv2 is replaced by video-pretrained V-JEPA 1 and V-JEPA 2 encoders at ViT-L/ViT-G scale" ([Zhang and Li, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)), and across released TD-MPC2 checkpoint sizes return falls as operator error grows ([Vakalis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)). Wachi et al.'s condition needs only a heavier unsafe tail, not a large average error, so a *more accurate* safety proxy can still be hacked at large N. For SafeDreamer specifically there is a gap rather than a verdict: it reports "nearly zero-cost performance", but no paper has measured its cost model under adversarial or evolutionary pressure, and whether Safety-Gymnasium allows the exact state restore needed for counterfactual replay against a SafeDreamer checkpoint was not checked.

Measures of exploitability now exist but have never been applied to constraints under adaptive search. Gao's √KL axis, Wachi's hacking probability as a function of N and Bhamidipaty's ε-exploitation are three. A fourth is the **exploitability gap** XGap = Ĵ_model(π̂) − J_env(π̂) for the optimiser's chosen policy, whose authors find "essentially no work... that reports an exploitability gap" ([Yu et al., preprint, Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)). DSAGE's finding that only about 0.06% of surrogate-archive solutions landed in the same cell when evaluated for real is the QD analogue ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.04199)). For CMA-ES, every ingredient is computable: the KL divergence between the generation-g and initial Gaussians has a closed form, contamination is the false-safe rate of probe-feasible candidates under the initial distribution, and amplification is the extra false-safe mass that selection adds. Your locked study is, in effect, the first adaptive data point on Wachi's curve.

Evolution as a falsifier is mature everywhere except world-model safety. Cyber-physical systems have robustness-guided falsification ([S-TaLiRo, TACAS 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-19835-9_21)), adaptive stress testing ([Lee et al., JAIR 2020](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.12190)) and BO combined with CMA-ES ([BEACON, preprint 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05925)). Software engineering tests deep RL agents with surrogate-guided evolution: Indago trains a failure classifier as surrogate fitness and finds 50% more failures that are 78% more diverse ([Biagiola and Tonella, ACM TOSEM 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12751)); INDAGO-Nexus applies multi-objective evolutionary search to agents **including a humanoid walker** ([Bartlett, Liem and Panichella, SSBSE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14727)); and QD fault-diversity testing moved from a workshop ([Mazouni et al., AST@ICSE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15065)) to ACM TOSEM on 29 June 2026 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3821533)). QD red-teaming is mainstream for agents and models: MADRID ([AAMAS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13460)), Rainbow Teaming ([NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16822)) and Q-DIG for VLA robot policies ([Srikanth et al., preprint, Mar 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12510)). For world models the 2026 work is all non-evolutionary: BasinLens; WMAttack, which raised DreamerV3's normalised reward drop from 0.497 to 1.034 on Atari ([Guo et al., preprint, revised 5 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23220)); and Balogh and Jelasity, who generated valid chess move sequences that force invalid predictions and found that "none of the models are sound" and that board-state probes have "no causal role" in prediction, a warning for linear probes on latents ([ICLR 2026 per arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05903)).

The repair loop also exists without evolution or safety. PROWL trains a PPO adversary to expose high-error trajectories of a 1.3B diffusion world model and fine-tunes the model on them; with weak KL anchoring its adversary found "camera-thrashing rather than learnable failures", leading the authors to conclude that "high prediction error alone is not a useful reward signal" ([Güzel et al., preprint, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18803)). What is solved is falsifying fixed systems and agents, including by evolution; surrogate-guided evolutionary testing of deep RL agents; non-evolutionary failure discovery in world models; and non-adaptive safety-hacking theory. What is open is an exploitability curve against evolutionary pressure (population, generations, KL of the search distribution) for any learned dynamics model, falsification aimed at a world model's *safety predictions* as distinct from its pixel error or an agent's behaviour, and repairs judged by a *fresh* adversary rather than by the failures already found. Evolution brings three things no gradient adversary provides: search over policies, which is where exploitation happens; a built-in pressure dial, which turns falsification power into a measured variable; and archives that map false-safe regions. The remedies that worked for LLMs also map one-to-one onto selection operators, all acting on selection rather than on the model, which is where your noise-plus-penalty arm already acts.

| LLM remedy | Evolutionary analogue |
|---|---|
| Choosing n (HedgeTune) | Choosing the population or generation count that maximises real safety |
| Worst-case ensemble objectives, or pessimistic error models | Ranking candidates on a lower confidence bound |
| χ² coverage control | A KL or step-size trust region on CMA-ES |

### (c) ROSARL presupposes the absorbing set it ought to discover

ROSARL exists in three materially different versions, and citations must follow the content.

| Version | Title | Venue and status | Date | What it contains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | ROSARL: Reward-Only Safe Reinforcement Learning | arXiv preprint, "under review" ([2306.00035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00035)) | 31 May 2023 | Minmax penalty min{R_MIN, (R_MIN − R_MAX)·D/C}, using diameter D and controllability C; safety-bounds theorem; limitations section |
| v2 | Same title | Reinforcement Learning Safety Workshop at RLC 2024, Amherst; non-archival workshop paper ([PDF](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf)) | August 2024 | Exact threshold definition; bounds; estimation theorem; "Estimating the Minmax penalty ... accurately is NP-hard" |
| v3 | An Unreasonably Simple Approach to Safe RL | Reinforcement Learning Journal vol. 7, presented at RLC 2026, Montréal; archival and peer-reviewed ([RLJ](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.html)) | August 2026 | No theorems; practical R_unsafe = V_MIN − V_MAX; empirical claims; method renamed TRPO-ValueRange |

Any sentence about a "minimal" or "provably sufficient" penalty, diameter, controllability or NP-hardness must cite v1 or v2, while the method and its benchmarks should cite v3. Steven James's IJCAI 2026 overview still describes the method as "learning the smallest penalty that provably suffices", citing the 2023 theory ([IJCAI 2026](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/0906.pdf)).

The practical rule is the theory with controllability forced to 1. v1 justifies this by noting that "C = 1 if safe goals are reachable and the environment is deterministic", and in stochastic tasks v2 relies on a ratchet that makes the penalty more negative each time the agent enters an unsafe state, with no bound on how far it goes ([RLSW 2024](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf)). v3 restricts itself to settings where "the environment terminates immediately when the safe condition is violated" and modifies every benchmark to terminate on violation, including HumanoidVelocity. Its own tables show the cost of caution: on continual PointGoal1, TRPO-ValueRange reaches cost 0.08 and return 1.87 against cost 0.62 and return 10.17 for TRPO-Lagrangian, and in Pillar at noise levels of 5 and above its goal success is 0.00 ([RLJ 2026 PDF](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf)). v1's dropped limitations section names both roots of this: the method "is only applicable to unsafe terminal states", and treating every terminal state as maximally unsafe "leads to very risk-averse policies", so v1 proposes a weight on the penalty for "different degrees of safety" ([arXiv §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035)).

Nobody has built on ROSARL technically. Semantic Scholar lists eight citing works from 2023 to September 2026, all of them one-line mentions in related work, motivation sentences or the IJCAI self-citation; SafeDreamer cites it only in related work ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2306.00035/citations)). Searches for model-based, offline, world-model or evolutionary use of the minmax or value-range penalty returned nothing. That is a negative search result, but it makes your CMA-ES adaptation with branch-return extrema the first on two axes at once. The closest sufficiency theory sits outside ROSARL's citation graph. Safe value functions show that a finite penalty always exists and that larger penalties "do not harm optimality" ([Massiani et al., IEEE TAC 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12204)). SMBPO derives a model-based penalty with a safety horizon ([Thomas, Luo and Ma, NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789)). Sauté RL corresponds to an infinite penalty ([Sootla et al., ICML 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06558)), P3O shows exactness with a finite penalty factor ([IJCAI 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0520.pdf)), and lexicographic RL gives the clean "safety first, then return" ordering ([Skalse et al., IJCAI 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/476)). On the evolutionary side sit Deb's feasibility rule ([Deb 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7825(99)00389-8)) and stochastic ranking, which "was designed to deal with the inherent shortcomings of a penalty function" ([Runarsson and Yao 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.873238)).

The evolutionary reading clarifies what ROSARL's penalty size actually does. CMA-ES is invariant to order-preserving transforms of fitness, so v3's learning-dynamics argument for the smallest sufficient penalty disappears, and the penalty matters only through whether it achieves dominance and how it trades probability against return when fitness is averaged over rollouts. With undiscounted episodic fitness and R_unsafe = V_MIN − V_MAX, a violator scores at most V_MIN, so dominance holds, but only if the tracked extrema truly bound every realised return. It fails under discounting, and it fails when the extrema are under-estimated, which is likely early in a run or under an imperfect model. With n rollouts and mean fitness, a safer candidate is guaranteed to outrank a riskier one only if |R_unsafe| exceeds (V_MAX − V_MIN)/Δp, where Δp is the smallest difference in violation probability to be resolved and is at least 1/n. That is exactly the D/C structure of the 2023 bound, with the rollout count supplying the controllability term that practical ROSARL drops, and writing R_unsafe = −(V_MAX − V_MIN)/δ turns it into an interpretable dial. This derivation comes from the research notes rather than a published source and needs checking before it is claimed.

Your locked study adds evidence on a point no ROSARL paper discusses: a penalty is only as good as the failure detector that triggers it. Without noise, the model imagined failure on 0.47% of high-pressure branches, and the ROSARL-style penalty moved real failures only from 29.56% to 27.47%. With latent noise, imagined failures rose to 4.95%, and adding the penalty moved real failures from 25.66% to 22.36%. The study's report warns that this ordering does not establish a tested interaction, but the pattern connects (c) back to (a): in imagination, a minmax penalty inherits the world model's blindness.

The absorbing set is the deeper problem. Irreversibility can now be estimated without hand labels from self-supervised temporal precedence ([Grinsztajn et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/0e98aeeb54acf612b9eb4e48a269814c-Paper.pdf)), reset-policy values ([Leave No Trace](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06782)), binary-search labelling (PAINT) and behaviour encodings ([RSA, NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117025)). Theory now gives sublinear regret and mentor queries in irreversible MDPs ([Plaut et al., JMLR vol. 27, 2026](http://jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2248.html)), feasible initial conditions can be discovered alongside the policy ([So et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15817)), imagination-based recoverability guides where to explore ([RECON, preprint, 27 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33336)), and a ground-truth reset oracle now spans eight manipulation settings in five physics engines ([REVERSAL-BENCH, preprint, 15 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17745)). Yet every latent Hamilton-Jacobi filter still gets its failure set from a classifier ([LatentSafe, RSS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)) or from uncertainty as a proxy (UNISafe), and none asks whether the labelled failures are irreversible at all.

Your own measurement shows why that matters (`notes/terminationIsNotIrreversibility.md`). Walker2d's "unhealthy" flag (0.8 < z < 2.0 and |pitch| < 1, hand-set in the Gymnasium source) fires while the robot is still standing, and CEM on the true simulator recovers from 96% of flagged states. Settled-fallen Walker2d states are 0.0% irrecoverable at a 4-second horizon and 3.3% at 2 seconds, while Hopper is 10% irrecoverable at 4 seconds, and Hopper's apparent boundary vanishes when the recovery tolerance is relaxed from 0.10 to 0.15 rad. ROSARL with the benchmark flag is therefore optimally cautious about states the robot could recover from, and ROSARL with true irreversibility would have almost no absorbing states on Walker2d and would degenerate to plain RL. Irreversibility has to be indexed by horizon and search budget to be usable, and that horizon is itself a natural safety dial.

What is solved is exact-penalty theory in undiscounted shortest-path tasks with known diameter and controllability, label-efficient reversibility estimation in discrete and manipulation settings, and latent safety filters given a failure classifier. What is open is measuring benchmark failure flags against an optimised recovery oracle, defining failure as "irrecoverable within horizon H under search budget B", plugging a learned absorbing set into ROSARL, understanding how errors in V_MIN and V_MAX propagate into safety (extremes are the statistics most biased by selection and model error, and monotone min and max never forget an outlier), and finding an estimable stand-in for controllability. Evolution's contribution starts from the fact that your recovery oracle already *is* an evolutionary search, since CEM is an estimation-of-distribution algorithm. "Irreversible" can therefore be defined operationally as "a CMA-ES or CEM recovery search with budget B fails within H", a MAP-Elites archive over torso height, pitch and angular velocity becomes an empirical viability kernel, and PAINT-style binary search along evolved trajectories finds the point of no return with O(log T) oracle calls.

### (d) No paper counts the real failures a safety model costs

Reporting of training-time violations is inconsistent. SafeDreamer reports a cost rate over all training ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.07176)), LAMBDA's "cost regret" is no better than its baselines ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09802)), and PIGDreamer and Nightmare Dreamer report no training tally. The deeper pattern is that every near-zero-violation result moves the failure knowledge somewhere else and never charges for it.

| Method (status) | How it limits real failures | Where the failure knowledge actually comes from |
|---|---|---|
| ActSafe (ICLR 2025) | Optimistic on dynamics, pessimistic on constraints; "does not incur any costs during learning" on a GP pendulum | A 200,000-step warm-up collected "in a controlled/supervised setting" ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09486v2)) |
| SOOPER (ICLR 2026) | Falls back on a conservative prior policy, with guarantees throughout training | Priors "obtained from offline data or simulators" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19612)) |
| SafeDojo (preprint, Jun 2026) | All RL inside a video world model; 76.0% task success and 70.0% safe success on a real Franka | "Binary collision labels from SafeLIBERO simulation" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20698)) |
| FARL (preprint, Jan 2026) | An offline world-model safety critic and recovery policy guard exploration; 73.1% fewer intervention-requiring failures | Offline failure data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07821v1)) |
| PROCO (preprint, May 2026) | Synthesises counterfactual unsafe samples for offline safe RL | An LLM-grounded conservative cost plus model rollouts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)) |
| Cao and Bansal (preprint, Sep 2026) | An adaptive conformal radius grows pessimism with observed error | 150 failure trajectories among 500 on hardware ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34300)) |

SPOWL states the dilemma in one sentence: "Learning an accurate world model requires encountering potentially unsafe states, while perfect constraint avoidance demands complete prior knowledge of hazardous situations" ([SPOWL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04828)). FOSP adds that "imbalanced datasets cause dynamics model failures in predicting costs accurately", and that safe-only data makes the policy over-aggressive while unsafe data makes it over-conservative ([ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04942v2)).

Formal treatments of a failure budget exist, but none targets a safety model's accuracy. The only literal one, excursion search, spends a fixed number of tolerated failures to lower regret in constrained Bayesian optimisation ([Marco et al., preprint, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07443)). CMDP learning theory gives zero violation with high probability given a known safe policy ([Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/8ec2ba5e96ec1c050bc631abda80f269-Paper.pdf)) and Õ(√ST) step-wise violations with matching lower bounds when no safe actions are known ([Xiong, Du and Huang, preprint, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06064)). Catastrophe avoidance treats mentor queries as the scarce resource ([Plaut, Zhu and Russell, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08062)). The closest formal statement of "learn the boundary without crossing it" learns a constraint "up to arbitrary precision" without violation with high probability, but only for low-dimensional GP models ([Bottero et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/c628644624c1be9c8cfb1541fa6421fd-Abstract.html)). GECCO already treats unsafe evaluations as a first-class metric, in "Are Evolutionary Algorithms Safe Optimizers?" ([Kim, Allmendinger and López-Ibáñez, GECCO 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12622)) and in Safe CMA-ES, which reaches zero median unsafe evaluations on benchmark functions given safe seeds ([Uchida et al., GECCO 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10534)).

Acquisition aimed at failure boundaries is active and has an evolutionary branch. Coverage-aware active evaluation pairs cheap proxy evaluations with sparse target evaluations and finds about **twice as many failures** as random sampling and active-learning baselines ([Parashar et al., preprint, 13 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)); its proxy-plus-target structure is LeWM plus snapshot branches almost exactly, but it has no evolutionary search. In search-based testing, NSGA-II plus decision trees found 78% more distinct critical scenarios ([Ben Abdessalem et al., ICSE 2018](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/33706/1/ICSE-Main-24.pdf)), and DeepJanus finds *pairs* of near-identical inputs that straddle the failure frontier ([Riccio and Tonella, ESEC/FSE 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02787)). Failure synthesis is routine in manipulation: Dream2Fix injects perturbations inside Ctrl-World, four verifiers reject 41.9% of synthesised rollouts, and the resulting data raise correction accuracy from 19.7% to 81.3% ([Li et al., preprint, Mar 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13528)), while Fail2Progress uses Stein variational particles, a population method, to generate simulated environments that resemble one observed real failure ([Huang et al., CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01746)). Every such paper uses hand-designed perturbation families, and none measures how many synthetic failures are worth one real failure for a world model's own safety predictions.

What is solved is zero-violation learning given prior knowledge, violation-regret theory in tabular and GP settings, and evolution plus classifiers for mapping failure regions when failures are free. What is open is a failure-budgeted objective for safety-model accuracy (the false-safe rate as a function of real failures spent), a measure of the information each real failure contributes so that candidates can be ranked before paying for them, accounting for the failure knowledge hidden in priors, warm-ups, simulators and LLMs, the exchange rate between synthetic and real failures, and any of this for pixel-trained latent models. Your stage-6 repair, which sampled failure-rich and ordinary examples equally and used privileged simulator labels after the fact, is one point on a curve whose shape nobody has drawn. Evolution is the natural acquisition engine here. The population, evaluated in imagination, proposes a batch and only elites near the predicted boundary are verified, which is GECCO's long-standing surrogate pre-screening and infill practice applied to labels rather than optima. DeepJanus-style frontier pairs make every real failure a maximally informative boundary label, and QD descriptors over failure modes target coverage directly.

### (e) One-step accuracy is the wrong currency for selection

Your repair result is the clearest local instance. Detection rose by 56.4 points (paired interval +48.0 to +61.2), but the selected controller failed on 3 of 24 episodes against 2 of 24. The Wilson 95% intervals (4.3% to 31.0% and 2.3% to 25.8%) overlap almost entirely, Fisher's exact test gives p = 1.0, and detecting 8% against 12.5% at 80% power would need roughly 800 rollouts per arm. The honest reading is "better detection did not produce measurably safer selection", not "it made selection worse". The same report adds a telling detail: detection with refreshed real history reached 67.7%, but with the real action tape over the full branch it fell to 31.2%, so one-step repair does not repair rollouts.

Model-based RL has known the general shape of this since 2020. The likelihood of one-step predictions "is not always correlated with control performance" ([Lambert et al., L4DC 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04523)); value equivalence defines models by the decisions they support relative to a policy set ([Grimm et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506)); and MuZero-style models "cannot effectively generalize to evaluate unseen policies" ([He, Moerland and Oliehoek, ECAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00840)). Decision-focused learning now handles predicted constraint parameters, with a tunable trade-off between suboptimality and feasibility ([Mandi et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04951)). On LeWM itself, ranking-style goal costs improve planning ([Temporal-Distance JEPA, preprint, Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25337); [Traj-LeWM, preprint, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14125)), and fine-tuning DINO-WM on planner-chosen actions relabelled by the simulator raised PushT success from 76% to 92% ([Agrawal et al., preprint, revised 1 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09929)). None of these targets constraint decisions. A safety-specific caution comes from preference-based cost learning, where Bradley-Terry models "fail to capture the asymmetric, heavy-tailed nature of safety costs, resulting in risk underestimation" ([PbCRL, ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23565)), so a pure ranking loss on safety needs a calibrated absolute term alongside it.

World-model evaluation has started to name the right quantities. Yu et al.'s decision-centric ladder runs from logged-future prediction (L1) to fixed-policy ranking agreement (L6) and optimisation utility (L7, measured by XGap); ranking agreement appears in only about twelve works and XGap in essentially none ([preprint, Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)). Evaluator world models rank small curated sets, as when WorldGym reaches Pearson 0.78 over 17 tasks ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00613)), whereas CMA-ES ranks hundreds of candidates per generation, all close to the current mean, which is where ranking is hardest. ASAR shows the goal-cost version: raising a latent planner's proposal pool from 72 to 288 cut the feasibility of minimum-cost selection on Cube from 37.5% to 6.2% ([Li et al., preprint, revised 31 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)). Your repair sits at L1, the decision lives at L6 and L7, and the ladder predicts exactly the non-transfer you observed. A feasibility-first rule gives a sharper mechanism: only pairs that straddle the predicted feasibility boundary affect selection, so global detection can improve while accuracy on boundary pairs among the selected elites does not.

Evolutionary computation settled the ranking-versus-regression question for comparison-based optimisers more than a decade ago, and its theory applies directly. Rank-SVM surrogates for CMA-ES appeared at PPSN 2010 ([Loshchilov, Schoenauer and Sebag](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/5ac8c165-56c9-486a-bc02-2ba60d686f79/conferencedetails)). lq-CMA-ES trusts its surrogate only when the rank correlation on recent population samples is high, and otherwise adds population points to the model ([Hansen, GECCO 2019](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3321707.3321842)). If the population Kendall τ between surrogate and objective stays above a threshold close to 1, the information-geometric optimiser behind CMA-ES improves monotonically ([Akimoto, GECCO 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02638)). Model-management strategies are robust only above an accuracy threshold, with pre-screening degrading most gracefully ([Hanawa, Harada and Miura, preprint, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00844)), and Runarsson applied approximate ranking with constraint surrogates under stochastic ranking two decades ago ([PPSN 2004](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-30217-9_41)). None of this has been applied to a shared dynamics model plus safety probe that must rank controllers by their episode-level failure probability.

What is solved is the diagnosis that fit loss differs from decision loss, value-aware and decision-focused losses for reward and for static constraints, and ranking surrogates for numeric black-box optimisation. What is open is a constraint XGap as a function of selection pressure, ranking agreement measured on the optimiser's own population, calibration of a learned safety head conditional on selection inside an evolutionary loop, and training a cost model to be equivalent over a moving policy set. Evolution contributes the moving policy set itself, since the population *is* the set that value equivalence needs, together with evolution control as a budgeted active-learning policy with theory behind it. Akimoto's threshold gives a pre-registrable hypothesis: compute Kendall τ between probe-predicted and snapshot-verified failure order each generation, and if τ is far below 1, CMA-ES has no guarantee of improving true safety, however good one-step detection is.

### 1. Adaptive safety hacking on a world model

**Pitch.** Show how the probability that CMA-ES selects an imagined-safe but really-unsafe controller scales with KL-measured selection pressure on the Walker2d LeWM, split that gap into contamination and amplification, and identify which selection operators bound it.

**Why it is open.** Wachi, Tanabe and Akimoto prove the phenomenon only for non-adaptive best-of-N and name adaptive search as future work, no paper reports an exploitability curve against evolutionary pressure for any learned dynamics model, and Yu et al. find almost no XGap reporting at all. Your locked study is the only adaptive data point, and its +10.39-point widening needs a law rather than a single contrast.

**Closest competitor.** Wachi, Tanabe and Akimoto ([preprint, 24 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22915)), with Gao et al. (ICML 2023) as the template for the curve and Bhamidipaty et al. (preprint, May 2026) for the formal definition of exploitability.

**Experiment.** Reuse the locked-study harness, with its paired seeds, fresh episodes and 0.8-second snapshot branches, and extend the pressure grid to populations of 16, 64, 256 and 1,024 and to 32 or more generations. Compute √KL between the generation-g and initial search Gaussians in closed form, together with a best-of-N-equivalent "effective N", and fit Gao-style curves to the real-minus-imagined failure rate of the selected elites so that the slope becomes an exploitability coefficient for the world model. Measure contamination directly as the false-safe rate of probe-feasible candidates under the initial distribution, and amplification as its increase across generations, and test Wachi's heavy-tail condition empirically by comparing the upper tails of imagined progress for truly safe and truly unsafe probe-feasible candidates. Then compare selection-side remedies at a matched real budget: a KL or step-size trust region (the analogue of coverage control), stopping at a generation chosen on a small real validation set (HedgeTune's analogue), lower-confidence-bound ranking over a probe ensemble, your existing latent noise and ROSARL-style penalty with the 1/Δp dial made explicit, and top-k snapshot re-evaluation. If time allows, add a second model (a LeWM retrained with a higher failure share, or a released TD-MPC2 checkpoint) to test whether the coefficient shrinks without vanishing, which is the "strong model" question in miniature.

**Main risk.** Akimoto is a CMA-ES theorist and his group has publicly named this extension, so post to arXiv as early as GECCO's rules allow and stress the world model, the control setting and the exact counterfactual ground truth, which their paper lacks. A second risk is that the curve is noisy rather than lawful; the decomposition and the remedy comparison still stand if the fit is poor. The paper must also clear the bar your study did not, by finding at least one remedy that makes evolution in imagination *improve* on the starting controller's real failure rate rather than merely limiting the damage.

### 2. A QD lie detector for world-model safety predictions

**Pitch.** Use MAP-Elites as a falsifier that maps where LeWM plus probes are confidently false-safe, verify every elite by snapshot replay, and show that repairs trained on QD-found failures shrink problem 1's exploitability coefficient under a *fresh* evolutionary attack, more per real rollout than random, uncertainty-guided or RL-adversary data.

**Why it is open.** BasinLens is non-evolutionary, compares only random search, GP-UCB and CEM, and targets prediction error rather than safety. PROWL uses a PPO adversary on pixel error and found that error alone is a poor adversarial signal. DSAGE has no world model and no safety head. The software-testing line tests *agents* using a surrogate, whereas this idea inverts the roles and tests the *surrogate* using the real simulator.

**Closest competitor.** PROWL ([preprint, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18803)) for the repair loop and BasinLens ([preprint, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22421)) for failure discovery on LeWM, with DSAGE (NeurIPS 2022) and DSA-ME (GECCO 2022) as the GECCO-legible templates and INDAGO-Nexus (SSBSE 2025) as the software-testing precedent with a walker.

**Experiment.** Search over start snapshots plus action-perturbation schedules, or over controller parameters, with fitness equal to the predicted safety margin weighted by task aggressiveness. Use descriptors for speed relative to the 2.3415 m/s limit, torso pitch at the branch root, joint-limit proximity and the probe-margin bin, and build the archive with CMA-MAE in pyribs. Report verified false-safe cells per real rollout and archive coverage against random sampling, a BasinLens-style uncertainty-guided search, CEM and a PPO adversary. For the repair half, apply your stage-6 predictor recipe to QD-found failures and compare it with matched budgets of random, boundary-heuristic and uncertainty-selected data, then re-attack with a new seed and a different optimiser and re-measure the coefficient.

**Main risk.** Repairs may patch the failures found but not fresh ones, as in Go and in your own null controller result, so treat re-attack as the primary outcome to keep a negative result informative. "When World Models Lie" and "When the World Lies" already use the lie metaphor, so choose another title.

### 3. Rank-managed safety surrogates

**Pitch.** Manage the LeWM safety probe as a CMA-ES surrogate judged by its Kendall τ against the population's true failure order, use snapshot-verified top-k and boundary pairs to train a ranking loss, and test whether Akimoto's τ threshold predicts when selection in imagination stops harming real safety.

**Why it is open.** No surrogate-assisted EA uses a shared dynamics model plus safety probe scored on ranking of constraint violation, ranking agreement has been measured only on curated sets of 3 to 17 policies, and nobody has applied selection-conditional calibration to a learned safety head. Your own interpretation names the missing link: whether the model "can reliably rank controller risk over complete closed-loop rollouts".

**Closest competitor.** Akimoto ([GECCO 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02638)) and lq-CMA-ES (GECCO 2019) on the EC side; Yu et al. ([preprint, Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)) and feasibility-aware decision-focused learning ([NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04951)) on the ML side.

**Experiment.** Report, for every generation, the Kendall τ and boundary-pair accuracy between predicted and verified failure order, the false-safe rate among elites, and the constraint XGap of the selected controller. Compare four arms at a matched real-step budget: your existing regression repair; no repair with top-k snapshot re-evaluation only; a ranking loss on boundary pairs; and a ranking loss plus a calibrated absolute term, which answers PbCRL's warning about tail risk. Confirm the final controllers with paired snapshot evaluation.

**Main risk.** Statistical power. Population-level ranking metrics are cheap, but distinguishing 8% from 12.5% in selected-controller safety needs hundreds of rollouts per arm, and a τ near 1 may be out of reach, which would turn the result into a clean negative.

### 4. The failure-efficiency curve

**Pitch.** Define the curve of held-out false-safe rate against cumulative real failures spent, and show that population-based acquisition (DeepJanus-style frontier pairs, QD coverage of failure modes) reaches a target false-safe rate with fewer real failures than random, probe-margin or LeWM-disagreement acquisition.

**Why it is open.** No paper formalises the number of real failures a safety *predictor* needs. Existing budgets target regret, violations or mentor queries, and zero-violation methods hide their failure cost in priors.

**Closest competitor.** Parashar et al. ([preprint, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)), Bottero et al. (NeurIPS 2022), Marco et al. (preprint, 2020) and Fail2Progress (CoRL 2025).

**Experiment.** Hold the controller fixed, sweep failure budgets from 8 to 256 failing branches, retrain with the stage-6 recipe under each acquisition rule, and measure the held-out false-safe rate and boundary-pair accuracy. Add an arm of LeWM-imagined synthetic failures to estimate the synthetic-for-real exchange rate. The RLxF workshop result predicts that the disagreement arm will do worst, which is falsifiable.

**Main risk.** The curves may be flat or cross, and reviewers may ask whether a better predictor improves decisions, which is problem 3. This works best as the efficiency metric inside problem 2 rather than as a stand-alone paper.

### 5. Horizon-indexed irreversibility as ROSARL's absorbing set

**Pitch.** Replace Walker2d's hand-set "unhealthy" flag with irreversibility defined as failure of a budgeted CMA-ES recovery search within horizon H, map the empirical viability kernel with MAP-Elites using PAINT-style binary search to spend O(log T) oracle calls per trajectory, and show how a ROSARL-style penalty behaves when its absorbing set is learned rather than labelled.

**Why it is open.** No paper measures benchmark failure flags against an optimised recovery oracle, nobody combines learned irreversibility with ROSARL, ROSARL v3 assumes termination at violation, and v1 named terminal-only failure and risk aversion as its limits.

**Closest competitor.** PAINT ([NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10765)), REVERSAL-BENCH ([preprint, Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17745)), FGE ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15817)), and ROSARL itself.

**Experiment.** Run a factorial design that crosses the absorbing set (the benchmark flag, or learned irreversibility at H of 1, 2 and 4 seconds) with the penalty (Deb's rule, the ROSARL range, or the range divided by δ) and the evaluation (imagined or real). Report oracle calls per located boundary, and add Hopper, where 10% of fallen states are irrecoverable at 4 seconds.

**Main risk.** On Walker2d the learned absorbing set is almost empty at long horizons, and Hopper's boundary moves with the recovery tolerance, so reviewers may see the label as arbitrary and the evolutionary content as thin for GECCO. This is the supervisor-facing paper, and its natural home may be RLC or a workshop, although its headline result ("termination is not irreversibility") can strengthen the motivation sections of problems 1 and 2.

The strongest single submission combines problem 1 as the headline with one remedy arm from problem 2 or problem 3: evolution in imagination amplifies false-safe errors along a measurable law, and an evolution-native remedy shrinks the law's coefficient under a fresh attack. That gives the quotable finding recent winners share, and it rests almost entirely on infrastructure you have already validated.

## Conclusion

Three communities reached the same conclusion in 2026 from different directions: LLM safety theory through safety hacking, latent-world-model audits through attractors and ranking defects, and robotics through optimism bias all show that optimisers find the places where a learned model is confidently wrong. Every published remedy acts on the model or the filter, through conformal radii, uncertainty penalties, adversarial fine-tuning or coverage control. The selection operator, which decides *which* errors get harvested, is the one lever nobody has studied with exact ground truth, and evolutionary computation is the only field with theory for adaptive selection under surrogate error, in the information-geometric framework and in Akimoto's rank-correlation results. That makes the open core of this intersection genuinely evolutionary rather than evolution applied to someone else's problem.

The 2026 literature also changes what your results mean. "Evolution exploits world models" has been known since 2018 and is not a contribution; "the safety-hacking probability under adaptive selection grows like this, splits into contamination and amplification like this, and these selection operators bound it" is, and your locked study is its first data point. The ROSARL question deepens in the same way. What matters is less the size of the penalty than what triggers it: a minmax penalty is only as safe as the failure set that fires it, in imagination that set inherits the model's blindness, and on Walker2d the benchmark's version is mostly recoverable. Learning about danger while visiting it rarely therefore means measuring safety where selection pushes, with every real visit counted.
