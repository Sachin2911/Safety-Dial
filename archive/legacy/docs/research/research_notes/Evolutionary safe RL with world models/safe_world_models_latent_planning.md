## Q1. Safe model-based / world-model RL and latent safety filters: what each does, its constraint mechanism, its benchmarks and its limits

### Takeaway
There are two strands here, and both are crowded. The first is Dreamer-family constrained RL on Safety-Gymnasium navigation tasks: SafeDreamer, LAMBDA, Safe SLAC, PIGDreamer and Nightmare Dreamer, mostly using Lagrangians, with SafeDreamer's online planner also using feasibility-first constrained CEM. The second is Hamilton-Jacobi reachability-based latent safety filters from the Bajcsy group: LatentSafe, UNISafe, LatentCBF and the partial-observability diagnostics. In 2026 a third strand appeared on JEPA world models: SLS² (conformal robust MPC on a LeWM variant, including Push-T), FARM (failure readouts from frozen JEPA states) and AdaJEPA (test-time adaptation). Most of this is preprint work, and it overlaps directly with the student's Push-T/LeWM setting.

### Cited Findings

**Classic safe MBRL (2018–2022)**
- **SMBPO, "Safe RL by Imagining the Near Future"** (Thomas, Luo, Ma). **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2021]**; arXiv 2202.07789 was posted 15 Feb 2022. Unsafe states are made absorbing with a large terminal penalty −C, so the method is a reward penalty and not a constraint. It assumes that any irrecoverable state reaches an unsafe state within H* steps, and under that assumption shows that a large enough C makes the optimal policy avoid unsafe states using only short-horizon predictions. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/73b277c11266681122132d024f53a75b-Abstract.html)
  - Practical details: an ensemble of probabilistic dynamics models with MBPO-style rollouts and H=10 (H=5 and 10 were tried). The environments are Hopper, Cheetah-no-flip, Ant and Humanoid. **Safety is the usual MuJoCo termination (health) condition, with the alive bonus removed** so that reward does not implicitly encode safety. Baselines: MBPO (C=0), MBPO+bonus, Recovery RL, Lagrangian relaxation, SQRL and RCPO, over 5 seeds. The paper reports similar or better return with "a fraction of the violations". Larger C means fewer violations but slower reward learning. Source: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789)
- **MPC-RCE, "Constrained Model-based RL with Robust Cross-Entropy Method"** (Liu, Zhou, Chen, Zhong, Hebert, Zhao). arXiv 2010.07968, Oct 2020, revised Mar 2021. **[venue not shown on arXiv; unverified]**. It uses an NN ensemble model inside MPC. The trajectory cost is **worst case over ensemble members at each step** (Σβᵗ max_b c(s^b_{t+1})), and reward is the ensemble mean. **Elite rule:** select the feasible set Ω; if Ω is empty, sort all samples by ascending cost; otherwise sort Ω by descending reward. The authors argue that with a penalty CEM (r − λc), when few samples are feasible, the elite set becomes biased toward unsafe actions. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07968); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968)
  - Safety Gym violations in the first 10k steps, MPC-RCE vs the MPC-CEM baseline: PointGoal1 16 vs 184.33; PointGoal2 231 vs 746; CarGoal1 7 vs 103.67. Stated limitation: the cost model is a single-step predictor and the lookahead is myopic. Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968)
- **CAP, Conservative and Adaptive Penalty** (Ma, Shen, Bastani, Jayaraman). **[peer-reviewed: AAAI 2022]**. It "inflates predicted costs using an uncertainty-based penalty". Policies that satisfy the conservative constraint are proven feasible in the true environment. The penalty is adapted online. The paper states the motivating problem explicitly: "imperfect models can result in real-world constraint violations even for actions that are predicted to satisfy all constraints." Source: [arXiv 2112.07701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07701)
- **LOOP / Safe-LOOP** (Sikchi, Zhou, Held). **[peer-reviewed: CoRL 2021, PMLR v164]**. This is H-step lookahead with a learned model and a terminal value from off-policy RL, plus Actor Regularized Control (ARC). The safe variant optimises a constrained H-step objective so that cumulative constraint cost over the planning horizon stays below a threshold. It is evaluated on navigation environments. Source: [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/sikchi22a.html); [ar5iv 2008.10066](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10066)
- **LAMBDA, "Constrained Policy Optimization via Bayesian World Models"** (As, Usmanova, Curi, Krause). arXiv 2201.09802, Jan 2022. **[venue unverified this session; believed ICLR 2022]**. A Bayesian world model is used to maximise *optimistic* upper bounds on the task objective and *pessimistic* upper bounds on safety constraints. It is evaluated on Safety-Gym with claimed gains in sample efficiency and constraint violation. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09802)
- **Safe SLAC** (Hogewind, Simão, Kachman, Jansen). arXiv 2210.01801, Oct 2022. **[venue unverified]**. A safety critic on the SLAC stochastic latent model, applied to safe RL from pixels. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01801)

**Dreamer-family safe RL (2023–2026)**
- **SafeDreamer** (Huang, Ji, Xia, Zhang, Yang). **[peer-reviewed: ICLR 2024]**; arXiv 2307.07176 v1 posted Jul 2023. Three variants:
  - **OSRP:** online planning with **Constrained CEM (CCEM)**, citing Wen & Topcu 2018 and Liu et al. 2020.
  - **OSRP-Lag:** CCEM with a PID Lagrangian inside the sort key.
  - **BSRP-Lag:** background planning, where an actor is trained in imagination with an augmented Lagrangian.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07176); [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - **The CCEM elite rule is feasibility-first.** Let A_s be the set of sampled trajectories whose estimated cost J′_C = (J_C,H / H)·L is below the cost limit b. If |A_s| < N_s (too few safe trajectories), all samples are ranked by −J′_C. Otherwise only A_s is ranked, by J_R (OSRP) or by J_R − λ_p·J_C (OSRP-Lag). The top-k become elites. Planner settings: H=15, 500 samples, 6 iterations. Source: [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
  - Stated limitations: tasks are trained independently; online planning is slow, which motivates BSRP; and limited planning trajectory length and number mean OSRP and OSRP-Lag struggle to discover the optimal SafetyPointPush1 strategy that BSRP-Lag finds. The cost threshold must be set above 0 "due to errors in trained cost model and cost value model". Source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3); [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **PIGDreamer** (Huang et al.). **[peer-reviewed: ICML 2025]**. Formalises Asymmetric Constrained POMDPs and uses privileged information during training, through representation alignment and an asymmetric actor-critic. Reports outperforming existing safe RL methods. Source: [arXiv 2508.02159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02159)
- **Nightmare Dreamer** (Oseni, Wang, Zhu, Corah). arXiv 2601.04686, Jan 2026. **[workshop: RSS'25 Multi-Objective Optimization and Planning in Robotics]**. A learned world model predicts safety violations, and actions are planned using those predictions. On Safety-Gymnasium image tasks it claims nearly zero violations and about 20× efficiency over model-free baselines. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04686)
- **SPOWL, "Safe Planning and Policy Optimization via World Model Learning"** (Latyshev, Gorbov, Panov). arXiv 2506.04828, Jun 2025. It appears in an IOS Press FAIA volume (2026 listing). **[venue details unverified]**. It adaptively switches between model-based planning and direct policy execution to cope with model inaccuracy, and uses dynamic safety thresholds. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04828); [FAIA listing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA251029)
- **SafeDojo** (Tang et al.). arXiv 2606.20698, Jun 2026. **[preprint]**. Model-based safe RL for VLA policies on an interactive *video* world model, with a safety head predicting per-step cost from imagined frames. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20698)

**Latent safety filters: HJ reachability in world-model latents**
- **Latent Safety Filters, "Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis"** (Nakamura, Peters, Bajcsy). **[peer-reviewed: RSS 2025]**. HJ reachability is performed in the latent space of a generative world model. Constraint specification becomes a latent classification problem (a failure classifier), and the result safeguards arbitrary policies. Hardware: a Franka arm that must not spill a bag's contents or topple objects. Source: [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935); [RSS](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p113.html)
- **UNISafe, "Uncertainty-aware Latent Safety Filters for Avoiding OOD Failures"** (Seo, Nakamura, Bajcsy). **[peer-reviewed: CoRL 2025, PMLR 305:4442–4472]**. Source: [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/seo25a.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1)
  - **Mechanism:** epistemic uncertainty is the Jensen-Rényi divergence across an ensemble of next-latent predictors (a separate module regressing the pretrained latent dynamics). An OOD threshold is calibrated with class-conditioned, trajectory-level conformal prediction. The failure set is augmented as F_known ∪ {u > ε}, and HJ reachability is solved in latent+uncertainty space by RL in imagination (DDQN or SAC). At runtime the filter uses least-restrictive switching.
  - **Benchmarks and results:** RGB Dubins car, with 94% balanced accuracy vs 83% for LatentSafe; IsaacLab vision block-plucking, with 72% safe success and 20% failure vs 68%/30% for LatentSafe; and hardware Jenga.
  - **Limitations:** no system-level guarantee; the ensemble "may not faithfully capture" true uncertainty; aleatoric uncertainty is ignored in the safety Bellman equation.
- **LatentCBF, "How to Train Your Latent Control Barrier Function"** (Nakamura, Bishop, Man, Johnson, Manchester, Bajcsy). arXiv 2511.18606, Nov 2025. **[preprint]**. Prior latent filters switch discretely and so hurt task performance. The paper shows that the value function's Lipschitz constant scales with the margin function's, and it adds gradient penalties and mixed-distribution training. It enables smooth CBF filtering and roughly doubles task completion over switching filters. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606)
- **"How Well Do Latent World Models Understand Partially Observable Safety Constraints?"** (Kim, Nakamura, Bajcsy). arXiv 2510.06492, Oct 2025. **[preprint]**. It defines two failure modes: **estimation gaps**, where current observations do not reveal safety-critical quantities, and **prediction gaps**, where failures are observable once they occur but cannot be anticipated. It gives two diagnostics: a mutual-information safety-observability measure and a rollout-based future-safety predictability measure. Mitigations are privileged multimodal supervision and **conformal risk calibration**. Demonstrated on Franka cooking tasks, "albeit with increased conservativeness". Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)
- **Latent control barrier certificates** (Anand, Kolathaya). arXiv 2507.13871, Jul 2025. **[preprint, 6 pages]**. A neural barrier and a safe controller are learned semi-supervised in a world model's latent space. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13871)

**JEPA-specific safety and robustness work (2026, all preprints)**
- **LeWM / LeWorldModel** (Maes, Le Lidec, Scieur, LeCun, Balestriero). arXiv 2603.19312, v1 13 Mar 2026, v3 3 Jun 2026. **[preprint]**. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312); [HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)
  - **Model:** an end-to-end JEPA from pixels with two losses, next-embedding prediction and SIGReg (Gaussian latent regulariser). It has about 15M parameters and trains on one GPU.
  - **Planning:** CEM with 300 samples, 30 iterations on Push-T (10 elsewhere), top-30 elites and horizon 5 (frameskip 5). It plans up to about 48–50× faster than DINO-WM.
  - **Environments:** Push-T, Reacher, Two-Room, OGBench-Cube.
  - **Stated limitations:** short horizons, dependence on offline data coverage, and need for action labels.
  - **No safety or constraint component.**
- **SLS², "Pixels to Proofs: Probabilistically-Safe Latent World Model Control via Parallel Conformal Robust MPC"** (Nath, Srinivasan, Yin, Jiang, Fang, Chou). arXiv 2606.15594, 14 Jun 2026. **[preprint]**. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15594)
  - **World model:** a **LeWM variant** with Markovian latent state [z, Δz, …], an MLP predictor, and SIGReg plus temporal-straightening losses.
  - **Optimiser:** gradient-based GPU System Level Synthesis, which reduces to iLQR in the nominal case. Solve time is about 0.36–0.65 s/step on an RTX 4090. **It is not a sampling or evolutionary planner.**
  - **Three uses of conformal prediction:** (a) ellipsoidal latent dynamics-error sets, using a learned covariance plus a split-conformal quantile with per-step miscoverage δ/T; (b) a conformalised in-distribution latent support (a Gaussian density sublevel set), and reachable tubes are constrained to stay inside it; (c) a conformalised latent constraint classifier, with the threshold set as a quantile of scores on violating samples. Robust constraint tightening is propagated along the tube. Theorem 1 gives probability ≥ 1−δ of staying safe.
  - **Tasks:** Reacher, OGBench Cube (gripper-height constraint), **Push-T**, MuJoCo rope with an obstacle, and real dual-arm KUKA rope.
  - **Robust task completion,** SLS² vs constrained iLQR with ID vs HJ-filtered: Cube 94.29% vs 27.78% vs 0%; Rope 88.57% vs 61.11% vs 37.14%; Reacher 100% vs 97.22% vs 100%.
  - **Nominal success:** Push-T 51.54% vs DINO-WM 2.52%, under SLS²'s own protocol and not comparable to the LeWM or DINO-WM papers.
  - **Stated limitations:** Push-T remains a bottleneck because the planner relies on Euclidean latent goal distance; conformal exchangeability may not hold.
  - Baselines include Le-WM, DINO-WM, PLDM, an HJ filter, Latent Policy Barrier (LPB) and constrained iLQR. These details come from a tool summary of the HTML.
- **FARM, "Reading Failure Signals from the Internal Predictive States of a Frozen Robotic World Model"** (Pei et al.). arXiv 2609.11445, 10 Sep 2026. **[preprint]**. A 33,985-parameter supervised readout over **frozen VLA-JEPA predictive states** acts as an online failure monitor producing step-wise failure and trajectory-risk scores. Pooled AUROC/AUPRC is 85.68/88.59 in 5-fold cross-validation over 7 source tasks. It is best of 15 baselines on a 10-task benchmark and was tested on PIPER X, SO-101 and Franka hardware, with 0.2256 ms latency. It monitors failures; it does not plan. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)
- **AdaJEPA, "An Adaptive Latent World Model"** (Wang, Bounou, LeCun, Ren). arXiv 2606.32026, 30 Jun 2026. **[preprint]**. Test-time adaptation inside MPC: the model executes, observes the transition, and takes a self-supervised update before replanning, with as little as one gradient step per replan. It targets test-time distribution shift on goal-reaching tasks. **There is no explicit safety or constraint mechanism.** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32026)
- **"Calibrated Predictive Safety for Heterogeneous Robots: An Action-Conditioned JEPA Framework with Model-Based Safety Shields"** (Zhong, Liu, Wang). arXiv 2608.17496, 18 Aug 2026. **[preprint, simulation only]**. Pipeline: a proposer generates K candidates; a frozen-encoder AC-JEPA rolls each out; calibrated heads score risk and progress; a deterministic per-embodiment shield removes inadmissible candidates; and a "fallback ladder" handles an empty admissible set. This is structurally a **feasibility filter on sampled candidates**. On LIBERO-Long it improves success over a shield-only baseline and reduces collision false negatives at matched recall. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17496)

### Inferences
- The student's **"Safe CEM with feasibility-first ranking" is in substance the CCEM/RCE elite rule** of Wen & Topcu (2018), Liu et al. (2020) and SafeDreamer OSRP (ICLR 2024). The paper must present it as a known baseline and not as a contribution. Moving it to a JEPA (LeWM) model is new only in the choice of model, and SLS² already does conformal-robust safe MPC on a LeWM variant including Push-T.
- The student's Push-T finding (75% of false-safe decisions come from imagined dynamics, not from reading real observations) maps closely onto Kim et al.'s **prediction gap vs estimation gap** split. It should be framed and cited that way. It is the student's strongest diagnostic link to the latent-safety-filter literature.
- The Walker "health rule" matches how SMBPO defined safety, as MuJoCo termination. That gives the student a citable precedent for health-as-constraint, but it is not the Safety-Gymnasium velocity cost (see Q4).

### Gaps
- LAMBDA's venue and per-task numbers, and Safe-LOOP's quantitative results, were not verified this session.
- Wen & Topcu (2018): the abstract confirms constrained CE for expected trajectory-cost constraints, feasibility without needing a feasible initial policy, and an ODE convergence analysis. The exact elite rule was not read from the paper; it is inferred from how SafeDreamer and RCE describe CCEM.
- The Latent Policy Barrier (LPB) paper, a baseline in SLS², was not located or verified.
- SLS² numbers come from a tool summary of the HTML. Spot-check the tables before quoting them in a paper.

## Q2. Evolutionary and sampling-based planners under constraints in learned models: who uses feasibility-first ranking or EC constraint handling, and how does it compare with penalties?

### Takeaway
In learned-model MPC, constraint handling is dominated by three approaches:
- **CEM with feasibility-first elites** (CCEM, RCE, SafeDreamer OSRP), sometimes mixed with a Lagrangian;
- **penalty costs** (SMBPO, plain MPC-CEM, Lagrangian variants);
- **CBF shields or barrier-guided sampling around MPPI** (Shield-MPPI, BR-MPPI).

Population-based EC constraint handling beyond Deb-style feasibility rules is essentially absent from world-model planning. That includes stochastic ranking, ε-constraint, multi-objective constraint treatment, constrained or safe CMA-ES, and constrained QD. The only direct evidence on penalty vs feasibility-first in learned-model MPC is MPC-RCE, which favours feasibility-first combined with ensemble pessimism.

### Cited Findings
- **Deb's feasibility rules** (Deb, 2000, *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering*) **[peer-reviewed]**: a feasible solution beats an infeasible one; two feasible solutions are compared by fitness; two infeasible solutions by constraint violation. No penalty parameter is needed. Source: [IAS repository](https://repository.ias.ac.in/9407)
- **Wen & Topcu, Constrained Cross-Entropy Method for Safe RL** **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2018]**: CE-based policy search under expected finite-trajectory cost constraints. It "explicitly tracks its performance with respect to constraint satisfaction", learns feasible policies without assuming a feasible initial policy, and has an ODE-based convergence analysis. Source: [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/34ffeb359a192eb8174b6854643cc046-Abstract.html)
- **RCE (MPC-RCE):** feasible-first elite selection on *worst-case ensemble cost*. When feasible samples are few, penalty CEM's elite set "becomes biased toward unsafe actions". Violations in the first 10k steps are roughly 4–15× lower than the CEM baseline on Safety Gym Point/Car Goal tasks (numbers in Q1). Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968)
- **SafeDreamer OSRP vs OSRP-Lag:** a pure feasibility-first CCEM, compared with a hybrid that ranks the feasible set by J_R − λJ_C. Both had difficulty discovering the optimal SafetyPointPush1 strategy that the background-planning BSRP-Lag found. Source: [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **OmniSafe** includes model-based safe planners **CCEPETS** (constrained CE + PETS), **RCEPETS** (robust CE + PETS), **CAPPETS**, **SafeLOOP**, PETS and LOOP. They are benchmarked on SafetyPointGoal1-v0 and SafetyCarGoal1-v0 with cost limit 1.0 for 1e6 steps. Source: [OmniSafe MB benchmark](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/tree/main/benchmarks/model-based); [OmniSafe paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09304)
- **iCEM** (Pinneri et al.). arXiv 2008.06389, Aug 2020. **[venue unverified; believed CoRL 2020]**. CEM with temporally correlated (coloured-noise) actions and memory, needing 2.7–22× fewer samples with 1.2–10× better performance. **No constraint handling is described in the abstract.** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06389)
- **PETS** (Chua et al.) **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2018]**: probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling, which is the standard ensemble-plus-CEM substrate that CCEPETS and RCEPETS extend. Source: [arXiv 1805.12114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114)
- **MPPI with constraints**:
  - **Shield-MPPI** (Yin, Dawson, Fan, Tsiotras), arXiv 2302.11719, Feb 2023, RA-L 2023 per DOI 10.1109/LRA.2023.3315211. It uses a discrete-time CBF as a shield that filters MPPI's actions to make the safe set forward-invariant, with fewer samples than robust MPPI variants. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11719); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2023.3315211)
  - **BR-MPPI**, arXiv 2506.07325, Jun 2025, **[preprint]**: puts CBF-like barrier-rate conditions into MPPI's *sampling*, using learned signed distance fields. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07325)
  - **Manifold-Constrained MPPI**, arXiv 2605.24813, 2026, *Int. J. Control, Automation, and Systems*: notes that standard MPPI "relies on cost-based soft penalties that cannot guarantee hard-constraint satisfaction". Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24813)
  - All three use known or analytic dynamics, not latent world models.
- **MuJoCo MPC (MJPC) "Predictive Sampling"** (Howell et al., Dec 2022): a minimal derivative-free shooting planner that "turned out to be surprisingly competitive" with iLQG and gradient descent. It uses known MuJoCo dynamics. Source: [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- **Evolutionary planning in learned world models:**
  - **EPLS, "Evolutionary Planning in Latent Space"** (Olesen, Nguyen, Palm, Risi) **[peer-reviewed: EvoApplications/EvoStar 2021]**. A VAE plus MDRNN world model, with **Random Mutation Hill Climbing** over action sequences in latent space. The model is refined iteratively with planner rollouts. **No constraints.** Source: [arXiv 2011.11293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11293); [ITU](https://pure.itu.dk/en/publications/evolutionary-planning-in-latent-space/)
  - **Ha & Schmidhuber "World Models"** (2018) trained the controller **with CMA-ES inside the dream**. This is policy search, not MPC. Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- **QD in learned models:**
  - **DA-QD** (Lim, Grillotti, Bernasconi, Cully), arXiv 2109.08522, Sep 2021, **[venue unverified]**: QD in *imagination* with a probabilistic NN ensemble whose disagreement measures epistemic uncertainty. It is 20× more sample-efficient than QD baselines and supports zero-shot repertoire learning. **No safety constraints.** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08522)
  - **RF-QD on a physical robot** with a learned dynamics model **[GECCO 2023 Companion poster]**. Source: [arXiv 2304.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12080)
- **EC safe optimisation (GECCO community):**
  - **"Are Evolutionary Algorithms Safe Optimizers?"** (Kim, Allmendinger, López-Ibáñez) **[GECCO 2022]**: formalises safe optimisation problems, where evaluating an unsafe solution causes irrevocable loss, and benchmarks EAs against SafeOpt-style GP methods. Source: [arXiv 2203.12622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12622)
  - **Safe CMA-ES** (Uchida, Hamano, Nomura, Saito, Shirakawa) **[GECCO 2024]**: GP-estimated Lipschitz constants of the safety functions, with samples projected into the estimated safe region. It reduces unsafe evaluations relative to BO and EA baselines. Source: [arXiv 2405.10534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10534)
  - Both are black-box optimisation, not world-model planning.
- **Evolutionary safe RL:** **Safety-Oriented Search** (Marchesini, Corsi, Farinelli) **[AAAI 2022]** combines evolutionary search with deep RL. "Safe mutations" use visited unsafe states, with sample-based bound estimation, on Safety Gym. It is model-free. Source: [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20737)
- **JEPA planners use CEM by default:** LeWM uses CEM (300 samples, 30 elites) Source: [LeWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3). Temporal-Distance-JEPA also uses CEM, per a search excerpt, unverified Source: [arXiv HTML 2607.25337](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25337v2). The 2026 JEPA shield paper uses propose → roll out → shield-filter → fallback, which is again feasibility filtering Source: [arXiv 2608.17496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17496).

### Inferences
- In EC terms, CCEM/RCE/OSRP is **Deb's feasibility rule embedded in CEM's truncation selection**. The EC literature offers many unexplored alternatives for latent planners: stochastic ranking, ε-constraint relaxation, treating violation as a separate objective (NSGA-II style), augmented-Lagrangian CMA-ES and safe CMA-ES projection. None were found evaluated inside learned or latent world-model MPC. A controlled comparison of constraint-handling techniques, measured by *true* violation and *false-safe rate* under an imperfect model, is a gap that GECCO reviewers would recognise.
- Penalty vs feasibility-first is only lightly studied in MBRL (MPC-RCE's ablation and SafeDreamer's OSRP vs OSRP-Lag). Neither study measured how ranking interacts with **model error at the constraint boundary**. That is exactly the student's existing measurement (45% false-safe at zero margin).

### Gaps
- No verified paper uses **CMA-ES as an MPC optimiser with constraints inside a learned or latent world model**. Searches returned only Ha & Schmidhuber (policy search) and EPLS (RMHC, unconstrained).
- No paper was found using **NSGA-II or multi-objective EAs for action-sequence planning in learned world models**. The arXiv search for NSGA-II + MPC + learned returned only MPC cost-weight tuning.
- No **constrained or safety-aware QD in a world model** was found. A search surfaced QD for safety-critical scenario and terrain generation (for example arXiv 2506.01362), but these were not verified.
- Head-to-head numbers for penalty CEM vs CCEM vs MPPI-CBF on the same latent model were not found.

## Q3. How optimisers exploit world-model errors in safety-relevant ways, and which mitigations are used

### Takeaway
Exploitation of model error by the optimiser is long established, from ME-TRPO in 2018 and Ha & Schmidhuber's CMA-ES dream exploit to CAP's "predicted-feasible but really infeasible". In 2025–2026 it has been demonstrated specifically for sampling planners on latent world models. The clearest result is "proposal overgeneration": a larger candidate pool makes minimum-cost selection *more* likely to pick infeasible sequences. Related findings are broken action ranking in frozen JEPAs, intervention gaps, and backdoors steering CEM. The mitigations in use are:
- ensembles with pessimistic or worst-case costs;
- uncertainty-penalised costs;
- conformal calibration of dynamics errors, constraint classifiers, OOD thresholds or risk;
- OOD support constraints;
- temperature or noise injection;
- test-time adaptation;
- frequent replanning, which masks errors rather than fixing them.

### Cited Findings
- **Ha & Schmidhuber (2018), World Models.** A CMA-ES-evolved controller found an "adversarial policy" in the dream: it moved so that the monsters "never shoots a single fireball". Raising the sampling temperature τ was the mitigation. At τ=0.10, dream score was 2086±140 but real score 193±58. At τ=1.15, dream score was 918±546 and real score 1092±556. Source: [worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/); [arXiv 1803.10122](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)
- **ME-TRPO** (Kurutach et al., 2018): "the learned policy tends to exploit regions where insufficient data is available for the model", which is mitigated with a model ensemble. Source: [arXiv 1802.10592](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10592)
- **MBPO** (Janner et al., NeurIPS 2019): short model rollouts branched from real data, to limit compounding model bias. Source: [arXiv 1906.08253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253)
- **CAP** (AAAI 2022): "imperfect models can result in real-world constraint violations even for actions that are predicted to satisfy all constraints". The fix is an uncertainty-inflated cost. Source: [arXiv 2112.07701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07701)
- **MPC-RCE:** worst-case-over-ensemble cost. **SafeDreamer:** uses H-step cost-model rollouts as an "alternative estimation for avoiding errors of the cost critic". It needs a cost limit above 0 because of cost-model and critic errors, and it attributes Safe SLAC's cost floor to "inaccuracies of the world model". Source: [ar5iv RCE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968); [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **"Action from Adjacent Set in Physical Space Outperforms the Best Prediction in World Models"** (Li, Liu, Liu, Fang). arXiv 2607.23602, Jul 2026. **[preprint]**. In sampling-plus-latent-world-model controllers, "residual prediction error can give an infeasible sequence an anomalously low cost, and a larger proposal pool gives such errors more chances to outrank feasible alternatives". The authors call this **proposal overgeneration**. In the Cube task, raising the proposal budget from 72 to 288 cut the feasibility of min-latent-cost selection from .375 to .062 for position targets and from .344 to .031 for position+yaw targets, even though every larger pool contained a feasible sequence. Their fix, ASAR, aggregates an "adjacent set" of low-cost proposals by early-action-prefix similarity instead of taking the argmin. It adds 28.0/24.0/18.7 percentage points of success at 72/144/288 proposals on 75 Carry-and-Release queries. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)
- **ARC-Bench** (Zhang, Li). arXiv 2609.05461. The abstract page gave a date of 12 Aug 2026, which does not match a 2609 identifier. **[preprint]**. Frozen JEPA-WM checkpoints with DINOv2, V-JEPA 1 and V-JEPA 2 encoders often rank candidate actions wrongly by latent distance: on manipulation "the top-scored candidate is almost always suboptimal", and rankings invert in mazes. **Closed-loop replanning masks the defect**: success collapses when replanning frequency drops. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)
- **"The Intervention Gap in Latent World Models"** (Vakalis). arXiv 2608.29998, Aug 2026. **[preprint]**. Open-loop imagined transitions do not move task variables the way real interventions do, across TD-MPC2, DreamerV3 and PreJEPA. On Cheetah, imagined 5-step effects are worse than predicting zero effect, while reward prediction stays accurate. **Ensemble disagreement poorly ranks errors outside the training data.** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)
- **"Closing the Train-Test Gap in World Models for Gradient-Based Planning"** (Parthasarathy, Kalra, Agrawal, LeCun, Bounou, Izmailov, Goldblum). arXiv 2512.09929, Dec 2025. **[preprint]**. World models are trained for next-state prediction but used to optimise action sequences. The paper proposes train-time data synthesis that lets gradient planning match or beat CEM at about 10% of the time budget. A search-engine excerpt of the full text describes "Adversarial World Modeling" that targets regions where the model is poor and says optimising through learned models "induce[s] adversarial inputs". The fetched abstract summary did not use that term, so the method naming is unconfirmed. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09929); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09929)
- **"When the World Lies: Backdoor Attacks on Latent World Models"** (Riaño, Abad, Picek, Urbieta). arXiv 2609.15781, Sep 2026. **[preprint]**. A poisoned checkpoint reshapes local latent dynamics so that "the victim's own optimization (Dreamer-style actor training in imagination, or MPC/CEM planning over predicted futures) re-discovers the attacker's target action". This shows directly that CEM will find and use whatever the model's local dynamics reward. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15781)
- **"Where World Models Break: Natural-Input Failure Discovery"** (Shi et al.). arXiv 2608.22421, Aug 2026. **[preprint]**. It formalises budgeted search for rare condition-action inputs that trigger severe prediction failures. Its method, BasinLens, uses uncertainty-guided global search with typed local replacements; per the abstract it is **not** an EA, CMA-ES or adversarial method. It finds "reproducible and locally persistent failure modes" that average-case metrics hide. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22421)
- **UNISafe** shows world-model hallucination under OOD actions: "the robot teleports away from the failure region". It mitigates this with ensemble Jensen-Rényi uncertainty, a conformal OOD threshold, and an augmented failure set used in reachability. Without uncertainty, LatentSafe "overconfidently misclassifies unsafe OOD actions as safe". Source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1)
- **SLS²** puts conformal margins on latent dynamics error, conformal in-distribution support, and a conformalised constraint classifier (threshold from violating samples) into robust MPC, with tightening along tubes, to avoid "over-optimistic plans". Source: [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15594)
- **Conformal planning (pre-world-model):** Lindemann et al. (arXiv 2210.10254, Oct 2022, **[venue unverified]**) use conformal prediction regions on predicted trajectories of *other agents* inside MPC for provable probabilistic safety (CARLA). This is the template that SLS² and UNISafe adapt to latents. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10254)
- **Kim, Nakamura, Bajcsy (2025):** conformal risk calibration mitigates prediction gaps, at the cost of conservativeness. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)
- **AdaJEPA (2026):** online self-supervised adaptation of a JEPA during MPC addresses distribution shift for goal reaching. It does not address safety. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32026)
- **PROCO** (Xue et al., arXiv 2605.01356, May 2026, **[preprint]**): offline safe RL that uses a learned dynamics model to generate counterfactual unsafe samples. It targets "safe-but-infeasible states", which satisfy constraints now but violate them within a few steps, and grounds cost knowledge from an LLM. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)

### Inferences
- Proposal overgeneration is the goal-cost version of the optimiser's curse. Feasibility-first ranking should make it *worse at the constraint boundary*: when real feasible trajectories are scarce, the predicted-feasible set over-represents trajectories whose constraint predictions are most optimistic. The student's 45% false-safe rate at zero margin is consistent with this. **No paper was found that measures how false-safe rate scales with population size, elite fraction or iteration count (selection pressure) in a constrained latent planner.** That is a natural, cheap, evolutionary-computation-native experiment on one RTX 5090.
- Most mitigations act on the *model* (ensembles, adaptation, adversarial data) or on the *constraint threshold* (conformal margins). Few act on the *optimiser's selection operator*. ASAR is one exception, for goal costs. EC has selection-side tools that have not been tried here: robust or noisy-fitness evaluation, resampling, re-evaluation of elites under ensemble members, and selection on lower confidence bounds.
- The student's negative result fits a 2026 trend: predictor fine-tuning with boundary-focused data did not consistently help and hurt ordinary prediction. AdaJEPA reports adaptation helps for goal reaching, but no paper was found showing it fixes *constraint* false-safes. Conformal margins (SLS², UNISafe, Kim et al.) trade false-safes for conservativeness.

### Gaps
- No verified study quantifies false-safe or false-negative rates of constraint predictions as a function of planner hyperparameters.
- ARC-Bench's date discrepancy is unresolved.
- The exact method naming in 2512.09929 is unconfirmed.

## Q4. Standard benchmarks for safe world-model RL; whether SafetyWalker2dVelocity is used in model-based safe RL; reported numbers

### Takeaway
The de facto benchmark for safe *world-model* RL is **Safety-Gymnasium navigation tasks with Point or Car agents, especially the vision-only variants**:
- SafeDreamer: PointGoal1/2, PointPush1, PointButton1, CarGoal1 (vision), plus vector PointGoal1 and CarGoal1;
- OmniSafe model-based: PointGoal1 and CarGoal1;
- MPC-RCE: Safety Gym Point/Car Goal.

Latent safety-filter and JEPA-safety papers instead use bespoke robot tasks: Dubins car, block plucking, Jenga, cooking, Push-T, Cube, rope, LIBERO. No model-based or world-model safe-RL paper verified this session reports results on **SafetyWalker2dVelocity**. Velocity tasks have no vision variant in the docs, and the student's health rule differs from the official velocity cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Safety-Gymnasium** (Ji et al.) **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2023]**: single- and multi-agent safety tasks with vector and vision-only inputs, plus the SafePO library of 16 algorithms. Source: [arXiv 2310.12567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12567)
- **Safe Velocity tasks (v1):** cost = 1 if speed exceeds the threshold, else 0. Speed is √(vx²+vy²), except for Swimmer, which uses x-velocity. Thresholds are 50% of the max velocity PPO reached after 1M steps: **Walker2d 2.3415**, Hopper 0.7402, Ant 2.6222, Humanoid 1.4149, HalfCheetah 3.2096, Swimmer 0.2282. v1 raised all thresholds relative to v0; for example Hopper went from 0.37315 to 0.7402. The documentation lists no vision variants. Source: [Safety-Gymnasium docs](https://safety-gymnasium.readthedocs.io/en/stable/environments/safe_velocity.html)
- **SafeDreamer settings and numbers:**
  - Five vision tasks (SafetyPointGoal1, PointGoal2, PointPush1, PointButton1, CarGoal1) plus vector PointGoal1 and CarGoal1. **Cost limit 2.0**, which the authors call lower than "the conventional value of 25.0". Baselines (LAMBDA, Safe SLAC, MBPPO-Lag) were rerun with their limits changed from 25 to 2.0. Main results run to 2M steps with 5 runs.
  - BSRP-Lag "attains rewards similar to ... LAMBDA, with a substantial 94.3% reduction in costs".
  - Car-Racing: BSRP-Lag 763.1±1.34 reward and 0.04±0.01 cost, vs DreamerV3 775.1±0.56 and 580.1±2.35.
  - FormulaOne: BSRP-Lag 25.3±0.51 and 0.43±0.08, vs DreamerV3 32.2±1.22 and 56.36±2.32.
  - The main Safety-Gymnasium comparisons are given as curves and rliable aggregates, not tables.
  - Source: [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ece182f93af26c64187ba3f7dfd4309a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **OmniSafe model-based benchmark:** SafetyPointGoal1-v0 and SafetyCarGoal1-v0 only, with cost limit 1.0 and 1e6 steps. A tool summary of the page says SafeLOOP reached costs of about 0.04–0.16 with rewards of about 22–23; not independently checked. Source: [OmniSafe MB benchmark](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/tree/main/benchmarks/model-based)
- **MPC-RCE:** Safety Gym PointGoal1/2 and CarGoal1/2. Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.07968)
- **SMBPO:** Hopper, Cheetah-no-flip, Ant and Humanoid, with termination as the unsafe event. **Walker2d is not in its environment list.** Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789)
- **Latent safety-filter benchmarks:** Dubins car (RGB), IsaacLab block plucking and hardware Jenga (UNISafe); Franka bag and toppling tasks (Latent Safety Filters); Franka cooking tasks (Kim et al.). Source: [UNISafe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1); [LSF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)

  - LeWM: Push-T, Reacher, Two-Room, OGBench-Cube Source: [LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)

  - JEPA shields: LIBERO-Long Source: [2608.17496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17496)
  - FARM: 10-task failure benchmark plus 3 real robots Source: [FARM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)
  - Related latent planners: DINO-WM ([arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983)) and PLDM ([arXiv 2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14819)) are the main JEPA-style planning baselines used by LeWM and SLS².
- **Velocity tasks in offline safe RL:** **B2R** (Su et al., **[NeurIPS 2025]**) satisfies constraints on 35 of 38 safety-critical tasks; the fetched abstract did not name the benchmarks. A search snippet claimed it reports Walker2dVelocity results, but this was not verified. Source: [arXiv 2509.25727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25727)

### Inferences
- A GECCO paper on SafetyWalker2dVelocity would sit outside the standard safe-world-model benchmark set. That is defensible as "a locomotion constraint where false-safe imagination matters", but reviewers may ask why not Safety-Gymnasium Point/Car Goal vision tasks, the SafeDreamer setting. ## Q5. Where the literature is crowded and where an evolutionary angle is under-explored

### Takeaway
Avoid proposing a new Lagrangian or Dreamer-based safe RL agent, a new HJ latent safety filter, or "conformal margins for LeWM MPC". All three are crowded or were taken in 2025–2026: SafeDreamer and its successors; the Bajcsy-group filters; SLS². The under-explored space where an evolutionary contribution is credible is **the optimiser itself as the source of safety failures in latent world models**. Specifically:
- how selection pressure and EC constraint-handling choices in CEM, CMA-ES or GA planners interact with model error to produce false-safe plans;
- EC-native mitigations: robust or noisy-fitness selection, ensemble re-evaluation of elites, multi-objective safety-margin handling, and QD maps of where the latent safety predictor fails.

### Cited Findings
- **Crowded: Dreamer-style constrained RL on Safety-Gymnasium.** SafeDreamer (ICLR 2024), PIGDreamer (ICML 2025), Nightmare Dreamer (RSS'25 workshop), SPOWL (2025), Safe SLAC and LAMBDA. Source: [SafeDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07176); [PIGDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02159); [Nightmare Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04686); [SPOWL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04828)
- **Crowded: latent HJ safety filters and CBFs.** LatentSafe (RSS 2025), UNISafe (CoRL 2025), LatentCBF (2025), partial-observability diagnostics (2025) and latent CBCs (2025). Source: [LSF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935); [UNISafe](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/seo25a.html); [LatentCBF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18606); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492); [Anand & Kolathaya](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13871)
- **Taken in 2026: conformal-robust safe MPC on LeWM, including Push-T** (SLS², with a gradient-based SLS/iLQR optimiser). Source: [SLS²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594)
- **Active in 2026: JEPA robustness audits** (ARC-Bench, the intervention gap, BasinLens failure discovery, backdoors, proposal overgeneration), **JEPA failure monitors** (FARM) and **JEPA adaptation** (AdaJEPA). Source: [ARC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461); [Intervention gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998); [BasinLens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22421); [Backdoor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15781); [ASAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602); [FARM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445); [AdaJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32026)
- **Sparse: evolutionary planning in learned latent models.** EPLS (RMHC, EvoApplications 2021, unconstrained) and the CMA-ES dream controller from 2018, which exploited the model. Source: [EPLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11293); [World Models](https://worldmodels.github.io/)
- **Sparse: QD with learned dynamics.** DA-QD (imagination with ensemble uncertainty) and RF-QD at GECCO'23 Companion, with no safety constraints. Source: [DA-QD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08522); [RF-QD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12080)
- **The GECCO community has safe-optimisation work, but not on world models:** "Are EAs Safe Optimizers?" (GECCO 2022) and Safe CMA-ES (GECCO 2024). Source: [Kim et al. GECCO'22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12622); [Safe CMA-ES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10534)
- **The goal-cost selection-pressure failure is documented in latent planners**: more proposals lead to more infeasible picks. This motivates studying the same effect for constraint predictions. Source: [ASAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)

### Inferences
These are candidate GECCO positions, ranked by novelty against what was found.
1. **Selection pressure as a safety variable: the "evolutionary optimiser's curse" for constraints.** Systematically measure the false-safe rate (predicted feasible, really unsafe) of constrained CEM, CMA-ES and GA planners on a JEPA model as population size, elite ratio, iterations and the constraint-handling rule vary. The rules to compare are Deb feasibility-first (= CCEM), penalty, ε-constraint, stochastic ranking and violation-as-objective. ASAR shows the effect for goal costs; no constraint-side study was found. The student's existing measurement infrastructure (false-safe at margin, Push-T diagnosis) maps onto this directly.
2. **Robust or pessimistic selection operators instead of model fixes.**
   - Re-evaluate elites under ensemble members or perturbed latents, as in RCE's worst case but used as a *selection* operator.
   - Rank on a lower confidence bound of constraint satisfaction.
   - Apply conformal-calibrated margins only at selection time.
   This contrasts with SLS² (gradient-based, model-side tubes) and UNISafe (RL reachability), and it suits the student's negative fine-tuning result: "fixing the model didn't help, so fix the search."
3. **Multi-objective treatment of safety margin vs task progress (NSGA-II-style planning).** This returns a Pareto set over margin and return instead of one threshold, which is relevant because conformal margins trade safety for conservativeness (Kim et al.; SLS²). No NSGA-II or MOEA action-sequence planner in a world model was found.
4. **QD or evolutionary stress-testing of latent safety predictors.** MAP-Elites over behaviour descriptors such as latent region or action pattern would map where the world model is false-safe. That complements BasinLens, which is explicitly non-EC, and FARM, which is a supervised readout, and it could feed boundary data back into training. The student's boundary-focused fine-tuning could be revisited with EC-discovered hard cases instead of random or boundary heuristics.
5. Avoid presenting feasibility-first CEM, conformal LeWM MPC, or a new Dreamer-Lagrangian agent as the contribution; use them as baselines.
