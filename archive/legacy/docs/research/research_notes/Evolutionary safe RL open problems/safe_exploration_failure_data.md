# Safe exploration as "learning what failure looks like without failing": failure-data acquisition for world models

Research date: 5 October 2026. Scope: methods that learn about unsafe regions (for a world model, a safety predictor or a policy) while limiting real failures, and whether "minimise the real failures needed to learn an accurate safety model" is formalised anywhere. Status tags: **[peer-reviewed: venue]**, **[workshop]**, **[preprint]**, **[venue unverified]**. "Snippet only" means the claim came from a search-result summary and the primary page was not read; treat it as a lead, not a fact.

Relation to earlier notes. ## 1. What do 2024-2026 safe exploration surveys and benchmarks list as open?

### Takeaway
Recent surveys still treat "zero-violation learning" as an open problem and say little or nothing about world models or evolutionary methods; the sharper 2026 critiques come from world-model position papers and workshop papers, which argue that model likelihood and model uncertainty are poor proxies for risk and that safety signals must be grounded in real outcomes. No survey found frames "how many real failures are needed to learn an accurate safety model" as a question.

### Cited Findings
- **Kushwaha, Ravish, Lamba, Kumar, Mahajan, "A Survey of Safe Reinforcement Learning and Constrained MDPs: A Technical Survey on Single-Agent and Multi-Agent Safety"**, arXiv 2505.17342 (v1 May 2025; v2 29 April 2026) **[preprint]**. It proposes five open problems; its outline names them P1 "Zero-violation", P2 "Partial Observability", P3 "Decentralized SafeMARL", P4 "Competitive SafeMARL", P5 "Non-stationarity". Three of five are multi-agent. The accessible text centres on policy gradients, Lagrangian methods, shields and formal verification; the excerpt read did not discuss world models or evolutionary methods. [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17342v2)
- **Eckel, Zhang, Bödecker, "Revisiting Safe Exploration in Safe Reinforcement Learning"**, arXiv 2409.01245 (2 September 2024) **[preprint]**. It argues standard training-time safety metrics "fail to distinguish how costs accrue, treating infrequent severe cost events as equal to frequent mild ones", and proposes **expected maximum consecutive cost steps (EMCC)** as a training-time safety metric. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01245)
- The standard training-time metric in world-model safe RL is the **cost rate**: SafeDreamer defines it as "the average cost throughout the entire training phase", ρc = Σ c_t / T over all T interaction steps. [SafeDreamer, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.07176) LAMBDA uses the same quantity under the name "cost regret". [LAMBDA, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.09802)
- **Ma, Huang, Kim, Jang, "Rethinking World Models for Safety-Critical Embodied Systems"**, arXiv 2609.03774 (3 September 2026, revised 1 October 2026) **[preprint, position paper]**. It states "high predictive likelihood and visual fidelity do not necessarily ensure that a model preserves the evidence required for safe decision-making", names three mismatches (likelihood versus risk, prediction versus intervention, finite-horizon prediction versus accumulated consequences), and lists five open challenges: identifying which futures matter, validating counterfactual reasoning, maintaining revisable safety memories, translating learned consequences into executable constraints, and determining "when evidence is sufficient to act". It proposes a "Risk-Informed World Model" (RIWM). It does not explicitly address failure-data scarcity. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03774)
- **Wang, "Learning from World Feedback: Why Model Uncertainty Fails as a Risk Signal in Model-Based RL"**, arXiv 2607.16591 (18 July 2026) **[workshop: ICML 2026 RLxF]**. In a collision-avoidance MPC task across four world-model architectures, dynamics-uncertainty penalties **raised collision rates from 26% to 34%**; replacing them with world-feedback signals (minimum lidar margin, time-to-collision, an outcome-supervised feedback model trained on prior collision labels) cut collisions to **1-14%** without retraining other components. Model uncertainty and task risk correlated at **r < 0.15**, because uncertainty lives in state-prediction space while risk lives at constraint boundaries. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16591)
- **Wan et al., "No Free Checker: A Survey of Verifiers for Robot Policies"**, arXiv 2609.09250 (8 September 2026) **[preprint survey]**. It covers about 150 verifiers (reward models, success or failure detectors, formal verifiers, world-model evaluation) and finds that "credibility falls as availability rises" across verifier families. Specific open problems on failure-data scarcity were not visible in the abstract. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)
- **FailureBench** is a 2026 benchmark of common real-world failure scenarios for real-robot RL, introduced with FARL (see Question 2), whose headline metric is the number of intervention-requiring failures during real-world RL. [FARL, arXiv 2601.07821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07821v1)
- Older reference surveys: García & Fernández, "A Comprehensive Survey on Safe Reinforcement Learning", **[peer-reviewed: JMLR 16, 2015]** [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v16/garcia15a.html); Gu et al., "A Review of Safe Reinforcement Learning: Methods, Theory and Applications", arXiv 2205.10330 (revised to v5) **[venue unverified this session]** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10330v5); "Safe Learning and Optimization Techniques: Towards a Survey of the State of the Art", arXiv 2101.09505 (2021), which defines SafeOpt-style safe optimisation as avoiding "evaluating any unsafe input points altogether during the search, i.e., the number of allowed failures is zero" (snippet only) [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.09505).

### Inferences
- **What is solved.** Measurement of training-time safety exists (cost rate, cost regret, EMCC, counts of interventions), and every serious world-model safe-RL paper since SafeDreamer reports at least a cost-rate curve.
- **What is open, per the 2026 literature.** (a) Zero-violation learning without strong priors (Kushwaha et al. P1). (b) Which futures matter for safety, and when evidence is sufficient to act (Ma et al.). (c) Model-internal uncertainty is a weak proxy for constraint risk (Wang 2026; consistent with the Intervention Gap result already in the earlier report). Point (c) matters directly for acquisition: an acquisition rule driven by world-model uncertainty is likely to spend its real samples in the wrong place.
- **What no survey asks.** None of the surveys read separates (i) failures *incurred* while learning from (ii) failures *used as labels* for a safety model, and none asks for a "failure-efficiency" curve (safety-model accuracy against real failures spent). That framing is the student's own, and it appears unclaimed at survey level.
- **Evolutionary angle.** No survey read lists evolutionary or population-based methods as a safe-exploration family, which leaves the GECCO framing uncontested but also means the student must define the problem from scratch.
- **Relevance to Walker2d LeWM.** The student's 11% to 68% one-step detection gain is a data-composition result. Wang (2026) gives an independent reason to expect that LeWM uncertainty would not have found those failure states on its own, which motivates acquisition rules that use real outcomes (snapshot branches) rather than latent disagreement.

### Gaps
- The full text of Kushwaha et al. Section 6 (problem statements for P1-P5) was not read; only the outline names were visible.
- No 2025-2026 survey dedicated to *safe exploration* (as opposed to safe RL generally) was found; searched twice with different phrasings.
- No new 2025-2026 safe-RL benchmark was found that reports failure-label efficiency for a safety model. CRAX (2026) was covered in the earlier report.

## 2. Model-based safe exploration with world models: how many real violations do they incur during training, and do they report it?

### Takeaway
Reporting is inconsistent. SafeDreamer, LAMBDA, SMBPO, CAP, ActSafe and FARL report training-time violations in some form (cost rate, cost regret, cumulative violations or intervention counts), whereas PIGDreamer and Nightmare Dreamer report only final or curve-level cost without a training-violation tally. Every method that learns its cost model online learns it from real violations; the only routes to near-zero training violations are strong priors (a safe policy, a conservative policy prior, a supervised warm-up, or simulator labels), and in 2026 several systems moved training entirely into a world model whose safety head was trained on simulator collisions.

### Inferences
- **What is solved.** (a) Large reductions in training-time cost relative to model-free baselines (SMBPO, SafeDreamer, ActSafe). (b) Provable safety throughout learning *given* a prior: GP regularity plus a warm-up (ActSafe) or a conservative policy prior (SOOPER). (c) Zero real violations during *policy* learning when a world model and a simulator-labelled safety head are available (SafeDojo).
- **Who holds the closest work.** The ETH Zürich Krause group (LAMBDA, ActSafe, SOOPER) owns "provably safe exploration with learned world models"; the earlier report did not cite ActSafe or SOOPER and should be updated. FARL (Xu group) owns "world-model safety critic trained offline to reduce real failures". Cao & Bansal own "adapt the safety filter from observed latent prediction error".
- **What remains open.** (1) None of these papers reports *how accurate the cost or safety model becomes per real violation spent*; they report policy outcomes. (2) None separates violations that taught the cost model something from violations that were wasted repeats. (3) Every zero-violation result moves the failure knowledge somewhere else (warm-up data, a safe prior, a simulator, an LLM prior in PROCO), so the hidden "failure cost" is never accounted for. (4) FOSP shows composition matters but varies it as a fixed offline ratio, not as an adaptive acquisition decision.
- **Where evolution could uniquely help.** A population evaluated in imagination produces many candidate behaviours that the model rates as near-boundary; selecting which few to verify for real is a surrogate-assisted EA decision with a long GECCO history (pre-screening, infill criteria). The DreamLedger and When-World-Models-Lie results suggest a natural population-level signal: the spread of imagined-versus-real error across elites, not per-state ensemble disagreement.
- **Relevance to Walker2d LeWM.** The student can do what none of the tabled papers did: hold the policy or controller fixed, vary only the failure data the probe or predictor sees, and plot detection or false-safe rate against *real failures spent*, counted exactly via snapshot branching. That converts the existing 11% to 68% result into the first point on a failure-efficiency curve.

## 3. Active learning and data acquisition targeted at safety boundaries: is evolution or a population ever used to choose the samples?

### Takeaway
Two mature but separate literatures exist. Safe Bayesian optimisation and safe active learning (SafeOpt, GoSafe/GoSafeOpt, information-theoretic safe exploration) learn constraints with zero or few violations, but in low-dimensional parameter spaces with GP models. Search-based software testing has used evolution plus a learned failure classifier to map failure regions since 2018 (NSGA-II-DT, DeepJanus, INDAGO, QD-based policy testing), but failures there are free simulator runs and the goal is to find failures, not to avoid them. No work was found that uses a population or evolutionary search to choose which *risky real samples* to spend in order to improve a *world model's safety predictions*, which is the student's niche.

### Cited Findings
**Safe BO and safe active learning (GP-based, constraint learning with few or no failures)**
- SafeOpt-style methods aim for zero failures; successors include SafeOpt-MC, Swarm-based SafeOpt, StageOpt, SafeMDP, GoOSE and others (snippet only, from a 2021 survey). [arXiv 2101.09505](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.09505)
- **GoSafe** ("Globally Optimal Safe Robot Learning") extends safe BO to discover disconnected safe regions but "cannot handle high-dimensional systems"; **GoSafeOpt** is presented as "the first provably safe and optimal algorithm that can safely discover globally optimal policies for systems with high-dimensional state space" **[peer-reviewed: Artificial Intelligence journal, 2023, per Elsevier article ID S0004370223000681]**. [GoSafe, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13281); [GoSafeOpt, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09562v3.pdf); [AIJ PDF](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/107381006/1_s2.0_S0004370223000681_main.pdf)
- **Bottero, Luis, Vinogradska, Berkenkamp, Peters, "Information-Theoretic Safe Exploration with Gaussian Processes"** **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2022]**: chooses "the most informative safe parameters to evaluate" using the GP posterior; safety "is not violated with high probability while exploring by learning about the constraint up to arbitrary precision". This is the closest formal statement of "learn the safety boundary as fast as possible without crossing it". [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/c628644624c1be9c8cfb1541fa6421fd-Abstract.html)
- The same authors' "Information-Theoretic Safe Bayesian Optimization", arXiv 2402.15347 (February 2024, revised May 2024) **[venue unverified]**, combines this criterion with BO in continuous domains "without extra hyperparameters". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15347)
- **Fiedler, Menn, Kreisköther, Trimpe, "On Safety in Safe Bayesian Optimization"** **[peer-reviewed: TMLR 2024]**: SafeOpt implementations typically use heuristic uncertainty scaling that "invalidate[s] safety guarantees"; proposes Real-β-SafeOpt and Lipschitz-only LoSBO (author list per search summary). [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2024/fiedler2024tmlr-safety)
- **Tebbe et al., "Efficiently Computable Safety Bounds for Gaussian Processes in Active Learning"** **[peer-reviewed: AISTATS 2024]**: safe active learning of physical systems; reduces the samples needed to estimate high safety probabilities. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/tebbe24a.html)
- Active level-set estimation (learning where a function crosses a threshold) is an established boundary-focused active-learning problem; a 2024 transfer-learning variant exists (snippet only). [arXiv 2410.05660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05660v1)
- **ActSafe** uses "the model epistemic uncertainty as an intrinsic reward and selects policies within the safe set that yield trajectories with high uncertainties", then switches to extrinsic reward after n* episodes. Its acquisition targets *dynamics* uncertainty, not the safety boundary. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09486v2)

**Boundary-focused failure discovery for robot policies (2026)**
- **Kim, "ROBOGATE: Adaptive Failure Discovery for Safe Robot Policy Deployment via Two-Stage Boundary-Focused Sampling"**, arXiv 2603.22126 (v1 23 March 2026; v4 20 April 2026) **[preprint, single author]**: Stage 1 Latin hypercube sampling over an 8-D parameter space; Stage 2 concentrates samples in the "30-70% success rate transition zone"; a logistic-regression risk model reaches AUC 0.780. The v4 abstract reports over 50,000 experiments across four embodiments; an earlier HTML version described 30,000 experiments on two embodiments, so figures changed across versions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22126)
- **Parashar, Luo, Sharma, Veer, Schmerling, Sobolewski, Yu, Fan, Pavone, "Coverage Aware Active Evaluation for Failure Discovery with Paired Systems"**, arXiv 2608.13719 (13 August 2026) **[preprint]**: under "limited testing budgets", uses cheap proxy evaluations plus sparse target-system evaluations, a "control-variate-inspired residual" risk model and "a support-aware mutual-information objective"; finds about **2× as many failures** as random sampling and active-learning baselines across driving, manipulation and quadruped tasks. No evolutionary search. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)

**Evolution choosing samples to characterise failure regions (search-based testing)**
- **Ben Abdessalem, Nejati, Briand, Stifter, "Testing Vision-Based Control Systems Using Learnable Evolutionary Algorithms"** **[peer-reviewed: ICSE 2018]** (author list from memory; title and venue verified): NSGA-II plus decision-tree classifiers; the search refines the classifiers "so that the models can accurately characterize critical regions"; **78% more distinct critical test scenarios** than a baseline EA on an industrial automotive system. [ICSE PDF](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/33706/1/ICSE-Main-24.pdf); [ICSE 2018 page](https://2024.esec-fse.org/details/icse-2018/icse-2018-Technical-Papers/30/Testing-Vision-Based-Control-Systems-Using-Learnable-Evolutionary-Algorithms)
- **NSGA-II-SVM**, "Guiding the Search Towards Failure-Inducing Test Inputs Using Support Vector Machines" **[workshop: DeepTest @ ICSE 2024]**: iteratively builds SVM models of the input space to steer search to failures. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12364v1); [DeepTest 2024](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2024/deeptest-2024-papers/2/Guiding-the-Search-Towards-Failure-Inducing-Test-Inputs-Using-Support-Vector-Machines)
- **DeepJanus** (Riccio & Tonella), "Model-based Exploration of the Frontier of Behaviours for Deep Learning System Testing" **[peer-reviewed: ESEC/FSE 2020]**: NSGA-II hybridised with novelty search finds *pairs* of similar inputs, one where the system behaves and one where it misbehaves, i.e. points straddling the failure frontier, while maximising diversity along the frontier. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02787); [FSE page](https://2020.esec-fse.org/details/fse-2020-papers/157/Model-Based-Exploration-of-the-Frontier-of-Behaviours-for-Deep-Learning-System-Testin)
- **INDAGO** (Biagiola & Tonella), "Testing of Deep Reinforcement Learning Agents with Surrogate Models" **[peer-reviewed journal, 2024; journal name not verified]**: trains a failure classifier on failure and non-failure environment configurations *from the DRL training logs*, then uses it as a surrogate with hill climbing or a genetic algorithm to generate failure-inducing configurations, which are "significantly more diverse". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12751)
- **Mazouni, Spieker, Gotlieb, Acher, "Testing for Fault Diversity in Reinforcement Learning"** **[workshop: AST @ ICSE 2024]**: applies quality-diversity optimisation to policy testing and finds "more diverse faults" than state-of-the-art policy-testing frameworks. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15065) A TOSEM journal version ("Fault Diversity in Reinforcement Learning Policy Testing", DOI 10.1145/3821533) appears in an index (snippet only). [citedrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/fault-diversity-in-reinforcement-learning-policy-testing/)

**Evolutionary safe optimisation (counts unsafe evaluations)**
- **Kim, Allmendinger, López-Ibáñez, "Are Evolutionary Algorithms Safe Optimizers?"** **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2022]**: formalises safe optimisation problems (SafeOPs) and benchmarks EAs against SafeOpt-style methods, using the number of unsafe evaluations as a metric. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12622)
- **Uchida et al., "CMA-ES for Safe Optimization"** **[peer-reviewed: GECCO 2024]**: zero median unsafe evaluations on benchmark functions (snippet-level summary; the earlier report also covered it). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10534)

### Inferences
- **What is solved.** In low-dimensional parameter spaces with smooth constraints, the boundary can be learned to arbitrary precision without violations w.h.p. (Bottero et al. 2022). In simulation testing, evolution plus a learned classifier finds and maps failure regions efficiently (Ben Abdessalem 2018; DeepJanus 2020; INDAGO 2024; Mazouni 2024).
- **Who holds the closest work.** For "spend a limited real budget where it reveals failure": Parashar et al. (August 2026), with proxy plus target systems, is the nearest modern analogue, and its proxy-plus-target structure maps closely onto LeWM (proxy) plus MuJoCo branches (target). For "evolution to map the boundary": DeepJanus's frontier pairs and NSGA-II-DT.
- **What remains genuinely open.** (1) Safe BO's guarantees need GP smoothness in a low-dimensional input; they do not transfer to choosing *states or branches* in a pixel-trained latent world model. (2) Testing work assumes failures are free and optimises failures found; it never asks how few failures a *safety model* needs. (3) Parashar et al. and RoboGate improve failure discovery for a fixed policy, not the accuracy of a predictor that a controller then trusts. (4) No paper was found that closes the loop: acquire failure branches, retrain the world model or probe, and measure its false-safe rate per failure spent.
- **Where evolution could uniquely help.**
  - *Frontier pairs as labels.* A DeepJanus-style multi-objective search over branch perturbations (joint torque noise, push impulses, action offsets from a snapshot) can find minimal safe/unsafe pairs. Each pair is a maximally informative label for a boundary classifier, and its unsafe half is the only real failure spent.
  - *Population as acquisition batch.* CMA-ES or MAP-Elites in LeWM imagination proposes a batch; only the elites whose predicted margin is near zero, or whose imagined-real disagreement history is worst, are verified in MuJoCo. This is the GECCO surrogate-assisted EA pattern applied to label acquisition rather than to optimisation.
  - *Diversity of failure modes.* QD over failure descriptors (contact type, gait phase, torso angle at failure) directly targets the coverage the 2026 work rewards (Parashar et al.; Mazouni et al.), and tests the earlier report's open question of whether contact diversity beats repeated severe failures.
- **Relevance to Walker2d LeWM.** The student already has every component: a proxy (LeWM plus probes), a target (exact MuJoCo branches), a fixed controller, and a measurable outcome (one-step and horizon false-safe rate). A comparison of acquisition rules at a matched failure budget (random failing branches; probe-margin boundary sampling; LeWM-disagreement sampling; CMA-ES frontier pairs; MAP-Elites failure-mode coverage) would be new. Wang (2026) predicts the disagreement arm will do poorly, which is a falsifiable hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that uses evolution or a population to choose real samples for improving a world model's or learned safety predictor's accuracy; searched with four phrasings across RL, testing and robotics. This is a negative search result, not proof of absence.
- The venue of Bottero et al. 2024 and of INDAGO's journal were not confirmed.
- Whether any safe-BO paper reports *label efficiency for the constraint* (rather than regret) as a headline metric was not checked beyond Bottero et al.

## 4. Synthesising failures without experiencing them

### Takeaway
2025-2026 robot-learning work routinely synthesises failures by perturbing successful trajectories, either in a physics simulator (Guardian, DenseReward), in an action-conditioned generative world model (Dream2Fix), or by generating simulation environments that resemble a single observed real failure (Fail2Progress); offline safe RL now synthesises counterfactual unsafe samples with model rollouts (PROCO). All of these rely on a verifier or a simulator to keep synthetic failures plausible, and none measures whether synthetic failures substitute for real ones in a world model's own safety predictions, which is exactly what the student's branching ground truth can test.

### Cited Findings
- **Dream2Fix**: Li et al., "Learning Actionable Manipulation Recovery via Counterfactual Failure Synthesis", arXiv 2603.13528 (13 March 2026) **[preprint]**. Perturbations are injected at keyframes inside the **Ctrl-World** action-conditioned generative world model (temporal delays for gripper closure; Gaussian noise on end-effector x-y). It produces over **120,000 paired failure-correction samples**; four verifiers (task validity, visual quality, kinematic safety, temporal coherence) **reject 41.9%** of synthesised rollouts. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B raised correction accuracy **from 19.7% to 81.3%**; real-world zero-shot recovery was 46% on its benchmark and 40% during OpenVLA deployment. It does not discuss how many real failures are needed. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13528)
- **Guardian**: Pacaud, Garcia, Chen, Schmid, "Guardian: Detecting Robotic Planning and Execution Errors with Vision-Language Models", arXiv 2512.01946 (v1 1 December 2025; v4 22 August 2026) **[preprint]**. It "perturb[s] successful manipulation trajectories to synthesize failures that reflect realistic failure distributions", building GuardianFail-36k and benchmarks RLBench-Fail, BridgeDataV2-Fail and UR5-Fail. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01946)
- **DenseReward**: Fang, Dong, Liu, Yang, Huo, Mu, Yao, Li, Szafir, Ding, arXiv 2607.13033 (July 2026) **[preprint]**. It decomposes manipulation into five phases and induces failures by perturbation (disabling collision avoidance, offsetting grasp targets, random rotations during transport, Gaussian action noise, collision-then-replan), yielding **27,000 episodes** with per-frame reward and failure labels. [Hugging Face paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13033)
- **Fail2Progress**: Huang, Alvina, Devendran Shanthi, Hermans **[peer-reviewed: CoRL 2025]**, arXiv 2509.01746. It uses **Stein variational inference** (a particle, i.e. population, method) to generate many simulation environments in parallel that resemble an observed real failure, "efficiently generating a dataset targeted to observed failures". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01746)
- **PROCO** (Question 2) synthesises "diverse counterfactual unsafe samples" from a learned dynamics model and an LLM-grounded conservative cost, for offline safe RL with almost no violations in the data. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)
- **SafeDojo** (Question 2) trains its world-model safety head on simulator collision labels, not real violations, then trains the VLA policy entirely in imagination. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20698)
- **Foresight**: Zhang, Lu, Wang, Kang, Kuo, Cheng, Wang, Jenkins, arXiv 2606.23085 (22 June 2026) **[preprint]**. A causal-transformer failure detector reads latents from an action-conditioned world model (frozen visual encoder plus trained predictor), trained "using only final task-level success or failure labels", with functional conformal thresholds; evaluated on LIBERO-Long, ManiSkill-Long, BEHAVIOR-1K and two real arms. The number of failure examples was not stated in the abstract. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23085)
- **FARL** trains its world-model safety critic offline before real exploration, which is a failure-knowledge-first design. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07821v1)
- Snippet-only 2026 titles in the same space, not read: "AEGIS: A Backup Reflex for Physical AI" [arXiv 2606.06660](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06660); "FAR: Failure-Aware Retry for Test-Time Recovery and Continual Policy Improvement" [arXiv 2607.01111](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01111); "FailBench: How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?" [arXiv 2609.03611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03611); "Failing Gracefully: Mitigating Impact of Inevitable Robot Failures" [arXiv 2608.05313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05313).

### Inferences
- **What is solved.** Perturbation-based failure synthesis works well enough to train failure *detectors* and *recovery* models for manipulation (Dream2Fix 19.7% to 81.3%; Guardian; DenseReward). Particle-based generation of failure-like simulator variants from a single real failure exists (Fail2Progress).
- **Who holds the closest work.** Dream2Fix for "imagine failures inside a generative world model"; Fail2Progress for "turn one real failure into many targeted synthetic ones"; PROCO for "counterfactual unsafe samples from model rollouts in safe RL".
- **What remains genuinely open.**
  1. *The blind-spot circularity.* A world model trained mostly on competent behaviour must imagine failures it has rarely seen. Dream2Fix needs four verifiers and still rejects 41.9%; PROCO needs an LLM prior. Nobody measures, against exact ground truth, how faithful imagined failure dynamics are near the boundary of a pixel-trained latent model.
  2. *Substitution rate.* No paper reports how many synthetic failures are worth one real failure for a safety predictor (an exchange rate), or when synthetic failures stop helping.
  3. *Which perturbations to synthesise.* All papers use hand-designed perturbation families (gripper delay, xy noise, rotations). Searching that perturbation space for informative failures is unaddressed.
- **Where evolution could uniquely help.** The perturbation space is a black-box, non-differentiable search space (branch time, impulse direction, torque noise schedule), which is natural for CMA-ES or MAP-Elites. Fail2Progress already shows a population method (SVGD particles) generating targeted failure environments; an EC version would add explicit diversity (QD descriptors over failure modes) and frontier targeting (DeepJanus pairs). In the student's setting, MuJoCo branches are the verifier, so every synthetic failure can be checked rather than filtered heuristically.
- **Relevance to Walker2d LeWM.** The repair result (11% to 68%) used *real* failures. A direct follow-up is a three-way comparison at matched real-failure budget: (a) real failing branches only; (b) LeWM-imagined failures from perturbed latents, labelled by the probe or a decoded health rule; (c) a small number of real failures used to seed evolutionary search for further failures, then verified. If (b) fails and (c) succeeds, the paper shows that the world model cannot teach itself about failure, but a population can make each real failure count for more.
- **Staleness flag.** The earlier report suggested "seeding the snapshot bank with perturbed and failing rollouts is cheap insurance" without citing precedents; Dream2Fix, Guardian, DenseReward and Fail2Progress are those precedents and should be cited.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 paper was found that generates failures inside a *JEPA-style latent* world model (as opposed to a video generator or simulator) and validates them against a simulator's ground truth.
- Fail2Progress's numbers (how many real failures seed how many synthetic environments, and the gain) were not extracted.
- Whether Foresight or FARM report data-efficiency in failure labels was not extracted.
- One search summary claimed that generative augmentation guided towards "danger" can produce physically implausible scenes whose gains do not transfer; the source paper was not identified, so it is not cited as a finding.

## 5. Is there a formal notion of a "failure budget", and results on how few failures are needed?

### Takeaway
Yes, in three partial forms, none of which targets the accuracy of a learned safety model. (1) Constrained BO "under a limited budget of failures" (Marco et al. 2020) is the only literal failure-budget formalisation found: the user fixes how many failures are tolerable and the algorithm spends them to lower regret. (2) CMDP learning theory bounds cumulative violations (Õ(√K) in general; zero or bounded given a known safe policy; matching Õ(√ST) step-wise lower bounds without safe actions). (3) Catastrophe-avoidance theory with a mentor (Plaut et al., ICML 2025) treats queries for help as the scarce resource. "Minimise the real failures needed to reach a target safety-predictor accuracy" was not found formalised anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- **Marco, von Rohr, Baumann, Hernández-Lobato, Trimpe, "Excursion Search for Constrained Bayesian Optimization under a Limited Budget of Failures"**, arXiv 2005.07443 (15 May 2020) **[venue unverified; arXiv says submitted to a conference]**. It proposes "a novel decision maker grounded in control theory that controls the amount of risk we allow in the search as a function of a given budget of failures", motivated by failures as informative feedback ("a tolerable failure in exchange for faster learning"), and reports more efficient budget use and lower regret than prior constrained BO. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07443)
- **Liu et al., "Learning Policies with Zero or Bounded Constraint Violation for Constrained MDPs"** **[peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2021]**: prior results allow Õ(√K) violation with Õ(√K) regret over K episodes; with a known strictly safe policy, zero violation holds with arbitrarily high probability while keeping Õ(√K) regret ("optimistic pessimism in the face of uncertainty"); without a known safe policy, violation can be bounded (summary from search plus proceedings PDF link; author list not re-read). [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/8ec2ba5e96ec1c050bc631abda80f269-Paper.pdf)
- **Xiong, Du, Huang, "Provably Safe Reinforcement Learning with Step-wise Violation Constraints"**, arXiv 2302.06064 (February 2023, revised June 2023) **[venue unverified this session]**: without assuming safe actions exist, SUCBVI guarantees Õ(√ST) step-wise violations and Õ(√(H³SAT)) regret, with lower bounds showing optimality in S and T; a reward-free variant (SRF-UCRL) explores with Õ(√ST) violations. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06064)
- **Plaut, Zhu, Russell, "Avoiding Catastrophe in Online Learning by Asking for Help"** **[peer-reviewed: ICML 2025]**, arXiv 2402.08062 (v1 February 2024; v6 August 2025). The objective is the *product* of per-round catastrophe-avoidance probabilities with limited mentor queries; without a mentor, algorithms "either query constantly or risk near-certain failure"; if the mentor's policy class is learnable, regret and query rate both go to zero. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08062) A follow-up, "Asking for Help Enables Safety Guarantees" (arXiv 2502.14043), argues that avoiding catastrophe also yields sublinear regret in any MDP including irreversible ones (snippet only). [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14043v1)
- **ActSafe Theorem 4.8**: with probability at least 1 - δ, J_c(π_n) ≤ d for all episodes n ≥ 0, i.e. zero constraint violation throughout, under regularity assumptions and with prior knowledge; the authors say "learning safely typically requires some form of prior knowledge about the problem". [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09486v2)
- **Bottero et al. 2022**: no violation w.h.p. while learning the constraint "up to arbitrary precision" (the safe-BO analogue of a zero failure budget). [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/c628644624c1be9c8cfb1541fa6421fd-Abstract.html)
- **Conservative Safety Critics** (Bharadhwaj et al., arXiv 2010.14497) learns a conservative safety estimate and "provably upper bound[s] the likelihood of catastrophic failures at every training iteration" (snippet only; venue believed ICLR 2021, not verified). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14497)
- **ESB-CPO**, "Efficient Exploration Using Extra Safety Budget in Constrained Policy Optimization", arXiv 2302.14339: loosens the constraint early in training with an "extra safety budget" and tightens it later, a scheduled violation allowance (snippet only). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14339v2)
- **"Safe Exploration in RL: Training Backup Control Barrier Functions with Zero Training Time Safety Violations"**, arXiv 2312.07828: zero training-time violations given a safe but trivial-reward initial policy (snippet only). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07828)
- In EC, the number of unsafe evaluations is the standard metric for safe optimisation (Kim et al., GECCO 2022; Safe CMA-ES, GECCO 2024). [arXiv 2203.12622](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12622); [arXiv 2405.10534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10534)
- The earlier report noted the rule of three: with zero violations in n episodes, the 95% upper bound on P(violation) is about 3/n, so certifying below 1% needs about 300 clean episodes. [earlier report](../../reports/Evolutionary%20safe%20RL%20with%20world%20models.md)

### Inferences
- **What is solved.** (a) Zero-failure learning is achievable w.h.p. when you already know something safe (a safe policy, a safe seed set, a conservative prior, GP smoothness). (b) Without that knowledge, Õ(√ST) step-wise violations is achievable in the tabular case and Xiong et al.'s lower bounds match it in S and T, so some failures are unavoidable and the question becomes how to spend them. (c) Marco et al. show that, at least in BO, treating failures as a budget to be spent deliberately lowers regret.
- **Who holds the closest work.** Marco et al. (Trimpe group) for the literal budget; Liu et al. and Xiong et al. for violation-regret theory; Plaut et al. for catastrophe with limited help; Bottero et al. for "learn the constraint precisely without failing".
- **What remains genuinely open.**
  1. **A failure-budgeted objective for safety-model accuracy.** All formalisations optimise reward regret or policy safety. None defines the target as a *safety predictor's* error (for example false-safe rate at margin δ over horizon H) as a function of real failures spent. That is a label-complexity question where the costly label is the positive (failure) class.
  2. **Failure value.** No measure was found of the information each real failure contributes to a safety model, which would let an acquisition rule rank candidate failures before paying for them.
  3. **Accounting for hidden failures.** Zero-violation results move failure knowledge into priors, warm-ups, simulators or LLMs (ActSafe, SOOPER, SafeDojo, PROCO); no paper charges those to a common budget.
  4. **High-dimensional, pixel-latent settings.** The theory is tabular or GP-based; nothing connects it to a JEPA latent model.
- **Where evolution could uniquely help.** EC already counts unsafe evaluations as a first-class metric (SafeOPs, Safe CMA-ES), and surrogate-assisted EA already decides which candidates deserve an expensive true evaluation. A GECCO paper can therefore define a **failure-budgeted surrogate-assisted search**: the population runs in LeWM imagination; each generation may spend at most b real failing branches; selection of which candidates to verify is the experimental factor. Population statistics (rank disagreement between imagined and verified fitness, per-niche failure counts in a QD archive) give acquisition signals that a single-trajectory learner does not have.
- **Relevance to Walker2d LeWM.** Exact snapshot branching gives two things the theory papers lack: a precise count of real failures per acquisition decision, and the ground-truth false-safe rate after each update. A natural headline figure is "probe or predictor false-safe rate versus cumulative real failures", with one curve per acquisition rule, at fixed controller and training budget. The existing 11% to 68% result is a single point (full failure-rich data) on that plot; the open question is the shape of the curve below it.

### Gaps
- Excursion search's publication venue was not found; it may have remained a preprint.
- The exact lower bounds in Liu et al. 2021 and the zero-violation sample-complexity result seen in one search summary (Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) episodes with a Slater constant ζ) were not traced to a primary source, so the latter is not cited as a finding.
- No work on active learning of a *classifier* where querying the positive class is costly or dangerous was checked; this is the closest statistical-learning framing of "few failures for an accurate safety model" and deserves one targeted search before submission.
- Conservative Safety Critics, ESB-CPO and the backup-CBF paper were seen only in snippets.
