# ROSARL, its follow-ups, and learning which states are irreversible

Scope: the ROSARL line of work (reward-only safe RL with the minmax or value-range penalty), every work found that cites or builds on it, the theory of when a penalty is sufficient, and the state of the art (weighted to 2025 to October 2026) in automatically discovering irreversible states, points of no return, or what should count as failure. Written 5 October 2026 for an Honours GECCO 2027 project (LeWM latent world model on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1, linear safety probes, CMA-ES, a ROSARL-style return-range penalty using observed branch-return extrema, MuJoCo snapshot branching as ground truth). All papers were checked on a primary source (arXiv abstract or API record, publisher page, proceedings PDF) unless marked otherwise. Primary PDFs of all three ROSARL versions were downloaded and read in full text.

Local context used: the earlier report `docs/research/reports/Evolutionary safe RL with world models.md` (3 October 2026) and the note `notes/terminationIsNotIrreversibility.md` (20 September 2026), which records that a CEM search on the true simulator recovers from 96% of the states where SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1 fires its "unhealthy" termination, and that settled-fallen Walker2d states are 0.0% irrecoverable at a 500-step (4 s) horizon (Hopper 10%).

## 1. What exactly does ROSARL claim, in which versions, under which assumptions, and with what limitations?

### Takeaway
ROSARL exists in three versions that differ materially: a 2023 arXiv preprint and a 2024 RLC workshop paper carry the theory (a "Minmax penalty" bounded by the environment's diameter D and controllability C, proved to make optimal policies minimise the probability of reaching unsafe absorbing states, and NP-hard to compute exactly), while the archival 2026 RLJ/RLC paper drops all theorems and presents only the practical rule R_unsafe = V_MIN − V_MAX with an empirical "maximal safety" claim. The practical rule silently sets C = 1 (correct only for deterministic outcomes), depends on episode termination at violation, and is markedly over-conservative in the authors' own tables (PointGoal1 continual return 1.87 against 10.17 for TRPO-Lagrangian; zero goal success in the Pillar task at noise of 5 or more).

**Setting and objective**

- v1: undiscounted MDPs modelling stochastic shortest path (SSP) problems with a non-empty set of absorbing goal states G and unsafe absorbing states G! ⊂ G; restricted to deterministic proper policies (those guaranteed to reach an absorbing state), assuming improper policies have values unbounded below. Source: [arXiv 2306.00035, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035).
- v1 Definition 1: a policy is "safe" if it minimises P(s_T ∈ G!) from every state among proper policies. v2 adds Remark 1: equivalently it maximises the probability of reaching safe goals G \ G!, and "looping forever in a non-absorbing region of the state space is neither proper, nor safe, nor optimal". Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035), [RLSW 2024 PDF](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v3 re-frames the objective as "maximally safe RL": "maximise the probability of satisfying the safety condition", which "is exactly almost-sure safety (Sootla et al., 2022)" when probability 1 is attainable and otherwise "the agent should be as safe as the environment permits". It uses Sauté's safety-augmented MDP (state × safety indicator z, discounted with γ) and restricts attention to settings "where the environment terminates immediately when the safe condition is violated (z > 0)". Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF, §2](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).

**The minmax penalty and its bounds (theory only in v1 and v2)**

- v1 defines diameter D = max over states and proper policies of the expected steps to reach G, and controllability C = min over pairs of distinct policies π1 ≠ π2 of max over states of |P^π1(safe) − P^π2(safe)|; C = 1 for deterministic tasks and C tends to 0 as stochasticity grows. The Minmax penalty is R̄_MIN = min{R_MIN, (R_MIN − R_MAX)·D/C} for controllable MDPs (C > 0). Source: [arXiv §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035).
- v1 Theorem 1 (Safety Bounds): (i) if every unsafe-transition reward is below R̄_MIN, the optimal policy is safe for all task rewards R; (ii) if every unsafe reward is above R̄_MIN, the optimal policy is unsafe for all R. The authors call it "a strict upper bound" and say any R̄_MIN − ε guarantees safe optimal policies; the chain-walk example shows diameter alone is "the worst choice" and that controllability "may be the most critical factor". Source: [arXiv §3 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035).
- v2 changes the formalism: the Minmax penalty R_Minmax is now defined as the exact threshold (Definition 2: below it the optimal policy is safe for all R; above it there exists an R making the optimal policy unsafe), and C is redefined as the smallest non-zero probability of reaching safe goals under any proper policy from any internal state. It introduces R̄_MIN := (R_MIN − R_MAX)·D/C and R̄_MAX := R_MIN·D′ (D′ the diameter over safe policies). Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §3](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v2 Theorem 2 (Safety Bounds): R̄_MIN ≤ R_Minmax ≤ R̄_MAX for controllable environments; any penalty below R̄_MIN gives optimal policies that minimise P(unsafe) and maximise P(safe goal); rewards above R̄_MAX "may have optimal policies that do not minimise the probability of reaching unsafe states". Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v2 Theorem 1 (Estimation): a model-based policy-evaluation algorithm over all deterministic policies converges to D and C for any controllable environment. v2 Theorem 3 (Complexity): "Estimating the Minmax penalty R_Minmax accurately is NP-hard", because the diameter requires solving a longest-path problem. Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §3.3 and §4](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v3 contains no theorems, no diameter, no controllability, and no Limitations section. The diameter-style bound is attributed instead to earlier goal-reaching work: "Nangue Tasse et al. (2020; 2025) assign unintended terminal goals a fixed reward satisfying R̄min ≤ min{Rmin, (Rmin − Rmax)D}" in a "different deterministic goal-reaching setting" (Boolean Task Algebra, NeurIPS 2020; lattice composition, JAIR 82, 2025). Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF, §5](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- v3 justifies V_MIN − V_MAX by "the intuition of regret minimization" and a worst-case argument (an unsafe behaviour might offer the highest return while the safest path offers the lowest), and shows with the Lava gridworld that R_unsafe = −V_MAX is insufficient (for R_MAX = 0 or a −1 step reward it can lead into lava at every slip probability). Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF, §3.1](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- Steven James's IJCAI 2026 overview still describes the method as "learning the smallest penalty that provably suffices [Nangue Tasse et al., 2023]", i.e. it cites the v1 theory. Source: [Semantic Scholar citation context](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2306.00035/citations), [IJCAI 2026 PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/0906.pdf).

**How it is estimated in practice**

- All versions use the same online estimator: initialise R_MIN = R_MAX = 0; each step update R_MIN, R_MAX from the observed reward; update V_MIN ← min(V_MIN, R_MIN, V(s_t)) and V_MAX ← max(V_MAX, R_MAX, V(s_t)) using the learned critic; on an unsafe transition replace the reward with the penalty before the RL update. v1 uses R̄_MIN = min(R_MIN, V_MIN − V_MAX); v2 and v3 use V_MIN − V_MAX. Source: [arXiv Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035), [RLSW §4](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf), [RLJ Algorithm 1](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- The bridge from theory to practice is the identity (R_MIN − R_MAX)·D/C = (D·R_MIN − D·R_MAX)/C = (V_MIN − V_MAX)/C, after which C is dropped: v1 says "we need only estimate V_MIN and V_MAX, since C = 1 if safe goals are reachable and the environment is deterministic"; v2 says "Since the controllability C is also expensive to estimate, it is not explicitly considered". Source: [arXiv §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035), [RLSW §4](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v2 states the mechanism that is meant to stand in for C: every time the agent enters an unsafe state, "r_t ← R̄_MIN, R_MIN ← R̄_MIN, and then R̄_MIN ← R̄_MIN − V_MAX. This means that when the estimated V_MAX is greater than zero, the penalty estimate R̄_MIN become more negative every time the agent enters an unsafe state.". Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §4](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- Because V_MAX uses max(V_MAX, R_MAX, V(s_t)), all versions call the estimate "optimistic" and say no ε margin is needed. Source: [arXiv §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035), [RLJ §3.2](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- v2 reports a gap between theory and learned penalty in the deterministic Lava gridworld: the bound is R̄_MIN = −9.9 (C = 1, D = 9) but the agent learns about −1.1 and is still safe; the penalty becomes larger as slip probability rises. Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §5.1](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v3 claims "no new hyperparameters" and plug-in use with "any base RL algorithm" that keeps value estimates. Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF, abstract and §3.2](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).

**What guarantees hold**

- Formal guarantees exist only for the exact penalty in undiscounted SSPs with terminal unsafe states, known (or exactly evaluated) D and C, and optimal policies (v1 Theorem 1; v2 Theorems 1 to 3). v2 explicitly says "While the theoretical Minmax penalty is guaranteed to lead to optimal safe policies, it is unclear whether this also holds for the practical estimate". Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §5](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v2 states that its Safety Gym Pillar domain "does not satisfy the shortest path setting we assume since: it is discounted, optimal policies are not guaranteed to reach G and policies that do not reach G are not guaranteed to have value functions that are unbounded from below (due to the dense rewards)". Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF, §5.2](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- v3 makes only empirical claims (contributions: a parameter-free penalty; "superior robustness to environmental stochasticity"; reward augmentation that can "match or exceed" constrained methods). Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF, contributions page](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).

- The authors frame lower return as intended: "This safety-first behaviour can reduce success rate or return relative to methods that accept greater violation risk" and, for continual PointGoal1, "its longer survival and lower return are not explained by simply remaining stationary" because it has the highest success rate there. ### Inferences
- Cite carefully: the method and its empirical claims should cite RLJ 2026; any sentence about the penalty being "minimal", "provably sufficient", bounded by diameter and controllability, or NP-hard must cite the RLSW 2024 workshop paper (or arXiv 2023), because RLJ 2026 contains none of that theory.
- The practical rule is the theory with C forced to 1, so it is only "sufficient" (in the theorem's sense) when safety outcomes are deterministic under deterministic policies. In stochastic settings the extra pressure comes from the ratchet v2 describes (the penalty drops by about V_MAX at each violation once the critic has learned penalised values), which is a heuristic stand-in for 1/C with no bound on how far it goes. The Pillar collapse at noise of 5 or more (success 0.00, about 970 steps survived) is consistent with the ratchet over-shooting into "never approach the goal".
- v1's controllability (minimum over all pairs of distinct policies of the maximum difference in safety probability) is 0 whenever two distinct deterministic policies differ only in safety-irrelevant actions, which is the usual case in any non-trivial MDP; v1 handles this by hand in the chain-walk example ("only 2 relevant deterministic proper policies"). v2's redefinition (smallest non-zero safe-reach probability) avoids this. This is our reading of the two definitions, not something the authors state.
- In deterministic MuJoCo with deterministic policies, C = 1 and the theoretical bound is (R_MIN − R_MAX)·D with D near the 1000-step time limit, which is far larger in magnitude than any practical V_MIN − V_MAX; the gap between bound and learned penalty that v2 reports in the gridworld (−9.9 against −1.1) will be orders of magnitude larger in Walker2d.
- "No new hyperparameters" hides three design choices: zero initialisation of the range, use of the current (biased) critic V(s_t) for the extrema, and the requirement that the environment terminate at violation. Monotone min and max mean any transient critic overestimate is never forgotten.
- What is minimised in stochastic settings: in v1 and v2, P(reaching an unsafe absorbing state) over proper policies (equivalently P(reaching a safe goal)); in v3, the probability of any violation before termination or truncation, with return as a tie-breaker. Both are lexicographic "safety first, then return" objectives, implemented approximately through a scalar.

- v3's Algorithm 1 orders the R_MIN update before the reward replacement, so whether the v2 ratchet still operates through R_MIN in v3, or only through the critic's learned values, cannot be confirmed from the paper text; the code at `mark-nemecek/rosarl` was not inspected.
- No version reports the learned penalty's numerical value in the continuous-control tasks in a table (only plotted training curves), so the size of V_MIN − V_MAX relative to episode returns cannot be quoted.

## 2. Who cites or builds on ROSARL, and what theory says when a penalty is sufficient?

### Takeaway
ROSARL has a thin citation record: Semantic Scholar lists 8 citing works (2023 to September 2026) and none extends the method; mentions are one-line related-work citations (SafeDreamer, an ICLR 2026 reachability paper, reviews) plus a self-citation in Steven James's IJCAI 2026 overview. No model-based, offline, world-model or evolutionary use of the minmax or value-range penalty was found, so the student's CMA-ES adaptation with snapshot-branch return extrema appears to be the first. The closest theory is older and outside the citation graph: Massiani et al.'s Safe Value Functions (a finite penalty always suffices and larger penalties do not hurt optimality), Thomas et al.'s model-based sufficient penalty with a horizon H, early-terminated MDPs, Sauté RL, exact-penalty results, lexicographic RL, and Deb's feasibility rule as the infinite-penalty limit in evolutionary computation.

**Theory on when a penalty is sufficient**

- Safe Value Functions (Pierre-François Massiani, Steve Heim, Friedrich Solowjow, Sebastian Trimpe; IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 68(5), 2023, pp. 2743 to 2757; peer-reviewed): ties penalties to viability theory; "there always exists a finite penalty that induces" a safe value function, and the penalty "is not unique, but upper-unbounded: larger penalties do not harm optimality"; the key intuition is that "states outside the viability kernel are less attractive than states inside it". Source: [arXiv 2105.12204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12204), [summary of abstract via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.12204).
- Safe RL by Imagining the Near Future (Garrett Thomas, Yuping Luo, Tengyu Ma; NeurIPS 2021): a model-based algorithm "that heavily penalizes unsafe trajectories"; per the RLJ 2026 related work it derives "a sufficient safety-violation penalty using empirical reward extrema and a safety-horizon parameter H, within a model-based approach that predicts unsafe future trajectories". Source: [arXiv 2202.07789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07789), [RLJ 2026 PDF §5](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- Safe Exploration by Solving Early Terminated MDP (Hao Sun, Ziping Xu, Meng Fang, Zhenghao Peng, Jiadong Guo, Bo Dai, Bolei Zhou; arXiv preprint, July 2021): per RLJ 2026, augments state with safety-budget information, uses an absorbing terminal reward, and shows "a sufficiently adverse terminal reward preserves the corresponding safe optimal policies". Source: [arXiv 2107.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04200), [RLJ 2026 PDF §5](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- Sauté RL (Aivar Sootla et al.; ICML 2022): state augmentation with the remaining safety budget; ROSARL v3 notes that Sauté corresponds to R_unsafe = −∞. Source: [arXiv 2202.06558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06558), [RLJ 2026 PDF §2](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- Penalized Proximal Policy Optimization, P3O (IJCAI 2022): uses an exact penalty function and demonstrates "the exactness of penalty-based methods with a finite penalty factor". Source: [IJCAI 2022 PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0520.pdf).
- CaT: Constraints as Terminations for Legged Locomotion RL (Elliot Chane-Sane, Pierre-Alexandre Leziart, Thomas Flayols, Olivier Stasse, Philippe Souères, Nicolas Mansard; arXiv March 2024, recorded on HAL as a 2024 conference paper): "any violation of a constraint triggers a probability of terminating potential future rewards", with minimal changes to PPO, validated on the Solo quadruped. Source: [arXiv 2403.18765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18765), [HAL](https://hal.in2p3.fr/LAAS-GEPETTO/hal-04523167v1).
- Reinforcement Learning with a Terminator (Guy Tennenholtz, Nadav Merlis, Lior Shani, Shie Mannor, Uri Shalit, Gal Chechik, Assaf Hallak, Gal Dalal; NeurIPS 2022): episodes "may be interrupted by an external non-Markovian observer"; the agent learns the parameters of the termination process with state-wise confidence bounds and uses a dynamic discount incorporating the termination probability. Source: [arXiv 2205.15376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15376).
- Lexicographic Multi-Objective RL (Joar Skalse, Lewis Hammond, Charlie Griffin, Alessandro Abate; IJCAI 2022): action-value and policy-gradient algorithms that converge to lexicographically optimal policies, applied to imposing safety constraints. Source: [IJCAI 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/476).
- Scalar reward is not enough (Peter Vamplew et al.; Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems, 2022): argues scalar reward is insufficient for some aspects of intelligence and for "human-aligned artificial general intelligence due to unacceptable risks of unsafe or unethical behaviour". Source: [arXiv 2112.15422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15422). ROSARL v1 and v3 instead invoke the reward hypothesis and ask "can a single scalar reward signal be sufficient to learn policies that behave safely?". Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF §1 and §6](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- A 2025 technical survey of safe RL and CMDPs states that for finite CMDPs an optimal stationary policy exists and, with multiple constraints, may need to be randomised (secondary source). Source: [ResearchGate survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392085280_A_Survey_of_Safe_Reinforcement_Learning_and_Constrained_MDPs_A_Technical_Survey_on_Single-Agent_and_Multi-Agent_Safety).

**Deb's feasibility rules and stochastic ranking as limiting cases**

- Deb (2000) ranks any feasible solution above any infeasible one, feasible solutions by objective, and infeasible ones by constraint violation, with no penalty parameter. Source: [Deb 2000, CMAME](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7825(99)00389-8).
- Runarsson and Yao's stochastic ranking "was designed to deal with the inherent shortcomings of a penalty function", framing penalties as under- or over-penalisation; in over-dominance "all feasible solutions are preferred over infeasible solutions"; as P_f → 0 the ranking becomes dominated by feasibility. Source: [Runarsson and Yao 2000, IEEE TEC](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.873238), [secondary description](https://neura.market/ai-and-ml-research-papers/stochastic-ranking-for-constrained-evolutionary-optimization).
- The earlier report proposed that under rank-based selection with single-episode evaluation and termination on violation, ROSARL's penalty reduces to Deb's first rule, differing in ordering violators by pre-violation return and in multi-rollout aggregation. Source: local file `docs/research/reports/Evolutionary safe RL with world models.md`, lines 99 to 101.

### Inferences
- Targeted web searches for "minmax penalty", "value range penalty", and "Nangue Tasse" combined with world model, model-based or offline returned only the ROSARL versions themselves and unrelated penalty papers (an absence, so no source can be cited).
- Nobody has built on ROSARL technically. The student's adaptation (CMA-ES, branch-return extrema, imagined returns) is therefore new ground on two axes at once: first evolutionary use and first model-based use. The nearest model-based relative is Thomas et al. (2021), which predates ROSARL and uses a horizon H rather than a value range.
- Massiani et al. and ROSARL agree that some finite penalty suffices, but disagree on whether larger penalties cost anything: Massiani et al. say larger penalties "do not harm optimality", ROSARL's argument for the minimum is about learning speed and variance (Figure 3d). Under rank-based selection (CMA-ES is invariant to order-preserving fitness transformations), that learning-dynamics argument disappears: any penalty that preserves the same ranking produces the same search. In EC, the size of the penalty therefore matters only through (a) whether it achieves dominance and (b) how it trades probability against return when fitness is averaged over stochastic rollouts.
- Refinement of the Deb bridge (to replace the earlier report's unqualified version). With undiscounted episodic fitness F = G_pre + R_unsafe and R_unsafe = V_MIN − V_MAX, a violator scores at most V_MAX + V_MIN − V_MAX = V_MIN, which is no more than any safe score, so dominance holds, but only if V_MIN and V_MAX truly bound all realised returns (including partial pre-violation returns). It fails (i) under discounting, because a violation at step T contributes γ^T·R_unsafe and a late violator can approach V_MAX; (ii) when the extrema are under-estimated, as with observed branch-return extrema early in a run or in an imperfect model. Ties at exactly V_MIN are possible.
- The probability-aggregated case recovers ROSARL's controllability term. With n rollouts and mean fitness F = (1 − p)·E[R | safe] + p·(E[G_pre | violation] + R_unsafe), a safer candidate is guaranteed to outrank a riskier one only if |R_unsafe| exceeds (V_MAX − V_MIN)/Δp, where Δp is the smallest difference in violation probability to be resolved; with empirical p̂ that is at least 1/n. This is exactly the D/C structure of the v1 and v2 bound (range divided by the smallest probability difference). Practical ROSARL (C = 1) is therefore lexicographic only for deterministic outcomes, and for stochastic ones it implements an implicit exchange rate between probability and return. Writing R_unsafe = −(V_MAX − V_MIN)/δ makes that exchange rate an explicit, interpretable dial δ ("a δ increase in violation probability is worth the whole return range").
- Lexicographic RL and Deb's rule are the clean, exact versions of "safety first, then return". ROSARL's contribution, in that light, is a way to obtain lexicographic behaviour from a single scalar without knowing the scales; EC does not need that trick for ranking, but a hybrid ERL (gradient learner plus population) or an imagination-trained critic still does.

## 3. What is the state of the art in learning which states are irreversible or should count as failure, and does anyone combine it with ROSARL?

### Takeaway
Irreversibility can now be estimated without hand labels in several ways (self-supervised temporal precedence, reset-policy values, behaviour encodings, imagination rollouts, budgeted mentor queries), and latent Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters learn failure-avoiding value functions from pixels; but in every latent HJ method found, the failure set itself still comes from labels or from a proxy (epistemic uncertainty), and none asks whether the labelled failures are actually irreversible. No 2025 to 2026 paper was found that measures benchmark terminations against ground-truth recoverability, as the student's Walker2d result does, and no paper combines learned irreversibility with ROSARL's absorbing-state penalty. The strongest September 2026 entries (Cao and Bansal on world-model errors in latent HJ filters, REVERSAL-BENCH's reset oracle, RECON's imagination-based recoverability) sit right beside the student's question without answering it.

### Cited Findings

**Reversibility and points of no return (RL)**

- Leave No Trace (Benjamin Eysenbach, Shixiang Gu, Julian Ibarz, Sergey Levine; arXiv November 2017, published at ICLR 2018 per common record, venue not re-verified here): learns a forward and a reset policy; "By learning a value function for the reset policy, we can automatically determine when the forward policy is about to enter a non-reversible state, providing for uncertainty-aware safety aborts". Source: [arXiv 1711.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06782).
- There Is No Turning Back (Nathan Grinsztajn, Johan Ferret, Olivier Pietquin, Philippe Preux, Matthieu Geist; NeurIPS 2021): "approximate reversibility can be learned through a simple surrogate task: ranking randomly sampled trajectory events in chronological order", fully self-supervised "without any priors"; RAE adds a penalty for transitions below a reversibility threshold, RAC rejects sampled actions below threshold β; in synthetic tasks policies "never fail ... even without access to the reward function". The precedence-to-reversibility link holds for a fixed policy, and the policy-independent guarantee needs a policy that is "stochastic enough" (every action probability above some ρ > 0). Source: [NeurIPS 2021 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/0e98aeeb54acf612b9eb4e48a269814c-Paper.pdf).
- When to Ask for Help: Proactive Interventions in Autonomous RL, PAINT (Annie Xie, Fahim Tajwar, Archit Sharma, Chelsea Finn; NeurIPS 2022): learns to detect and avoid irreversible states and asks for help on entering them. It queries binary reversibility labels and exploits that "all states following an irreversible state will be irreversible and all states preceding a reversible state will be reversible", using binary search along each trajectory so that "Identifying s_{k+1}, the first irreversible state, generates the labels for the entire trajectory"; label cost is O(N log |τ|max) against O(N |τ|max) for prior methods. Source: [arXiv 2210.10765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10765), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10765).
- Robust and Scalable Autonomous RL in Irreversible Environments, RSA (Sang-Hyun Lee; NeurIPS 2025): notes prior autonomous RL "rely on task-specific knowledge to identify irreversible states"; RSA avoids irreversible states "without task-specific knowledge" by encoding "the behaviors exhibited in those states rather than the states themselves", with a learning-progress curriculum. Source: [NeurIPS 2025 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117025), [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dAAz7afWJR).
- Safe Learning Under Irreversible Dynamics via Asking for Help (Benjamin Plaut, Juan Liévano-Karim, Hanlin Zhu, Stuart Russell; JMLR vol. 27, 2026, peer-reviewed): regret and mentor queries both sublinear in the horizon for MDPs with irreversible dynamics and infinite state spaces, using mentor help plus transfer between similar states; "may be the first formal proof that it is possible for an agent to obtain high reward while becoming self-sufficient in an unknown, unbounded, and high-stakes environment without resets". Source: [JMLR](http://jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2248.html).
- Learning to Undo: Rollback-Augmented RL with Reversibility Signals (Andrejs Sorstkins, Omer Tariq, Muhammad Bilal; arXiv October 2025, "Submitted to PLOS ONE"; preprint): an online per-state-action estimator Φ of "the likelihood of returning to a prior state within a fixed horizon K" adjusts TD penalties and triggers rollback; toy domains (CliffWalking, Taxi). Source: [arXiv 2510.14503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14503).
- REVERSAL-BENCH (Riyaaz Shaik, Chandru Venkataraman; arXiv 15 September 2026; preprint, 8 pp.): a continuous reversibility parameter ρ ∈ [0, 1] and "a reset oracle, a ground-truth verification mechanism to test state recoverability across eight manipulation settings in five physics engines"; as irreversibility rises, "reset-free agents are consistently absorbed into irrecoverable states" while episodic agents are not. Source: [arXiv 2609.17745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17745).
- Beyond Conservatism: Recoverability-Conditioned Exploration for Model-Based Imitation Learning, RECON (Xuanlin Chen, Ziyue Wang, Xunlan Zhou, Yuan-yih Shang, Qiang Wu, Shenghua Wan; arXiv 27 September 2026; preprint): explorer guided by "epistemic uncertainty conditioned on recoverability estimated from multi-step main-policy imagination" in a Dreamer-style world model; recoverability is used to choose where to collect data, not as a failure or termination signal. Source: [arXiv 2609.33336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33336).
- Solving Parameter-Robust Avoid Problems with Unknown Feasibility, FGE (So et al.; ICLR 2026): since "whether this problem has a solution depends on the feasibility of the specified set, which is unknown a priori", FGE "simultaneously identifies a subset of feasible initial conditions under which a safe policy exists" and learns the policy; over 50% more coverage on MuJoCo and pixel-based Kinetix tasks. Source: [arXiv 2602.15817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15817).
- Massiani et al. define safety through the viability kernel ("the largest safe set") and show penalties can make optimal control stabilise it. Source: [arXiv 2105.12204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12204).

**Latent Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters (where the failure set comes from)**

- Generalizing Safety Beyond Collision-Avoidance via Latent-Space Reachability Analysis, "Latent Safety Filters"/LatentSafe (Kensuke Nakamura, Lasse Peters, Andrea Bajcsy; RSS 2025, peer-reviewed): HJ reachability in the latent space of a generative world model from raw RGB, without recovery demonstrations; the failure set is a learned classifier ("This transforms nuanced constraint specification to a classification problem in latent space"); world models are RSSM in simulation and DINO-WM on hardware; the authors state the method "does not ensure safety in all cases" because of world-model inaccuracy and partial observability. Source: [arXiv 2502.00935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935), [project page](https://kensukenk.github.io/latent-safety/).
- Uncertainty-aware Latent Safety Filters for Avoiding OOD Failures, UNISafe (Junwon Seo, Kensuke Nakamura, Andrea Bajcsy; CoRL 2025): uses "the world model's epistemic uncertainty as a proxy for identifying unseen potential hazards", calibrates the uncertainty threshold by conformal prediction, and runs reachability in an augmented latent-plus-uncertainty space. Source: [arXiv 2505.00779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779), [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10665479).
- AnySafe: Adapting Latent Safety Filters at Runtime via Safety Constraint Parameterization in the Latent Space (Sankalp Agrawal, Junwon Seo, Kensuke Nakamura, Ran Tian, Andrea Bajcsy; arXiv September 2025; preprint). Source: [arXiv 2509.19555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19555).
- Designing Latent Safety Filters using Pre-Trained Vision Models (Ihab Tabbara, Yuxuan Yang, Ahmad Hamzeh, Maxwell Astafyev, Hussein Sibai; arXiv September 2025; preprint). Source: [arXiv 2509.14758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14758).
- Safe Control using Learned Safety Filters and Adaptive Conformal Inference (Sacha Huriot, Ihab Tabbara, Hussein Sibai; L4DC 2026 per arXiv comment). Source: [arXiv 2604.18482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18482).
- When World Models Lie: Adaptive Safety Analysis Under Wrong Imaginations (John Cao, Somil Bansal; arXiv 28 September 2026; preprint): latent HJ value functions "can inherit [world-model] errors and produce overconfident safety estimates"; ensemble disagreement and value-consistency residuals "can remain small even when the world model's predictions deviate from observations"; the method builds Adaptive Conformal Inference sets from "discrepancies between predicted and observation-inferred latent states" and minimises the learned value over them, with a finite-time coverage guarantee. Source: [arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300).

None derives "failure" from an optimised recovery search with a stated budget, which is what the student's CEM oracle does.
- Nobody combines learned irreversibility with ROSARL. This is the most direct open slot: ROSARL needs a set of absorbing unsafe states and "terminates immediately when the safe condition is violated"; every evaluation so far hand-labels that set (hazard contact, velocity threshold) and forces termination.
- The Walker2d finding cuts both ways for ROSARL. With the benchmark's termination as G!, ROSARL "provably" minimises the probability of reaching a set that is 96% recoverable, i.e. it is optimally safe with respect to the wrong set, which is one concrete source of its over-conservatism. With true irreversibility as G!, Walker2d has essentially no absorbing failures at H = 500, so ROSARL would degenerate to plain RL. Irreversibility therefore has to be horizon-indexed ("cannot recover within H steps under budget B") to be usable, and that horizon is itself a natural dial.
- PAINT's binary search along a trajectory is the label-efficient template for the student's oracle: snapshot branching plus CEM recovery at O(log T) points per trajectory finds the point of no return with far fewer expensive oracle calls than scoring every state, which directly serves "visit the dangerous part as little as possible". PAINT's monotonicity assumption (once irreversible, always irreversible) holds by definition for a fixed horizon only if recoverability is monotone along the trajectory, which the student can test empirically.
- Cao and Bansal (September 2026) is the closest current work on the student's "imperfect model" concern: they show latent HJ values inherit world-model optimism and that standard uncertainty signals miss it. It uses observed latent error, which requires real observations; the student's snapshot branching offers a stronger, exact ground truth for the same question in simulation.
- FARM shows that a frozen JEPA-style world model's internal states already linearly or near-linearly encode failure, consistent with the student's linear safety probes on LeWM; but FARM still needs failure labels, so it inherits whatever the labels get wrong.

## 4. What remains open, and where could world models or evolutionary methods uniquely help (with relevance to the Walker2d LeWM setup)?

### Takeaway
Six sub-problems are open, and in each the evolutionary angle is concrete rather than decorative: (1) what counts as absorbing failure, (2) estimating the value range from an imperfect model, (3) stochasticity and the controllability term that practical ROSARL drops, (4) over-conservatism, (5) the missing risk dial, and (6) query-efficient discovery of the point of no return. The strongest GECCO-shaped contribution is to treat irreversibility as the outcome of a budgeted evolutionary recovery search, map it with quality diversity or binary search under a snapshot oracle, feed that learned absorbing set into a ROSARL-style penalty, and expose the 1/Δp exchange rate as an explicit dial, all measured against exact MuJoCo ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- Label dependence is acknowledged by the method's design: v3 restricts itself to settings where the environment terminates at violation and modifies every benchmark to do so. Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf). Latent HJ filters still define failure via a classifier. Source: [LatentSafe project page](https://kensukenk.github.io/latent-safety/). Source: or via uncertainty as a proxy. Source: [UNISafe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00779).
- Value-range estimation depends on the critic: V_MIN and V_MAX are updated with V(s_t), and V_MAX is "estimated optimistically". Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF §3.2](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf). World-model-derived values can be "overconfident" and standard uncertainty signals can miss model error. Source: [Cao and Bansal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300).
- Exact computation is NP-hard and the practical estimate drops controllability. Source: [RLSW 2024 PDF](https://www.raillab.org/publication/nangue-2024-rosarl/nangue-2024-rosarl.pdf).
- Over-conservatism and a requested dial: v1 proposed weights on the penalty for "degrees of safety". Source: [arXiv §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00035); v3 shows near-zero success at high noise and large return losses. Source: [RLJ 2026 PDF Tables 2, 3, 7](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2026/papers/Paper96.pdf).
- Label-efficient discovery: binary search for the first irreversible state gives O(N log |τ|) labels. Source: [PAINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10765); sublinear mentor queries are possible in theory. Source: [Plaut et al. JMLR 2026](http://jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2248.html).
- GECCO 2026 workshops listed "Surrogate-assisted identification of the feasible region" and "Safety assurance" topics. Source: as cited in the earlier report (local file, line 170) from the [GECCO 2026 workshop page](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Workshop?itemId=8255); not re-verified here.

### Inferences

**(1) What counts as the absorbing failure set**
- Solved: self-supervised or low-label irreversibility estimators exist for discrete or manipulation settings (Grinsztajn 2021, PAINT 2022, RSA 2025, Plaut 2026); latent HJ filters work from pixels given a failure classifier (LatentSafe 2025, UNISafe 2025).
- Closest work: PAINT (label-efficient boundary search), REVERSAL-BENCH (ground-truth reset oracle, September 2026), FGE (identifying feasible initial conditions, ICLR 2026), Cao and Bansal (model-error-aware latent safety, September 2026).
- Open: no one measures benchmark failure flags against an optimised recovery oracle, defines failure as "irrecoverable within horizon H under search budget B", or plugs a learned absorbing set into ROSARL.
- Evolutionary angle: the recovery oracle is already an evolutionary search (CEM is an estimation-of-distribution algorithm), so "irreversible" can be defined operationally as "a CMA-ES or CEM recovery search with budget B fails within H". A MAP-Elites archive over a state descriptor (torso height, pitch, angular velocity) can map recoverability across the state space, i.e. an empirical viability kernel; surrogate-assisted EC can cut oracle calls. For Walker2d this yields the headline that the benchmark's failure set and the learned one barely overlap, and the horizon H becomes a principled replacement for the hand-set 0.8 m and 1 rad constants.

**(2) Value-range estimation error, especially from an imperfect model**
- Solved: nothing specific to ROSARL; generic conformal and adaptive conformal tools exist for latent safety values (UNISafe, Huriot et al. L4DC 2026, Cao and Bansal 2026).
- Open: how errors in V_MIN and V_MAX propagate into the penalty, and from there into safety. Extrema are the statistics most sensitive to model error and selection: the maximum of many imagined branch returns is biased upwards (winner's curse), inflating |R_unsafe| and making the policy more conservative, while an under-estimated range breaks dominance and lets violators through. Monotone min and max never forget an outlier.
- Evolutionary angle: CMA-ES inside LeWM is exactly the process that finds the imagined extremes. Re-evaluating only the population's extreme returns on true MuJoCo snapshots (a small, paired budget) and comparing imagined against real ranges measures this bias directly. Robust statistics (high quantiles instead of maxima), re-evaluation, and noise-aware ranking are standard EC tools that map onto this problem.

**(3) Stochasticity and the dropped controllability term**
- Solved: in theory (v1 and v2), via D/C; in practice only by the ratchet heuristic.
- Open: an estimable stand-in for C. The derivation in Section 2 shows that with n rollouts, the EC analogue of 1/C is 1/Δp, with Δp at least 1/n for empirical violation rates.
- Evolutionary angle: the population naturally supplies many candidates with estimated violation probabilities; the smallest probability gap the selection must respect can be set from the rollout count, giving a computable C for EC. Walker2d itself is deterministic, so stochasticity would have to be added (action noise, initial-state noise), or the imagined model's sampling noise becomes the stochasticity.

**(4) Over-conservatism**
- Solved: not addressed by the authors beyond v1's suggested weights.
- Open: separating over-conservatism caused by a mislabelled absorbing set (Walker2d: 96% of "failures" recoverable) from over-conservatism caused by an over-sized penalty (ratchet, optimistic V_MAX).
- Evolutionary angle: a factorial study is cheap in EC: absorbing set {benchmark flag, learned irreversibility at H} × penalty {Deb rule, ROSARL range, range/δ} × evaluation {imagined, real snapshot}. Rank-based selection removes the learning-dynamics confound that ROSARL's own Figure 3d is about, so any remaining conservatism is attributable to the label or the aggregation.

**(5) The missing risk dial**
- Solved: lexicographic RL and chance-constrained formulations exist; ROSARL deliberately has no parameter.
- Open: an interpretable dial that keeps ROSARL's scale-free property.
- Evolutionary angle: two candidate dials fall out naturally: δ in R_unsafe = −(V_MAX − V_MIN)/δ (the exchange rate between violation probability and return), and the irreversibility horizon H (how long the agent is allowed to take to recover before a state counts as failure). A MAP-Elites or multi-objective archive over (p̂ violation, return) gives the whole dial curve in one run, which a gradient learner cannot.

**(6) Learning about danger while visiting it as little as possible**
- Solved: PAINT's logarithmic labelling; Plaut et al.'s sublinear mentor queries (theory).
- Open: the same economy with an expensive simulator oracle and a learned world model, in continuous control.
- Evolutionary angle: binary search for the point of no return along evolved trajectories, with CEM recovery checks at O(log T) snapshots, and an active-learning loop in which the LeWM probe proposes candidate boundary states and the snapshot oracle verifies only those. The quantity to report is oracle calls per correctly located boundary.

**Relevance to the Walker2d LeWM set-up, summarised**
- Using ROSARL with the benchmark flag as G! on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1 would produce a policy that is optimally cautious about states it could recover from; the student's data quantify how wrong that set is, which is a stronger and more novel result than any penalty tweak.
- The velocity cost in Safety-Gymnasium does not terminate by default (ROSARL v3 had to modify HumanoidVelocity to terminate), so a ROSARL arm on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1 must either terminate on the velocity violation or adopt a horizon-indexed irreversibility label; the paper should state which.
- The student's "observed branch-return extrema" are realised-return extremes, not critic-value extremes, so the range will be wider than ROSARL's in stochastic settings and biased by the number of branches and by imagination error; both effects are measurable with paired snapshot re-evaluation.

### Gaps
- None of the evolutionary contributions above has been tested; they are proposals derived from the literature and the student's prior finding.
- Whether a GECCO 2027 workshop again lists surrogate-assisted feasible-region identification or safety assurance is unknown until the 2027 calls appear.
- The ratchet's behaviour under rank-based EC (where no critic exists unless one is added) is unknown; with pure episodic fitness the penalty would be driven only by the observed return extrema, so the RLSW mechanism may not occur at all.
