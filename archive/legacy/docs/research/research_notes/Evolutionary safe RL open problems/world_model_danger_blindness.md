# World-model blindness to rare danger, and uncertainty as a detector of safety-relevant errors

Scope note. Research date: 5 October 2026. All papers below were opened on a primary source (arXiv abstract or HTML page, conference proceedings) in this session unless marked "[from 3 Oct notes]" (verified in the earlier session, not re-opened) or "[unverified]". "Preprint" means no peer-reviewed venue is shown on the arXiv page. The setting throughout is the student's: a LeWM-style JEPA trained from pixels on SafetyWalker2dVelocity-v1, mostly on competent walking; linear probes for torso height, pitch and speed; CMA-ES; a ROSARL-style penalty; and exact MuJoCo snapshot branching as ground truth, with a measured false-safe rate of about 45% (0.8 s horizon, zero margin; from the 3 October report).

## Q1. Is there evidence that even strong world models are specifically wrong in rare, safety-critical or failure states?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the 2025 to 2026 evidence now points to a specific mechanism rather than generic inaccuracy: world models trained mostly on successful or nominal data develop an **optimism or attractor bias**, in which imagined rollouts from off-nominal states drift back towards well-represented, high-performing behaviour, so failures are "imagined away". This appears across Dreamer (RSSM), TD-MPC2, LeWM and other JEPAs, and multi-billion-parameter video world models (Cosmos, DreamDojo), and **scale does not fix it**: in one benchmark a 14B model preserved failures far less often than its 2B sibling (12.8 vs 48.7). The answer to "what if the model is really good, like SafeDreamer?" is that SafeDreamer's own ablations say safety depends on how accurately the model predicts safety-related states, and that SafeDreamer learns online from its own violations, which an offline model trained on competent walking never sees.

### Cited Findings

**Dreamer / RSSM family**
- "Biased Dreams: Limitations to Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification in Latent Dynamics Models" (Berger, Frauenknecht, Trimpe, Leibe; **RLC 2026** per arXiv comment; v1 28 Apr 2026, v2 27 Jul 2026). On four DMC tasks (Cartpole Swingup, Cheetah Run, Hopper Hop, **Walker Run**), 5 runs each, RSSM and Cat-RSSM trained 1M steps, they find an **attractor phenomenon**: "RSSM rollout converges to familiar latent regions, where uncertainty diminishes despite elevated proprioceptive discrepancy." From out-of-distribution initial states, "latent rollouts are biased toward well-supported regions that appear to coincide with high-performing behaviors in dense-reward tasks", which can cause overestimated returns. The authors call it a "structural limitation" of RSSMs rather than a training defect ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25416); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25416)).
- "Imagined Rollouts are Kinematic, Not Dynamic: A Diagnosis of Long-Horizon World-Model Failure" (Schäfer, Moller, Gao, Oefinger, Schmidt, Betz; **workshop paper, RSS 2026 Robot World Model Workshop**; 7 Jul 2026). On a released DreamerV3 walker checkpoint, the imagined kinematic-consistency error is about "two orders of magnitude above that of matched real-physics rollouts", and when friction crosses the gait-collapse boundary the imagined error "remains statistically constant even as policy rewards collapse" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05966)).
- "The Intervention Gap in Latent World Models" (Vakalis; preprint; 30 Aug 2026). Across released **TD-MPC2** checkpoint sizes, "episode return falls as an operator-error diagnostic on task observables grows, while reward-prediction error stays small and nearly flat". In DreamerV3, "the posterior distribution, not its sample, carries the current query" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)).

**JEPA family (LeWM, DINO-WM, V-JEPA)**
- Same intervention-gap paper: "On Cheetah, three **LeWorldModel** checkpoints capture the current task query and support decodable real intervention effects; however, their imagined five-step effects are **worse than predicting no effect** and worse than an environment-endpoint oracle. The failure is task-direction rotation with excess gain, not feature collapse." Five PreJEPA seeds show an oracle-relative deficit without the severe pattern ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)). This is the closest published result to the student's model family and a locomotion task.
- "QQWorld: Quantile-Quantile Matching for World Model Regularization" (Yu, Hu, Xu; preprint; 30 Jul 2026). LeWM's Epps-Pulley SIGReg objective has corrective gradients that "rapidly vanish for isolated tail samples, leaving heavy-tailed deviations insufficiently controlled"; QQ matching gives "thinner latent tails" and higher planning success across four environments ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28415)). The search-engine excerpt adds that extreme latents "can push the learned dynamics into poorly represented regions" (excerpt only, not in the abstract).
- "Where World Models Break: Natural-Input Failure Discovery" (Shi, Liang, Feng, Tang, Chen, Li; preprint; 23 Aug 2026). BasinLens found "reproducible and locally persistent failure modes that conventional evaluations fail to reveal" in **LeWM (PushT, TwoRooms), DINO-WM (PushT, Wall), JEPA-WM (PointMaze), DIAMOND and IRIS (Atari)**, at budgets of 16, 32 and 64 queries. Average-error benchmarks "fail to stress-test the model against catastrophic collapses under rare or unobserved condition-action combinations" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22421); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22421)).
- "ARC-Bench: Closed-Loop Replanning Masks Broken Action Ranking in Frozen JEPA World Models" (Zhang, Li; preprint; arXiv lists v1 as 12 Aug 2026, inconsistent with a 2609 identifier). On official JEPA-WM checkpoints "the top-scored candidate is almost always suboptimal"; the defect persists with V-JEPA 1 and **V-JEPA 2** encoders at ViT-L/ViT-G scale; "closed-loop success rates therefore systematically overstate the rankability of frozen latent representations" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)).
- "IntPhys 2" (Bordes, Garrido, Kao, Williams, Rabbat, Dupoux; Meta; preprint; 11 Jun 2025): on a violation-of-expectation benchmark for permanence, immutability, continuity and solidity, "most models perform at chance levels (50%)" while humans achieve "near-perfect accuracy" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09849)). A search excerpt attributed 50% to SAYCam-trained V-JEPA and 52% to VideoMix22M-trained V-JEPA, and a 50 to 54% range for all models; these specific numbers were **not** confirmed on the abstract page [unverified].
- LeWM itself reports that it "consistently assigns higher surprise to frames containing physical violations", significant for teleportation in all environments (paired t-test, p<0.01), with weaker, non-significant responses to colour changes; it states that it "relies on offline datasets with sufficient coverage" ([LeWM v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3)).

**Video and foundation world models (Cosmos, DreamDojo, Wan, Veo, Genie)**
- "MiraBench: Evaluating Action-Conditioned Reliability in Robotic World Models" (Yang, Shen, Mi, Zhang, Zhou, Ji, Dai, Chen, Chen, Yang; PKU-Alignment group, the Safety-Gymnasium/SafeDreamer lab; preprint; 28 May 2026). Its Level 3, **Optimism Bias Detection**, perturbs successful actions with six failure-inducing types (insufficient grip force, premature release, carry slip, contact oscillation, wrist tilt, approach overshoot) and scores whether the model still renders the failure. Failure-preservation scores: DreamDojo-2B 48.7, **DreamDojo-14B 12.8**, DreamDojo-GR1-2B 23.1, DreamDojo-GR1-14B 12.8, Cosmos-GR1-2B 17.5, Cosmos-GR1-14B 15.4; across all 12 configurations 12.8 to 87.2 (WAN 2.1 highest). Findings quoted: "optimism bias is pervasive across current systems"; "Success-only post-training improves the wrong capability" (task completion 14.0 to 92.0 while failure preservation falls 48.7 to 23.1); "Scaling and extra fine-tuning are not reliable fixes... failure preservation drops from 48.7 to 12.8" from 2B to 14B; a visually strong model ("Happy Horse") still shows a 62.1% bias rate in the pilot ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)).
- "RoboTrustBench" (Li, Wang, Mei, Majumdar, Chen, Zhu; preprint; v2 31 Aug 2026), seven video world models (HunyuanVideo-1.5, Wan2.2, Cosmos-2B, Cosmos-14B, LingBot-World, Veo-3.1-Fast, Kling-v2.6). Scene entity alignment around 0.7 but interaction rationality around 0.5 to 0.6, so "reliable contact modeling remains a major bottleneck"; Veo-3.1-Fast "still does not reliably suppress unsafe generations"; conclusion: "visual coherence and surface-level instruction following are insufficient" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01600)).
- "Ctrl-World" (Guo, Shi, Chen, Finn; **ICLR 2026**). Trained on DROID (about 76k successful and 19k failed trajectories), it ranks policies well, but shows "gaps in... precise modeling of complex physics dynamics such as collisions, objects sliding away, rotations"; "there are still many failure modes outside the data distribution"; and it "tends to underestimate the execution success rate" ([ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- "How Far Is Video Generation from World Model: A Physical Law Perspective" (Kang et al.; **ICML 2025**, PMLR 267). In a 2D collision testbed, scaling gives perfect in-distribution generalisation and measurable combinatorial scaling, but **failure out of distribution**; models show "case-based" generalisation, mimicking the nearest training example; "scaling alone is insufficient" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02385v2); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kang25g.html)).
- "Do generative video models understand physical principles?" (Physics-IQ; Motamed et al.; **WACV 2026**): physical understanding of Sora, Runway, Pika, Lumiere, SVD and VideoPoet is "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" across 396 real videos ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Motamed_Do_Generative_Video_Models_Understand_Physical_Principles_WACV_2026_paper.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09038)).
- Counterpoint: "Evaluating Gemini Robotics Policies in a Veo World Simulator" (Gemini Robotics Team et al.; preprint; v1 11 Dec 2025, v2 6 Jan 2026) shows a fine-tuned Veo can be used for nominal, OOD and safety evaluation, validated against 1600+ real evaluations of eight checkpoints, and red-teaming in edited scenes surfaced unsafe behaviours such as gripper contact with a human hand ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10675)). Precision and recall of those safety predictions against hardware were not available in the abstract.

**SafeDreamer's own evidence (answers "what if it's really good?")**
- SafeDreamer (Huang, Ji, Xia, Zhang, Yang; **ICLR 2024**) ablations: "when the world model accurately predicts safety-related states, it is more likely to contribute to safety improvements, thereby achieving zero cost"; removing the reconstruction loss made "the cost fail[] to decrease", indicating "a correlation between the predictive ability of the cost model and the reconstruction capability"; a larger stochastic state gave a larger cost decrease; cost-loss weighting matters, hypothesised to be "due to the unbalanced distribution of cost in the environment"; the cost threshold cannot be zero because of "potential errors in the trained cost model and cost value model" (threshold 2.0 for vision tasks) ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3)).

### Inferences
- **Mechanism, not magnitude.** Three independent lines (Biased Dreams' attractor, MiraBench's success-only post-training result, Schäfer's flat imagined error across gait collapse) converge on one picture: a model trained on success-dominated data has an attractor around competent behaviour, so near-failure states are rolled back towards success. A LeWM trained on competent walking should be expected to imagine a stumbling walker re-stabilising. That is a direct, testable explanation of the 45% false-safe rate, and it predicts that false-safes concentrate on trajectories starting near the edge of the data support rather than being spread uniformly.
- **The sign of the bias tracks the data mix.** Ctrl-World, trained with about 20% failures, *underestimates* success; DreamDojo/Cosmos after success-only post-training *overestimate* it. This is my inference from two papers, not a controlled result, but it suggests the student can manipulate optimism directly by changing the failure share in LeWM's training data, which is a cheap experiment with snapshot branching.
- **"Really good" models are not exempt.** SafeDreamer is strong partly because it is trained online on its own violations; its ablations tie safety to cost-state prediction accuracy and show cost imbalance matters. An offline model trained on competent PPO data lacks exactly that data. The right reply to the user's pushback is: quality on nominal data does not transfer to rare states (Kang et al.; MiraBench scaling; IntPhys 2), and the only "really good" safe world models observed their failures.
- **Walker2d relevance.** Falls in Walker are observable (torso height and pitch are in the probes), so in Kim, Nakamura and Bajcsy's terms this is a *prediction gap*, not an *estimation gap* ([Kim et al., preprint, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492) [from 3 Oct notes]). The velocity constraint may be partly an estimation gap if speed is not recoverable from the frame stack; the probes' accuracy on speed near the threshold should be reported separately.
- **Status.** Solved: demonstrating that strong models fail out of distribution. Closest work: Biased Dreams (RSSM), intervention gap (LeWM on Cheetah), MiraBench (video WMs). Open: no paper quantifies a *constraint predictor's* false-safe rate as a function of distance from the training support for a JEPA on locomotion, and none links the attractor effect to safety decisions. EC angle: an optimiser is exactly the thing that walks policies to the support boundary, so evolution both exposes and is harmed by the attractor (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of Genie 3 failure-state fidelity was verified. A "WorldRoamBench" (12 interactive world models including Genie 3) appeared only in a search snippet [unverified].
- No study measured SafeDreamer's own false-safe rate against ground truth; its ablations report cost curves, not detection metrics.
- No direct evidence for V-JEPA 2-AC or DINO-WM on fall-like failure states; the JEPA evidence is on manipulation, mazes and Cheetah, not on falls.
- MiraBench's test-set size for the optimism-bias level was not stated in the main text, so the precision of the 12.8 to 87.2 range is unknown.

## Q2. How well do ensembles, latent disagreement, conformal methods, density/OOD scores and other epistemic estimates flag safety-relevant errors in latent world models?

### Takeaway
Poorly as a stand-alone risk signal, and the reason is now explained: disagreement measures *local, one-step* epistemic uncertainty near the training support, but safety errors are *trajectory-level* and live on *constraint boundaries*. Disagreement collapses inside latent rollouts (attractor), ranks errors only near support, and in one workshop study was nearly uncorrelated with collision risk (r < 0.15) and made collisions worse when used as a penalty. What works better in 2025 to 2026 is (a) outcome-supervised failure readouts from frozen latents (FARM, AUROC 85.7), (b) online prediction-error residuals with adaptive conformal calibration (Cao and Bansal, Sept 2026), and (c) conformal tubes that are valid but conservative (SLS², UNISafe, Kim et al.). None of these has been evaluated against ground-truth counterfactuals at the scale the student's snapshot branching allows.

### Cited Findings
- **Disagreement does not track compounding error.** Biased Dreams (RLC 2026): M=5 external ensemble of one-step latent predictors, Geometric Jensen-Shannon divergence. "Although uncertainty captures initial distribution shifts, it does not track the compounding proprioceptive errors"; in closed-loop prior rollouts "ID uncertainty remains low and stable, whereas OOD uncertainty starts elevated, but rapidly converges to ID levels"; "ensemble disagreement captures local epistemic uncertainty" but this "does not necessarily extend to global trajectory-level model error" ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25416)).
- **Disagreement ranks error only near support.** Intervention gap (preprint, Aug 2026): in DreamerV3 "ensemble disagreement ranks error only near training support"; "a frozen support-aware score degrades held-out error ranking in both tested transfer directions while native disagreement remains informative in both" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)).
- **Uncertainty is not risk.** "Learning from World Feedback: Why Model Uncertainty Fails as a Risk Signal in Model-Based RL" (Zhaohui Wang; **workshop: ICML 2026 RLxF**; 18 Jul 2026): "dynamics-based uncertainty penalties increase collision rates from 26% to 34%", uncertainty is "anti-correlated with safety in this regime"; "model uncertainty has support over state-prediction space, whereas task risk has support over constraint boundaries, with empirical correlation r < 0.15"; outcome-based signals (minimum lidar, time-to-collision, an outcome-supervised feedback model) cut collisions to 1 to 14% without retraining ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16591)). Single-author workshop paper; treat as suggestive.
- **Ensembles in latent safety filters.** UNISafe (Seo, Nakamura, Bajcsy; **CoRL 2025**, PMLR 305): Jensen-Rényi divergence across an ensemble of next-latent predictors, threshold set by class-conditioned trajectory-level conformal prediction, failure set augmented with {u > ε}; 94% vs 83% balanced accuracy over LatentSafe on RGB Dubins; 72% safe success and 20% failure vs 68%/30% on IsaacLab block plucking; the authors concede the ensemble "may not faithfully capture" true uncertainty ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/seo25a.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- **Adaptive conformal on prediction residuals (newest).** "When World Models Lie: Adaptive Safety Analysis Under Wrong Imaginations" (Cao, Bansal; preprint; **28 Sep 2026**): builds online uncertainty sets with Adaptive Conformal Inference "from discrepancies between predicted and observation-inferred latent states", minimises the learned safety value over the set, gives "a finite-time coverage guarantee for the adaptive uncertainty radius", and "significantly reduces failures relative to state-of-the-art latent safety filters while preserving task completion" in simulation and hardware ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)).
- **Conformal robust MPC on a LeWM variant.** SLS² (Nath, Srinivasan, Yin, Jiang, Fang, Chou; preprint; 14 Jun 2026): conformal latent error bounds plus a conformalised latent constraint checker inside GPU system-level-synthesis robust MPC; "improves both goal-reaching performance and safety" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594)). Per the 3 Oct notes it also conformalises an in-distribution latent support (Gaussian density sublevel set), so it combines a density/OOD gate with error tubes; those internals came from a tool summary and were not re-checked here.
- **Conformal risk calibration costs conservativeness.** Kim, Nakamura, Bajcsy (preprint, Oct 2025): prediction-gap mitigation by conformal risk calibration works "albeit with increased conservativeness" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- **Conformal false-safe calibration for vision control.** "How Safe Will I Be Given What I Saw? Calibrated Prediction of Safety Chances for Image-Controlled Autonomy" (Mao, Umasudhan, Ruchkin; preprint; v1 12 Aug 2025, v3 13 Mar 2026): VAE latents plus recurrent latent forecasters with conformal calibration; domain-adapted evaluators "maintain high accuracy and substantially lower false positive rates under distribution shift"; composite predictors beat monolithic ones at long horizons ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09346)).
- **Certified trust horizons.** "Conformal Orbit-Valid Trust Horizons for Equivariant World Models" (Hongbo Wang; preprint; 23 Jun 2026): split-conformal calibration of a horizon curve from one-step residuals; "zero anti-conservative violations" across 50 audits (95% upper bound 5.8%); median certified-to-measured horizon ratio 0.67 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24946)).
- **Supervised failure readouts on frozen latents.** FARM (Pei, Luo, Wang, Lv, Guo, Zhong, Ci; preprint; 10 Sep 2026): a 33,985-parameter readout over frozen VLA-JEPA predictive states, 85.68/88.59 pooled AUROC/AUPRC out-of-fold over seven tasks, best "Seen" performance among 15 baselines on a 10-task benchmark, transfer across PIPER X, SO-101 and Franka, 0.2256 ms added latency ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)).
- **Prediction-residual anomaly monitors.** Domberg and Schildbach (preprint, 4 Mar 2025) monitor "discrepancies between a world model's predictions and observed system behavior" to trigger interventions; detected geometric and gravity changes in simulation and unexpected forces on a quadcopter ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02552)). RAPT (Munn, Tidd, Bohm, Gallagher, Howard; preprint; v2 21 Jul 2026): self-supervised 50 Hz recurrent probabilistic trajectory monitor for humanoids; 37% higher true-positive rate than the strongest baseline at 0.5% episode-level false-positive rate in four Isaac Lab tasks; 89% TPR over 78 hardware trials ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01515)).
- **Dense calibrated uncertainty in video world models.** C³ (Mei, Yin, Baker, Shorinwa, Majumdar; preprint; v1 5 Dec 2025, v2 10 Mar 2026): trains video models with strictly proper scoring rules for subpatch-level latent uncertainty, calibrated in distribution and useful for OOD detection on Bridge and DROID ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05927)).
- **Probabilistic JEPAs.** VJEPA (Yongchao Huang; preprint 20 Jan 2026; listed as an ICML 2026 poster on [icml.cc](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61579), page not opened) learns a variational predictive distribution over latents with credible intervals by sampling ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14354)). Branch-JEPA (Song et al.; preprint; v3 3 Aug 2026) replaces the point successor with a finite set of weighted latent branches trained by an energy score ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05238)). Neither evaluates safety-error detection.
- **Survey framing of the metric.** "Security of World-Model-Based Embodied AI" (Liu et al.; survey preprint; 30 Jul 2026) names the "predictive safety illusion" ("an incorrect but confident prediction can make an unsafe action appear certified") and proposes a "predicted-safe-but-actually-unsafe rate", "false safe certificates", "intervention recall" and "uncertainty calibration error" as metrics ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28226v1)).

### Inferences
- **The student's metric already has a name.** The 45% figure is precisely the "predicted-safe-but-actually-unsafe rate" that the July 2026 survey proposes but no benchmark yet reports with exact counterfactual ground truth. Using that terminology links the GECCO paper to the security and latent-safety literatures.
- **What to expect if the student adds an ensemble to LeWM.** From Biased Dreams and the intervention gap: disagreement will flag the *first* step off support and then fade inside rollouts, so a terminal-step disagreement threshold at 0.8 s will miss many false-safes; per-step maximum disagreement along the imagined branch, or disagreement measured only at the branch root, is the more defensible variant. From the RLxF workshop paper: an uncertainty *penalty* inside CMA-ES fitness could make things worse, because it pushes candidates towards regions the model knows well (the attractor) rather than away from the constraint boundary. Both are testable predictions, cheaply, with snapshot branching.
- **Residual-based and outcome-supervised signals look stronger than disagreement.** FARM (supervised readout), RAPT and Domberg (residuals), and Cao and Bansal (residual plus ACI) all use observed outcomes. For the student, the analogue is a probe-based failure head trained on branched ground truth, with adaptive conformal calibration of its threshold as CMA-ES moves the population: a calibration set that moves with the search distribution.
- **Corrections to the 3 October report (stale or imprecise points).**
  - The report summarises the intervention gap as "ensemble disagreement ranks out-of-distribution errors poorly". The abstract is more specific: in DreamerV3 disagreement "ranks error only near training support", and an added *support-aware* score was *worse*, while native disagreement "remains informative" in both transfer directions. The report should say "informative near support, degrades beyond it".
  - The report attributes the Cheetah "worse than predicting no effect" result generally; the abstract attributes it to **three LeWorldModel checkpoints**. This is the student's own model family and should be cited as such.
  - The report does not cover Biased Dreams (RLC 2026, peer-reviewed, includes Walker Run), the RLxF uncertainty-vs-risk result, Cao and Bansal (28 Sep 2026), FARM's position relative to these, C³, VJEPA/Branch-JEPA, QQWorld (LeWM heavy tails), MiraBench or RoboTrustBench. Each changes the framing of the mitigation arms.
  - BasinLens is confirmed non-evolutionary and was tested **on LeWM** (PushT, TwoRooms) against random search, GP-UCB and CEM only. The report's "explicitly non-EC" note stands, and the LeWM detail strengthens the novelty claim for an EC failure search.
  - ARC-Bench's date mismatch persists (v1 shown as 12 Aug 2026 under a 2609 identifier).
- **Status.** Solved: calibrated, conservative safety sets for latent models (SLS², UNISafe, Kim et al.); supervised failure readouts (FARM). Closest work: Cao and Bansal (adaptive conformal on residuals), UNISafe. Open: (1) any detector whose false-safe rate is measured *under optimisation pressure*, where the candidates are chosen *because* the model says they are safe; (2) trajectory-level epistemic estimates that do not collapse in latent rollouts; (3) whether conformal guarantees survive selection, since selecting the best of N candidates breaks exchangeability between calibration and test points (my inference; no paper found testing this). EC angle: rank-based selection and re-evaluation schedules are the levers that interact with (3).

### Gaps
- No paper reports AUROC or false-safe rates for ensemble disagreement as a detector of *constraint* errors in a JEPA world model; FARM is supervised and on VLA-JEPA, not LeWM.
- No head-to-head comparison of disagreement vs density (for example, Gaussian log-density under SIGReg's isotropic prior) vs conformal residuals for flagging false-safes was found. LeWM's SIGReg prior makes a Gaussian density score natural; QQWorld's heavy-tail finding suggests it would be miscalibrated in the tails.
- The RLxF result (r < 0.15) is from one environment and one author; replication status unknown.
- Whether Cao and Bansal's ACI radius remains valid when the action sequence is chosen by an optimiser is not addressed in the abstract.

## Q3. What benchmarks or evaluations exist for world-model safety or failure prediction?

### Takeaway
2026 produced several world-model reliability benchmarks, but all target manipulation video models, JEPA action ranking or average physics. MiraBench's "optimism bias" level is the closest analogue to danger blindness. **No benchmark measures a world model's false-safe rate as a constraint checker in a CMDP (Safety-Gymnasium) with exact counterfactual ground truth, and none studies how that rate changes under optimisation pressure.** The student's snapshot-branching harness is, in effect, a missing benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **MiraBench** (preprint, 28 May 2026): three levels (Physics Adherence, Action-Following Fidelity, Optimism Bias Detection) over 12 vector- and text-conditioned world models; failure preservation 12.8 to 87.2 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)).
- **RoboTrustBench** (preprint, v2 31 Aug 2026): six dimensions, 13 criteria including Safety Risk Identification, seven video WMs; counterfactual scenes show models "satisfy infeasible instructions by hallucinating missing objects" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01600)).
- **ARC-Bench** (preprint, Aug/Sep 2026): no-leak fixed-candidate protocol for action rankability of frozen JEPA-WMs; replanning masks ranking failures ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)).
- **BasinLens failure discovery** (preprint, 23 Aug 2026): on a 12,016-candidate grid at budget 64, BasinLens crossed the failure threshold in 54.7% of runs vs 40.6% for GP-UCB and 10.9% for random search; threshold-averaged discovery 0.572 vs 0.474 vs 0.255 (p=0.004 vs GP-UCB) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22421)).
- **FARM's 10-task failure-monitoring benchmark** with 15 baselines and three real robots ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11445)).
- **Intervention-fidelity audit** (intervention gap): matched-intervention, "capture-first" protocol across TD-MPC2, DreamerV3, LeWM, PreJEPA ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)).
- **ACPC** ("Diagnosing JEPA World Models with Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency"; An et al.; preprint; 13 Aug 2026): a bisimulation-style diagnostic that "bounds the perturbation-induced change in multi-step prediction error and planner cost", validated on LeWM and PLDM under blur and resize ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12939)).
- **Physics benchmarks:** Physics-IQ (WACV 2026) ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Motamed_Do_Generative_Video_Models_Understand_Physical_Principles_WACV_2026_paper.html)); IntPhys 2 (preprint 2025) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09849)); Kang et al. 2D collision testbed (ICML 2025) ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kang25g.html)).
- **World-model-based policy safety evaluation:** Veo world simulator red-teaming (preprint) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10675)); Ctrl-World policy ranking (ICLR 2026) ([PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- **Survey of 160 benchmarks** ("Do World Models Make Better Robots?"; Jena et al.; preprint; 30 Aug 2026): "only 11 (7%) build an explicit VLA-versus-world-model contrast" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29669)). A search excerpt adds that "counterfactual capability [is] almost entirely unmeasured and only four benchmarks turn[] prediction into executed action" (excerpt, not in abstract) [unverified wording].
- **Perspective:** "Rethinking World Models for Safety-Critical Embodied Systems" (Ma, Huang, Kim, Jang; perspective preprint; v2 1 Oct 2026) names three mismatches ("likelihood versus risk, prediction versus intervention, and finite-horizon prediction versus accumulated consequences") and lists "validating counterfactual reasoning" and "determining when evidence is sufficient to act" as open challenges ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03774)).
- **Driving (rare-event evaluation):** D2RL, "Dense reinforcement learning for safety validation of autonomous vehicles" (Feng et al.; **Nature 615:620-627, 2023**) edits the MDP to remove non-critical states, accelerating evaluation by 10³ to 10⁵ times "without loss of unbiasedness" ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10460886)). World Engine (arXiv 2606.19836) and CounterScene (arXiv 2603.21104) on counterfactual safety-critical scenario generation were seen only in search snippets [unverified].

### Inferences
- Every benchmark above either (a) scores video plausibility with judges or VLMs, (b) audits goal-cost ranking, or (c) monitors failures after the fact. None uses a simulator in which **every imagined branch can be replayed exactly**. Snapshot branching makes the student's harness a natural "counterfactual safety fidelity" benchmark for latent world models on Safety-Gymnasium, with metrics already named in the literature (false-safe rate, intervention recall, calibration error).
- MiraBench's six hand-designed perturbations are the obvious place for an evolutionary contribution: a search over failure-inducing perturbations replaces hand design (see Q6).
- **Status.** Solved: physics-plausibility and video-trust benchmarks; ranking audits. Closest: MiraBench (optimism bias), ARC-Bench, BasinLens. Open: a CMDP-grounded, counterfactual, optimisation-aware benchmark. Walker2d relevance: SafetyWalker2dVelocity gives a cost signal and a health termination signal, so the harness can score both a soft constraint (velocity) and a catastrophic one (fall).

### Gaps
- No world-model safety benchmark on Safety-Gymnasium or on any locomotion fall prediction was found.
- TouchSafeBench (collision grounding for VLMs, arXiv 2605.31196) and a claim that "Ward et al. at ICRA 2026" showed foundation world models detect bimanual failures appeared only in a search summary [unverified].
- Precision and recall of the Veo simulator's safety findings against real hardware were not available.

## Q4. How do people learn failure dynamics from few real failures, and what remains open?

### Takeaway
The working toolkit is: (1) **rebalancing with importance weights** from a learned criticality model, (2) **densifying** training on critical states (D2RL), (3) **boundary-focused sampling** of the 30 to 70% success region, (4) **synthetic or counterfactual failures** from a world model, and (5) **safe active exploration** that is optimistic on reward and pessimistic on constraints (ActSafe). Two 2026 results show the stakes: success-only fine-tuning destroys failure preservation in video world models, and a criticality-weighted sampler cut failure rates by 51 to 67%. What is open is doing this for a *pixel-trained latent world model* with a measured false-safe target, and knowing which failures to collect when collection is the scarce resource.

### Cited Findings
- **Criticality-weighted sampling.** "Self-Evolving Learning for Embodied AI with Criticality Model" (He, Tian, Fan, Pi, Lu, Su, Shi, Feng; preprint; 30 Jul 2026): datasets "become dominated by nominal scenarios, while rare failure cases" are overlooked; a state-wise criticality model predicting future failure guides sampling, with importance weighting to keep the objective unbiased; 51 to 67% relative reduction in failure rates vs baseline training, 8 to 25% vs state-of-the-art VLAs, across quadruped locomotion, manipulation, VLA benchmarks and real robots ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28251)).
- **Densified learning on critical states.** D2RL (Nature 2023) removes non-critical states and reconnects critical ones; 10³ to 10⁵ times acceleration with unbiased estimates ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10460886)).
- **Boundary-focused sampling.** ROBOGATE (Azuki Kim; preprint; v4 20 Apr 2026): Latin hypercube over an 8-D parameter space, then sampling concentrated "in the 30-70% success rate transition zone"; 50,000+ Isaac Sim experiments; logistic risk model AUC 0.780; a GR00T model at 97.65% on LIBERO scored 0% on 68 industrial scenarios ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22126)).
- **Success-only data harms failure modelling.** MiraBench: success-only post-training raised task completion from 14.0 to 92.0 while failure preservation fell from 48.7 to 23.1 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)). Ctrl-World notes that DROID's failure trajectories are "crucial" for controllability and that "many failure modes [remain] outside the data distribution" ([ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- **Cost imbalance inside SafeDreamer.** Cost-loss weighting affects convergence, attributed to "the unbalanced distribution of cost in the environment" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3)).
- **Synthetic transitions with hallucination control.** WorldSample (Xue et al.; preprint; 2 Jul 2026): grounds world-model generation on real rollouts and uses Policy-Paced Learning to schedule synthetic samples, "balancing useful augmentation against value overestimation"; +28% success, 59% fewer training steps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02431)).
- **Counterfactual unsafe samples.** PROCO (Xue et al.; preprint; May 2026) uses a learned dynamics model to generate counterfactual unsafe samples for "safe-but-infeasible states" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01356)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- **Safe active exploration.** ActSafe (As, Sukhija, Treven, Sferrazza, Coros, Krause; **ICLR 2025**): a calibrated probabilistic model, optimistic in epistemic uncertainty for exploration and pessimistic for safety constraints; claims the first safety and finite-sample guarantees for safe exploration in continuous model-based RL, with a practical variant for visual control ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09486); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29177)).
- **Learned failure classifiers.** LatentSafe (Nakamura, Peters, Bajcsy; **RSS 2025**) turns constraint specification into latent failure classification ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00935)) [from 3 Oct notes]; Kim et al. add privileged multimodal supervision ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06492)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- **Nightmare Dreamer** (workshop, RSS 2025 MOOP; arXiv Jan 2026) learns to predict violations and plans with them ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04686)) [from 3 Oct notes].

### Inferences
- **Reframing the user's question for the student's setting.** In MuJoCo, "visiting" a dangerous state costs nothing physically; the scarce resource is the **real-evaluation budget** (snapshot branches replayed) and the **labelled failure data** used to retrain LeWM or its probes. "Learning about the dangerous part while visiting it as little as possible" then becomes a budgeted active-learning and rare-event estimation problem: choose which branches to replay so as to most reduce the false-safe rate per replay. This is the regime in which ActSafe, D2RL and criticality weighting were designed, but none of them uses a latent world model as the thing being corrected.
- The student's earlier finding that boundary-focused predictor fine-tuning did not consistently help is consistent with Biased Dreams: if the dynamics predictor's attractor is the cause, retraining only the probe or classifier near the boundary cannot fix rollouts that have already drifted back to the competent-gait region. That points to fixing the *dynamics data* (adding falls and recoveries to the LeWM training set) or the *search*, rather than the probe.
- **Status.** Solved: unbiased rare-event acceleration for evaluation (D2RL); criticality-weighted data rebalancing for policies. Closest work for world models: MiraBench (diagnosis), WorldSample and PROCO (synthetic data), ActSafe (exploration with guarantees). Open: (1) a sample-efficient procedure that adds the *fewest* real failures to a JEPA's training set to reach a target false-safe rate; (2) whether synthetic failures from the model itself help or reinforce the attractor (the "tails collapse first" concern from a non-academic source was not counted as evidence); (3) importance-weighted estimators of the false-safe rate that remain unbiased when the proposal comes from an optimiser.

### Gaps
- No paper found that retrains a JEPA world model with targeted failure data and reports the resulting change in false-safe rate.
- No scaling curve of "number of real failures in training data vs failure-preservation score" was found; MiraBench compares training regimes, not amounts.
- Venue of the 2026 criticality-model paper is not stated on arXiv.

## Q5. Is there a notion of "how good is good enough", or results showing safety performance scaling with model accuracy?

### Takeaway
No general answer exists. Three partial notions are in use: (a) **local and calibrated** (a conformal error radius smaller than the safety margin over a certified horizon: SLS², UNISafe, conformal trust horizons); (b) **decision-relevant** (accuracy only matters on safety-related states, per SafeDreamer, and on candidate *ranking*, per ARC-Bench); and (c) **empirical prediction of closed-loop performance** (validation loss and multi-step RMSE keep improving after closed-loop performance collapses). The perspective literature names "determining when evidence is sufficient to act" as an open problem. No scaling law linking world-model accuracy to safety violations was found; where scale was varied, failure fidelity got worse, not better.

### Cited Findings
- SafeDreamer: safety improves "when the world model accurately predicts safety-related states"; cost threshold must be above zero because of cost-model error ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07176v3)).
- "Predicting Closed-Loop Performance of Latent World Models" (Smolyanskiy; preprint; 2 Jul 2026): "validation loss and multi-step prediction RMSE keep improving long after closed-loop performance has collapsed"; over 40 metrics against a CEM-MPC oracle on LunarLander with an RSSM, the best predictor is a Reward Observability Fraction, combined into a checkpoint-selection score ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01736)).
- "Do Better Imagined Rollouts Mean Better Robot Control?" (Raghavan, Singh; preprint; 2 Sep 2026): replay error correlates with closed-loop error more strongly than 20-step rollout error (Spearman ρ 0.923 vs 0.774); rollout metrics chose a different best model than closed-loop performance in **18 of 24** conditions vs 5 of 24 for replay; agreement falls from ρ 0.916 to 0.774 as measurement updates are removed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02811)). The task is differential-drive path tracking, not a learned visual world model.
- Intervention gap: across TD-MPC2 sizes, return tracks an operator-error diagnostic while reward-prediction error stays "small and nearly flat" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998)).
- ARC-Bench: frequent replanning hides ranking failures, so closed-loop success "systematically overstate[s]" model quality ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)).
- MiraBench: 2B to 14B lowered failure preservation from 48.7 to 12.8 within DreamDojo ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)); Kang et al.: scaling fixes in-distribution but not OOD prediction ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kang25g.html)).
- Conformal trust horizons: certified horizon at median 0.67 of the measured one, with no anti-conservative violations in 50 audits ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24946)).
- SLS²: a probability of at least 1−δ of staying safe given conformal error sets and constraint tightening ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15594)); theorem details [from 3 Oct notes].
- Theory: return-error bounds for training in imagination tighten with smaller Lipschitz constants of dynamics, reward and policy ([Timor et al., preprint, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06732)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- Perspective: "determining when evidence is sufficient to act" listed as open ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03774)).
- Ctrl-World ranks policies correctly while underestimating absolute success, showing that ranking fidelity can be good enough for evaluation when absolute fidelity is not ([ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0ae94013da7cd459402fd77874e09ee3-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### Inferences
- For a CMA-ES user, the relevant accuracy is **ranking accuracy near the feasibility boundary among the candidates the optimiser actually proposes**, not average latent MSE. Rank-based selection is invariant to monotone miscalibration, so a model can be badly calibrated yet "good enough" if it orders candidates correctly by safety. That gives the student a concrete, EC-native definition: "good enough" means the false-safe rate of the *selected* elites stays below a target at a given population size and selection intensity. I found no paper defining sufficiency this way.
- The selection-pressure effect is the optimiser's curse applied to safety: when many candidates are scored with noisy estimates, the selected maximum is biased (Smith and Winkler, "The Optimizer's Curse", Management Science 2006 [not re-verified this session]). ASAR's pool-size result (feasibility of min-cost selection falling from 0.375 to 0.062 as proposals go from 72 to 288) is the latent-planner instance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)) [from 3 Oct notes]. So "good enough" is not a property of the model alone; it depends on how hard the optimiser leans on it.
- **Status.** Solved: calibrated local guarantees under exchangeability. Closest: SLS², conformal trust horizons, Smolyanskiy. Open: a sufficiency criterion that accounts for optimisation pressure, and any empirical curve of safety violations against model accuracy at fixed optimiser settings. Walker2d relevance: the student can produce exactly that curve by varying LeWM checkpoints (or training-data failure share) and CMA-ES population size, with exact ground truth, which no paper has done.

### Gaps
- No scaling law for safety vs world-model accuracy or size was found for any model class.
- No result on whether conformal guarantees survive best-of-N selection was found.

## Q6. Where could evolutionary or population-based search uniquely help?

### Takeaway
Four places, each supported by a documented gap: (1) **failure discovery in world models**, where the 2026 state of the art (BasinLens) compares only against random search, GP-UCB and CEM, and MiraBench hand-designs its six failure perturbations; (2) **diverse coverage of failure modes** with MAP-Elites, which has precedent in scenario generation and an emerging theory of what archives can and cannot certify; (3) **adaptive importance sampling for false-safe rates**, where CEM and CMA-ES are natural proposal learners; and (4) **selection operators robust to model error**, because the core failure (optimisation pressure harvesting false-safe errors) lives in the selection step that EC controls. The student's setting is unusually well-suited because snapshot branching provides cheap, exact counterfactual labels, the expensive ingredient every other approach lacks.

### Cited Findings
- BasinLens was evaluated against random search, GP-UCB and CEM; "No genetic algorithms or CMA-ES were tested" (per the fetched HTML); it tested LeWM among five model families ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22421)).
- MiraBench's optimism-bias level uses six fixed perturbation types ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29360)).
- QD scenario generation: Fontaine and Nikolaidis (**RSS 2021**) show MAP-Elites finds diverse scenarios that minimise a tested algorithm's performance, beating Monte Carlo and CMA-ES ([RSS PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p036.pdf)) [from 3 Oct notes]. DSAGE (Bhatt, Tjanaka, Fontaine, Nikolaidis; **NeurIPS 2022**) runs QD on a deep surrogate of agent behaviour and confirms with real evaluations ([NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/f649556471416b35e60ae0de7c1e3619-Abstract.html)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- "Quality-Diversity Stress Tests for Process Reward Models: What Archive Coverage Can and Cannot Certify" (Shihab, Afrin; preprint; 8 Aug 2026): MAP-Elites archives can bound tail risk and average severity, but "worst-case guarantees cannot be bounded by archive fitting error"; a repair protocol cut exploit rates from 14.8% to 3.7% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08008)).
- Criticality-weighted importance sampling (2607.28251) and D2RL (Nature 2023) show the value of proposal distributions concentrated on failure ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28251); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10460886)).
- DA-QD (**ICRA 2022**) is about 20 times more sample-efficient than model-free QD by searching in an ensemble model's imagination, but real coverage falls below the imagined repertoire because of model error ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.08522)) [from 3 Oct notes]. GuSS lists "incorporating model uncertainty with QD" as future work ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09743v2)) [from 3 Oct notes].
- Biased Dreams: rollouts are pulled to high-performing attractors, which an optimiser seeking high imagined return will preferentially select ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25416)).
- RLxF workshop: an uncertainty penalty raised collisions from 26% to 34% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16591)).

### Inferences
- **(1) Evolutionary failure discovery against LeWM.** Use CMA-ES or MAP-Elites over *start states plus action perturbations* (or over policy parameters) to maximise the gap between imagined and branched-real safety margin. Fitness is the false-safe magnitude, which snapshot branching measures exactly. BasinLens shows the problem is recognised and that LeWM breaks, but no EC baseline exists. This is a small, clean GECCO-sized contribution and also produces the training data that Q4 says is missing.
- **(2) A MAP-Elites map of danger blindness.** Descriptors such as predicted margin and real torso-height minimum (or speed and pitch at the branch root) give an archive whose cells are labelled by false-safe rate. Shihab and Afrin's result sets an honest claim: such an archive can certify tail-risk and average-severity statements over its coverage, not worst-case safety.
- **(3) CMA-ES as an adaptive importance sampler for the false-safe rate.** The cross-entropy method is the classical adaptive importance-sampling scheme for rare events, and CMA-ES is its covariance-adaptive relative; using either to propose branch start states, with likelihood-ratio weights, gives an unbiased false-safe estimate at a fraction of uniform-sampling cost. This connects the user's "visit danger as little as possible" question to a well-understood estimator (inference; I did not verify a 2025 to 2026 CMA-ES-for-IS-on-world-models paper).
- **(4) Selection operators as the mitigation.** Because the attractor makes the model optimistic exactly where the optimiser goes, and uncertainty penalties can backfire, mitigations that act on *selection* (re-evaluating the top-k with branches, lower-confidence-bound ranking from a probe ensemble, feasibility rules applied to a pessimistic margin, stochastic ranking) are the natural EC contribution. They need no change to LeWM and can be compared at a matched real-evaluation budget.
- **(5) Active learning loop.** Alternate: evolve to find false-safes, branch to label them, add them to LeWM or probe training, repeat. This is Ha and Schmidhuber's iterative loop with an EC failure finder and exact labels, and it directly answers "learn the dangerous part while visiting it as little as possible": each real replay is spent where the model is most confidently wrong.
- **Status.** Solved: QD for scenario generation on simulators; QD in ensemble imagination without safety. Closest: BasinLens (non-EC, on LeWM), DSAGE, Fontaine and Nikolaidis, GuSS. Open: EC failure discovery on latent world models; archives of false-safe regions; selection operators that bound false-safe rates under pressure; importance-sampled false-safe estimators driven by evolutionary proposals.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 paper applying CMA-ES, a GA or MAP-Elites to find failures of a *learned world model* (as opposed to a policy or simulator) was found; the absence is a negative search result over the queries used in this session and the 3 October session.
- No paper tests whether rank-based selection is measurably more robust to model miscalibration than penalty-weighted selection in imagination.
- Throughput of LeWM rollouts plus MuJoCo branching for population-scale failure search was not measured here.
