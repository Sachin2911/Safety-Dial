# GECCO and 2026 venue landscape for evolution, learned world models and safe RL

Notes compiled 5 October 2026. They add to, and correct where needed, the 3 October report ([Evolutionary safe RL with world models.md](../../reports/Evolutionary%20safe%20RL%20with%20world%20models.md)), its GECCO notes (`research_notes/Evolutionary safe RL with world models/gecco_fit_tooling_feasibility.md`) and `docs/gecco2027.md`. Items already covered there (ASAR, ARC-Bench, the intervention-gap study, world-model backdoors, FARM, JEPA shields, BasinLens, SLS², GuSS, DA-QD, Safe CMA-ES, QDHUAC) are not repeated except where a status changed.

Conventions. "Verified" means I read the arXiv abstract page or an official page. "Title only" means the item appears on an official list (GECCO accepted lists, arXiv API listing) but I did not read its content. "Peer-reviewed" means accepted at a refereed venue; arXiv-only items are preprints. GECCO full papers are in the ACM main proceedings; posters, workshop papers, late-breaking abstracts (LBAs) and Hot-off-the-Press (HOP) items are in the Companion proceedings. The ACM DL, dblp, the IEEE CIS special-issue page and the ACM TELO calls page all returned bot-protection or 403 errors, so nothing here was cross-checked against them. The arXiv API returned HTTP 429 (rate limited) after the first batch of queries, so the keyword sweep of arXiv is partial (see Gaps in section 4).

## 1. GECCO 2025 and 2026 accepted work touching world models, surrogate neuroevolution, safe or constrained policy search, QD-RL with safety, adversarial or failure search, ROSARL-like penalties

### Takeaway
Across every 2025 and 2026 list (full papers, posters, workshop papers, LBAs and HOP), there is still no GECCO paper that evolves policies inside a learned world model, and none that evaluates evolutionary methods on a constrained-MDP safe-RL benchmark. The 3 October screen missed a cluster of 2026 papers on adversarial and failure-seeking evolution: adversarial QD, LLM-driven adversarial program evolution, adversarial prompt illumination and evolved cues for safe overtaking. These matter because they show GECCO now rewards "evolution as a stress-tester". The companion volumes (workshops, LBAs, HOP), screened here for the first time, add nothing on world models or safe RL.

### Cited Findings
**Method of this screen**
- I downloaded the official 2025 and 2026 lists of full papers, posters, workshop papers, LBAs and HOP papers and keyword-screened every title for: world model, safe, safety, imagin, dream, model-based, latent, reinforcement, policy, open-ended, quality-diversity, MAP-Elites, evolution strategies, adversarial, failure, falsif and risk. Lists: [2025 papers](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted-Papers), [2025 posters](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted-Posters), [2025 workshop papers](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted+Workshop+Papers), [2025 HOP](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted+Hot+off+the+Press+Papers), [2025 LBAs](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Accepted+Late-breaking+Abstracts), [2026 papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Papers), [2026 posters](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Posters), [2026 workshop papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Workshop+Papers), [2026 HOP](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Hot+off+the+Press+Papers), [2026 LBAs](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Late-breaking+Abstracts).

**Adversarial and failure-seeking evolution at GECCO 2026 (not in the 3 October notes)**
- CS 2026 full paper, Anne, Syrkis, Elhosni, Turati, Manai, Legendre, Jaquier & Risi, "Tournament Informed Adversarial Quality Diversity" (verified; peer-reviewed; arXiv 27 January 2026). It extends Generational Adversarial MAP-Elites (GAME), which coevolves both sides of an adversarial problem, and adds an inter-variant tournament with six quality and diversity measures plus two tournament-informed task-selection methods. Tested on Pong, Cat-and-mouse and Pursuers-and-evaders. One author is from armasuisse Science+Technology, a defence agency ([arXiv 2601.19562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19562v1); [2026 papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Papers)).
- GECH 2026 full paper, Kumar, Bahlous-Boldi, Sharma, Isola, Risi, Tang & Ha, "Digital Red Queen: Adversarial Program Evolution in Core War with LLMs" (verified; peer-reviewed; arXiv 6 January 2026; MIT and Sakana AI). An LLM evolves each new Core War "warrior" to beat all previous ones. Warriors become more general against held-out human-written warriors and less behaviourally diverse across runs, which the authors read as convergent evolution ([arXiv 2601.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03335); [2026 papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Papers)).
- EML 2026 full paper, Sygkounas, Hazra, Persson, Zuidberg Dos Martires & Loutfi, "COvolve: Adversarial Co-Evolution of Large-Language-Model-Generated Policies and Environments via Two-Player Zero-Sum Game" (verified via search snippet of the arXiv record; peer-reviewed). The LLM writes both environments and policies as Python. A mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium meta-policy prevents forgetting. Domains: urban driving, symbolic mazes and geometric navigation ([arXiv 2603.28386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28386); [2026 papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Papers)).
- Other 2026 full papers, title only ([2026 papers](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Accepted+Papers)):

  - CS: Pex, Marzougui et al., "Stability-Driven CPG Design for More Effective Quality-Diversity in Evolutionary Robotics".

**ROSARL status (venue landscape)**
- The supervisor's group published "An Unreasonably Simple Approach to Safe RL" (Nangue Tasse, Nemecek, Love, James & Rosman) in the Reinforcement Learning Journal vol. 7 (2026), presented at RLC 2026 in Montréal. ### Inferences
- The gap the 3 October report identified (no GECCO paper with evolution inside a learned world model, and none on CMDP safe RL) survives a wider screen that now includes the companion proceedings. For GECCO 2027 the combination is novel, but no GECCO precedent sets reviewer expectations.
- 2026 shows a clear GECCO appetite for adversarial and failure-seeking evolution (GAME-style adversarial QD, Digital Red Queen, COvolve, adversarial prompt illumination). A paper that frames evolution as an adversary of the world model's safety predictor, measuring how selection finds "imagined-safe but really unsafe" policies, would sit inside this trend rather than outside it.
- The RLC 2026 paper appears to be the published form of the ROSARL idea, renamed. The student should cite it as the peer-reviewed reference and confirm the naming with the supervisor. This updates the 3 October advice to "settle the ROSARL citation".

### Gaps
- Full texts of the title-only papers, especially the Tsinghua surrogate-assisted EMARL paper, the 2025 ENUM re-evaluation paper and the 2026 LLMfwEC surrogate-fitness-limitations workshop paper, were not read. The ACM DL was blocked.
- Student Workshop papers are included in the workshop list, but competition entries were not screened.

**Safety of policies trained or planned in learned world models (direct neighbours)**
- Cao & Bansal, "When World Models Lie: Adaptive Safety Analysis Under Wrong Imaginations" (verified; preprint; 28 September 2026; cs.RO). It uses Hamilton-Jacobi reachability safety values with Adaptive Conformal Inference over "discrepancies between predicted and observation-inferred latent states". It evaluates safety pessimistically, stays "minimally conservative when the world model is accurate" and comes with a finite-time coverage guarantee. Simulation and hardware; no evolutionary component ([arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)).
- Wen, Xu, Wang, Lin, Meng, Fu, Wang & Cheng, "Lucid Dreaming for World Models: Learning to Doubt Imagination and Decide by Trust" (verified; preprint; 29 September 2026). LucidWM uses Subjective Logic to give each imagined transition a "doubt" score and accumulates trust along rollouts. It is compared against seventeen uncertainty metrics on four base world models; trust-based selection cut navigation steps from 362 to 190 ([arXiv 2609.37156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37156v1)).
- Ma, Huang, Kim & Jang, "Rethinking World Models for Safety-Critical Embodied Systems" (verified; 6-page perspective; v1 3 September, v2 1 October 2026). It lists five open problems: identifying consequential futures; validating counterfactual reasoning; revisable safety memories; translating learned consequences into executable constraints; and deciding when evidence suffices to act versus "sense, defer, or abstain" ([arXiv 2609.03774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03774)).
- Tang et al., "SafeDojo: Safe Reinforcement Learning for VLA via Interactive World Model" (verified; preprint; 15 June 2026; 18 authors). It runs online RL inside an interactive video world model with a "Lagrangian-based constrained GRPO objective" and a "lightweight safety head" predicting per-step cost from latents. Evaluated on SafeLIBERO and a real Franka arm. The abstract reports no imagined-versus-real violation comparison ([arXiv 2606.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20698)).
- Wendl, As, Prajapat, Pollak, Coros & Krause, "Safe Exploration via Policy Priors" (SOOPER; verified; ICLR 2026 per the PDF header; v1 27 January, revised 13 August 2026). It uses probabilistic dynamics models with a conservative prior policy as fallback, with safety guarantees throughout learning and cumulative-regret bounds. This is directly on "learn while visiting danger as little as possible", but it is not evolutionary ([arXiv 2601.19612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19612)).

**Adversarial stress-testing of world-model agents (non-evolutionary so far)**
- Guo, Liang, Fu, Guo, Balogh, Jelasity & Tao, "WMAttack: Automated Attack Search for Adversarial Evaluation of World-Model Agents" (verified; preprint; 22 May, revised 5 September 2026). Self-correcting attack search raised the normalised reward drop on DreamerV3 from 0.497 to 1.034 on Atari and from 0.319 to 0.682 on DMC. The search mechanism is not specified in the abstract ([arXiv 2605.23220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23220)).
- Zhang, Tan, Li, Zhu, Li & Gu, "ARB4WM: An Adversarial Robustness Benchmark for World Models in Continuous Control" (verified; preprint; 15 June 2026). It tests four Dreamer-style agents on 20 MetaWorld tasks and the DeepMind Control Suite with white-box attacks. Attacks on value, latent and RSSM dynamics are "as damaging as direct policy disruption" ([arXiv 2606.16605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16605)).

**QD in latent spaces and imagination**
- Bendib, Perrin-Gilbert & Sigaud, "Behavioral Foundation Models for Quality Diversity" (BFM-QD; verified; NeurIPS 2026 per arXiv; 28 September 2026). It runs QD in the compact latent space of a pretrained behavioural foundation model as a "gradient-free policy improvement operator" without critic training, and beats parameter-space QD on locomotion, navigation and manipulation, especially with sparse rewards. It does not use a world model or imagination and has no safety component ([arXiv 2609.35615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35615)). This corrects the 3 October report's table, which described BFM-QD under "Evolve policies inside a latent world model": its latent is a policy latent, not a dynamics model.
- No July to October 2026 successor to DA-QD (QD with an imagined repertoire in a learned dynamics model) was found by the arXiv sweep or web searches. The 2025 Extract-QD framework (QD under noise or uncertainty) remains the closest award-winning QD work (see section 5).

**Evolutionary RL and neuroevolution (new in the window)**
- Nisioti, Cossu, Korte & Risi, "Continual Reinforcement Learning with Neuroevolution" (verified; preprint; 1 October 2026). ES "most consistently achieves a good stability-plasticity trade-off", while GAs are more plastic but forget more. ES finds wider weight-space neighbourhoods. Novelty search raises plasticity but worsens forgetting. RL plasticity-loss symptoms do not appear in neuroevolution ([arXiv 2610.01583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01583)).

**LLM-driven evolution (AlphaEvolve or FunSearch style)**
- Oved, Pony, Naparstek & Barzelay (IBM Research), "Evolution or Illusion? Rethinking Evaluation in LLM Evolutionary Search" (preprint; 17 September 2026; seen as an arXiv PDF in search results, abstract via search snippet only). It finds that single-budget evaluations mislead. The best split between seeds and iterations changes with strategy, task and budget, and on one task the worst strategy at one seed is the best at forty seeds. It proposes reporting the seeds-by-iterations frontier ([arXiv 2609.19799](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19799)).
- Guo, Li, Feng & Zhang, "Code Evolution for Control: Synthesizing Policies via LLM-Driven Evolutionary Search" (verified; preprint; 11 January 2026). EvoToolkit evolves populations of policy programs into "compact, human-readable control policies that can be directly inspected, modified, and formally verified" ([arXiv 2601.06845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06845)).
- Title or snippet only, adjacent: "SafeEvolve: Harness-Policy Co-Evolution from Agent Experience for Safety Alignment" (arXiv 2609.02786, 2 September 2026; LLM agents, not control) ([arXiv 2609.02786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02786)); "Self-Reports Are Not Verification: Environment-Grounded Auditing of LLM Operators in Evolutionary Search" ([arXiv 2609.00652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00652)); CoMAP, "Co-Evolving World Models and Agent Policies for LLM Agents" (EMNLP 2026 main, per arXiv comment; [arXiv 2606.02372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02372)).
- I found no 2026 paper that uses LLM-driven evolution to design cost functions, penalties or constraints for Safety-Gymnasium-style safe RL. The nearest prior is E²CFD, LLM cost-function design for safe RL from 2024 ([arXiv 2407.05580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05580)), and it was surfaced only by search, not read.

**Open-endedness with world models**
- Genie 3 (Google DeepMind, announced 5 August 2025) generates real-time interactive worlds at 24 fps and 720p, and DeepMind positions it as an "unlimited curriculum" for agents such as SIMA ([DeepMind Genie 3 blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)).
- The July to October 2026 open-ended world-model papers found by the arXiv sweep are generative-video or code-world-model systems, not evolutionary (all title only): "Oneira: From Open-Ended Generation to Open-World Interaction in Video World Models" (2610.01614, 1 October); "Code Plans, Diffusion Renders: Open-Ended Generative World Modeling" (2609.26458); "Code World Model: Coding Agent as World Brain" (2608.25927); "AutoWorldModel-Bench: A State-Centric Benchmark for Automated World-Model Research" (Electronic Arts; 2608.11216) ([arXiv 2610.01614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01614); [arXiv 2609.26458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26458); [arXiv 2608.25927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25927); [arXiv 2608.11216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11216)).
- No 2026 OMNI-EPIC successor that couples a learned world model with open-ended task generation and safety was found. OMNI-EPIC itself remains the reference ([arXiv 2405.15568](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15568)).

### Inferences
- Three September 2026 preprints (Cao & Bansal; LucidWM; Ma et al.'s perspective) show that "world models are confidently wrong, and safety reasoning must adapt to that" became a live topic in the month before the GECCO 2027 writing window. All three act on the filter or the model, none on the search or selection operator. The student's distinctive angle stays open: how selection pressure amplifies the predictor's false-safe errors, and which evolution-native mitigations (re-evaluation, margins, rank-based or feasibility rules, ROSARL-style penalties) cut real violations per real sample. It must cite these three as related work.
- Ma et al.'s open problems ("identifying consequential futures", "sufficient evidence thresholds… sense, defer, or abstain") are close paraphrases of the student's unifying question. Citing them gives the paper a ready-made motivation sentence from the ML side.
- The "Evolution or Illusion?" critique suggests that, if the student adds LLM-driven evolution, GECCO and ML reviewers will expect multi-seed, multi-budget reporting. This raises the cost of an LLM component inside a two-month window.
- BFM-QD (NeurIPS 2026) makes "QD in a learned latent" mainstream ML. A QD-in-JEPA-imagination paper would need to distinguish itself through the dynamics model and the safety measurement, not through the latent-space QD idea.

### Gaps
- The arXiv sweep was partial because of rate limiting: safe RL × evolution, QD × safety, LLM reward design × safety, AlphaEvolve × RL and Genie × open-endedness were not run through the API. The `abs:` phrase search may also miss plural or hyphenated variants ("world-model", "world models").
- No paper evolving policies inside a JEPA, RSSM or video world model in July to October 2026 was found. This is absence of evidence from incomplete searches, not proof.
- WMAttack's search mechanism (possibly evolutionary or sampling-based) was not stated in the abstract.

## 5. Best-paper nominees and winners in NE, EML, CS and GA (and adjacent tracks), 2024 to 2026, and why they stood out

### Takeaway
GECCO now publishes official winner pages for 2024 and 2025, and they correct the 3 October notes. Nisioti et al. won the 2025 BBSR+ENUM award (the notes called it a nominee). Extract-QD won the 2025 CS+NE award. "Evolution of Inherently Interpretable Visual Control Policies" won the 2025 EML award. In 2024, QJED won CS+L4EC and TensorNEAT won GECH+NE. The 2026 official "Best Paper Awards" page is a stale copy of the 2025 winners. Secondary sources confirm two 2026 winners: the CS award for an LLM/VLM open-endedness study (Picbreeder replicated with VLMs) and the RWA award. The RL-adjacent winners share one of four signatures: a step change in scale or speed, a learned model that makes evolutionary evaluation cheaper or robust to uncertainty, a surprising head-to-head finding against RL on new benchmarks, or a big-question science study using foundation models.

### Cited Findings
**Official winners, GECCO 2024** ([2024 Best Paper Awards](https://gecco-2024.sigevo.org/Best-Paper-Awards.html))
- **CS + L4EC:** Templier, Grillotti, Rachelson, Wilson & Cully, "Quality with Just Enough Diversity in Evolutionary Policy Search" (JEDi). It learns the behaviour-to-fitness relationship so that ES evaluations concentrate on promising regions, and beats both QD and ES on mazes and on Brax control with large policies (verified in the 3 October notes via [arXiv 2405.04308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04308)).
- **GECH + NE:** Wang, Zhao, Liu, Sun & Cheng (SUSTech), "Tensorized NeuroEvolution of Augmenting Topologies for GPU Acceleration" (TensorNEAT), up to 500× faster than traditional NEAT ([SUSTech news](https://newshub.sustech.edu.cn/en/?p=43596); [arXiv 2404.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01817v1)).
- **EML:** Woodward, Bandhey, Moore & Urbanowicz, "Survival-LCS: A Rule-Based Machine Learning Approach to Survival Analysis" (not RL).
- **GA + THEORY:** Baumann, Rutter & Sudholt, "Evolutionary Computation Meets Graph Drawing: Runtime Analysis for Crossing Minimisation on Layered Graph Drawings". The only GA nominee, Festa, Dixit & Tumer, "Reinforcing Inter-Class Dependencies in the Asymmetric Island Model", did not win ([2024 nominations](https://gecco-2024.sigevo.org/Best-Paper-Nominations)).
- **RWA:** Nikfarjam et al., "Quality Diversity Approaches for Time-Use Optimisation to Improve Health Outcomes".
- Other 2024 nominees in relevant tracks: EML, "NEvoFed" (Custode, Iacca et al.) and Dixit & Tumer, "Informed Diversity Search for Learning in Asymmetric Multiagent Systems"; ENUM, Porter & Arnold, "Direct Augmented Lagrangian Evolution Strategies" ([2024 nominations](https://gecco-2024.sigevo.org/Best-Paper-Nominations)).

**Official winners, GECCO 2025** ([2025 Best Paper Awards](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Best-Paper-Awards))
- **BBSR + ENUM:** Nisioti, Plantec, Llera Montero, Pedersen & Risi, "When Does Neuroevolution Outcompete Reinforcement Learning in Transfer Learning Tasks?". It introduces two benchmarks ("stepping gates" and "ecorobot") and finds NE often beats PPO-family baselines at transfer, with 10 trials and non-parametric tests ([arXiv 2505.22696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22696)).
- **CS + NE:** Flageat, Huber, Helenon, Doncieux & Cully, "Extract-QD Framework: A Generic Approach for Quality-Diversity in Noisy, Stochastic or Uncertain Domains" (verified; arXiv 10 February 2025). It unifies existing uncertain-domain QD methods "within a modular view" and derives Extract-ME, which "consistently outperforms or matches the best existing methods", and augments PGA-ME "with improved performance in uncertain domains at no additional evaluation cost" ([arXiv 2502.06585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06585)).
- **EML:** De La Torre, Nadizar, Lavinas, Luga, Wilson & Cussat-Blanc, "Evolution of Inherently Interpretable Visual Control Policies". It evolves size-constrained graph-based GP policies for vision-based control on three Atari games, and shows that reading the true computational graph explains more than post-hoc explainability methods applied to neural networks. It motivates this by "safety-critical domains" (DOI 10.1145/3712256.3726332) ([GP bibliography entry](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/de-la-torre_2025_GECCO.html)).
- **GA + SI:** Schmidbauer & Sudholt, "Empirical Linkage Learning Provably Builds Truthful Models on Concatenated Traps and H-IFF"; Asadi, Nowé & Ghofrani, "Congestion-Aware Multi-Agent Path Planning for Pick-up and Delivery Tasks" (two awards listed).
- **Notable non-winning 2025 nominees:** NE, Abrantes, Lange & Tang (Sakana AI), "Competition and Attraction Improve Model Fusion"; NE, Huang, Xue, Sun & Zhang, "Evolving Comprehensive Proxies for Zero-Shot NAS"; NE, Patterson, Karns, Lyu & Desell, "Visualizing the Dynamics of Neuroevolution with Genetic Distance Projections"; EML, Gonzalez, Dixit & Tumer, "Dynamic Influence For Coevolutionary Agents"; BBSR, Malan & Muñoz, "Why We Should be Benchmarking Evolutionary Algorithms on Neural Network Training Tasks" ([2025 nominations](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Best-Paper-Nominations)).

**GECCO 2026 nominees (official) and known winners (secondary sources)**
- The official 2026 "Best Paper Awards" page lists exactly the 2025 winners, including Nisioti et al. and Extract-QD. It is a stale copy and must not be cited as 2026 results ([2026 Best Paper Awards page](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Best+Paper+Awards)).
- 2026 nominees ([2026 nominations](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Best+Paper+Nominations)):
  - **CS:** Earle, Arulkumaran, Kumar, Dai, Togelius & Risi, "In Search of the Ingredients of Open-Endedness: Replicating Picbreeder with Large Vision-Language Models". **Won the CS award** (CS was paired with SI in 2026) according to Sakana AI's project page ("Best Paper (Complex Systems)") and Sakana AI's X post ([Sakana project page](https://pub.sakana.ai/picbreeder-vlm/); [Sakana AI on X](https://x.com/SakanaAILabs/status/2079028703275974878)). The paper (verified; arXiv v1 1 April, v2 27 May 2026; 26 pages, 21 figures) replaces Picbreeder's human users with VLM agents, and compares the results against the historical human archive using "metrics of phylogenetic complexity and visual and semantic salience and novelty". It finds clear qualitative differences: the VLM agents make "smaller semantic leaps" and fixate more. It then tests three interventions (exploratory randomness in selection, behavioural variation between agents, memory), which partially close the gap ([arXiv 2605.23908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23908); [Sakana project page](https://pub.sakana.ai/picbreeder-vlm/)).
  - **EML:** Nasir, Li, James & Togelius, "Mortar: Evolving Mechanics for Automatic Game Design" (Wits co-author Steven James). It pairs QD with an LLM to evolve game mechanics, scored by whether synthesised games preserve a skill-based ordering of players; includes a user study (verified via search snippet of the arXiv record; arXiv 31 December 2025) ([arXiv 2601.00105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00105v1)). Also nominated: Koohy & Bayne, "Distributional Value Estimation Without Target Networks for Robust Quality-Diversity" (see the 3 October notes). The 2026 EML winner was not found.
  - **NE:** Shahabi Sani, Najiantabriz, Shafaei & Hougen, "Evolving Multi-Channel Confidence-Aware Activation Functions for Missing Data with Channel Propagation" (not RL). Outcome not found.
  - **GA:** Ganci & Correia, "Dropout-Inspired Strategies For Enhanced Exploration In Evolutionary Algorithms" (title only; GA was paired with THEORY). Outcome not found.
  - **BBSR:** Da Ros, Začiragić, Plaat, Bäck & van Stein, "Assessing Reproducibility in Evolutionary Computation: A Case Study using Human- and LLM-based Assessment"; Vermetten & Rook on Pareto-preserving transformations.
  - **RWA:** Shahrzad, Gajawelli, Maile, Saggar & Miikkulainen, "Evolution With Purpose: Hierarchy-Informed Optimization of Whole-Brain Models". **Won the RWA award** per Cognizant AI Lab (23 July 2026). HICO introduces parameters following the brain's functional hierarchy, giving models that are "more stable, generalize more effectively across individuals, and produce behaviorally meaningful predictions" ([Cognizant blog](https://www.cognizant.com/us/en/ai-lab/blog/gecco-best-paper-evolutionary-ai-whole-brain-modeling)). Also nominated in RWA: "From Prompts to Pareto Fronts: Using Code LMs as Semantic Genetic Operators for Compiler Bug Discovery".
- Award mechanics in 2026: nominations by track chairs; "the final choice will be made by GECCO attendees"; joint awards for BBSR-ENUM, CS-SI and GA-THEORY ([2026 nominations](https://gecco-2026.sigevo.org/Best+Paper+Nominations)).

### Inferences
- **Corrections to the 3 October notes and report:**
  - Nisioti et al. was a 2025 winner, not just a nominee.
  - The 2026 nominee list the notes used omitted the CS nominee (the Picbreeder study, which won), the GA nominee and the RWA nominees.
  - The notes' framing that "analysis or benchmark papers also succeed" is stronger than stated: the 2025 BBSR+ENUM winner was a benchmark-plus-finding paper, and the 2026 CS winner is an analysis paper with partly negative results.
- **What made the RL-adjacent winners stand out:**
  - **Scale or speed step change:** TensorNEAT, up to 500× faster.
  - **A learned model that spends evaluations wisely under uncertainty:** JEDi focuses ES evaluations using a learned behaviour-to-fitness map. Extract-QD improves QD in noisy or uncertain domains "at no additional evaluation cost".
  - **A surprising head-to-head finding against RL on purpose-built benchmarks:** Nisioti et al.
  - **A big-question, foundation-model-powered study with a human baseline and causal interventions:** the Picbreeder-VLM paper.
  - **Interpretability for control:** EML 2025.
  - None involved safety or world models.
- The two winning lineages closest to the student's unifying question ("learn about the dangerous part of the world while visiting it as little as possible") are JEDi and Extract-QD. Both are about allocating costly real evaluations under model uncertainty, and both come from Cully-linked groups whose work recurs among CS winners (2024, 2025). Framing the paper as "evolution that spends almost no real evaluations near danger, using a world model whose errors it measures and corrects" aligns it with what has already won.
- LLM and VLM work is rising among 2026 nominees: 4 of the 22 nominations involve LLMs or VLMs (CS Picbreeder-VLM, EML Mortar, BBSR reproducibility assessment, RWA code-LM operators), and the CS winner is one of them. "Wow" at GECCO 2026 meant asking a big question about open-endedness with frontier models. A safety-in-imagination paper does not need an LLM, but a clean, quotable headline finding is the common denominator: for example, "selection pressure turns a 45% false-safe predictor into X% real violations, and Y fixes it at Z real samples".
- Wits has recent proximity to these awards: Mortar (EML 2026 nominee) is co-authored by Steven James, who is also a co-author of the supervisor's RLC 2026 safe-RL paper.

### Gaps
- Official 2026 winners for EML, NE, GA+THEORY, BBSR+ENUM and the other tracks were not found. The official 2026 awards page is stale, and SIGEVOlution's Summer 2026 issue (per its index) did not appear to carry award results ([SIGEVO articles](https://sig.sigevo.org/articles)).
- Exact attendee vote counts and the reasons the committees or attendees chose the winners are not published; the "why they stood out" analysis is my inference from paper content.
- The GA 2026 nominee (dropout-inspired exploration) and the NE 2026 nominee were not read.
