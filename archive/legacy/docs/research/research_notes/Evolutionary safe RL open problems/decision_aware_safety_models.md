# Decision-aware safety models: the gap between prediction accuracy and selection quality

Scope note for the report writer. Research date 5 October 2026. Every paper below was checked on a primary page (arXiv abstract or HTML, PMLR, OpenReview/ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML virtual page, JAIR, IJCAI, DOI or institutional repository) unless marked UNVERIFIED. "Preprint" means no peer-reviewed venue was found on the primary page. Items already covered in the 3 October report (`reports/Evolutionary safe RL with world models.md`) are only re-cited where new detail was found; a list of what that report lacks or gets stale is at the end of Key Question 5.

The student's own result frames everything below: repairing the LeWM predictor raised one-step failure detection from 11.3% to 67.7%, but the controller the repaired model selected failed in 3 of 24 real rollouts against 2 of 24 for the starting controller. Computed here (not from a source): the Wilson 95% intervals are 4.3% to 31.0% and 2.3% to 25.8%, and Fisher's exact two-sided p = 1.0. The two controllers are statistically indistinguishable, and roughly 800 rollouts per arm would be needed to detect 8% against 12.5% at 80% power. So the honest statement is "better one-step detection did not produce measurably safer selection", not "it made selection worse".

## 1. Objective mismatch and decision-aware model learning: what is known, and has it been applied to safety or constraints?

### Takeaway
The mismatch between model-fitting loss and decision quality is established theory and practice (Lambert et al. 2020; value equivalence; VaGraM; MuZero; decision-focused learning), and 2024 to 2026 work has refined it (calibration of value-aware losses, a TMLR taxonomy, a June 2026 decision-centric evaluation ladder for world models, plan-aware cost learning on LeWM itself). Application to safety is thin: decision-focused learning now handles predicted constraint parameters (NeurIPS 2025), but no paper found trains a world model or cost predictor so that constraint-satisfaction decisions, rather than reward decisions, come out right.

### Cited Findings
**Foundations (older, cited only as needed)**
- Lambert, Amos, Yadan, Calandra, "Objective Mismatch in Model-based Reinforcement Learning", L4DC 2020, PMLR 120:761-770 (peer-reviewed; arXiv v1 11 Feb 2020). Core claim: "the likelihood of one-step ahead predictions is not always correlated with control performance"; models "effective for a specific task do not necessarily need to be globally accurate"; they propose only "an initial method" of re-weighting dynamics-model training. [arXiv 2002.04523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04523)
- Grimm, Barreto, Singh, Silver, "The Value Equivalence Principle for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning", NeurIPS 2020 (peer-reviewed). Two models are value equivalent with respect to a set of policies and a set of functions if they yield the same Bellman updates; as the policy and function sets grow, the class of value-equivalent models shrinks until it collapses to the true model. The principle is stated to underlie MuZero, the Predictron, VPN and TreeQN. [arXiv 2011.03506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506); [NeurIPS page](https://nips.cc/virtual/2020/poster/18779)
- Grimm et al., "Proper Value Equivalence", NeurIPS 2021 spotlight (peer-reviewed). [arXiv 2106.10316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10316)
- He, Moerland, Oliehoek, "What model does MuZero learn?", ECAI 2024 (peer-reviewed per the author's page). A simplified MuZero loss upper-bounds the proper value-equivalence loss, but "MuZero's learned model cannot effectively generalize to evaluate unseen policies". [arXiv 2306.00840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00840); [ECAI PDF](https://fransoliehoek.net/docs/He24ECAI.pdf)
- Voelcker, Liao, Garg, Farahmand, "Value Gradient weighted Model-Based Reinforcement Learning" (VaGraM), ICLR 2022 spotlight (peer-reviewed). The model influences a Dyna agent only through its value function, so model error matters only where it changes value predictions; VaGraM weights model error by the value gradient and is more robust than maximum likelihood under small model capacity and distracting state dimensions on MuJoCo. [arXiv 2204.01464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01464); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6704)
- Elmachtoub and Grigas, "Smart 'Predict, then Optimize'", Management Science 68(1):9-26 (peer-reviewed; arXiv 2017). SPO loss measures decision error induced by a prediction; SPO+ is a convex, statistically consistent surrogate; gains are largest when the prediction model is misspecified. [arXiv 1710.08005](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005)
- Mandi, Kotary, Berden, Mulamba, Bucarey, Guns, Fioretto, "Decision-Focused Learning: Foundations, State of the Art, Benchmark and Future Opportunities", JAIR 2024 (peer-reviewed survey; 11 methods on 7 problems; code at PredOpt). [JAIR](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/15320); [arXiv 2307.13565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13565). (Author list beyond Mandi taken from the arXiv record title search; only "Mandi et al." was confirmed in the fetched snippet.)

**2024 to 2026 follow-ups**
- Wei, Lambert, McDonald, Garcia, Ma (author list per arXiv; only "Wei et al." confirmed in fetched snippet), "A Unified View on Solving Objective Mismatch in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning", TMLR 2024 (peer-reviewed). Surveys solution categories (value-aware, policy-aware, control-as-inference, etc.) and proposes a taxonomy; traces "model predictive accuracy is often not correlated with action quality" to the mismatch. [arXiv 2310.06253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06253); [TMLR via mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2024/wei2024tmlr-unified)
- Voelcker, Pedan, Ahmadian, Abachi, Gilitschenski, Farahmand, "Calibrated Value-Aware Model Learning with Probabilistic Environment Models", ICML 2025, PMLR 267:61745-61768 (peer-reviewed). Value-aware losses "as normally used, are uncalibrated surrogate losses that do not always recover the correct model and value function"; corrections are proposed; deterministic latent models can suffice for value prediction but calibrated stochastic models remain advantageous. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/voelcker25a.html)
- Yu (corresponding), Zhang, Sheng, Ren, Lin (Nanjing University), "How Should World Models Be Evaluated for Embodied Decision-Making? A Decision-Making-Centric Position", arXiv 2606.15032 v2, 28 June 2026 (preprint). Argues the generative world-model literature has "re-encountered objective mismatch without adopting the tools developed to address it". Proposes an L0 to L7 evidence ladder (L1 logged-future prediction; L5 reward/value/outcome fidelity; L6 fixed-policy ranking agreement; L7 planning/optimisation utility) with metrics including pairwise ranking accuracy (PRA), mean maximum rank violation (MMRV) and an exploitability gap XGap = Ĵ_model(π̂) − J_env(π̂) for the optimiser's chosen policy. Survey finding: fixed-policy ranking agreement appears in only about twelve works and "we find essentially no work in these tables that reports an exploitability gap". Safety appears only as an open problem ("Safety requires worst-case evaluation") and as constraints inside the task-feature map. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)
- Bai and Xiong, "Temporal-Distance JEPA: Plan-Aware Representation Learning for Latent World Model Predictive Control", arXiv 2607.25337 (v1 28 Jul, v2 29 Jul 2026; preprint). Keeps the LeWM encoder-predictor and mines a directed temporal cost with same-trajectory positives and cross-trajectory negatives plus a rollout-consistency term matched to the planner horizon; Two-Room 100.0% vs LeWM 97.4%, OGB-Cube +14.2 points. No ranking-accuracy metric reported; no safety content. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25337)
- Huang, Ding, Wan, Liu, Zhang, Zhang, Chen, Zhang, Huang, "Traj-LeWM: Path-Aware World-Model Planning via Latent Trajectory Cost", arXiv 2608.14125, 14 Aug 2026 (preprint). Learns a latent trajectory cost by "trajectory-preference supervision", combined with endpoint distance "into candidate ranking"; Push-T +3, OGBench-Cube +14, Reacher +7, Two-Room +7 points over LeWM. No safety content. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14125)
- Agrawal, Kalra, Parthasarathy, LeCun, Bounou, Izmailov, Goldblum, "Closing the Train-Test Gap in World Models for Gradient-Based Planning", arXiv 2512.09929 (v1 10 Dec 2025, revised 1 Oct 2026; preprint). Online World Modelling fine-tunes DINO-WM on planner-produced action sequences relabelled by the true simulator (DAgger for world models); Adversarial World Modelling uses FGSM perturbations of latents and actions. With AWM, success rises PushT 76% to 92%, PointMaze 68% to 98%, Wall 80% to 96%, Reacher 42% to 76%, at an order of magnitude less wall-clock than CEM. No ranking metric. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09929)

**Application to constraints and safety**
- Mandi, Defresne, Berden, Guns, "Feasibility-Aware Decision-Focused Learning for Predicting Parameters in the Constraints", NeurIPS 2025 poster (peer-reviewed; arXiv 6 Oct 2025). Two MLE-based losses, one penalising infeasibility and one penalising suboptimality, with "a tunable parameter to balance suboptimality and feasibility"; generic constrained optimisation, not only LP/ILP. [arXiv 2510.04951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04951); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116915)
- Li, Ruan, Geng, "Safe Reinforcement Learning with Preference-based Constraint Inference" (PbCRL), ICML 2026 (accepted per arXiv comment; arXiv 2603.23565, 24 Mar 2026). Learns costs from pairwise preferences and finds that "popular Bradley-Terry (BT) models fail to capture the asymmetric, heavy-tailed nature of safety costs, resulting in risk underestimation"; adds a dead-zone mechanism. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23565)
- A targeted search for decision-aware or value-equivalent model learning in constrained MDPs ("value-equivalent model constrained MDP cost", "decision-aware model learning safe RL") returned no paper that trains the dynamics or cost model by its effect on constraint decisions. [search results included He et al. and constrained MCTS discussion only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00840)

### Inferences
- What is solved: the general diagnosis (fit loss ≠ decision loss), value-weighted and value-equivalent losses for reward, decision-focused losses for combinatorial objectives, and now feasibility-aware losses for predicted constraint parameters in static optimisation.
- Closest work to the student's problem: Yu et al. 2606.15032 (names XGap and ranking agreement as the right evidence for world models), Mandi et al. NeurIPS 2025 (feasibility-aware decision loss), and the plan-aware LeWM papers (TD-JEPA, Traj-LeWM), which already show that adding a ranking-style signal to LeWM improves goal-cost planning.
- Genuinely open: a value-equivalence or decision-focused objective for the cost side of a CMDP, where the decision is "is this policy feasible" and the relevant error is a false-safe ranking at the feasibility boundary. The student's 11.3% to 67.7% repair is an L1-level (one-step, logged-data) improvement; the decision lives at L6/L7. The ladder predicts exactly the observed non-transfer.
- Value equivalence is defined relative to a policy set Π. An evolutionary population is a concrete, changing Π. He et al.'s finding that MuZero-style models do not generalise to unseen policies implies a decision-aware safety model must be refitted as the population moves, which is what EC "evolution control" already does for surrogates (Key Question 3).
- PbCRL is a caution for any pure ranking loss on safety: Bradley-Terry ranking underestimates heavy-tailed risk, so a ranking loss should be paired with a calibrated absolute term (or a lexicographic feasibility label) rather than replace it.
- Walker2d LeWM relevance: the probes are a cost model sitting on a JEPA predictor; TD-JEPA and Traj-LeWM show decision-aware heads can be trained on LeWM cheaply. The missing piece is a cost head trained on policy-level failure orderings instead of frame-level labels.

### Gaps
- No primary source found applying VaGraM, value equivalence or MuZero-style losses to constraint costs or CMDPs. Absence is from searches, not proof.
- Full author lists for the Wei et al. TMLR paper and the Mandi et al. JAIR survey were not individually confirmed on the fetched pages.
- Policy-aware model learning (Abachi et al.), game-theoretic MBRL (Rajeswaran et al. 2020) and Ni et al.'s self-predictive RL analysis were not re-verified in this pass; they may be relevant to "fitting the model where the policy goes" and to JEPA-style self-prediction.

## 2. Ranking defects in world models, policy-ranking accuracy as a metric, and selection-aware calibration

### Takeaway
2026 has produced direct evidence that latent world models mis-rank candidates (ARC-Bench, ASAR) and a theory that exploitation, defined as a model reversing the true preference between two policies, is essentially unavoidable on large policy sets. Separately, a world-model-as-policy-evaluator literature (2025 to 2026) now reports ranking metrics (Pearson, MMRV) on small fixed sets of policies. What nobody measures is ranking accuracy on safety under optimisation pressure, that is, the exploitability gap for constraints on a population that an optimiser is actively steering.

### Cited Findings
**Ranking defects and exploitation**
- Zhang and Li, "ARC-Bench: Closed-Loop Replanning Masks Broken Action Ranking in Frozen JEPA World Models", arXiv 2609.05461 (preprint; 15 pages). Tests JEPA-WM, DINOv2-based and V-JEPA 1/2 (ViT-L/ViT-G) checkpoints; "on the official manipulation audits the top-scored candidate is almost always suboptimal, and the same inversion appears in the maze domains"; when replanning frequency is reduced, success "collapses in both a navigation and a manipulation domain". No safety constraints. Date: the fetched abstract page reported submission on 12 August 2026, which conflicts with a 2609 identifier; the 3 October report's "date mismatch" flag therefore stands. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05461)
- Li, Liu, Liu, Fang, "Action from Adjacent Set in Physical Space Outperforms the Best Prediction in World Models" (ASAR), arXiv 2607.23602 (v1 26 Jul 2026, v2 31 Aug 2026; preprint, cs.RO). Mechanism: "residual prediction error can give an infeasible sequence an anomalously low cost" as pools grow. Cube feasibility fell from 37.5% to 6.2% (position) and 34.4% to 3.1% (position and yaw) as proposals went from 72 to 288. Remedy: reconstruct an action from an adjacent set of low-cost proposals sharing early actions; Kernel ASAR gained 18.7 to 28.0 points on 75 carry-and-release queries. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23602)
- Bhamidipaty, Whitammer, Abel, Kochenderfer, Ramamoorthy, "Imperfect World Models are Exploitable", arXiv 2605.15960 (v1 15 May, v2 18 May 2026; preprint). Defines a world model as exploitable if it "implies that one policy should be strictly preferred over another while the environment's true transition model implies the reverse"; proves exploitation is "essentially unavoidable on large policy sets"; unlike reward hacking, the conditions that guarantee unhackability on finite policy sets "have no counterpart that precludes exploitation"; derives a "safe horizon" within which a relaxed exploitation can be avoided. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)
- Bhamidipaty, Kochenderfer, Ramamoorthy, "RENEW: Towards Learning World Models and Repairing Model Exploitation from Preferences", arXiv 2607.14180, 15 Jul 2026 (preprint). Dynamics Learning from Human Feedback with "a Bradley-Terry preference loss over trajectory log-likelihoods under a learned dynamics model"; uses epistemic uncertainty "to focus finetuning where the model is most exploitable"; Jumanji and classic control. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14180)
- Raghavan and Singh, "Do Better Imagined Rollouts Mean Better Robot Control? A Controlled Study of World-Model Evaluation Under Feedback", arXiv 2609.02811, 2 Sep 2026 (preprint). Replay RMSE correlated with closed-loop error at Spearman ρ = 0.923, 20-step open-loop rollout error at ρ = 0.774; replay picked the best estimator in 19 of 24 conditions, rollout in 6. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02811) (already in the 3 October report; numbers reconfirmed.)

**Policy-ranking accuracy as a world-model metric**
- Fu, Norouzi, Nachum, Tucker, Wang, Novikov, Yang, Zhang, Chen, Kumar, Paduraru, Levine, Paine, "Benchmarks for Deep Off-Policy Evaluation" (DOPE), ICLR 2021 (peer-reviewed). Supplies policy collections for benchmarking OPE on high-dimensional control. [arXiv 2103.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16596). Its ranking metrics (absolute error, rank correlation, regret@k) are from my knowledge of the paper; the fetched abstract did not show them, so treat as UNVERIFIED in this pass.
- Jin, Zhang, Qin, Zhang, Yuan, Li, Liu, "Supervised Off-Policy Ranking" (SOPR), ICML 2022 (peer-reviewed; arXiv v1 3 Jul 2021). Learns a hierarchical-Transformer policy scorer with a pairwise ranking loss, trained on a set of policies with known real performance, because policy selection "requires ranking capabilities rather than precise performance estimates"; reports rank correlation, regret and stability. [arXiv 2107.01360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01360)
- Li, Zhu, Wen, Shen, Xu, "WorldEval: World Model as Real-World Robot Policies Evaluator", arXiv 2505.19017, 25 May 2025 (preprint). Claims strong correlation with real performance and that it "functions as a safety detector to prevent dangerous actions by newly developed robot models"; uses MMRV and Pearson r. Quantitative safety-detection numbers were not on the abstract page. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19017)

[arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00613); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008029)

[arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06949). dWorldEval, arXiv 2604.22152 (preprint): Pearson r ≈ 0.9. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22152v1). - Wan et al., "No Free Checker: A Survey of Verifiers for Robot Policies", arXiv 2609.09250 (8 Sep, revised 13 Sep 2026; preprint). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09250)

**Winner's curse and selection-aware calibration**
- Smith and Winkler, "The Optimizer's Curse: Skepticism and Postdecision Surprise in Decision Analysis", Management Science 52(3):311-322, 2006 (peer-reviewed; correction 2007 to Figure 3). Even unbiased estimates yield an expected disappointment for the selected alternative; Bayesian shrinkage is the proposed remedy. [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/798952)
- Jin and Ren, "Confidence on the Focal: Conformal Prediction with Selection-Conditional Coverage", JRSS Series B 2025 (peer-reviewed). Marginal conformal sets "may fail to provide valid coverage for the focal unit(s) due to selection bias", the winner's curse; gives finite-sample selection-conditional coverage for top-K, optimisation-based and conformal-p-value selection rules. [arXiv 2403.03868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03868)
- Bastani, Bastani, McLaughlin, "Beating the Winner's Curse via Inference-Aware Policy Optimization", arXiv 2510.18161 (20 Oct 2025, revised 7 Feb 2026; preprint). Optimises jointly for estimated improvement and the probability the improvement survives held-out significance testing; characterises the Pareto frontier; treatment-policy setting. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18161)

### Inferences
- What is solved: measuring fixed-policy ranking agreement (L6) for video world models on a handful to a few dozen policies; diagnosing action-ranking inversions in frozen JEPA models (ARC-Bench); showing goal-cost exploitation grows with candidate pool size (ASAR); a formal definition of exploitation as pairwise preference reversal.
- Closest work: Yu et al.'s XGap is the exact quantity the student measured (imagined-safe vs real-failing selected controller), but defined for return. Bhamidipaty et al. give theory that a CMA-ES search over a continuous policy space will find reversals. Jin and Ren give the statistical tool for calibrating a safety prediction conditional on being selected.
- Genuinely open: (a) XGap for constraints, as a function of selection pressure; (b) ranking agreement measured on the optimiser's own population rather than on a curated policy set (the evaluator literature ranks 3 to 17 policies; CMA-ES ranks hundreds per generation, all near the current mean, which is where ranking is hardest); (c) selection-conditional calibration of a learned safety predictor inside an evolutionary loop; (d) whether the safe horizon of Bhamidipaty et al. corresponds to the student's 0.8 s validated horizon.
- For a feasibility-first rule, only a subset of pairs matters: pairs that straddle the predicted feasibility boundary. A global metric such as one-step detection recall or overall Spearman ρ can improve while boundary-pair accuracy among selected elites does not. This is a plausible mechanism for the student's null result.
- Walker2d LeWM relevance: snapshot branching gives exact per-candidate failure labels for the whole population, so PRA, MMRV, regret@k and a constraint XGap can be computed per generation at known cost. The evaluator-world-model literature cannot do this cheaply because it relies on real robot trials.

### Gaps
- ARC-Bench and ASAR do not report safety-cost ranking; WorldEval and Veo mention safety detection but no predicted-vs-real unsafe-rate statistics were found on primary pages.
- MMRV is attributed in these papers to Li et al. 2024 (SIMPLER); that original was not fetched here.
- No source found that applies selection-conditional conformal methods (Jin and Ren) to a learned safety or cost model used for policy selection.
- The ARC-Bench submission date remains inconsistent between identifier and abstract page.

## 3. Surrogate-assisted evolution: ranking versus regression, quality by rank correlation, model management, constrained SAEAs, and whether the population is the training distribution

### Takeaway
Evolutionary computation settled the ranking-versus-regression question for comparison-based optimisers more than a decade ago: rank-SVM surrogates for CMA-ES (2010 to 2012), a rank-correlation acceptance test in lq-CMA-ES (2019), ranking-difference error and uncertainty-based pre-selection in DTS-CMA-ES, and a 2022 theorem that a Kendall-τ threshold close to 1 gives monotone improvement. Online SAEAs almost always fit the surrogate on the population's own recent evaluations, so "fit where selection happens" is standard EC practice. Constrained SAEAs with feasibility classifiers and constraint surrogates for CMA-ES also exist. None of this has been applied to a learned dynamics model plus safety probe that must rank controllers' episode-level failure probability.

### Cited Findings
**Rank-based surrogates for CMA-ES**
- Loshchilov, Schoenauer, Sebag, "Comparison-Based Optimizers Need Comparison-Based Surrogates", PPSN XI, 2010 (peer-reviewed). Rank-SVM surrogate inside CMA-ES, invariant to monotone fitness transformations, with the CMA covariance inside the Gaussian kernel so the model adapts "to the currently explored region". [EPFL Infoscience record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/5ac8c165-56c9-486a-bc02-2ba60d686f79/conferencedetails)
- Loshchilov, Schoenauer, Sebag, "Self-Adaptive Surrogate-Assisted Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy" (s*ACM-ES), GECCO 2012 (peer-reviewed). Adjusts surrogate lifelength and hyper-parameters online. [arXiv 1204.2356](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.2356)
- Hansen, "A Global Surrogate Assisted CMA-ES" (lq-CMA-ES), GECCO 2019 (peer-reviewed). Linear, diagonal-quadratic or full-quadratic model; "used as a surrogate fitness for CMA-ES if the rank correlation between true fitness and surrogate value of recently sampled data points is high; otherwise, further samples from the current population are successively added as data to the model". Implemented in pycma. [IP Paris record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/rank-based-linear-quadratic-surrogate-assisted-cma-es/); [DOI 10.1145/3321707.3321842](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3321707.3321842). The specific τ threshold was not confirmed in this pass.
- Bajer, Pitra, Repický, Holeňa, "Gaussian Process Surrogate Models for the CMA Evolution Strategy", Evolutionary Computation 27(4):665-697, 2019 (peer-reviewed). Benefits of GP uncertainty in choosing which points to evaluate truly (DTS-CMA-ES). [DOI 10.1162/evco_a_00244](https://doi.org/10.1162/evco_a_00244); [ASEP record](https://asep.lib.cas.cz/arl-cav/en/detail-cav_un_epca-0506867-Gaussian-Process-Surrogate-Models-for-the-CMAES/). Related: Pitra, Bajer, Holeňa, "Doubly Trained Evolution Control for the Surrogate CMA-ES", PPSN 2016 (peer-reviewed), and work measuring "the error between the predicted and genuine population ordering". [ASEP record](https://asep.lib.cas.cz/arl-cav/en/detail-cav_un_epca-0466878-Doubly-Trained-Evolution-Control-for-the-Surrogate-CMAES/); [arXiv 1709.10443](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.10443)
- Akimoto, "Monotone Improvement of Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms with a Surrogate Function", GECCO 2022 (peer-reviewed). If the population Kendall τ between surrogate and objective stays above a threshold sufficiently close to one, IGO (rank-μ CMA-ES instantiation) gives monotone decrease of expected objective value; analyses the acceptable threshold on convex quadratics and Pearson correlation as an alternative. [arXiv 2204.02638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02638); [ACM DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3512290.3528690)
- Dufossé and Hansen, "Augmented Lagrangian, penalty techniques and surrogate modeling for constrained optimization with CMA-ES", GECCO 2021 (peer-reviewed). [HAL](https://hal-udl.archives-ouvertes.fr/INRIA/hal-03196365v1)

**Evolution control and accuracy thresholds**
- Jin, Olhofer, Sendhoff, "A Framework for Evolutionary Optimisation with Approximate Fitness Functions", IEEE TEVC 6(5):481-494, 2002 (peer-reviewed): the origin of individual- and generation-based evolution control. [Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/a-framework-for-evolutionary-optimisation-with-approximate-fitnes)
- Jin, "Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation: Recent advances and future challenges", Swarm and Evolutionary Computation 1(2):61-70, 2011 (peer-reviewed). Covers SAEAs for expensive, dynamic, constrained and multimodal problems. [Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2978603)
- Hanawa, Harada, Miura, "Impact of Surrogate Model Accuracy on Performance and Model Management Strategy in Surrogate-Assisted Evolutionary Algorithms", arXiv 2503.00844, 2 Mar 2025 (preprint). Using a pseudo-surrogate with adjustable accuracy: pre-selection improves steadily with accuracy; individual- and generation-based control are robust only "when the accuracy surpasses a certain threshold"; generation-based wins over a broad range, individual-based at low accuracy, pre-selection at high accuracy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00844)

**Classification and relation surrogates (2024 to 2026)**
- Relation (pairwise) surrogates recast learning as binary classification of which of two solutions is better; examples: arXiv 2504.19411 (comparison-relationship surrogate for expensive multi-objective optimisation, April 2025, preprint) and arXiv 2407.15600 (pairwise-relation-assisted multi-objective evolutionary NAS, preprint). Search-result text describes three paradigms: regression, classification and relation modelling. [arXiv 2504.19411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19411); [arXiv 2407.15600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15600)

### Inferences
- What is solved: ranking-based surrogate training and acceptance for comparison-based ES; rank correlation as the surrogate-quality measure; theory linking a τ threshold to monotone improvement; strategy choice by accuracy level; constraint surrogates and feasibility classifiers for numeric problems.
- Population as training distribution: yes, routinely. lq-CMA-ES adds points from the current population until the rank test passes; DTS-CMA-ES retrains after evaluating part of the current population; ACM-ES puts the CMA covariance in its kernel; the 2025 ERL classifier trains on sampled population policies. In DFL, the solution cache is the same idea. The novelty for the student is not "train on the population" but what is trained: a shared dynamics model plus safety probe whose errors are correlated across all candidates, with labels at the level of whole-policy failure probability.
- Akimoto's result implies a quantitative test the student can run: compute population Kendall τ between probe-predicted and snapshot-verified failure ordering each generation. If τ is far below 1, CMA-ES has no guarantee of improving the true safety objective, regardless of one-step detection accuracy.
- Hanawa et al. suggest that the student's repaired model may still be below the accuracy threshold at which generation-based use of the model helps, and that pre-selection (screen with the model, verify the top few truly) degrades most gracefully.
- Runarsson 2004 is the most direct EC precedent for "ranking-aware surrogate plus constraint handling by stochastic ranking", which aligns with the student's planned stochastic-ranking arm.

### Gaps
- No SAEA paper found where the surrogate is a learned dynamics model shared across candidates (as opposed to a direct genotype-to-fitness model) and quality is measured by ranking of constraint violation.
- The lq-CMA-ES acceptance threshold and the exact DTS-CMA-ES "ranking difference error" definition were not confirmed on primary text in this pass.
- The 2026 GECCO "Surrogate-Assisted Evolutionary Multi-Agent RL with Adaptive Fitness Evaluation" paper remained unreadable; the exact-title search returned only older ERL surrogate papers (for example SAC-ERL, [arXiv 2201.00129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00129)).

## 4. Has anyone trained a world model or safety predictor to rank candidate controllers' safety correctly, especially with the population as the training signal?

### Takeaway
No. The closest pieces exist separately: SOPR trains a policy scorer with a pairwise ranking loss on policies of known performance; RaM trains ranking surrogates for offline design selection; Mandi et al. train decision models with ranking losses over a cache of candidate solutions; Online World Modelling fine-tunes a DINO-WM on planner-chosen actions relabelled by the simulator; RENEW repairs exploitation with preference losses; TD-JEPA and Traj-LeWM add ranking-style goal costs to LeWM; ERL classifiers pre-select population policies. None targets safety or constraint violation, and none evaluates the model by the safety of what selection then picks.

### Cited Findings
- SOPR: pairwise ranking loss over policies with known real performance; metrics rank correlation and regret (ICML 2022). [arXiv 2107.01360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01360)
- RaM: learning-to-rank surrogate and AUPCC for selecting top designs (ICLR 2025). [arXiv 2410.11502](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11502v2)
- Mandi et al. 2022 and Mulamba et al. 2021: ranking and contrastive losses over a growing cache of candidate solutions. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/mandi22a.html); [IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/390)
- Agrawal et al. (OWM/AWM): world model fine-tuned on trajectories "produced by the planner but corrected by true simulator rollouts"; requires simulator access, which the authors call impractical for real-world settings. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09929)
- Zhang et al., "JEPA-TTT: Persistent Test-Time Training of Latent World Models for Planning under Dynamics Shifts", World Models in Physical AI Workshop at NeurIPS 2026 (accepted workshop paper; arXiv 2610.00722, 30 Sep 2026). Adapts only the latent predictor on the agent's own deployment transitions (encoder and reward head frozen); after 500 test episodes, latent prediction error falls 83% and planning improves 153% over the frozen JEPA on eight dynamics shifts in four environments. No safety or ranking content. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00722) (resolves the 3 October report's open item on JEPA-TTT: not a penalty-CEM or safety competitor.)
- RENEW: preference loss over trajectory likelihoods, focused by epistemic uncertainty where the model is "most exploitable". [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14180)
- Li et al. 2025 ERL classifier surrogate trained on population policies. [arXiv 2505.19423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19423)
- PbCRL: preference-learned costs underestimate heavy-tailed safety risk under Bradley-Terry. [arXiv 2603.23565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23565)
- Yu et al.: essentially no world-model paper reports an exploitability gap; safety needs worst-case evaluation. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)
- Bhamidipaty et al.: exploitation (pairwise preference reversal) essentially unavoidable on large policy sets. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)

### Inferences
- The open problem, stated precisely: train and evaluate a world model plus safety predictor by the correctness of its safety ordering over the candidates an optimiser is actually choosing between, with labels obtained from cheap exact ground truth, and report the constraint exploitability gap of the selected controller. This combines L6 ranking agreement and L7 XGap (Yu et al.) on the cost side, with population-sourced labels (SAEA practice) and decision-focused ranking losses (Mandi et al.; RaM).
- The student's assets make this unusually cheap: exact MuJoCo snapshot branching is a free, noise-controlled "true simulator relabelling" step, which OWM needs and real-robot evaluator papers lack.
- A defensible design: each generation, verify a small top-k plus boundary sample of the population through snapshots; add these as labelled pairs to a ranking loss on the probe (and optionally the predictor); compare at matched real-step budget against (i) the existing BCE/MSE repair, (ii) no repair with top-k real re-evaluation only, and (iii) a calibrated absolute loss plus ranking loss (to answer PbCRL's tail-risk concern). Report per-generation Kendall τ on failure ordering, boundary-pair accuracy, false-safe rate among elites, and constraint XGap.
- Safety-specific twist not present in the reward-ranking literature: under Deb's feasibility rule, only orderings across the predicted feasibility boundary affect selection, so the training signal should concentrate on near-boundary pairs. This is a hard-negative mining argument; no source found that makes it for safety.
- Power: any claim about selected-controller safety needs hundreds of real rollouts per arm or paired snapshot comparisons, given the 2 of 24 versus 3 of 24 result.

### Gaps
- WoVR ("World Models as Reliable Simulators for Post-Training VLA Policies with RL", arXiv 2602.13977) appeared in search text as refining the world model with rollouts of the evolved policy (PACE); not verified on the primary page.
- No paper found on safety-ordering metrics (for example Kendall τ on P(violation)) for any learned world model.
- No GECCO, PPSN, TEVC or Evolutionary Computation journal paper found in 2024 to 2026 that measures a surrogate's ranking accuracy on constraint violation of RL policies.

## 5. Where could an evolutionary method uniquely contribute?

### Takeaway
Evolution supplies three things decision-aware ML lacks for this problem: a concrete, moving policy set on which to define value or cost equivalence; mature, theory-backed machinery for spending scarce true evaluations to keep a surrogate's ranking accurate where selection happens (evolution control, rank-correlation acceptance, pre-selection); and rank-based selection operators whose invariance means only the model's ordering matters. A GECCO paper can therefore reframe "repair the predictor" as "manage the safety surrogate by its ranking on the population", and test it with exact ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- Rank-based CMA-ES needs only orderings; comparison-based surrogates preserve invariance to monotone transformations. [Loshchilov et al. 2010](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/5ac8c165-56c9-486a-bc02-2ba60d686f79/conferencedetails)
- Kendall-τ threshold near 1 gives monotone improvement for surrogate-assisted IGO/CMA-ES. [Akimoto 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02638)
- lq-CMA-ES grows the model with current-population samples until rank agreement is high. [Hansen 2019](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/rank-based-linear-quadratic-surrogate-assisted-cma-es/)
- Model management choice depends on surrogate accuracy thresholds. [Hanawa et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00844)
- Value equivalence is relative to a policy set; models trained on one set fail on unseen policies. [Grimm et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506); [He et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00840)
- Approximate ranking with constraint surrogates under stochastic ranking. [Runarsson 2004](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-30217-9_41)
- Exploitation is unavoidable on large policy sets, with a safe horizon as mitigation. [Bhamidipaty et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)
- Decision-centric world-model evaluation calls for ranking agreement and XGap. [Yu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15032)

### Inferences
- Contribution 1 (diagnostic, cheapest, thesis-safe): per-generation safety-ranking audit of LeWM plus probes on the CMA-ES population: Kendall τ and boundary-pair accuracy of predicted vs snapshot-verified failure, elite false-safe rate, constraint XGap of the selected controller. This is the cost-side analogue of ARC-Bench and ASAR, and fills the "essentially no XGap" gap Yu et al. report. It also explains the student's null result in L1-vs-L7 terms.
- Contribution 2 (method): "safety surrogate evolution control". Use a rank-correlation acceptance test (lq-CMA-ES style) on the safety ordering, with snapshot verification of a top-k plus boundary sample each generation; feed verified pairs into a ranking loss on the probe. Compare against BCE/MSE repair at a matched real-step budget. Akimoto's threshold and Hanawa's strategy thresholds give pre-registered hypotheses.
- Contribution 3 (bridge to ROSARL): under rank-based selection, a ROSARL-style return-range penalty acts like a feasibility-first rule (3 October report). The same analysis says such rules only care about boundary orderings, which ties the supervisor's penalty to the ranking-surrogate requirement: a predictor need only rank correctly across the feasibility boundary, not predict costs accurately.
- Contribution 4 (stretch): a QD archive as a diverse policy set Π for training a cost-equivalent model (proper value equivalence over many policies), testing whether archive diversity improves safety ranking on unseen elites, which He et al.'s result predicts it should.
- Unifying question fit ("learn about the dangerous part of the world while visiting it as little as possible"): EC's evolution control is a budgeted active-learning policy for exactly that. Snapshot branching makes each "visit" a controlled, counted real evaluation.

#### Where the 3 October report is stale or incomplete
- It does not cite Yu et al. 2606.15032 (June 2026), which names policy-ranking agreement and the exploitability gap as the missing world-model evidence and reports that essentially no paper measures XGap. This strengthens its novelty claim and supplies vocabulary (L0 to L7, PRA, MMRV, XGap).
- It does not cite the exploitation theory and repair line (Bhamidipaty et al. 2605.15960, May 2026; RENEW 2607.14180, July 2026).
- It treats the remedy space as acting on the selection operator; it lacks the decision-aware model-training alternatives that now exist for LeWM-like models (OWM/AWM revised 1 Oct 2026; TD-JEPA July 2026; Traj-LeWM August 2026) and in DFL (Mandi et al. ICML 2022; feasibility-aware DFL, NeurIPS 2025) and offline MBO (RaM, ICLR 2025).
- It lacks the EC surrogate-quality theory (Akimoto 2022), the 2025 accuracy-threshold study (Hanawa et al.), and Runarsson's 2004 constrained approximate ranking, all of which turn "top-k real re-evaluation" into a principled evolution-control arm.
- Open item resolved: JEPA-TTT (2610.00722) is a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper on test-time adaptation of the predictor with no safety or penalty-CEM content.
- Open item still unresolved: ARC-Bench's date (abstract page reports 12 August 2026 against a 2609 identifier).
- Its H1 (elite false-safe rate exceeds the unconditional rate) is the optimiser's curse (Smith and Winkler 2006) applied to constraints; it could cite Jin and Ren (JRSS-B 2025) for a selection-conditional calibration arm.

### Gaps
