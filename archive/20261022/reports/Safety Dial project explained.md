# Steering Frozen Robot Policies Toward Safety

**Sachin Mohan's project asks whether one small learned controller can help several already-trained robot policies finish manipulation tasks safely more often, without retraining those large policies and without receiving more simulator experience than competing methods.** The simplest picture is a skilled but inconsistent chef, a hygiene inspector with a clipboard, and a small recipe card that nudges the chef's private dice before each choice. The chef is a frozen vision-language-action policy, the inspector is ManiGuard's passive safety monitor, the dice are the policy's native random sampling noise, and the recipe card is the compact controller. Evolution tries recipe cards, keeps promising ones and mutates them, but the scientific burden is much higher than showing that one evolved card works once. The study must establish that steering helps, that changing the steering with the scene and instruction helps, that evolution beats equally funded fixed rules, random search and ordinary learning, and that the same controller transfers. **As of the latest 20 October 2026 report, the mechanism and its bounded influence are implemented, while the full Jar comparison is still running; no physical safe-completion, evolutionary or cross-family advantage has yet been established.** A new world model, an action-changing safety filter and ROSARL are research context rather than components of this implementation. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md); [repository authority](../AGENTS.md))

## The project searches for a better choice, not a new brain

Start at the top. The broad research area is safe AI through evolutionary algorithms: can black-box search help an intelligent system achieve its task while respecting safety requirements, especially when it is difficult to hand-design a perfect reward or differentiate through the whole world? Sachin's current question is a deliberately narrower, testable version of that theme. He is not trying to solve every form of robot safety, prove a formal guarantee, or build a universal robot. He is asking whether a **small reusable steering rule can select better behavior that is already available inside frozen robot policies**. ([Allocated topic](../docs/allocatedTopic.md); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

Imagine several specialist apprentice chefs. One specializes in closing and moving jars, another in lids, another in clutter, and so on. Each chef can see the workspace, read the order and produce a short coordinated sequence of movements. Each has already been trained, so the project does not rewrite the chef's brain. Sometimes, however, the chef chooses a bad approach, loses the object, breaks a physical rule or simply runs out of time. ManiGuard supplies these specialist policies, their simulated workspaces and physical safety specifications. Its families include Jar, Lid, Stack, Clutter, Cabinet and Dusty, with different task sequences and violations. For Jar, for example, merely moving the jar to a goal is not enough if the requested sequence required closing it first and keeping it closed while carrying. This distinction matters because an earlier run reached the benchmark's spatial goal with an open jar. ([Requested-sequence contract](../docs/research/research_notes/ManiGuard%20phase2%20completion%20contract.md); [six-hypothesis study](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20six-hypothesis%20study.md))

The inspector in the analogy is easy to misunderstand. **The ManiGuard monitor watches what physically happens and labels temporal safety violations, but it does not grab the robot's arm, block a command or repair an action.** The current controller also does not receive the monitor state as live input. The monitor's verdict is evidence used after an episode to assess a candidate controller. Thus the current question is about learning better execution under measured constraints, not continuing a task after a learned safety filter has intervened. ([Initial adapter screens](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20initial%20adapter%20screens.md); [current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

Why touch the policy's dice? A flow-based action policy begins generation from Gaussian noise and gradually transforms that noisy starting point into a structured chunk of actions. Different starting samples can lead to different approaches, contacts or grasps. The controller makes a small structured change to that **internal starting sample before the policy finishes deciding what to do**. It is not random shaking added to the physical robot, and it is not a late clip applied after the policy has already chosen its movement. A useful analogy is gently biasing the rough sketch from which the chef plans a maneuver, while leaving the chef responsible for turning the sketch into a coordinated movement. The hypothesis is that this earlier intervention can expose useful alternatives while preserving more of the large policy's learned coordination. That preservation, like the usefulness of the alternatives, remains a hypothesis. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [policy wrapper](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent_policy.py))

This choice follows earlier negative and corrective evidence. A six-hypothesis ManiGuard study spent 196 engineering episodes on replanning, motion moderation, prerequisite guards, release delay, retry and hand-back without establishing a repeatable safe-completion repair. A later trace diagnostic found that a uniform motion cap changed only 55 of 18,657 recorded controls and that a feedback trigger was eligible on only 3 of 482 prefixes. An earlier literal ManiGuard dial also lost its small protected comparison: the evolved endpoint produced 4 safe completions and 2 violations in 8 episodes, while identity produced 5 safe completions and no violations. These results did not prove that evolution is useless. They showed that **an optimizer cannot rescue a control lever that rarely activates or lacks useful behavioral authority**, and they motivated intervening inside action generation instead. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [earlier dial pilot](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20evolutionary%20safety%20dial%20pilot.md))

The name “Safety Dial” therefore needs care. It does not mean there is a single setting that moves monotonically from risky to safe. The present controller has 176 weights and changes its output with the cameras, instruction, robot state and recent history. Its desired outcome is not caution by itself. A robot that never moves can avoid many violations while accomplishing nothing. The desired outcome is **requested safe completion: finish the requested sequence and incur no physical violation**. ([Current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

## Two loops turn observations into evolutionary evidence

At secondary-school level, the system works like this. The simulator provides two camera views and the robot's measured state. The policy also receives a language instruction such as a request to close and move a jar. The small controller looks at compact features of those inputs and recent executed motion, returns four steering numbers, and uses them to nudge the policy's internal noise. The frozen policy converts the modified noise, images and instruction into a short action sequence. The robot executes only the first part, observes the new situation, and asks again. Meanwhile the passive monitor records violations. After the full episode, completion and violation labels are used to judge the controller. ([Runtime implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent_runtime.py); [controller implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent.py))

This creates two different feedback loops. The **fast loop** runs within an episode:

`cameras + instruction + robot state + executed history -> 44 features -> four steering values -> modified native noise -> frozen policy -> action chunk -> executed prefix -> new observation`

The **slow loop** runs across episodes:

`completion and violation outcomes -> candidate score -> retain and mutate, or resample -> controller for future episodes`

Safety information travels through the slow evaluation loop. It does not enter the deployed controller as an immediate warning signal. This boundary is scientifically important: allowing one method to see live monitor state while another cannot would give it a different information advantage and turn the project into a different form of feedback control. ([Current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md); [repository controls](../AGENTS.md))

At undergraduate level, let \(\phi_t\) be the 44-dimensional causal feature vector available before policy query \(t\). Let \(W\) be the learned 4 by 44 matrix, \(B\) the fixed four-direction basis in sampling space, \(\epsilon_t\) the policy's ordinary Gaussian noise, and \(\alpha\) the maximum steering scale. The implemented controller forms

`z'_t = epsilon_t + alpha B tanh(W phi_t)`, with `alpha = 0.35` in the pilot.

The hyperbolic tangent bounds each of the four controller outputs. The basis maps them onto the seven arm-supported model coordinates and repeats the offset across the policy's 16-position action chunk. The other input-noise coordinates retain their native randomness. The frozen policy then decodes a 16 by 32 model-space chunk, although the physical robot command has only seven arm joint targets plus one gripper command. Those two spaces are related by the policy, but they are not the same thing. Model coupling can also cause an arm-supported input perturbation to affect other decoded outputs. ([Controller implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent.py); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

The 44 inputs combine normalized joint and finger state, the last executed arm action relative to current joints, recent end-effector motion, a constant feature, and frozen projections of both real camera images and the actual instruction. Family identity, task identity, goal state and monitor state are excluded. This makes the architecture general in intent: the same matrix must infer an appropriate response from observations and language rather than read a label saying “Jar.” The current pilot executes an eight-action prefix from each generated chunk before querying again, and it records executed-action history because that is what physically caused the next observation. ([Feature implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent.py); [runtime implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent_runtime.py); [current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

“Frozen” is a strict experimental contract, not just a casual description. The large family-specific pi0.5 weights, preprocessing, output transforms and normal query cadence remain unchanged. At \(W=0\), the implementation calls the untouched native inference route so the baseline retains its usual noise distribution, random-number advancement and numerical path. Passing an all-zero noise tensor would create a different policy and would not be a valid identity condition. The intended long-term test pairs one shared controller with each family's own separately fine-tuned frozen checkpoint. It does not claim that a single foundation checkpoint can solve a wholly unseen family. ([Policy wrapper](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent_policy.py); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

## Five comparisons separate adaptation from evolution

Evolution changes only \(W\), not the large policy, monitor, cameras or task definitions. In the current pilot, four controller candidates are evaluated on physical cases. The evolutionary arm keeps its best current candidate and produces nearby Gaussian mutations from its best parents. The matched random-search arm also keeps a best candidate, but its other candidates are fresh uniform samples from the same bounded weight domain. Both arms begin from the same four initial controllers and receive the same episode information. This is a small evolutionary process: variation produces descendants, episode outcomes determine fitness, and selection retains candidates. Its proposed advantage is that nearby controllers may produce partly heritable behavioral improvements, allowing selection and mutation to refine a useful tendency rather than repeatedly starting over. Whether the landscape actually has that property is one of the questions being tested. ([Candidate search implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent.py); [current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

The pilot selection score is `int(requested safe completion) - 2 * int(raw violation)`. A safe requested completion scores 1, a safe unfinished episode scores 0, and any violating episode scores -2, even if the benchmark goal was reached. This scalar helps rank candidates, but it is not an adequate scientific result. It cannot preserve the scientific difference between an unsafe completion and a safe timeout. Every report must therefore preserve the four outcome cells: **safe complete, unsafe complete, safe unfinished and unsafe unfinished**. It must also retain requested-sequence completion so that a controller cannot exploit a goal condition while skipping a prerequisite such as jar closure. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [requested-sequence contract](../docs/research/research_notes/ManiGuard%20phase2%20completion%20contract.md))

The required comparisons form a causal ladder rather than a leaderboard:

| Comparison | Plain-language question | Claim it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native policy versus any steering | Does this interface help at all? | The frozen policy contains useful alternatives reachable through this steering interface. |
| Tuned fixed bias versus conditional controller | Does looking at the scene and instruction matter, or is one constant correction enough? | Observation and instruction conditioning adds value beyond a simple reusable rule. |
| Matched random search versus evolution | Do retention, ancestry and local mutation help beyond trying the same number of controllers? | The evolutionary search process uses the physical budget more effectively than random sampling. |
| Ordinary learning versus evolution | Is evolution preferable to a competent learner with the same observations and output authority? | A practical evolutionary advantage, rather than an optimizer substitution. |
| New tasks and an adaptation-held-out family | Does the same frozen controller keep working outside its adaptation cases? | Within-family reuse and, at the strongest level, cross-family adapter transfer. |

This ladder is necessary because earlier UNISafe work produced a powerful warning. On 64 reserved starts, native, fixed hold, random search, policy gradient and evolution completed 2, 55, 56, 54 and 54 tasks respectively. Evolution made a large improvement over native behavior, but a simple fixed grasp-maintenance rule and random search matched or beat it, and evolution recorded 8 violating starts against native's 6. The result supported a behavioral repair, not evolutionary superiority or safer behavior. The lesson carried into the present design is to ask what changed and to keep the simplest serious explanation in the comparison. ([UNISafe adaptation study](../docs/research/reports/UNISafe%20grasp%20maintenance%20and%20evolutionary%20adaptation.md); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

Fairness also requires matching costs, not merely saying every method received the same number of episodes. One controller can time out after 2,000 controls while another terminates early, so equal episode counts can mean unequal physical experience. The study must charge every adaptation episode, discarded candidate, elite reevaluation, interrupted attempt, decoder probe and training datum. It should compare learning curves at common executed-control counts and report policy or model queries and elapsed time separately. Information, features, output domain, tuning effort and finalist protection must also be matched. Giving ordinary learning a privileged dense reward would be a different system comparison, just as denying it the semantic features used by evolution would be unfair in the opposite direction. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [repository controls](../AGENTS.md))

The frozen minimum pilot plans 60 valid full episodes with no more than four infrastructure replacements. Four development episodes check integration and capacity. Eight shared initialization episodes evaluate four initial controllers on two training cases. Evolution and random search then receive 16 descendant episodes each. Finally, the native policy, selected fixed bias, evolutionary finalist and random-search finalist receive 16 independent checking episodes, four per condition. Timeouts, weak outcomes and violations are valid evidence, not replaceable failures. The finalists must be sealed before independent checking so evaluation does not quietly become more training. Four checking episodes per condition and one optimizer seed make this a screening pilot, not a powered superiority study. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

Even a violation-free condition cannot be called safe in an absolute sense. If zero violations are observed in \(n\) independent episodes, a rough 95 percent upper bound on the true violation rate is \(3/n\), not zero. With only four independent checking episodes, zero observed violations would still leave an extremely broad bound. Repeated frames from one trajectory and variants of one base scene are not independent safety trials. A paper-level study therefore needs replicated optimizer runs, clustering by base task, an effect regarded as meaningful, a prospectively declared safety margin and enough independent evidence. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [repository controls](../AGENTS.md))

## Only bounded steering authority is established so far

The status of each claim is easiest to understand by refusing to collapse “built,” “tested” and “works” into one word.

| Evidence status on 20 October 2026 | What is supported | What is not supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Established implementation fact** | The 4 by 44 controller, semantic and robot-history features, explicit-noise policy path, physical runtime, evolutionary and random-search machinery, accounting, recovery and independent verification exist. | Implementation does not imply useful behavior. |
| **Completed authority evidence** | All 320 allowed decoder calls completed. All four directions passed the declared influence test in Jar and Clutter, and same-noise identity pairs matched. | This does not show better trajectories, safer outcomes or transfer. |
| **Completed development evidence** | Four unmatched development episodes exercised the physical path. One Clutter negative-bias episode safely completed, while the other three did not requested-complete. | Different tasks and seeds make these episodes unsuitable for estimating a bias advantage. |
| **Completed shared initialization** | Eight Jar training episodes gave the two optimizers identical starting data. All eight reached 2,000 controls. | There were zero requested completions, so this panel establishes no safe-completion benefit. |
| **Work in progress** | The frozen Jar EA versus random-search descendants have begun, with finalist sealing and protected checking planned. | No winner or evolutionary advantage can be claimed before the comparison and independent checking finish. |
| **Implemented but unfunded comparison interface** | `ParameterReinforce` can update a bounded distribution over the same 176 weights and passes CPU checks. | It has no physical evidence and is not the competent state-conditioned latent SAC baseline needed for a strong paper-level comparison. |
| **Untested future claims** | Conditional advantage, optimizer superiority, behavioral mechanism, cross-family transfer and the proposed latent extensions remain legitimate hypotheses. | None is currently an empirical result. |

The decoder audit is a real but limited achievement. On saved observations from both Jar and Clutter, bounded perturbations changed decoded executed-prefix arm commands enough to pass the predeclared direction test, while paired identity calls reproduced the same outputs and random-number transitions. In Jar, perturbed arm-command root-mean-square differences ranged from about 0.0011 to 0.0064 radians; in Clutter, from about 0.0007 to 0.0070 radians. These changes show that the controller is wired into the intended mechanism and has numerical authority. **They do not show that the altered command survives closed-loop feedback, reaches a better contact state, completes a task or avoids a violation.** ([Current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

The shared initialization panel is equally important to interpret honestly. Native, positive bias, negative bias and a random conditional controller each ran on two Jar training cases. All eight episodes timed out at the 2,000-control horizon with no requested or raw benchmark completion. Their violation counts were 1 of 2, 1 of 2, 0 of 2 and 2 of 2 respectively. The negative bias led the scalar training score because it avoided violations, but neither of its episodes grasped the jar and one never contacted it. That is **lower measured violation with noncompletion, not improved safe completion**. Initial robot inputs matched across nominal pairs, yet both camera images differed in all six other-controller comparisons with native, even though recorded noise streams matched. Those facts make the panel valid shared training data but not a clean set of identical physical counterfactuals. ([Current pilot report](../docs/research/reports/ManiGuard%20latent%20evolution%20pilot.md))

Several unknowns can therefore lead to scientifically different failures. The perturbations may be numerically visible but behaviorally too weak. The frozen Jar policy may have a competence floor that no small controller can repair. Useful behavior may exist but be difficult to find under sparse, noisy episode feedback. The four basis directions may be wrong, or useful directions may not align across separately fine-tuned family checkpoints. A controller may reduce motion or contact and thereby reduce violations while increasing unfinished episodes. Evolution may find a good controller but fail to outperform fixed bias, random search or ordinary learning once costs are matched. A positive result on inspected Jar tasks may disappear on protected cases or another family. A timeout alone distinguishes none of these explanations; trajectory evidence is needed. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [safe manipulation review](../docs/research/reports/Safe%20manipulation%20progress%20and%20robustness.md))

These distinctions make negative results valuable. If steering changes nothing meaningful, the interface lacks useful authority. If steering helps but a fixed bias wins, the representation may be useful while conditioning is unnecessary. If conditional adaptation helps but random search or ordinary learning ties evolution, the adapter survives and the evolutionary claim fails. If separate controllers work but one shared controller does not, the method may be reusable without being transferable. If all methods remain unfinished, the most likely next question concerns base-policy competence rather than optimizer choice. The research objective is a defensible answer, not an evolutionary victory. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

## The contribution depends on explanation and transfer

This project belongs to safe reinforcement learning in the broad sense because it studies adaptive control under both task and physical-safety outcomes, and because ordinary learning is an essential comparator. It is not currently fine-tuning the base policy with a standard safe-RL algorithm. Its pilot objective also contains an explicit violation penalty of -2, so it should not be described as penalty-free learning. The defensible claim is that candidates are selected from direct physical specification outcomes while every raw outcome remains visible. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md); [controller implementation](../experiments/helpers/maniguard_latent.py))

Latent world models are part of the project's intellectual history, not the present machinery. Earlier Push-T, Walker, LeWM and UNISafe investigations raised important questions about prediction, readout error, imagined rollouts and safety filters. Here, “latent steering” means changing a frozen action generator's sampling input and using frozen image and text representations. The controller does not learn a dynamics model, imagine futures or rank action candidates with a new world model. Online evolutionary action search with a learned scorer or world model is listed as a later, higher-dependency avenue, not the primary route. ([Repository authority](../AGENTS.md); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

ROSARL is also context rather than an implemented module. It contributes to the broader safe-RL discussion about reward design for unsafe terminal outcomes under particular assumptions. It does not itself predict hidden manipulation hazards or guarantee safe intermediate exploration, and no measured ROSARL-specific ManiGuard deficit currently justifies integrating it. Likewise, the current study does not assume a learned safety filter, action replacement or task continuation after intervention. Adding any of those components would answer a different and larger question. ([Evolutionary opportunities review](../docs/research/reports/Evolutionary%20opportunities%20for%20safe%20robotics.md); [repository authority](../AGENTS.md))

Evolution is central because the complete controller, contact-rich simulator and temporal outcome labels can be treated as a black box. Candidate weights can be scored without differentiating through robot physics. That explains why an evolutionary algorithm is relevant, but not why it should win: ordinary reinforcement learning also handles nondifferentiable environments. The evolutionary contribution must therefore be visible in evidence about retention, mutation, lineages, behavioral change and performance per total interaction. A conventional EA can support a good empirical paper if it reveals a reproducible physical effect and survives strong comparisons. Merely swapping in an EA library is not novelty. ([Safe manipulation review](../docs/research/reports/Safe%20manipulation%20progress%20and%20robustness.md); [current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

Close precedents already exist for state-conditioned noise steering, frozen pi0.5 steering, frozen-VLA evolutionary search and critic-free evolutionary VLA improvement. The intended novelty is consequently the **tested combination** of a wholly frozen family policy, one reusable compact observation- and instruction-conditioned controller, direct physical specification outcomes, complete interaction accounting, an explained safe-completion benefit, an evolutionary advantage over fair alternatives, and evidence about transfer. This is a contribution to establish, not a novelty fact already earned by combining familiar ingredients. A reserved Jar improvement would still be a legitimate narrow contribution, but it would not establish whole-suite dominance, real-robot safety or a universal guarantee. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

The primary untested extensions stay close to the native-noise idea. Bias-and-scale steering would change both the center and spread of the policy's sampling distribution. Persistence would carry part of one steering choice into the next query. Response-informed coordinates would build the basis from directions that measurably change decoded actions. Small internal activation gates would modulate selected frozen-model activations associated with outcomes. Each adds a separate hypothesis and control: two scalar knobs need grid baselines, persistence needs reset and memoryless controls, response magnitude proves influence rather than usefulness, and outcome-associated activations prove correlation rather than causality. If native-noise steering cannot produce relevant bounded alternatives within its declared allowance, a bounded direct-action residual is the designated representation fallback. Guarded subgoal programs, quality-diversity repertoires, evolutionary scene acquisition and online search with a learned scorer have more prerequisites and are not current components. ([Current direction](../Evolutionary%20improvement%20on%20ManiGuard.md))

The Feynman test is whether Sachin can explain each boundary in ordinary language and predict what each possible result means. The following questions expose gaps more effectively than repeating terminology.

| Self-test | An answer that shows understanding |
| --- | --- |
| Explain the project without saying “latent,” “VLA,” “EA” or “safe RL.” | “I keep the robot's main brain fixed, learn a small rule that nudges how it chooses among its possible moves, and test whether keeping and varying good rules beats simpler alternatives on both finishing and breaking rules.” |
| Who acts, and who only judges? | The frozen robot policy plus compact controller generate actions. The ManiGuard monitor watches the physical trace and labels violations. |
| What exactly evolves? | The 176 bounded weights in \(W\), not the base policy, camera encoder, monitor or task definition. |
| Why is this not random shaking? | The controller adds a structured, scene-conditioned offset to the policy's own starting noise before coordinated actions are decoded. |
| What did the 320-call audit prove? | The four bounded directions reproducibly influence decoded prefixes in Jar and Clutter. It did not prove useful physical behavior, safety or transfer. |
| Why must fixed bias be included? | It tests whether one simple constant correction explains the gain, as happened in the earlier UNISafe study. |
| Why must random search and ordinary learning be included? | Random search tests whether inheritance and local mutation matter. Ordinary learning tests whether evolution is preferable to another competent way of learning the same controller. |
| If fixed bias wins, what survives? | Native-noise steering may still be useful, but observation conditioning and evolutionary superiority are not supported. |
| Why are zero violations not enough? | The robot may have done nothing, and a finite sample with zero observed events still permits a nonzero true violation rate. Completion, violations and unfinished outcomes must be shown together. |
| What is the strongest transfer test? | Freeze one controller learned without a family, pair it with that held-out family's own frozen specialist policy, and test it on reserved tasks. This is adapter transfer, not one universal robot policy. |
| When is the direct residual fallback justified? | After a frozen, finite audit shows that bounded native-noise steering lacks relevant behavioral authority, not simply because one evolutionary score is poor. |

## Conclusion

The deepest idea is not “use evolution to make a robot safe.” It is **test whether a small shared rule can reliably select better behavior already latent in frozen specialist policies, then identify whether evolution is responsible for finding that rule efficiently**. This framing turns one vague claim into a sequence of falsifiable questions and keeps useful partial results alive even when the strongest hypothesis fails.

The current evidence has crossed the first engineering threshold: the controller is real, its identity path is protected, and its bounded directions influence the intended frozen decoders. It has not crossed the decisive scientific threshold of improved requested safe completion on independent physical cases. Finishing the protected Jar comparison, adding competent ordinary learning and optimizer replication, and only then testing a frozen shared controller across families is the path from a working mechanism to a defensible contribution.
